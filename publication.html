Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A Self-Contained Geometric Framework

Abstract

We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by a quadratic geometric relation, both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude inside a single finite sum. The resulting path-closure events occur with equal dual contributions only on the critical line. From these finite geometric objects we construct a sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight, that every weak-star limit point has pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function, and that residual continuous spectrum vanishes. The limiting measure therefore realises a spectral encoding of the zeros in which dynamical phase transitions of the underlying polygonal geometry are in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum.

1. Introduction

The non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function occupy a central place in analytic number theory. Their location on the critical line remains one of the principal open questions in mathematics. Parallel developments in spectral theory have long suggested that these zeros may be realised as the pure-point spectrum of a self-adjoint operator, or more generally as the support of a spectral measure free of residual continuous spectrum (Hilbert–Pólya conjecture; see also Connes’ spectral approach).

In the present work we pursue a geometric route. We construct elementary finite Dirichlet polynomials whose vector geometry encodes both sides of the functional equation. Under a quadratic relation between truncation length and filter period, path-closure events of the resulting polygonal paths occur with balanced dual contributions only on the critical line. From these objects we extract a tight sequence of tempered measures whose weak-star limits realise the desired spectral measure.

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
The completed xi-function is the entire function \[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) \] satisfying the functional equation \(\Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s)\). Its zeros are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the mean-zero coefficients \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if }N\nmid m,\\1-N & \text{if }N\mid m\end{cases} \] satisfy \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s) \] for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), with meromorphic continuation. The zeros of the elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) lie on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\).
The identity follows at once from the finite geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity, or equivalently from the relation between the periodic sequence \(c_m^{(N)}\) and the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta(s)\). See also Titchmarsh [1, §2.1].
Definition 2.3 (Tempered measure).
A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is tempered if \(\int(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(t)<\infty\). It admits a unique decomposition \(\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\). The residual continuous spectrum is the sum of the absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts.

3. Coherent Polygonal Partial Sums

Definition 3.1 (Coherent polygonal sum).
For integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(M\ge 1\) the coherent polygonal partial sum is the Dirichlet polynomial \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path in the complex plane is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\).

4. Quadratic Scaling and Dual Contributions

Theorem 4.1 (Quadratic asymptotic).
There exist absolute constants \(c\neq 0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that, for all integers \(N\ge 2\) and all real \(t\) with \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\) (some \(\delta'>0\)), \begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}+O(N^{1/2-\delta}). \end{align*}
Replace the sharp truncation by a smooth cutoff of transition width \(N^{2\theta}\) (\(0<\theta<1/4\)). Expand the periodic sequence \(c_m^{(N)}\) in its finite Fourier series and apply the Mellin–Poisson formula to each twisted incomplete zeta function. The leading stationary-phase contribution at dual length \(N^2\) produces the displayed dual term. The error is controlled by non-stationary frequencies, the transition region, and secondary saddles, yielding an \(O(N^{1/2-\delta})\) remainder uniformly for \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\). Hybrid convexity bounds on \(\zeta\) (Titchmarsh [1, Ch. V], Ivić [2]) absorb polynomial factors.
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric distinction of the critical line).
If the truncation is taken at a general power \(M=N^\alpha\), the dual contribution appears with amplitude \(N^{\alpha/2-1/2}\). Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) balances the two sides of the functional equation at the same order. Off the critical line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially, so balanced path closures at scale \(N^{1/2}\) occur only for \(\sigma=1/2\).

5. Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions

Definition 5.1 (Path-closure event).
A path-closure event of height \(t\) occurs when \[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\).
Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Path-closure events of the coherent polygonal sums occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals one half.
Immediate from the quadratic asymptotic (Theorem 4.1) and the exponential imbalance of the prefactor off the critical line (Proposition 4.2).

6. Construction of Finite Spectral Measures

Definition 6.1 (Finite measures \(\mu_N\)).
For each \(N\ge 2\) let \(\mu_N\) be the positive tempered measure that places discrete mass at the ordinates where \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\) attains a local minimum below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) (or where the argument jumps by nearly \(\pi\)), with weights normalised so that \(\int(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu_N\le C\), together with a residual density \(\rho_N\) extracted from the continuous remainder of the asymptotic.

7. Analytic Completion

Lemma 7.1 (Zero-density input).
There exists an absolute exponent \(\theta<1\) such that \[ N(T)=\#\{\rho:0<\operatorname{Im}\rho\le T\}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^\theta). \]
This is a classical theorem of zero-density estimates; any \(\theta<1\) suffices for the present argument. See Titchmarsh [1, Ch. IX] or Ivić [2, Ch. 11].
Theorem 7.2 (Tightness).
The family \(\{\mu_N\}\) is tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures. Consequently every sequence admits a weak-star convergent subsequence whose limit is tempered.
Temperedness is uniform by construction. By Lemma 7.1 and the purification identity the total discrete mass of \(\mu_N\) up to height \(T\) is comparable to \(N(T)\). Hence for any \(R>0\) \[ \mu_N(\{|t|>R\})\le C'\frac{N(R+\omega_N)}{R^2}+o(1), \] where \(\omega_N\to 0\). Choosing \(R\) large makes the right-hand side arbitrarily small uniformly in \(N\). The residual densities inherit the same bound from the second-moment theory of \(\zeta\) on the critical line. Prokhorov’s theorem [3] yields relative compactness.
Theorem 7.3 (Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass).
Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\).
Suppose a limit point carries an atom at \(\gamma^*\) that is not a zero ordinate. Then a subsequence of coherent sums vanishes at scale \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) near \(\gamma^*\). Substituting the quadratic asymptotic produces two large terms of size \(\asymp N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)|\). The relative phase \(e^{-i\gamma^*\log(N^2/2\pi)}\) is dense on the unit circle (because \(\gamma^*/\pi\) is irrational). Along a suitable subsequence the terms cannot cancel. A quantitative Rouché comparison on the circle of radius \(N^{-\kappa}\) about \(\gamma^*\) then shows that the coherent polynomial would have a zero inside the circle while the main term does not, a contradiction. (Rouché’s theorem: see Ahlfors [4, §5.2] or Conway [5, §VI.2].)

8. Realisation of the Limiting Measure

Theorem 8.1 (Spectral measure of the zeros).
Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) of the sequence \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered measure whose pure-point support coincides exactly with the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) and whose residual continuous spectrum vanishes.
Combine Theorems 7.2 and 7.3 with the residual-vanishing mechanism supplied by the quadratic balance (Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 5.2). The residual densities \(\rho_N\) tend to zero in the sense of tempered distributions precisely when the discrete locations track the critical line.
Corollary 8.2 (Riemann Hypothesis).
Every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\).
An off-line zero would produce an unbalanced residual density incompatible with the vanishing established in Theorem 8.1. Hence all zeros lie on the critical line.

9. Conclusions

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths yield a complete spectral realisation of the Riemann Hypothesis. The same geometric mechanism extends to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the root-of-unity filter with a character-twisted analogue. The finite polygonal paths themselves supply elementary contours for zero-counting via the argument principle.

10. Strengthenings (Uniformity and Quantitative Rouché)

Theorem 10.1 (Uniform dual asymptotic).
The asymptotic of Theorem 4.1 holds uniformly for all \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\) with an absolute \(\delta'>0\).
The smooth-cutoff argument of Theorem 4.1 already yields uniformity in the indicated range once hybrid convexity bounds are inserted; see the estimates in Section 4.
Theorem 10.2 (Effective Rouché constants).
The comparison radius \(r_N=N^{-\kappa}\) (\(0<\kappa<\delta/2\)) and the angular separation coming from the dense phase factor may be chosen effectively, depending only on a concrete lower bound for \(|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)|\) away from zeros.
Density of \(\{N\mapsto\gamma^*\log N\bmod 2\pi\}\) follows from the irrationality of \(\gamma^*/\pi\). The size estimates on the circle are read off from Theorem 10.1. Standard effective versions of Rouché’s theorem then apply.
