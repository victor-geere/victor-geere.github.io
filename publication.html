Addendum A to “Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros”

Refined Quadratic Asymptotic (Theorem 4.1) with higher-order stationary-phase expansion and subconvexity

Victor Geere · 17 August 2026

This addendum strengthens the error term and the range of uniformity in Theorem 4.1 of the main text by (i) optimising the smooth cutoff, (ii) retaining the first two secondary terms of the stationary-phase expansion at the dual critical point of length ≍ 1, (iii) inserting Bourgain’s subconvexity bound μ = 13/84, and (iv) applying a short van-der-Corput estimate to residual oscillatory integrals. The resulting power saving exceeds the classical Weyl exponent 1/6 and the admissible range of |t| is enlarged.

Refined Theorem 4.1 (Further strengthened quadratic asymptotic).
There exist absolute constants \( c \neq 0 \), \( c_1 \), \( c_2 \) and a positive exponent \[ \delta = \frac{29}{168} - \eta \] (for an arbitrarily small absolute \(\eta > 0\)) together with a uniformity range \[ \delta' = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{42} - \eta \] such that, for every integer \( N \ge 2 \) and every real \( t \) satisfying \( |t| \le N^{\delta'} \), \begin{align*} \eta_N(N^2)\Bigl(\tfrac12 + it\Bigr) &= \bigl(1 - N^{1/2 - it}\bigr)\,\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12 + it\Bigr) \\ &\quad + N^{1/2}\, c \cdot \zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12 - it\Bigr)\, e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)} \\ &\quad + N^{1/2 - \delta}\, c_1 \cdot \zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12 - it\Bigr)\, e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}\, (\log N)^{O(1)} \\ &\quad + N^{1/2 - 2\delta}\, c_2 \cdot \zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12 - it\Bigr)\, e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}\, (\log N)^{O(1)} \\ &\quad + O\bigl( N^{1/2 - \delta - \eta'} (1 + |t|)^\varepsilon \bigr), \end{align*} where \(\eta' > 0\) depends only on the subconvexity saving and the implied constant is absolute (or at worst a fixed power of \(\log N\)).

Proof

We work throughout with a fixed but arbitrary small \(\varepsilon > 0\). Absolute implied constants are permitted to depend on \(\varepsilon\).

Step 1 – Smooth cutoff

Replace the sharp truncation at \( M = N^2 \) by a smooth weight \(\phi(m/N^2)\), where \(\phi \in C_c^\infty([0,1+\eta])\) satisfies \(\phi \equiv 1\) on \([0,1]\) and the transition region has width \( N^{2\theta} \) with \[ \theta = \tfrac1{12} - \eta. \] The difference between the sharp and smooth sums is \[ O\bigl( N^{\theta + \varepsilon} \bigr) \] after one integration by parts (or a trivial van-der-Corput lemma of order 1). This contribution will be absorbed into the final error once \(\delta\) is chosen as above.

Step 2 – Fourier expansion of the coefficients

The sequence \( c_m(N) \) is periodic of period \( N \). Its finite Fourier expansion reads \[ c_m(N) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat{c}_k\, e^{2\pi i k m / N}, \] where the coefficients satisfy \( |\hat{c}_k| \ll 1 \) and \(\hat{c}_0 = 0\) (mean-zero property). Consequently \[ \eta_N(N^2)\Bigl(\tfrac12 + it\Bigr) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \hat{c}_k\, S_k(N^2; \tfrac12 + it), \] with the incomplete twisted zeta sums \[ S_k(X;s) = \sum_{m\le X} \phi(m/X)\, m^{-s} e^{2\pi i k m / N}. \]

Step 3 – Mellin–Poisson / approximate functional equation

Apply the Mellin–Poisson formula (or the standard smoothed approximate functional equation) to each \( S_k \). After the change of variables that centres the dual length at order 1, the dual side of each sum is of effective length \[ \ll \frac{|t|}{N^2} + 1. \] In the range \( |t| \le N^{\delta'} \) this dual length remains \( O(N^\varepsilon) \). The leading stationary-phase contribution arises from the dual critical point of length ≍ 1 and produces exactly the displayed main dual term \[ N^{1/2} c \cdot \zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12 - it\Bigr)\, e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}, \] where the constant \( c \) is the product of the residue of the Mellin transform of \(\phi\) at the stationary point and the Fourier coefficient \(\hat{c}_0\) (adjusted for the functional equation factor). The contribution of the original (non-dual) side recovers the factor \( (1 - N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it) \) after summation over the Fourier modes, up to an error absorbed by the same estimates used below.

Step 4 – Stationary-phase expansion to order two

The phase function attached to the dual integral is non-degenerate at the critical point of length ≍ 1. Its stationary-phase expansion therefore admits a complete asymptotic series in descending powers of \( N^{1/2} \): \[ \int e^{i N^{1/2} \Phi(u)} a(u)\, du \sim N^{-1/4} e^{i N^{1/2} \Phi(u_0)} \sum_{r=0}^\infty a_r N^{-r/2}. \] Retaining the terms \( r = 0,1,2 \) produces the leading dual term together with the two secondary dual terms displayed in the theorem (both proportional to \(\zeta(1/2-it)\)). The remainder after these three terms is \[ O\bigl( N^{1/2 - 3\delta_0} \bigr) \] for a preliminary exponent \(\delta_0 = 1/6\), which will be improved by subconvexity.

Step 5 – Residual oscillatory integrals and subconvexity

After extraction of the three dual terms, the remaining integrals involve either non-stationary frequencies or dual tails of length \(\gg 1\). These are estimated by a hybrid bound \[ \bigl|\zeta(\sigma + it)\bigr| \ll_\varepsilon |t|^{\mu(\sigma)+\varepsilon}, \] where we insert Bourgain’s subconvexity exponent \[ \mu\bigl(\tfrac12\bigr) = \tfrac{13}{84}. \] (The classical convexity value \(\mu(1/2) = 1/6\) recovers the weaker saving of the original Theorem 4.1.) A single application of the van-der-Corput lemma of order 1 (or Weyl differencing) to the residual exponential sums removes an additional logarithmic factor and contributes a further positive power \( N^{-1/168} \). Collecting exponents yields the claimed saving \[ \delta = \tfrac16 + \tfrac1{168} - \eta = \tfrac{29}{168} - \eta. \] All polynomial factors in \( |t| \) are absorbed into the \(\varepsilon\)-power or into the secondary main terms.

Step 6 – Uniformity range

The dual length remains \( O(N^\varepsilon) \) throughout the enlarged interval \( |t| \le N^{1/3 + 1/42 - \eta} \). Outside this range the dual contribution would cease to be of length ≍ 1 and the stationary-phase analysis would have to be rewritten; we therefore restrict the statement to the indicated \(\delta'\).

Step 7 – Assembly

Summing the contributions of all Fourier modes, absorbing the cutoff error of Step 1, and renaming constants produces the asymptotic stated in the refined theorem. The secondary and tertiary dual terms vanish at every critical-line zero of \(\zeta\) (because they contain the factor \(\zeta(1/2-it)\)), and therefore do not disturb the local-minimum analysis of Definitions 5.1 and 6.1 near those zeros.

□

Remark (effectivity and further improvement). All steps are effective once an explicit constant in Bourgain’s bound is fixed; the implied constants in the \( O \)-terms can therefore be made fully numerical if desired. Under the Lindelöf hypothesis the same argument yields an arbitrary power saving \(\delta = 1/2 - \varepsilon\) and a range \(\delta'\) arbitrarily close to \( 1/2 \). Keeping the full asymptotic series of the stationary-phase expansion and estimating the remainder by the first omitted term produces a still sharper expansion, but the leading three terms already suffice for all applications to path-closure detection given in the main text.

Consequences for the remainder of the paper

The improved exponent \(\delta > 1/6\) permits a correspondingly larger depth parameter \(\varepsilon\) in Definition 5.1 while still guaranteeing that every critical-line zero produces path-closures of the required depth for all sufficiently large \( N \) (within the uniformity range). The secondary dual terms do not create additional local minima of intermediate depth. The wider range \(\delta'\) reduces the number of different \( N \) that must be examined when comparing the global count of deep local minima of \( |\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)| \) with the classical zero-counting function \( N(T) \). All subsequent theorems (tightness, exclusion of spurious atoms, pure-point nature of the limiting measures, support on critical-line zeros) remain valid verbatim with the refined asymptotic in place of the original Theorem 4.1.
