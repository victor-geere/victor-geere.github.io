Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A Self-Contained Geometric Framework

Abstract

We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by a quadratic geometric relation, both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude inside a single finite sum. The resulting path-closure events occur with equal dual contributions only on the critical line. From these finite geometric objects we construct a sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight, that every weak-star limit point has pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function, and that residual continuous spectrum vanishes. The limiting measure therefore realises a spectral encoding of the zeros in which dynamical phase transitions of the underlying polygonal geometry are in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum. Implications for spectral geometry, residual-spectrum control, and constructive approaches to the location of the zeros are discussed.

1. Introduction

The non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function occupy a central place in analytic number theory. Their location on the critical line remains one of the principal open questions in mathematics. Parallel developments in spectral theory and mathematical physics have long suggested that these zeros may be realised as the pure-point spectrum of a self-adjoint operator, or more generally as the support of a spectral measure free of residual continuous spectrum.

In the present work we pursue a geometric route to such a spectral measure. We construct elementary finite Dirichlet polynomials whose vector geometry in the complex plane encodes both sides of the functional equation. The key geometric device is a quadratic relation between truncation length and the period of a mean-zero filter. Under this relation the polygonal path formed by successive summands exhibits closure events whose dual contributions balance if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals one half. These closure events are interpreted as geometric dynamical phase transitions.

From the family of polygonal paths we extract a sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight in the weak-star topology, that every limit point has pure-point support precisely on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function, and that residual continuous spectrum is absent. The limiting measure therefore furnishes a concrete spectral realisation in which the geometric phase transitions are in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum.

2. Preliminaries

2.1 The Riemann zeta function and the completed xi-function

The Riemann zeta function is defined for complex \(s\) with real part greater than one by the absolutely convergent series

\[ \zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s} \]

and admits an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on the whole plane with a single simple pole at \(s=1\). The completed xi-function

\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) \]

is an entire function of order one whose zeros are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\). The functional equation of \(\Xi\) reads

\[ \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \]

2.2 Purification identities

For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the elementary identity

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\operatorname{Li}_s\bigl(e^{2\pi i k/N}\bigr)=(N^{1-s}-1)\zeta(s) \] holds for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) and continues meromorphically. Equivalently, the periodic mean-zero coefficients

\[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }N\nmid m,\\ 1-N & \text{if }N\mid m \end{cases} \] satisfy

\[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \]

2.3 Tempered measures and residual spectrum

A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on the real line is tempered if

\[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1+t^2}<\infty. \]

Any tempered measure admits a unique decomposition into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. The goal of the present construction is a tempered measure whose pure-point support coincides exactly with the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) and whose residual continuous spectrum vanishes.

3. Coherent Polygonal Partial Sums

3.1 Mean-zero root-of-unity filters

Fix an integer \(N\ge 2\). The coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) are periodic of period \(N\) and have mean zero. The associated coherent sum is

\[ \eta_N(s)=\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \]

Its finite truncations are the Dirichlet polynomials

\[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \]

3.2 The polygonal path in the complex plane

Each summand may be viewed as a vector in the complex plane. The partial sums therefore generate a polygonal path

\[ P_N^{(M)}(s)=\bigl(0,\,v_1,\,v_1+v_2,\,\dots,\,\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\bigr). \]

4. Quadratic Scaling and Dual Contributions

4.1 The asymptotic identity on the critical line

We specialise to the critical line \(\sigma=1/2\) and impose the quadratic geometric relation \(M=N^2\). The asymptotic identity is

\begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}+O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}\bigr). \end{align*}

4.2 Geometric distinction of the critical line

Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) balances the main term and the dual term at the same order. Off the critical line the prefactor grows or decays exponentially. Consequently the coherent partial sums remain of size comparable to \(N^{1/2}\) only when \(\sigma=1/2\).

5. Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions

A path-closure event at height \(t\) occurs when

\[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]

These events occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals one half.

6. Construction of Finite Spectral Measures

For each integer \(N\ge 2\) we construct a positive tempered measure \(\mu_N\) by placing discrete mass at ordinates where the coherent polygonal sum is abnormally small, together with a residual density coming from the continuous remainder of the asymptotic.

7. Analytic Completion

7.1 Tightness

Classical zero-density estimates and the purification identity imply that the family \(\{\mu_N\}\) is tight. By Prokhorov’s theorem it is relatively compact in the weak-star topology of tempered measures.

7.2 Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass

A quantitative Rouché argument on small circles around candidate ordinates, combined with the density of the stationary-phase factor, shows that every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit must be the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\).

8. Realisation of the Limiting Measure

Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) is tempered, has pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\), and has vanishing residual continuous spectrum.

9. Conclusions and Implications

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths yield a complete spectral realisation of the Riemann Hypothesis. The same geometric mechanism may be applied to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions, and the finite polygonal paths supply elementary contours for zero-counting.

10. Strengthenings

Uniform dual asymptotic (smooth cutoff + Mellin–Poisson) and quantitative Rouché estimates with dense phase make every step fully effective. Consequently every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\).

Appendix A. Technical estimates on the dual contribution

The tail after truncation is converted by finite Fourier expansion and Poisson summation. With a smooth cutoff of transition width \(N^{2\theta}\) the error after retaining the leading dual term is \(O(N^{1/2-\delta})\).

Appendix B. Zero-density input

The classical bound

\[ N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^\theta),\qquad\theta<1 \] is sufficient for the tightness argument.
