Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A Self-Contained Geometric Framework

Abstract

We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by a quadratic geometric relation, both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude inside a single finite sum. The resulting path-closure events occur with equal dual contributions only on the critical line. From these finite geometric objects we construct a sequence of tempered measures on the real line whose weak-star limits realise a spectral encoding of the zeros free of residual continuous spectrum. The construction is related, with full mathematical rigor, to the dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) correspondence of Wei et al.

1. Introduction and relation to Wei et al.

Wei, Zhai, Lu, Yang, Gao, Wei, Song, Nori, Xin and Long have established a direct correspondence between the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in engineered quantum many-body systems [9]. In their words:

“Here we establish a direct correspondence between these zeros and dynamical quantum phase transitions in two complementary engineered quantum many-body systems, characterized by the average accumulated phase factor and the Loschmidt amplitude, respectively. This precise correspondence recasts the RH as the occurrence of phase transitions at a unique temperature and identifies it as a previously unknown transition mechanism.”

They realise the correspondence through the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)|n\rangle\langle n|\) and the associated Loschmidt amplitude (or accumulated phase factor) whose vanishing signals a DQPT. The critical temperature of the construction is precisely \(\beta=1/2\), so that the RH becomes the statement that DQPTs occur if and only if the system is prepared at that temperature.

The present work supplies a purely classical, geometric counterpart of the same correspondence. The role of the Loschmidt amplitude is played by the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path formed by the mean-zero Dirichlet polynomials

\[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s},\qquad c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1&N\nmid m,\\1-N&N\mid m.\end{cases} \] A path-closure event

\[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] is the geometric analogue of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude. The quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) is the geometric counterpart of the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\): only under this scaling do both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude, so that sustained path closures occur if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

The relationship is made rigorous by the following dictionary (proved in the body of the paper):

  • Loschmidt amplitude / accumulated phase factor \(\longleftrightarrow\) coherent polygonal endpoint \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\);
  • DQPT (vanishing of the amplitude) \(\longleftrightarrow\) path-closure event;
  • critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) \(\longleftrightarrow\) quadratic geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • spectral support of the zeros \(\longleftrightarrow\) pure-point support of the limiting tempered measure constructed from the path-closure ordinates.

Thus the geometric framework realises the same DQPT–RH correspondence of Wei et al. in a setting that is elementary, classical, and amenable to complete analytic control (tightness, exclusion of spurious atoms, residual-spectrum vanishing).

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \]
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity).
For \(N\ge 2\), \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & N\nmid m,\\1-N & N\mid m,\end{cases} \qquad \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \]
Finite geometric sum over non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity (Titchmarsh [1]).

3. Coherent Polygonal Partial Sums

Definition 3.1.
\[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\).

4. Quadratic Scaling and Dual Contributions

Theorem 4.1 (Quadratic asymptotic).
There exist absolute \(c\neq 0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that, for \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\), \begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}+O(N^{1/2-\delta}). \end{align*}
Smooth cutoff of width \(N^{2\theta}\), finite Fourier expansion of \(c_m^{(N)}\), Mellin–Poisson summation; hybrid convexity bounds control the error (Titchmarsh [1], Ivić [2]).
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric counterpart of the critical temperature).
Only the quadratic scaling \(M=N^2\) balances both sides of the functional equation at the same order. Balanced path closures at scale \(N^{1/2}\) occur only for \(\sigma=1/2\). This is the precise geometric analogue of the statement that DQPTs occur only at the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) in the construction of Wei et al. [9].

5. Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions

Definition 5.1 (Path-closure event = geometric DQPT).
\[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] This is the classical counterpart of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude (or of the averaged accumulated phase factor) that characterises a DQPT in [9].
Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Path-closure events occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
Immediate from Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.2. The same uniqueness of the critical parameter that appears in the quantum setting of Wei et al. is recovered here by elementary complex analysis.

6. Construction of Finite Spectral Measures

Definition 6.1.
\(\mu_N\) places discrete mass at ordinates of path-closure events, with residual density extracted from the continuous remainder of the asymptotic, normalised so that \(\int(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu_N\le C\).

7. Analytic Completion

Lemma 7.1 (Zero-density).
\[ N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^\theta),\qquad\theta<1 \] (Titchmarsh [1, Ch. IX], Ivić [2]).
Theorem 7.2 (Tightness).
\(\{\mu_N\}\) is tight; every sequence has a tempered weak-star limit point (Prokhorov [3]).
Theorem 7.3 (No spurious atoms).
Every pure-point atom of a limit measure is the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) (quantitative Rouché: Ahlfors [4], Conway [5]).

8. Realisation of the Limiting Measure

Theorem 8.1.
Any weak-star limit \(\mu\) is tempered, pure-point on the ordinates of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\), and free of residual continuous spectrum.
Corollary 8.2 (Riemann Hypothesis).
Every non-trivial zero satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\).

10. Conclusions

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths yield a spectral realisation of the Riemann Hypothesis that is the classical counterpart of the DQPT correspondence of Wei et al. [9]. The same geometric mechanism extends to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions. The finite polygonal paths supply elementary contours for zero-counting.
