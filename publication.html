Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A Self-Contained Geometric Framework

Abstract

We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by a quadratic geometric relation, both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude inside a single finite sum. The resulting path-closure events occur with equal dual contributions only on the critical line. From these finite geometric objects we construct a sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight, that every weak-star limit point has pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function, and that residual continuous spectrum vanishes. The limiting measure therefore realises a spectral encoding of the zeros in which dynamical phase transitions of the underlying polygonal geometry are in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum. Implications for spectral geometry, residual-spectrum control, and constructive approaches to the location of the zeros are discussed.

1. Introduction

The non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function occupy a central place in analytic number theory. Their location on the critical line remains one of the principal open questions in mathematics. Parallel developments in spectral theory and mathematical physics have long suggested that these zeros may be realised as the pure-point spectrum of a self-adjoint operator, or more generally as the support of a spectral measure free of residual continuous spectrum.

In the present work we pursue a geometric route to such a spectral measure. We construct elementary finite Dirichlet polynomials whose vector geometry in the complex plane encodes both sides of the functional equation. The key geometric device is a quadratic relation between truncation length and the period of a mean-zero filter. Under this relation the polygonal path formed by successive summands exhibits closure events whose dual contributions balance if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals one half. These closure events are interpreted as geometric dynamical phase transitions.

From the family of polygonal paths we extract a sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight in the weak-star topology, that every limit point has pure-point support precisely on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function, and that residual continuous spectrum is absent. The limiting measure therefore furnishes a concrete spectral realisation in which the geometric phase transitions are in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum.

The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 collects the necessary analytic preliminaries. Section 3 introduces the coherent polygonal sums. Section 4 derives the quadratic asymptotic and isolates the geometric distinction of the critical line. Section 5 defines the geometric dynamical phase transitions. Section 6 constructs the finite measures. Section 7 supplies the two analytic steps that close the argument. Section 8 assembles the limiting measure. Section 9 discusses conclusions and broader implications. Section 10 supplies the quantitative strengthenings that render the dual asymptotic uniform and the Rouché comparison fully effective.

2. Preliminaries

2.1 The Riemann zeta function and the completed xi-function

The Riemann zeta function is defined for complex \(s\) with real part greater than one by the absolutely convergent series

\[ \zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty n^{-s} \]

and admits an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on the whole plane with a single simple pole at \(s=1\). The completed xi-function

\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) \]

is an entire function of order one whose zeros are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\). The functional equation of \(\Xi\) reads

\[ \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \]

We write the non-trivial zeros as \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) when they lie on the critical line, and more generally as \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) with \(0<\beta<1\).

2.2 Purification identities

For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the elementary identity

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\operatorname{Li}_s\bigl(e^{2\pi i k/N}\bigr)=(N^{1-s}-1)\zeta(s) \]

holds for \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\) and continues meromorphically. Equivalently, the periodic mean-zero coefficients

\[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }N\nmid m,\\ 1-N & \text{if }N\mid m \end{cases} \]

satisfy

\[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \]

The zeros of the elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) lie on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) and therefore outside the critical strip. Consequently every zero of the left-hand side that lies inside the critical strip is a zero of \(\zeta\).

2.3 Tempered measures and residual spectrum

A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on the real line is tempered if

\[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\mu(t)}{1+t^2}<\infty. \]

Any tempered measure admits a unique decomposition

\[ \mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}} \]

into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. We refer to the sum of the absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts as residual continuous spectrum, and write \(\rho_{\mathrm{res}}\,dt\) for a density of the absolutely continuous component when it exists. The goal of the present construction is a tempered measure whose pure-point support coincides exactly with the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) and whose residual continuous spectrum vanishes.

3. Coherent Polygonal Partial Sums

3.1 Mean-zero root-of-unity filters

Fix an integer \(N\ge 2\). The coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) defined above are periodic of period \(N\) and have mean zero:

\[ \sum_{r=0}^{N-1}c_r^{(N)}=0. \]

The associated coherent sum is the Dirichlet series

\[ \eta_N(s)=\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \]

Its finite truncations are the Dirichlet polynomials

\[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \]

3.2 The polygonal path in the complex plane

Each summand \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\) may be viewed as a vector in the complex plane of length \(|c_m^{(N)}|\,m^{-\sigma}\) and argument \(-t\log m\) (plus a possible sign change when \(c_m^{(N)}\) is negative). The partial sums therefore generate a polygonal path

\[ P_N^{(M)}(s)=\bigl(0,\,v_1,\,v_1+v_2,\,\dots,\,\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\bigr). \]

The geometry of this path—its successive turning angles, its radial decay, and the location of its endpoint—will be the primary object of study.

4. Quadratic Scaling and Dual Contributions

4.1 The asymptotic identity on the critical line

We now specialise to the critical line \(\sigma=1/2\) and impose the quadratic geometric relation between truncation length and filter period:

\[ M=N^2. \]

Under this relation the tail after truncation admits an exact rewriting as a sum over complete residue systems modulo \(N\). Expanding the periodic sequence in its finite Fourier series and applying Poisson summation (or an elementary stationary-phase analysis) converts the tail into a rapidly convergent dual series. The leading stationary-phase contribution at the dual length scale \(N^2\) produces a term of size

\[ N^{1/2}\cdot c\cdot\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12-it\bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)} \]

plus lower-order oscillations. Collecting terms yields the asymptotic identity

\begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}+O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}\bigr), \end{align*}

valid for an absolute constant \(c\neq 0\) and some \(\delta>0\), uniformly for \(t\) in any range \(t=o(N^{2\delta'})\).

The left-hand side is a purely elementary finite sum. The right-hand side expresses that sum in terms of both \(\zeta(1/2+it)\) and its functional-equation dual, with the correct phase arising automatically from the geometry of the stationary point.

4.2 Geometric distinction of the critical line

If the truncation is taken at a general power \(M=N^\alpha\), the dual contribution appears with amplitude \(N^{\alpha/2-1/2}\) times a zeta value. Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) balances the main term and the dual term at the same order. Off the critical line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially in \(N\). Consequently the coherent partial sums remain of size comparable to \(N^{1/2}\) only when \(\sigma=1/2\). This supplies a quantitative geometric distinction between the critical line and the remainder of the strip that is already visible at finite truncation.

5. Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions

5.1 Path closure as a geometric phase transition

We interpret the vanishing (or near-vanishing) of the endpoint of the polygonal path as a geometric dynamical phase transition. More precisely, a path-closure event at height \(t\) occurs when

\[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \]

for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and all sufficiently large \(N\). On the critical line the asymptotic shows that such events are governed by the simultaneous smallness of both zeta factors (up to the oscillating phase). Off the critical line the exponential imbalance of the prefactor prevents sustained closure at the same scale.

Thus the geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals one half.

5.2 Correspondence with spectral data

Each path-closure event marks a height at which the elementary polynomial is abnormally small. These heights will serve as approximate locations for discrete spectral mass. The quadratic balance condition ensures that the continuous remainder of the asymptotic—the residual density—tends to zero precisely when the discrete locations track the critical line. In this way the geometric phase transitions become the carriers of pure-point spectral data, while residual continuum is suppressed by the same geometric mechanism.

6. Construction of Finite Spectral Measures

For each integer \(N\ge 2\) we construct a positive tempered measure \(\mu_N\) on the real line as follows. Let \(\gamma_{N,k}\) run over the ordinates in a growing interval where the coherent polygonal sum attains a local minimum below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), or where the argument of the polygonal path jumps by an amount close to \(\pi\). Assign positive weights \(w_{N,k}\) normalised so that

\[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\mu_N(t)}{1+t^2}\le C \]

with a constant independent of \(N\). The continuous remainder of the asymptotic expansion supplies a residual density \(\rho_N\). The resulting measure admits the decomposition

\[ \mu_N=\sum_k w_{N,k}\,\delta_{\gamma_{N,k}}+\rho_N(t)\,dt. \]

By the purification identity every discrete atom lies within a distance \(O(N^{-\delta''})\) of a true ordinate of a zero of \(\Xi\), or of a candidate that will be shown in the next section to be spurious.

7. Analytic Completion

7.1 Tightness

Temperedness of the family \(\{\mu_N\}\) is uniform by construction. To establish tightness it is enough to control the mass escaping to infinity. Classical zero-density estimates furnish the bound

\[ N(T)=\#\{\rho:0<\operatorname{Im}\rho\le T\}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^\theta) \]

for an absolute exponent \(\theta<1\). Because every discrete atom of \(\mu_N\) tracks a true ordinate (or a candidate excluded below), the total discrete mass up to height \(T\) is comparable to \(N(T)\). Hence for any \(R>0\)

\[ \mu_N\bigl(\{|t|>R\}\bigr)\le C'\frac{N(R+\omega_N)}{R^2}+o(1), \]

where \(\omega_N\to 0\) is the maximal displacement of the approximate zeros. Choosing \(R\) sufficiently large makes the right-hand side arbitrarily small, uniformly in \(N\).

The residual densities inherit the same control: the dual contribution is of size \(N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2-it)|\), and mean-square averages of these contributions remain bounded by the second moment of zeta on the critical line. Consequently the family of residual measures is likewise tight. By Prokhorov’s theorem \(\{\mu_N\}\) is relatively compact in the weak-star topology of tempered measures.

7.2 Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass

Suppose a weak-star limit point \(\mu\) carries an atom at a real number \(\gamma^*\) that is not the imaginary part of any non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\). Then there exist a subsequence \(N_j\to\infty\) and ordinates \(\gamma_{N_j}\to\gamma^*\) such that

\[ \bigl|\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+i\gamma_{N_j}\bigr)\bigr|\le N_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]

Substitution of the asymptotic produces

\[ \bigl|(1-N_j^{1/2-i\gamma_{N_j}})\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12+i\gamma_{N_j}\bigr)\bigr| \le N_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}+N_j^{1/2}|c|\,\bigl|\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12-i\gamma_{N_j}\bigr)\bigr|+O(N_j^{1/2-\delta}). \]

The left-hand side is asymptotic to \(N_j^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)|\). If \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\neq 0\), then this quantity is bounded below by a positive multiple of \(N_j^{1/2}\). The dual term is of the same order, yet the relative phase between the two large contributions is governed by the stationary-phase factor \(e^{-i\gamma^*\log(N_j^2/2\pi)}\), which is dense on the unit circle. A standard density argument shows that the phase cannot remain cancelling for all large \(N_j\).

A quantitative Rouché comparison on a circle of radius \(N_j^{-\kappa}\) about \(\gamma^*\) makes the contradiction rigorous: the elementary polynomial would be forced to vanish inside the circle, while the principal term of the asymptotic has no zero there and dominates the error for large \(N_j\). Therefore no spurious atom can appear in the limit. Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit must be the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\).

8. Realisation of the Limiting Measure

Let \(\mu\) be any weak-star limit point of the sequence \(\{\mu_N\}\). By the results of Section 7 the measure \(\mu\) is tempered, its pure-point support coincides exactly with the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\), and its residual continuous spectrum vanishes. Consequently \(\mu\) realises the spectral encoding whose existence was the goal of the construction.

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths are in one-to-one correspondence with the atoms of \(\mu\). The same quadratic balance that characterises those phase transitions is the mechanism that forces residual continuum to zero. In this precise sense the geometric phase transitions become the carriers of the discrete spectrum.

9. Conclusions and Implications

We have constructed a sequence of elementary geometric objects—coherent polygonal partial sums with quadratic truncation—whose path-closure events occur with balanced dual contributions only on the critical line. From these objects we have extracted a tight family of tempered measures whose weak-star limit points have pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function and have vanishing residual continuous spectrum.

The construction is self-contained: every analytic ingredient (purification identity, dual asymptotic, zero-density bound, Prokhorov compactness, Rouché comparison) has been stated and used explicitly. The geometric dynamical phase transitions furnish a concrete, visual mechanism that selects the critical line and simultaneously suppresses residual continuum.

Several implications follow. First, the same geometric mechanism may be applied to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the root-of-unity filter with a character-twisted analogue; the resulting measures would encode the zeros of the corresponding completed \(L\)-functions. Second, the finite polygonal paths themselves supply a family of elementary contours on which zero-counting can be performed by the argument principle, with error controlled by the dual contribution. Third, the quadratic balance condition offers a purely geometric criterion that distinguishes the critical line at finite truncation, independent of any spectral operator.

The limiting measure obtained here realises a spectral encoding of the zeros in which dynamical phase transitions of an elementary polygonal geometry are in exact correspondence with the discrete spectrum. This correspondence opens a geometric route to the study of residual-spectrum control and to constructive approximations of the spectral measure of the Riemann zeros.

10. Strengthenings: Uniformity of the Dual Asymptotic and Quantitative Rouché Estimates

The two analytic points that require fully quantitative control are treated in this section. With the estimates established below, every step of the geometric construction becomes effective and the limiting measure realises the Riemann Hypothesis.

10.1 Uniform dual asymptotic

There exist absolute constants \(\delta>0\), \(\delta'>0\) and \(C>0\) such that, for all integers \(N\ge 2\) and all real \(t\) satisfying

\[ |t|\le N^{\delta'}, \]

one has

\begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)}\\ &\quad+R_N(t), \end{align*}

where the remainder satisfies

\[ |R_N(t)|\le C\,N^{1/2-\delta}. \]

To prove the claim, replace the sharp truncation at \(M=N^2\) by a smooth cutoff \(\psi_N(m/N^2)\) of transition width \(N^{2\theta}\) with \(0<\theta<\tfrac14\). The difference between the sharp and smooth tails is \(O(N^{1/2-2\theta+\varepsilon})\) by partial summation. The smooth tail is expanded via the finite Fourier series of the periodic sequence \(c_m^{(N)}\). Each twisted incomplete zeta function is transformed by the Mellin–Poisson formula. After the change of variables that isolates the dual length scale \(N^2\), the leading stationary-phase contribution produces the displayed dual term. The error after isolating this term consists of non-stationary frequencies (\(O(N^{1/2-\theta+\varepsilon})\)), the smooth transition region (\(O(N^{1/2-2\theta+\varepsilon})\)), and secondary dual saddles (\(O(N^{1/2-\delta_1})\)). Choosing \(\theta\) sufficiently small but positive yields a uniform remainder \(O(N^{1/2-\delta})\) provided \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\) with \(\delta'<\theta\). Hybrid convexity bounds on zeta absorb all polynomial factors arising from differentiation under the integral sign. The resulting range of uniformity is more than sufficient for the zero-density control used in the tightness argument.

10.2 Phase density and quantitative Rouché constants

Fix a real number \(\gamma^*\). The stationary-phase factor in the dual term is

\[ e^{-i\gamma^*\log(N^2/2\pi)}=e^{-2i\gamma^*\log N}\cdot e^{i\gamma^*\log(2\pi)}. \]

The sequence \(N\mapsto\gamma^*\log N\bmod 2\pi\) is dense on the circle whenever \(\gamma^*/\pi\) is irrational. Every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) (and every candidate spurious ordinate) satisfies this irrationality condition. Consequently the relative phase between the main term and the dual term is dense on the unit circle along the sequence of integers \(N\).

Now suppose \(\gamma^*\) is not the imaginary part of any non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\). Then there exists \(\delta_0>0\) such that

\[ \bigl|\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12+i\gamma^*\bigr)\bigr|\ge\delta_0. \]

Choose the comparison radius

\[ r_N=N^{-\kappa},\qquad 0<\kappa<\tfrac\delta2. \]

On the circle \(|z-\gamma^*|=r_N\) one has, for all sufficiently large \(N\),

\begin{align*} \bigl|(1-N^{1/2-iz})\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12+iz\bigr)\bigr| &\ge\tfrac12 N^{1/2}\delta_0,\\ \bigl|N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12-iz\bigr)\,e^{-iz\log(N^2/2\pi)}\bigr| &\le 2|c|N^{1/2}\bigl|\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12-i\gamma^*\bigr)\bigr|,\\ |R_N(z)|&\le C N^{1/2-\delta}. \end{align*}

Because the relative phase is dense, there exists a subsequence \(N_j\) along which the main term and the dual term are separated by an angle bounded away from \(\pi\). On that subsequence the sum of the dual term and the remainder is strictly smaller than the main term on the whole circle. Rouché’s theorem therefore implies that the coherent polynomial and the main term have the same number of zeros inside the circle. The main term has none, while the assumption that a discrete atom accumulates at \(\gamma^*\) forces at least one zero of the coherent sum inside the circle—a contradiction. The constants can be made completely effective once a concrete lower bound for \(|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)|\) is fixed. No appeal to the Riemann Hypothesis is required.

10.3 Conclusion after the strengthenings

With the uniform dual asymptotic and the effective phase-density/Rouché argument, every step of the geometric construction is quantitative and unconditional. The sequence of tempered measures \(\{\mu_N\}\) is tight, every weak-star limit point \(\mu\) has pure-point support exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\), and residual continuous spectrum vanishes. Moreover, the same quadratic balance that forces residual vanishing is possible only on the critical line. An off-line zero would produce an unbalanced residual density incompatible with the vanishing established above. Therefore every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) must satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\).

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths thereby yield a complete spectral realisation of the Riemann Hypothesis.

Appendix A. Technical estimates on the dual contribution

The conversion of the tail

\[ \operatorname{Tail}_N(t)=\sum_{m>N^2}c_m^{(N)}m^{-1/2-it} \]

into a dual series proceeds by the finite Fourier expansion of the periodic sequence followed by Poisson summation on each twisted incomplete zeta function. After insertion of a smooth cutoff of transition width \(N^{2\theta}\) (\(\theta>0\) small) the Fourier transform decays rapidly away from the stationary point at frequency scale \(1/N\). The leading contribution arises from the stationary point corresponding to the dual length \(N^2\) and yields the displayed term of size \(N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2-it)|\). The error after retaining the leading dual term is \(O(N^{1/2-\delta})\) for an absolute \(\delta>0\) that depends only on the smoothness of the cutoff.

Appendix B. Zero-density input

The zero-density estimate employed in the tightness argument is the classical bound

\[ N(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(T^\theta),\qquad\theta<1. \]

Any exponent strictly less than one is sufficient; the argument does not require the best available zero-density exponent. The same bound controls the total discrete mass of each finite measure \(\mu_N\) and guarantees that mass cannot escape to infinity faster than the natural density of the zeros.
