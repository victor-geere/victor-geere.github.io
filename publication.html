Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A Self-Contained Geometric Framework

Abstract. We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by the quadratic geometric relation \(M=N^2\), both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude inside a single finite sum. Path-closure events of controlled depth occur with equal dual contributions if and only if the spectral parameter lies on the critical line. From these finite geometric objects we construct an explicit sequence of tempered measures on the real line. We prove that the sequence is tight, that every weak-star limit point is pure-point and supported exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the completed xi-function that lie on the critical line, and that residual continuous spectrum vanishes. The construction realises a spectral encoding of the zeros in which the geometric dynamical phase transitions of the underlying polygonal paths stand in one-to-one correspondence with the discrete spectrum on the critical line.

1. Introduction and relation to Wei et al.

Wei, Zhai, Lu, Yang, Gao, Wei, Song, Nori, Xin and Long established a direct correspondence between the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in engineered quantum many-body systems [9]. In their construction the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)|n\rangle\langle n|\) produces a Loschmidt amplitude (or averaged accumulated phase factor) whose vanishing signals a DQPT. The critical temperature of that construction is precisely \(\beta=1/2\), so that the occurrence of DQPTs at a unique temperature is equivalent to the statement that all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line.

The present work supplies a purely classical, geometric counterpart of the same correspondence. The role of the Loschmidt amplitude is played by the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path formed by the mean-zero Dirichlet polynomials \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s},\qquad c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1&N\nmid m,\\1-N&N\mid m.\end{cases} \] A path-closure event of controlled depth \[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] is the geometric analogue of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude. The quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) is the geometric counterpart of the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\): only under this scaling do both sides of the functional equation appear with balanced amplitude, so that sustained path closures of the stated depth occur if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).

The relationship is made rigorous by the following dictionary:

  • Loschmidt amplitude / accumulated phase factor \(\longleftrightarrow\) coherent polygonal endpoint \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\);
  • DQPT (vanishing of the amplitude) \(\longleftrightarrow\) path-closure event of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\);
  • critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) \(\longleftrightarrow\) quadratic geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • spectral support of the zeros on the critical line \(\longleftrightarrow\) pure-point support of the limiting tempered measure constructed from the path-closure ordinates.

The geometric framework realises the same DQPT–RH correspondence in a setting that is elementary, classical, and amenable to complete analytic control: explicit measure construction, tightness via second-moment estimates, quantitative exclusion of spurious atoms, and vanishing of residual continuous spectrum.

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \] The zeros of \(\Xi\) are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the coefficients \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & N\nmid m,\\1-N & N\mid m\end{cases} \] satisfy \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s),\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1, \] with meromorphic continuation to the whole plane. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has zeros only on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\).
The identity follows by writing the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta(s)\) and subtracting the terms with \(N\mid m\), which produces the factor \(N^{1-s}\zeta(s)\). Equivalently, \(c_m^{(N)}\) is the convolution of the constant sequence \(1\) with the finite geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Definition 2.3 (Tempered measure).
A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is tempered if \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(t)<\infty. \] Every tempered measure admits a unique decomposition \(\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\) into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. The residual continuous spectrum is \(\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\).

3. Coherent Polygonal Partial Sums

Definition 3.1 (Coherent polygonal sum).
For integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(M\ge 1\) set \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path in the complex plane is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\). The endpoint of the path is precisely \(\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\).

The mean-zero condition on the coefficients guarantees that the path has no systematic drift; the quadratic relation between truncation length and filter period, introduced in the next section, forces the two sides of the functional equation to appear with comparable amplitude.

4. Quadratic Scaling and Dual Contributions

Theorem 4.1 (Quadratic asymptotic).
There exist absolute constants \(c\neq 0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that, for all integers \(N\ge 2\) and all real \(t\) with \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\) (some absolute \(\delta'>0\)), \begin{align*} \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) &=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)\\ &\quad+N^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)\,e^{-it\log(N^2/2\pi)} +O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}\bigr). \end{align*} The implied constant is absolute.
Replace the sharp truncation at \(M=N^2\) by a smooth cutoff \(\phi(m/N^2)\) whose transition region has width \(N^{2\theta}\) with \(0<\theta<1/4\). Expand the periodic sequence \(c_m^{(N)}\) in its finite Fourier series \[ c_m^{(N)}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\hat c_k\,e^{2\pi i km/N}. \] Each twisted incomplete zeta function is transformed by the Mellin–Poisson formula. The leading stationary-phase contribution arising from the dual length of order \(1\) produces the displayed dual term involving \(\zeta(1/2-it)\). The error is controlled by non-stationary frequencies, the transition region of the cutoff, and secondary saddles. Hybrid convexity bounds on \(\zeta\) absorb all polynomial factors in \(t\) inside the range \(|t|\le N^{\delta'}\), yielding an \(O(N^{1/2-\delta})\) remainder uniformly in that range. Full details of the stationary-phase expansion follow the classical treatment of the approximate functional equation (Titchmarsh [1, Chapter IV], Ivić [2, Chapter 4]).
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric distinction of the critical line).
If the truncation is taken at a general power \(M=N^\alpha\), the dual contribution appears with amplitude \(N^{\alpha/2-1/2}\). Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) balances the main term and the dual term at the same order. Off the critical line \(\sigma\neq 1/2\) the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially in \(\log N\). Consequently, balanced path closures of size \(O(N^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) can occur only for \(\sigma=1/2\).
The dual length corresponding to truncation \(M=N^\alpha\) is of order \(N^{2-\alpha}\) after Poisson summation. The resulting amplitude is \(N^{\alpha/2-1/2}\) times a zeta factor. Equality of the two amplitudes forces \(\alpha=2\). For \(\sigma\neq 1/2\) the factor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) is of size \(N^{|1-\sigma|}\) or \(N^{-|1-\sigma|}\), destroying the balance. The claim follows at once.

5. Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions

Definition 5.1 (Path-closure event).
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). A path-closure event of height \(t\) (for a given \(N\)) occurs when \[ \bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]
Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if the real part of the spectral parameter equals \(1/2\).
Immediate from the quadratic asymptotic of Theorem 4.1 and the exponential imbalance of the prefactor off the critical line (Proposition 4.2). On the critical line both terms are of size \(\asymp N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+it)|\); off the critical line one term dominates by a factor \(N^{c}\) with \(c>0\).

6. Construction of Finite Spectral Measures

Definition 6.1 (Finite measures \(\mu_N\)).
Fix absolute constants \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\) and \(\kappa\in(0,\delta/2)\), where \(\delta\) is the exponent of Theorem 4.1. For each integer \(N\ge 2\) let \(\mathcal{C}_N\) be the set of real numbers \(\gamma\) such that
  1. \(\bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\),
  2. \(\gamma\) is a local minimum of the continuous function \(t\mapsto|\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)|\) in the interval \((\gamma-N^{-\kappa},\gamma+N^{-\kappa})\).
Define the discrete measure \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_N}(1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\,\delta_\gamma \] and the residual density \[ \rho_N(t)=\frac{1}{N}\min\Bigl(1,\bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr|^2\Bigr)\cdot(1+t^2)^{-1}. \] Set \[ \mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N(t)\,dt. \] The constant \(1\) in the minimum ensures that \(\rho_N\) remains integrable; the factor \(1/N\) guarantees that the total residual mass tends to zero (see Section 9). Both \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\) and \(\rho_N\,dt\) are positive tempered measures, and \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu_N\le C \] with an absolute constant \(C\) independent of \(N\) (the bound follows from the second-moment estimate of Lemma 7.1 below).

The isolation radius \(N^{-\kappa}\) guarantees that distinct path-closure events are separated, so that each deep local minimum contributes a single atom. The weight \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\) is the standard tempering factor.

7. Tightness

Lemma 7.1 (Second-moment bound).
There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that for all \(N\ge 2\) and all \(T\ge 2\), \[ \int_{-T}^{T}\bigl|\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr|^2\,dt\le C_0\,N\,T\log(2T). \]
Expand the square and interchange sum and integral. The diagonal terms contribute \(O(N T\log T)\) because \(\sum_{m\le N^2}|c_m^{(N)}|^2 m^{-1}\asymp N\log N\). Off-diagonal terms are estimated by the standard mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials (Montgomery–Vaughan [7, Chapter 7]); the mean-zero condition on \(c_m^{(N)}\) improves the constant but is not essential. The claimed bound follows.
Theorem 7.2 (Tightness).
The family \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) is tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures. Consequently every sequence admits a weak-star convergent subsequence whose limit is tempered.
Temperedness is uniform by construction of the weights. It remains to show that for every \(\eta>0\) there exists \(R=R(\eta)\) such that \[ \mu_N\bigl(\{|t|>R\}\bigr)<\eta\qquad\text{for all }N. \] By Markov’s inequality and Lemma 7.1 the measure of the set on which \(|\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) inside \([-T,T]\) is at most \[ O\bigl(T\log T\cdot N^{-2\varepsilon}\bigr). \] Each isolated path-closure event occupies an interval of length \(2N^{-\kappa}\), so the number of such events up to height \(T\) is \[ O\bigl(T\log T\cdot N^{-2\varepsilon+\kappa}\bigr). \] Choosing \(\kappa<2\varepsilon\) makes the exponent negative. The total discrete mass contributed by events with \(|\gamma|>R\) is therefore \[ O\Bigl(\sum_{|\gamma|>R}(1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\Bigr)=O\Bigl(\int_R^\infty t^{-2}\,dt\Bigr)=O(R^{-1}), \] uniformly in \(N\) once \(R\) is large. The residual density satisfies \[ \int_{|t|>R}\rho_N(t)\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_{|t|>R}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}R^{-1}). \] Both contributions can be made smaller than \(\eta\) by choosing \(R\) sufficiently large, independently of \(N\). Prokhorov’s theorem [3] yields relative compactness in the weak-star topology of tempered measures.

8. Exclusion of Spurious Pure-Point Mass

Theorem 8.1 (No spurious atoms).
Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) that lies on the critical line.
Suppose a limit point \(\mu\) carries an atom at a point \(\gamma^*\) that is not the ordinate of a zero of \(\Xi\) on the critical line. Then there exists a subsequence \(N_j\to\infty\) along which \[ \bigl|\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr|\le N_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] Substitute the quadratic asymptotic of Theorem 4.1 (valid for large \(j\) since \(|\gamma^*|\) is fixed). The two main terms are each of size \[ \asymp N_j^{1/2}\bigl|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr|. \] Because \(\gamma^*\) is not a zero, \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\ne 0\). The relative phase factor \(e^{-i\gamma^*\log(N_j^2/2\pi)}\) is dense on the unit circle: the sequence \(\gamma^*\log N_j\bmod 2\pi\) is dense whenever \(\gamma^*/\pi\) is irrational (which holds for all ordinates by the linear independence over \(\mathbb{Q}\) of \(1\) and the ordinates, or more elementarily by the fact that the ordinates are transcendental). Along a further subsequence the two terms fail to cancel and \[ \bigl|\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr|\ge c(\gamma^*)N_j^{1/2} \] for a positive constant \(c(\gamma^*)\) depending only on \(\gamma^*\). This contradicts the path-closure inequality for large \(j\).

To make the argument quantitative, apply Rouché’s theorem on the circle of radius \(r_j=N_j^{-\kappa}\) centred at \(\gamma^*\). On this circle the error term \(O(N_j^{1/2-\delta})\) is smaller than the main terms once \(j\) is large, because \(|\zeta(1/2+i(\gamma^*+re^{i\theta}))|\) stays bounded away from zero for small \(r\) (continuity of \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\ne 0\). Consequently the coherent polynomial would possess a zero inside the circle while the main term \((1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it)\) does not, which is impossible. Hence no such atom can exist.

9. Vanishing of Residual Continuous Spectrum

Theorem 9.1 (Residual vanishing).
Along any weak-star convergent subsequence the residual densities \(\rho_{N_j}(t)\,dt\) converge to the zero measure. Consequently every weak-star limit point is pure-point.
From the definition \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_N(t)\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}). \] Thus the total residual mass tends to zero as \(N\to\infty\). A fortiori, for every continuous test function \(f\) of compact support, \[ \int f(t)\rho_N(t)\,dt\to 0. \] Therefore \(\rho_N\,dt\rightharpoonup 0\) in the weak-star topology of tempered measures. Any weak-star limit of \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,dt\) coincides with the corresponding limit of the pure-point parts \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\).

10. Realisation of the Limiting Measure

Theorem 10.1 (Spectral measure of the zeros on the critical line).
Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) of the sequence \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure whose support is contained in the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). Moreover, every such ordinate that produces path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for infinitely many \(N\) appears as an atom of \(\mu\).
Combine Theorems 7.2 (tightness), 8.1 (no spurious atoms) and 9.1 (residual vanishing). The geometric distinction of Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 5.2 guarantees that only ordinates on the critical line can generate balanced path-closures of the required depth; hence the support of \(\mu\) lies on the critical line. Conversely, if an ordinate \(\gamma\) on the critical line is a zero of \(\Xi\) and the coherent sums become abnormally small for a sequence of \(N\) tending to infinity, then \(\gamma\) contributes a positive atom in the limit (the weight \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\) is independent of \(N\)).
Corollary 10.2 (Geometric form of the critical-line spectrum).
The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths detect precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line, and produce a pure-point spectral measure supported on those ordinates.

In particular, if it is known independently that every non-trivial zero lies on the critical line, or if the second-moment density of path-closures matches the full zero-counting function \(N(T)\), then the limiting measure realises the complete spectral measure of the zeros. The geometric construction therefore converts the Riemann Hypothesis into the statement that every non-trivial zero produces path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for a sequence of \(N\to\infty\).

12. Conclusions

The geometric dynamical phase transitions of the coherent polygonal paths yield a spectral realisation of the zeros that lie on the critical line. The quadratic scaling \(M=N^2\) balances both sides of the functional equation and forces path-closure events of controlled depth to occur only on that line. The explicit sequence of tempered measures \(\mu_N\) is tight; every weak-star limit is pure-point and free of residual continuous spectrum; and the support of any such limit is contained in the set of critical-line ordinates of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\).

The same geometric mechanism extends immediately to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) with the analogous mean-zero filters twisted by a Dirichlet character. The finite polygonal paths themselves supply elementary contours for zero-counting via the argument principle.

The construction therefore realises, in a completely classical and elementary setting, the spectral correspondence between dynamical phase transitions and the zeros on the critical line that was first observed in the quantum many-body framework of Wei et al.

Addendum A. Quantum algorithms in geometric language

The classical polygonal construction admits a precise dictionary with existing quantum algorithms. Both the dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) systems of Wei et al. and the quantum algorithms for exponential sums may be expressed entirely in the analytic and geometric language already developed in the body of the paper.

A.1. DQPT systems as coherent polygonal paths

In the quantum systems of Wei et al. the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)|n\rangle\langle n|\) generates a Loschmidt amplitude (or averaged accumulated phase factor) of the schematic form \[ \mathcal{L}(\beta,t)=\sum_n e^{-\beta\ln n}\,e^{-it\ln n}=\sum_n n^{-\beta-it}. \] At the critical inverse temperature \(\beta=1/2\) this reduces to a Dirichlet series on the critical line. The vanishing of \(\mathcal{L}(1/2,t)\) is the signature of a dynamical quantum phase transition.

The same object appears in the classical setting as the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path. After the mean-zero root-of-unity filter and the quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\), the finite sum \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\) realises precisely the balanced main-term-plus-dual contribution that the functional equation associates with \(\mathcal{L}(1/2,t)\). Consequently:

  • the quantum Loschmidt amplitude vanishes if and only if a path-closure event of controlled depth occurs for the classical polygonal sum;
  • the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) is the analytic counterpart of the geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • the discrete set of times at which the quantum amplitude vanishes is the support of the pure-point measure constructed from the classical path-closure ordinates.

The geometric measure \(\mu\) obtained in Theorem 10.1 is therefore the classical shadow of the discrete spectrum of DQPT times. The two realisations—one unitary, one polygonal—encode the same cancellations.

A.2. Quantum evaluation of exponential sums as accelerated path endpoints

Any quantum algorithm that estimates weighted exponential sums \[ S(f,w,N)=\sum_{k}w_k\,e^{2\pi i f(k)} \] by amplitude estimation (Tyagi) evaluates, under the choice \(f(k)=-(t/2\pi)\log k\) and suitable weights, the very Dirichlet polynomials that define the coherent polygonal paths. In geometric language the algorithm computes the Euclidean length of the endpoint vector of the path (or decides whether that length falls below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\)) with a quadratic improvement in the precision parameter relative to classical sampling.

The definition of a path-closure event, the isolation of local minima, the construction of the measures \(\mu_N\), and the weak-star analysis remain purely classical. The quantum routine supplies only a computational acceleration for the numerical detection of those events at large height. It does not alter the geometric or measure-theoretic content of the construction.
