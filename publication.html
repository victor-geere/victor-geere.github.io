Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A self-contained geometric framework, with continuous-radius interpolation

Victor Geere · 19 August 2026

Abstract. We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by the quadratic geometric relation \(M=N^2\), Euler–Maclaurin supplies the identity \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)+O(1+\lvert t\rvert), \] uniformly in \(N\) and \(t\). Path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) therefore occur at every critical-line zero once \(N\) exceeds an explicit threshold \(N_0(\gamma)\asymp(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\), and cannot occur at a non-zero of \(\zeta\) on the critical line. Off the critical line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) destroys the balance. From the finite path-closures we construct an explicit sequence of measures \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,dt\), with residual mass \(O(N^{-1})\) by construction. The family is tight; every weak-star limit is pure-point and supported exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line. The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent, in this construction, to every non-trivial zero producing such closures.

1. Introduction and relation to Wei et al.

Wei, Zhai, Lu, Yang, Gao, Wei, Song, Nori, Xin and Long established a direct correspondence between the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in engineered quantum many-body systems [11]. In their construction the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) produces a Loschmidt amplitude whose vanishing signals a DQPT. The critical temperature of that construction is precisely \(\beta=1/2\), so that the occurrence of DQPTs at a unique temperature is equivalent to the statement that all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line.

The present work supplies a purely classical, geometric counterpart of the same correspondence. The role of the Loschmidt amplitude is played by the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path formed by the mean-zero Dirichlet polynomials \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s},\qquad c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1&N\nmid m,\\1-N&N\mid m.\end{cases} \] A path-closure event of controlled depth \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] is the geometric analogue of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude. The quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) is the geometric counterpart of the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\).

The relationship is made rigorous by the following dictionary:

  • Loschmidt amplitude \(\longleftrightarrow\) coherent polygonal endpoint \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\);
  • DQPT (vanishing of the amplitude) \(\longleftrightarrow\) path-closure event of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\);
  • critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) \(\longleftrightarrow\) quadratic geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • spectral support of the zeros on the critical line \(\longleftrightarrow\) pure-point support of the limiting tempered measure constructed from the path-closure ordinates.

The continuous-radius interpolation of the filter, previously a separate argument, is Section 6. Nested-sphere geometry is recorded in nested_spheres_RH.html.

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \] The zeros of \(\Xi\) are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity, integer filter).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the coefficients \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & N\nmid m,\\1-N & N\mid m\end{cases} \] satisfy \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s),\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1, \] with meromorphic continuation to the whole plane. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has zeros only on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\).
Write the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta(s)\) and subtract the terms with \(N\mid m\), which produces the factor \(N^{1-s}\zeta(s)\). Equivalently, \(c_m^{(N)}\) is the convolution of the constant sequence \(1\) with the finite geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Definition 2.3 (Continuous mean-zero filter).
For real \(R\ge 2\) and a fixed even bump \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) with \(\int\varphi=1\), \[ c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\bigl(m/R-k\bigr). \] When \(R=N\) is an integer, the sharp-bump limit recovers \(c_m^{(N)}\). The associated coherent sum is \(\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)m^{-s}\) with \(X=R^2\), and \(\rho(R,s)=\lvert\eta_R(R^2)(s)\rvert\).
Definition 2.4 (Tempered measure).
A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is tempered if \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(t)<\infty. \] Every tempered measure admits a unique decomposition \(\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\) into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. The residual continuous spectrum is \(\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\).

3. Coherent polygonal partial sums

Definition 3.1 (Coherent polygonal sum).
For integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(M\ge 1\) set \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path in the complex plane is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\). The endpoint of the path is precisely \(\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\).

The mean-zero condition on the coefficients guarantees that the path has no systematic drift. The identity of Lemma 2.2 writes the infinite sum as \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\); the next section compares the quadratic truncation to that identity by Euler–Maclaurin.

4. Quadratic scaling and the explicit remainder

The truncated zeta series is related to \(\zeta\) by the standard Euler–Maclaurin formula (Titchmarsh [1, §4.11]; Montgomery–Vaughan [7, Theorem 1.12]): for \(x\ge 1\), \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\ge\sigma_0>0\) and \(s\ne 1\), \[ \sum_{n\le x}n^{-s}=\zeta(s)-\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\frac12 x^{-s}+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,x^{-\sigma}\bigr). \] Write \(D_x(s)\) for the left-hand side. Then, identically, \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=D_{N^2}(s)-N^{1-s}D_N(s). \]

Theorem 4.1 (Explicit remainder).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) and every real \(t\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) =(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)+O(1+\lvert t\rvert), \] with an absolute implied constant.
Apply the displayed Euler–Maclaurin formula at \(\sigma=1/2\) to both \(D_{N^2}\) and \(D_N\). The remainders are \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1})\) and \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1/2})\) respectively. Multiplication of the second remainder by \(N^{1-s}=N^{1/2-it}\) produces \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). The polar terms cancel: \[ \frac{(N^2)^{1-s}}{1-s}=N^{1-s}\cdot\frac{N^{1-s}}{1-s}. \] The order-zero boundary terms \(\tfrac12 x^{-s}\) contribute \(\tfrac12 N^{-1-2it}\) from \(D_{N^2}\) and \(\tfrac12 N^{1-s}N^{-s}=\tfrac12 N^{-2it}\) from the second sum, both \(O(1)\). Collecting the main terms gives \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) on the critical line.
Corollary 4.1.1 (Depth of closure at critical-line zeros).
Let \(\gamma\) be a real number with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] for all integers \(N\ge N_0(\gamma)\), where \(N_0(\gamma)=\bigl(C(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\bigr)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\) and \(C\) is the implied constant of Theorem 4.1. In particular \(\log N_0(\gamma)\ll\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\).
Theorem 4.1 and \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\) give \(\lvert\eta\rvert=O(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\). Compare with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric distinction of the critical line).
If the truncation is taken at a general power \(M=N^\alpha\), the same Euler–Maclaurin comparison produces a main term \((1-N^{1-\sigma-it})\zeta(\sigma+it)\) plus an error \(O(N^{\alpha(1/2-\sigma)}+(1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{1-\sigma-\alpha/2})\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma\). Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) at \(\sigma=1/2\) makes the error \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) while the prefactor of \(\zeta\) has size \(N^{1/2}\). Off the critical line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially in \(\log N\). Consequently balanced path-closures of size \(O(N^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) can occur only for \(\sigma=1/2\).
Repeat the proof of Theorem 4.1 with truncation \(M=N^\alpha\) and real part \(\sigma\). The polar terms cancel if and only if \(M=N^2\). For \(\sigma\ne 1/2\) one has \(\lvert 1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\rvert\asymp N^{\lvert 1-\sigma\rvert}\) for large \(N\), which is incompatible with a bound \(O(N^{1/2-\varepsilon})\) unless \(\zeta(\sigma+it)=0\) and the error happens to be that small. An off-line zero is not an atom of the measures constructed in Section 8, which live on the critical line; see Section 12.

5. Geometric dynamical phase transitions

Definition 5.1 (Path-closure event).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). A path-closure event of height \(t\) (for a given \(N\)) occurs when \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]
Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Balanced path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) can occur only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) produces such an event for all sufficiently large \(N\), with explicit threshold \(N_0(\gamma)\) as in Corollary 4.1.1.
The “only if” direction is Proposition 4.2. The existence statement is Corollary 4.1.1.

6. Continuous-radius interpolation

The integer theory is the one used for the measures. The continuous filter of Definition 2.3 interpolates it. This section records the identities that survive that interpolation, and the local diagonal as it actually evaluates (not as a constant \(c_1=\frac12\)).

Lemma 6.1 (\(C^1\) regularity).
After smoothing the upper limit, \(R\mapsto\eta_R(R^2)(s)\) is \(C^1\) on \([2,\infty)\) for each fixed \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)\). Differentiation under the sum is justified by \[ \sum_{m\le R^2}\lvert\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})\rvert\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R. \]
The coefficients are smooth in \(R\). On the support of \(\varphi\), \(\lvert\partial_R c_m\rvert\ll 1+\lvert\varphi'\rvert_\infty(1+m/R)\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the majorant. For the sharp truncation the map is piecewise \(C^1\), with jumps of size \(2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\) when \(R^2\) is an integer (Definition 6.2).
Definition 6.2 (Splitting of \(\partial_R\eta\)).
On the critical line, \[ \partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R), \] where \(A=\sum_{m\le R^2}(\partial_R c_m)m^{-1/2-i\gamma}\) is the interior coefficient derivative, \(B=c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}(R^2)^{-1/2-i\gamma}\cdot 2R\) is the Leibniz boundary, and \(E\) is the difference between this sharp splitting and a smooth cutoff of width \(R^{2\theta}\), \(\theta\in(0,1/4)\). Since \(\lvert(R^2)^{-1/2}\rvert=R^{-1}\), one has \(\lvert B\rvert=2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\). For the integer filter, \(N\mid N^2\), so \(c_{N^2}=1-N\) and \(\lvert B\rvert=2(N-1)\). The integer theorems of Sections 4–5 and 8–12 do not use \(B\).
Lemma 6.3 (Local diagonal).
On the packet \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\), with \(u=m/R-k\) and local mass \(M_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}\lvert c_m(R)\rvert m^{-1/2}\), \[ \partial_R c_m=-\varphi(u)+(k+u)\varphi'(u), \] and the diagonal \[ D_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}c_m(R)\,(\partial_R c_m(R))\,m^{-1} \] satisfies \[ D_k=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)}{k+u}\,du+R\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)^2}{k+u}\,du+R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}. \] The \(\varphi'\) term integrates by parts to zero by compact support of \(\varphi\). Apostol’s remainder [12] is \(R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}=\int\overline{B}_1 f_k'\) with \(\lvert\overline{B}_1\rvert\le\tfrac12\). Summing in \(k\) gives \[ \sum_{k=1}^R D_k=R\Bigl(\int\varphi^2\Bigr)\log R+O_\varphi(R)+\sum_k R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}, \] which is not a multiple of \(R^{-1}\rho^2\). The evaluation \(\int(1-R\varphi)(-\varphi)\,du=-\tfrac12\) is false (that integral equals \(-1+R\int\varphi^2\)).
Substitute the expressions for \(c_m\) and \(\partial_R c_m\), change variables, and integrate the \(\varphi'\) term by parts. The harmonic sum \(\sum_{k\le R}k^{-1}=\log R+O(1)\) produces the displayed main term. No identification of \(\sum_k\lvert\eta^{(k)}\rvert^2\) with \(\rho^2\) is claimed.

The integer depth bound of Corollary 4.1.1 does not pass through this local form. For real \(R\), the same Euler–Maclaurin comparison as Theorem 4.1 applies to the sharp filter at \(\lfloor R\rfloor\), and Lemma 6.1 controls the increment from \(\lfloor R\rfloor\) to \(R\) on compact ranges of \(t\); the measures below are indexed by integers \(N\).

7. Mean-value estimates

Theorem 7.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, explicit constant).
For a finitely supported sequence \((a_m)\) and \(T\ge 1\), \[ \int_0^T\Bigl\lvert\sum_m a_m m^{-it}\Bigr\rvert^2 dt=\sum_m\lvert a_m\rvert^2 T+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_m m\lvert a_m\rvert^2, \] with \(\lvert\theta\rvert\le 1\) (Montgomery–Vaughan [13]; [7, §7.3]). On the critical line, \(a_m=c_m m^{-1/2}\) therefore gives \[ \int_0^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt=T\sum_{m\le N^2}\frac{\lvert c_m\rvert^2}{m}+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_{m\le N^2}\lvert c_m\rvert^2. \] The coefficient sums are \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\), because the spikes \(c_m=1-N\) dominate. Mean-zero cancellation is in \(\sum c_m m^{-s}\), not in \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\). In particular the diagonal is not \(O(1)\), and \[ \int_{-T}^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt\ll TN\log N+N^3. \]
Expand the square. Off-diagonal integrals are bounded by \(\min(T,1/\lvert\log(n/m)\rvert)\). Hilbert’s inequality as generalised in [13] produces the constant \(2\pi\). For the spikes: there are \(N\) multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^2\), each with \(\lvert c_m\rvert^2=(N-1)^2\), whence \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\).
Lemma 7.2 (Weighted second moment, exact).
The Fourier transform of \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) is \(\pi e^{-\lvert\xi\rvert}\), so \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2}{1+t^2}\,dt =\pi\sum_{m,n\le N^2}c_m\overline{c}_n\,(mn)^{-1/2}\frac{\min(m,n)}{\max(m,n)}. \] The diagonal is \(\pi\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp\pi N\log N\). There is no remainder of size \(N^3\).
Expand the square and integrate termwise. Each pair \((m,n)\) produces \(\pi\exp(-\lvert\log(m/n)\rvert)\).
Lemma 7.3 (Pointwise from local means).
For \(F\in C^1(\mathbb{R})\) and \(H>0\), \[ \lvert F(\gamma)\rvert^2\le\frac2H\int_0^H\lvert F(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du+2H\int_0^H\lvert F'(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du. \] If \(F(t)=\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\), then \(\lvert F'\rvert\le 2(\log N)\,F_{\log}\). Taking \(H=1/\log N\) costs two logarithms relative to the local \(L^2\) mean, and does not produce a power of \(N^{-1}\). Depth of closure is not taken from this lemma; it is Corollary 4.1.1.
Write \(F(\gamma)=\frac1H\int_0^H\bigl(F(\gamma+u)-\int_0^u F'(\gamma+v)\,dv\bigr)du\) and apply \(\lvert x+y\rvert^2\le 2\lvert x\rvert^2+2\lvert y\rvert^2\).

8. Construction of finite spectral measures

Definition 8.1 (Finite measures \(\mu_N\)).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\) and an isolation exponent \(\kappa\in(0,\varepsilon/2)\). For each integer \(N\ge 2\) let \(\mathcal{C}_N\) be the set of real numbers \(\gamma\) such that
  1. \(\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\),
  2. \(\gamma\) is a local minimum of \(t\mapsto\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\rvert\) in \((\gamma-N^{-\kappa},\gamma+N^{-\kappa})\).
Define \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_N}(1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\,\delta_\gamma, \qquad \rho_N(t)=\frac1N\min\Bigl(1,\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2\Bigr)\,(1+t^2)^{-1}, \] and \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N(t)\,dt\). The factor \(1/N\) makes \(\int\rho_N=O(N^{-1})\) by construction. The atomic weights are independent of \(N\).

The isolation radius \(N^{-\kappa}\) separates distinct path-closure events. The weight \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\) is the standard tempering factor.

9. Tightness

Theorem 9.1 (Tightness).
The family \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) is tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures.
The residual satisfies \(\int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}\rho_N\le N^{-1}\int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}A^{-1})\), so residual tails vanish uniformly in \(N\) as \(A\to\infty\). For the atomic part, split \(\mathcal{C}_N=\mathcal{C}_N^{\le}\cup\mathcal{C}_N^{>}\) at height \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\). On \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\), Theorem 4.1 gives \[ \bigl\lvert(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}+O(N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}), \] hence \(\lvert\zeta(1/2+it)\rvert\ll N^{-\varepsilon}\). The zeros of \(\zeta\) on the critical line up to height \(T\) number \(O(T\log T)\). Each isolated minimum occupies an interval of length \(2N^{-\kappa}\), and \(\kappa<\varepsilon/2\) is smaller than the average gap \(1/\log T\) throughout \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2}\) for large \(N\). Thus \(\mathcal{C}_N^{\le}\) lies in the \(N^{-\kappa}\)-neighbourhood of those zeros, and \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>A\}\cap\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\bigr) \le\sum_{\lvert\gamma\rvert>A}(1+\gamma^2)^{-1}=O\bigl((\log A)/A\bigr), \] summing over zeros, uniformly in \(N\). On \(\lvert t\rvert>N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\), isolation alone yields \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\}\bigr) \ll N^{\kappa}\int_{N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}}^\infty\frac{dt}{1+t^2}\ll N^{\kappa-1/2+\varepsilon/2}. \] The exponent is negative by the choice of \(\kappa\). For any \(\eta>0\) choose \(A\) large enough that the zero-tail and the residual tail are each \(<\eta/3\); then choose \(N_1\) large enough that the high atomic mass is \(<\eta/3\) for \(N\ge N_1\); the finitely many measures with \(N<N_1\) are handled by enlarging \(A\). Prokhorov’s theorem [3] yields tightness.

Markov applied to Theorem 7.1 on the sublevel \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) produces measure \(O(TN^{2\varepsilon}\log N+N^{2+2\varepsilon})\), which does not save a negative power of \(N\). Tightness is taken from Theorem 4.1 and isolation, not from that Markov bound.

10. Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass

Theorem 10.1 (No spurious atoms).
Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is the imaginary part of a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) that lies on the critical line.
Suppose a limit point \(\mu\) carries an atom at \(\gamma^*\) with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\ne 0\). Then along a subsequence \(N_j\to\infty\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr\rvert\le N_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] Theorem 4.1 at the fixed height \(\gamma^*\) gives \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr\rvert \ge\bigl(N_j^{1/2}-1\bigr)\bigl\lvert\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\bigr\rvert-O(1+\lvert\gamma^*\rvert) \ge c(\gamma^*)N_j^{1/2} \] for large \(j\), a contradiction. There is no second main term of size \(N^{1/2}\lvert\zeta\rvert\) that could cancel the first: the polar terms of Euler–Maclaurin cancel, and the remainder is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). To localise, apply Rouché on the circle of radius \(r_j=N_j^{-\kappa}\) centred at \(\gamma^*\). On this circle \(\lvert\zeta(1/2+i(\gamma^*+re^{i\theta}))\rvert\) stays bounded away from zero for small \(r\) (continuity, and \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma^*)\ne 0\)), so the main term has modulus \(\asymp N_j^{1/2}\) while the error is \(O(1+\lvert\gamma^*\rvert+r_j)\). Hence \(\eta\) has no zero inside the circle, and no path-closure can sit at \(\gamma^*\).

11. Vanishing of residual continuous spectrum

Theorem 11.1 (Residual vanishing).
Along any weak-star convergent subsequence the residual densities \(\rho_{N_j}(t)\,dt\) converge to the zero measure. Consequently every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is pure-point.
\[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_N(t)\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}). \] Thus the total residual mass tends to zero. For every continuous test function \(f\) of compact support, \(\int f\rho_N\to 0\). Any weak-star limit of \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,dt\) therefore coincides with the corresponding limit of \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\). Existence of the limit is Theorem 9.1.

12. Realisation of the limiting measure

Theorem 12.1 (Spectral measure of the zeros on the critical line).
Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure whose support is exactly the set of imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line. Every such ordinate appears as an atom of \(\mu\), with mass \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\).
Tightness is Theorem 9.1. Residual vanishing is Theorem 11.1, so the limit is pure-point. Theorem 10.1 excludes atoms at non-zeros on the critical line. Proposition 4.2 excludes balanced closures off the line, so the support lies on the critical line. Conversely, if \(\gamma\) is a critical-line zero of \(\Xi\), Corollary 4.1.1 produces a path-closure of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for every \(N\ge N_0(\gamma)\). For all large \(N\) this closure is the unique local minimum in its isolation interval (the zeros are isolated, and \(N^{-\kappa}\) is eventually smaller than half the gap to the neighbouring zero). The atomic weight \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\) is independent of \(N\), so \(\gamma\) is an atom of every weak-star limit.
Corollary 12.2 (Conversion of the Riemann hypothesis).
The geometric construction realises the complete spectral measure of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) if and only if every non-trivial zero lies on the critical line. Equivalently: the Riemann hypothesis holds if and only if every non-trivial zero produces path-closure events of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) along a sequence \(N\to\infty\).
Theorem 12.1 identifies the support of \(\mu\) with the critical-line zeros. An off-line zero of \(\Xi\) is not an atom of \(\mu\), because the measures are built from ordinates on \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). It produces a path-closure in the critical-line family if and only if it is in fact on the line. Thus RH is precisely the statement that every zero appears in \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\).

14. Conclusions

Euler–Maclaurin on the mean-zero filter at quadratic truncation yields \(\eta=(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta+O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Path-closures of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) occur at every critical-line zero for all large \(N\), with an explicit threshold, and cannot occur at a non-zero on that line. The measures \(\mu_N\) are tight; the residual vanishes by construction; every weak-star limit is the tempered pure-point measure supported on the critical-line zeros of \(\Xi\). The Riemann hypothesis is the assertion that this is the complete set of non-trivial zeros.

The same geometric mechanism extends immediately to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) with the analogous mean-zero filters twisted by a Dirichlet character. The finite polygonal paths themselves supply elementary contours for zero-counting via the argument principle.

Addendum A. Quantum algorithms in geometric language

The classical polygonal construction admits a precise dictionary with existing quantum algorithms. Both the dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) systems of Wei et al. and the quantum algorithms for exponential sums may be expressed entirely in the analytic and geometric language already developed in the body of the paper.

A.1. DQPT systems as coherent polygonal paths

In the quantum systems of Wei et al. the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) generates a Loschmidt amplitude of the schematic form \[ \mathcal{L}(\beta,t)=\sum_n e^{-\beta\ln n}\,e^{-it\ln n}=\sum_n n^{-\beta-it}. \] At the critical inverse temperature \(\beta=1/2\) this reduces to a Dirichlet series on the critical line. The vanishing of \(\mathcal{L}(1/2,t)\) is the signature of a dynamical quantum phase transition.

The same object appears in the classical setting as the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path. After the mean-zero root-of-unity filter and the quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\), Theorem 4.1 identifies \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\) with \((1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it)\) up to \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Consequently:

  • the quantum Loschmidt amplitude vanishing is the analogue of a path-closure event of controlled depth for the classical polygonal sum;
  • the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) is the analytic counterpart of the geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • the discrete set of times at which the quantum amplitude vanishes is the support of the pure-point measure constructed from the classical path-closure ordinates.

The geometric measure \(\mu\) obtained in Theorem 12.1 is the classical shadow of the discrete spectrum of DQPT times. The two realisations—one unitary, one polygonal—encode the same cancellations.

A.2. Quantum evaluation of exponential sums as accelerated path endpoints

Any quantum algorithm that estimates weighted exponential sums \[ S(f,w,N)=\sum_{k}w_k\,e^{2\pi i f(k)} \] by amplitude estimation (Tyagi [14]) evaluates, under the choice \(f(k)=-(t/2\pi)\log k\) and suitable weights, the very Dirichlet polynomials that define the coherent polygonal paths. In geometric language the algorithm computes the Euclidean length of the endpoint vector of the path (or decides whether that length falls below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\)) with a quadratic improvement in the precision parameter relative to classical sampling.

The definition of a path-closure event, the isolation of local minima, the construction of the measures \(\mu_N\), and the weak-star analysis remain purely classical. The quantum routine supplies only a computational acceleration for the numerical detection of those events at large height. It does not alter the geometric or measure-theoretic content of the construction.

The classical polygonal framework and the quantum algorithms describe the same spectral objects from complementary viewpoints.

Addendum B. Refined quadratic asymptotic

Theorem 4.1 is already uniform in \(t\) with remainder \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is the input to depth-of-closure and to the exclusion of spurious atoms. For large \(\lvert t\rvert\) one may replace Euler–Maclaurin by the approximate functional equation, keeping the dual sums of lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). The following statement records the exponent that would follow from Bourgain’s subconvexity on those dual sums. It is not required for Theorems 9.1–12.1.

Refined remainder (optional).
There exist a positive exponent \(\delta=29/168-\eta\) and a range \(\delta'=1/3+1/42-\eta\) such that, for \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\), the remainder in Theorem 4.1 may be replaced by \(O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}(1+\lvert t\rvert)^\varepsilon\bigr)\) after inserting Bourgain’s bound \(\mu(1/2)=13/84\) and a van-der-Corput estimate of order 1 on the dual tails, provided an explicit constant in Bourgain’s bound is fixed.
On the range \(\lvert t\rvert\le 2\pi N\) both dual sums of the approximate functional equation are empty and Theorem 4.1 already gives \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is stronger. For \(2\pi N<\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\) write \[ D_x(s)=\zeta(s)-\chi(s)D_{\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi x)}(1-s)+R(x,s) \] and substitute \(x=N^2\) and \(x=N\) into \(\eta=D_{N^2}-N^{1-s}D_N\). The dual sums have lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). Estimate them by convexity or by Bourgain, and estimate the AFE remainders by van der Corput. Collecting exponents as in the previous seven-step sketch yields \(\delta=1/6+1/168-\eta\) from Weyl plus one van-der-Corput increment, or the displayed value from Bourgain. The dual terms vanish at critical-line zeros because they contain \(\zeta(1-s)\) or a short Dirichlet polynomial that is \(O(1)\) when the length is \(O(1)\).
