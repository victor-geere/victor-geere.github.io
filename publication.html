Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A self-contained geometric framework, with continuous-radius interpolation

Victor Geere · 19 August 2026

Abstract. We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from elementary Dirichlet polynomials constructed via a mean-zero root-of-unity filter, we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane. When the truncation length is linked to the filter period by the quadratic geometric relation \(M=N^2\), Euler–Maclaurin supplies, throughout the truncated strip \(\delta\le\operatorname{Re}s\le 1-\delta\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+O\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr). \] On the critical line the error is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) and the prefactor has size \(N^{1/2}\). Path-closure events are located as isolated minima in the whole strip, at relative depth \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert N^{-\varepsilon}\). A closure is balanced when the prefactor has size at least \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\) and \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). Balanced closures can occur only for \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\); every critical-line zero produces one once \(N\) exceeds an explicit threshold \(N_0(\gamma)\). The associated measures \(\mu_N\) (balanced) and \(\nu_N\) (unrestricted) live on the strip; both families are tight; every weak-star limit is pure-point, supported on zeros of \(\Xi\). Unrestricted closures track every zero of real part \(\ge\varepsilon\). Identity L — a lower bound \(\lvert\eta\rvert\ge c N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) at off-line zeros on the tightness range — would force the two families onto the critical line. It is open.

1. Introduction and relation to Wei et al.

Wei, Zhai, Lu, Yang, Gao, Wei, Song, Nori, Xin and Long established a direct correspondence between the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in engineered quantum many-body systems [11]. In their construction the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) produces a Loschmidt amplitude whose vanishing signals a DQPT. The critical temperature of that construction is precisely \(\beta=1/2\), so that the occurrence of DQPTs at a unique temperature is equivalent to the statement that all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line.

The present work supplies a purely classical, geometric counterpart of the same correspondence. The role of the Loschmidt amplitude is played by the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path formed by the mean-zero Dirichlet polynomials \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s},\qquad c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1&N\nmid m,\\1-N&N\mid m.\end{cases} \] A path-closure event of controlled depth \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] is the geometric analogue of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude. The quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) is the geometric counterpart of the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\).

The relationship is made rigorous by the following dictionary:

  • Loschmidt amplitude \(\longleftrightarrow\) coherent polygonal endpoint \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\);
  • DQPT (vanishing of the amplitude) \(\longleftrightarrow\) path-closure event of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\);
  • critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) \(\longleftrightarrow\) quadratic geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • spectral support of the zeros on the critical line \(\longleftrightarrow\) pure-point support of the limiting tempered measure constructed from the balanced path-closures in the strip.

The measures of Sections 8–12 are built from path-closures located in the truncated strip, not a priori on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\). Balanced closures are confined to \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) by Theorem 5.2; the identification of their limit with zeros of \(\Xi\) is Theorem 12.1. The continuous-radius interpolation of the filter, previously a separate argument, is Section 6. Nested-sphere geometry is recorded in nested_spheres_RH.html.

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \] The zeros of \(\Xi\) are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity, integer filter).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the coefficients \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & N\nmid m,\\1-N & N\mid m\end{cases} \] satisfy \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s),\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1, \] with meromorphic continuation to the whole plane. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has zeros only on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\).
Write the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta(s)\) and subtract the terms with \(N\mid m\), which produces the factor \(N^{1-s}\zeta(s)\). Equivalently, \(c_m^{(N)}\) is the convolution of the constant sequence \(1\) with the finite geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Definition 2.3 (Continuous mean-zero filter).
For real \(R\ge 2\) and a fixed even bump \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) with \(\int\varphi=1\), \[ c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\bigl(m/R-k\bigr). \] When \(R=N\) is an integer, the sharp-bump limit recovers \(c_m^{(N)}\). The associated coherent sum is \(\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)m^{-s}\) with \(X=R^2\), and \(\rho(R,s)=\lvert\eta_R(R^2)(s)\rvert\).
Definition 2.4 (Tempered measure).
A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is tempered if \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(t)<\infty. \] The same name is used for a positive Borel measure on the truncated strip \(S_\delta\subset\mathbb{C}\) of Definition 5.0 with \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(s)<\infty\). Every tempered measure admits a unique decomposition \(\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\) into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. The residual continuous spectrum is \(\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\).

3. Coherent polygonal partial sums

Definition 3.1 (Coherent polygonal sum).
For integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(M\ge 1\) set \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path in the complex plane is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\). The endpoint of the path is precisely \(\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\).

The mean-zero condition on the coefficients guarantees that the path has no systematic drift. The identity of Lemma 2.2 writes the infinite sum as \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\); the next section compares the quadratic truncation to that identity by Euler–Maclaurin.

4. Quadratic scaling and the explicit remainder

The truncated zeta series is related to \(\zeta\) by the standard Euler–Maclaurin formula (Titchmarsh [1, §4.11]; Montgomery–Vaughan [7, Theorem 1.12]): for \(x\ge 1\), \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\ge\sigma_0>0\) and \(s\ne 1\), \[ \sum_{n\le x}n^{-s}=\zeta(s)-\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\frac12 x^{-s}+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,x^{-\sigma}\bigr). \] Write \(D_x(s)\) for the left-hand side. Then, identically, \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=D_{N^2}(s)-N^{1-s}D_N(s). \]

Theorem 4.1 (Explicit remainder).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) and every real \(t\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) =(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)+O(1+\lvert t\rvert), \] with an absolute implied constant.
Apply the displayed Euler–Maclaurin formula at \(\sigma=1/2\) to both \(D_{N^2}\) and \(D_N\). The remainders are \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1})\) and \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1/2})\) respectively. Multiplication of the second remainder by \(N^{1-s}=N^{1/2-it}\) produces \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). The polar terms cancel: \[ \frac{(N^2)^{1-s}}{1-s}=N^{1-s}\cdot\frac{N^{1-s}}{1-s}. \] The order-zero boundary terms \(\tfrac12 x^{-s}\) contribute \(\tfrac12 N^{-1-2it}\) from \(D_{N^2}\) and \(\tfrac12 N^{1-s}N^{-s}=\tfrac12 N^{-2it}\) from the second sum, both \(O(1)\). Collecting the main terms gives \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) on the critical line.
Corollary 4.1.1 (Depth of closure at critical-line zeros).
Let \(\gamma\) be a real number with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] for all integers \(N\ge N_0(\gamma)\), where \(N_0(\gamma)=\bigl(C(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\bigr)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\) and \(C\) is the implied constant of Theorem 4.1. In particular \(\log N_0(\gamma)\ll\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\).
Theorem 4.1 and \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\) give \(\lvert\eta\rvert=O(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\). Compare with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Corollary 4.1.2 (Uniform threshold up to height \(T\)).
Let \(T\ge 2\) and \(N_0(T)=\bigl(C(1+T)\bigr)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\), with \(C\) as in Corollary 4.1.1. Then every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\) produces a balanced path-closure for all \(N\ge N_0(T)\). The number of such zeros is \(O(T\log T)\) (Titchmarsh [1, §9.3]).
The map \(\gamma\mapsto N_0(\gamma)\) is increasing in \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\), so \(N_0(\gamma)\le N_0(T)\) throughout \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\). Riemann–von Mangoldt supplies the count.
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric distinction of the critical line).
If the truncation is taken at a general power \(M=N^\alpha\), the same Euler–Maclaurin comparison produces a main term \((1-N^{1-\sigma-it})\zeta(\sigma+it)\) plus an error \(O\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-\sigma-\alpha\sigma}+(1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr)\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma\). The polar terms cancel if and only if \(\alpha=2\). Only the quadratic choice \(\alpha=2\) at \(\sigma=1/2\) makes the error \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) while the prefactor of \(\zeta\) has size \(N^{1/2}\).
Repeat the proof of Theorem 4.1 with truncation \(M=N^\alpha\) and real part \(\sigma\). The identity \( (N^\alpha)^{1-s}=N^{1-s}\cdot N^{1-s}\) holds if and only if \(\alpha=2\). The two remainder contributions are \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)M^{-\sigma})\) and \(N^{1-\sigma}O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{-\sigma})\).
Theorem 4.3 (Remainder in the strip).
Let \(\sigma_0\in(0,1/2)\) and let \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\in[\sigma_0,1-\sigma_0]\) and \(s\ne 1\). For every integer \(N\ge 2\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr). \] In particular \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr), \] and on \(\sigma=1/2\) this is Theorem 4.1.
Apply the Euler–Maclaurin formula of Section 4 to \(D_{N^2}\) and \(D_N\). The polar terms cancel as before. The order-zero boundary terms contribute \(\tfrac12 N^{-2s}-\tfrac12 N^{1-2s}=\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\). The remainder for \(D_{N^2}\) is \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{-2\sigma})\); multiplication of the remainder for \(D_N\) by \(N^{1-s}\) produces \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma})\).
Corollary 4.3.1 (Depth at zeros in the strip).
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\sigma\in[\sigma_0,1-\sigma_0]\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}. \] This is at most \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) as soon as \(N^{1-2\sigma}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). In particular, every zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) produces an unrestricted path-closure of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\) (depending on \(\gamma\)).
Theorem 4.3 and \(\zeta(\rho)=0\). Compare \(N^{1-2\sigma}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Corollary 4.3.2 (Floor lower bound on the tightness range).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\). If \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) is a zero of \(\Xi\) in the truncated strip and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), then for all sufficiently large \(N\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ge\tfrac14\,N^{1-2\sigma}. \] Consequently such a zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac14-\varepsilon/2\) cannot satisfy \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). This is Identity L\(_0\) of gaps.html. The range \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) is the tightness window of Theorem 9.1; it is the same window in which the polygonal path, completed to a continuous curve over \(\mathbb{R}\), has its floor under control (Lemma 4.3.3). On that window \(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2/N\le N^{-2\varepsilon}\to 0\), so the next Euler–Maclaurin remainder is absorbed.
Retain the Bernoulli term \(B_2=1/6\) in Euler–Maclaurin: it contributes \(-\frac{s}{12}N^{-2s}(1-N^{-2})\) to \(\eta\), of size \(O(\lvert s\rvert N^{-2\sigma})\). The next remainder is \(O_{\sigma_0}(\lvert s\rvert^2 N^{-2\sigma})\). At a zero the explicit half-term is \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\), of size \(\tfrac12(N-1)N^{-2\sigma}\). The ratio of the remainder to the half-term is \(O(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2/N)\). On the tightness range \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) this is \(O(N^{-2\varepsilon})\), which is less than \(1/8\) for large \(N\), uniformly in \(\gamma\). The parenthesis is then at least \(N/4\). Comparing \(N^{1-2\sigma}\) with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) yields the abscissa restriction. (The older bound \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le c_0 N^{1/2}\) with a small absolute \(c_0\) is weaker for large \(N\), since \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}=o(N^{1/2})\).)
Lemma 4.3.3 (Completed curve over \(\mathbb{R}\), tightness window).
For each fixed \(\sigma\in[\delta,1-\delta]\) the map \[ t\mapsto\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}, \] is a trigonometric polynomial, hence a real-analytic curve \(\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\). Completing the underlying polygonal path to a continuous fibre curve as in nested_spheres_RH.html (sampling error \(O(N^{-1})\) in Hausdorff distance on the fibre; interactive picture in viz.html) yields a continuous curve \(\gamma_{N,\sigma}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\) with \[ \bigl\lvert\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it)\bigr\rvert-\operatorname{dist}(0,\gamma_{N,\sigma}(t))\bigr\rvert\ll N^{-1}. \] The nested sequence of these curves, scaled by successive factors \(N^{-1}\), is the fractal geometry of the construction. Theorem 9.1 localises all but \(o(1)\) of the atomic mass to the window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\): that window is built into the fractal because isolation of minima at scale \(N^{-\kappa}\) and packing of the completed curve against the tempering measure \((1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\) produce a negative exponent only there. Consequently any lower bound for \(\operatorname{dist}(0,\gamma_{N,\sigma})\) on this window transfers to \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\rvert\) up to \(O(N^{-1})\). On the same window the unmatched last chord of the completed curve — the order-zero boundary term \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\) — has size \(\tfrac12 N^{1-2\sigma}\) and is not cancelled by the next Bernoulli remainder, which is Corollary 4.3.2. The unique self-similar scale at which this chord is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) while the prefactor is \(N^{1/2}\) remains \(\sigma=\tfrac12\).
The Dirichlet polynomial is a finite sum of characters \(t\mapsto m^{-it}\), hence real-analytic on \(\mathbb{R}\). Each edge of the polygonal path subtends an angle \(O(N^{-1})\) on a fibre of radius \(O(1)\), so the Hausdorff distance to a complete fibre curve is \(O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in \(t\) (nested_spheres_RH.html, Lemma 3.2). The endpoint of the polygon is \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\), so the displayed comparison of moduli follows. Tightness is Theorem 9.1: high-height packing on the completed curve contributes mass \(O(N^{3\varepsilon-1/2})\). Restricting to \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) therefore loses only \(o(1)\) mass, and is the range in which Corollary 4.3.2 applies. The last-chord identification is the order-zero Euler–Maclaurin boundary term already used there; \(O(N^{-1})\) is smaller than \(\tfrac14 N^{1-2\sigma}\) throughout \(S_\delta\).
Lemma 4.3.4 (Hilbert zoom; the scale of L).
The nested completed curves of Lemma 4.3.3, with fibre radii scaled by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) and truncation \(M=N^2\), form a self-similar approximating sequence in the same sense as the dyadic polygonal approximations to a Hilbert curve: granularity \(N^{-1}\) tends to zero, and the parameter interval on which the approximations are packed is the tightness window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), playing the role of the unit interval. The unique rescaling that keeps the \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) pictures in a compact family is division by the DQPT depth \(D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). Write \[ \widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(t)=D_N^{-1}\gamma_{N,\sigma}(t),\qquad \lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] A point of the window is a point of convergence of this zoom when \(\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(t)\to 0\) as \(N\to\infty\): that is precisely a balanced path-closure. As with Hilbert approximations, increasing granularity zooms in on those points and on no others if and only if the zoomed curve remains of order one off them. Identity L is that non-degeneracy statement: \(\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(\gamma)\rvert\ge c\) at every zero with \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\). The fractal therefore limits L: the comparison scale must be \(D_N\) and the height range must be the tightness window, because those are the zoom and the parameter interval built into the nested curves. It does not by itself evaluate the zoomed endpoint: Lemma 4.3.3 still gives \(\lvert\eta\rvert\ge\tfrac14 N^{1-2\sigma}-O(N^{-1})\), and Corollary 4.3.1 still gives \(\lvert\eta\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\).
Quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) at \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) makes the prefactor \(P_N\asymp N^{1/2}\) and the Euler–Maclaurin remainder \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Dividing by \(D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) is the only power of \(N\) for which both the typical path (size \(P_N\lvert\zeta\rvert\)) and the isolation scale \(N^{-\kappa}\) stay bounded, up to \(N^{\pm\varepsilon}\), on the window of Theorem 9.1. Nested fibres of radius \(\asymp N^{-1}\) are the analogue of Hilbert cells of side \(2^{-n}\): each stage refines the previous by a linear factor \(N\), while the quadratic link \(M=N^2\) is the analogue of the fact that a Hilbert cell at level \(n\) is traversed by a curve of length \(2^n\). The zoomed modulus is \(\lvert\eta\rvert/D_N\) up to \(O(N^{-1}/D_N)\to 0\), so non-collapse of \(\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(\gamma)\) is \(\lvert\eta(\sigma+i\gamma)\rvert\ge c D_N\), which is Identity L. The Euler–Maclaurin size of \(\eta\) at an off-line zero is not altered by the rescaling, so the zoom states L but does not replace Theorem 4.3.
Definition 4.4 (Three scales).
On \(s=\sigma+it\) in the truncated strip of Definition 5.0, write \[ P_N(s)=\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert,\qquad F_N(s)=(1+\lvert t\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma},\qquad D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] These are the main-term scale, the Euler–Maclaurin floor, and the DQPT depth. By Theorem 4.3, \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\rvert=P_N(s)\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert+O(F_N(s))\). The three scales are of the same order (up to \(1+\lvert t\rvert\)) if and only if \(\sigma=\tfrac12\). A relative closure is \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\); a balanced closure is \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le D_N\) together with \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\).
Identity L (open).
There exist \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) and \(c>0\) such that for every zero \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\), and every integer \(N\) large enough that \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ge c\,N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] By Lemma 4.3.4 this is exactly non-degeneracy of the Hilbert zoom \(\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}\) at off-line zeros: the fractal limits L to the scale \(D_N\) on the tightness window, which is the unique zoom in which the nested curves stay comparable to a Hilbert approximation on a unit interval. The identity remains open as an evaluation of that zoomed endpoint. Corollary 4.3.1 still gives \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\), which is \(o(D_N)\) throughout \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\), so Euler–Maclaurin predicts collapse of the same zoom at off-line zeros in the band. The sampling error \(O(N^{-1})\) cannot prevent that collapse. See gaps.html.
Proposition 4.6 (L excludes off-line balanced closures).
Assume Identity L. Then no balanced path-closure of generation \(N\), in the sense of Definition 5.1, can satisfy \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\) once \(N\) is large and \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). Consequently every balanced atom in the tightness range lies in the strip \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert<\delta\).
A balanced closure has \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). At a zero with \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\), Identity L gives the opposite inequality. At a non-zero, Theorem 10.1 (relative form) already excludes a persistent atom. The isolation neighbourhood of a closure has radius \(N^{-\kappa}\); for large \(N\) this cannot jump from \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\) to the critical line.

5. Geometric dynamical phase transitions

Definition 5.0 (Truncated strip).
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\). Write \[ S_\delta=\bigl\{s=\sigma+it:\delta\le\sigma\le 1-\delta\bigr\}. \] The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) vanishes only on \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) (Lemma 2.2), hence is bounded away from zero on \(S_\delta\): \(\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert\ge N^{1-\sigma}-1\).
Definition 5.1 (Path-closure events in the strip).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) and set the isolation exponent \(\kappa=\varepsilon\). For each integer \(N\ge 2\) let \(\Sigma_N\) be the finite grid \[ \Sigma_N=\Bigl(\bigl\{\tfrac12\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\delta+j N^{-\kappa}:j\in\mathbb{N}_0\bigr\}\Bigr)\cap[\delta,1-\delta]. \] The critical line is always a grid abscissa, so Corollary 4.1.1 applies on \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) without interpolation. Any finite set of additional abscissae in \([\delta,1-\delta]\), independent of \(N\), may be included in \(\Sigma_N\). A point \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\in\Sigma_N\) is an unrestricted path-closure of generation \(N\) when
  1. \(\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\) (relative depth, Definition 4.4),
  2. \(t\) is a local minimum of \(u\mapsto\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+iu)\bigr\rvert\) in \((t-N^{-\kappa},t+N^{-\kappa})\).
It is a balanced path-closure when, in addition,
  1. \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\),
  2. \(\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) (uniform DQPT depth).
Write \(\mathcal{C}_N\) for the set of balanced closures and \(\mathcal{U}_N\) for the set of unrestricted closures. Both are discrete subsets of \(S_\delta\). On \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) one has \(P_N\asymp N^{1/2}\), so relative and uniform depths coincide. The choice \(\kappa=\varepsilon\) makes a grid miss of size \(N^{-\kappa}\) leak a main term \(P_N O(N^{-\kappa})\) no larger than the relative threshold \(P_N N^{-\varepsilon}\). The tightness exponent \(2\kappa-1/2+\varepsilon=3\varepsilon-1/2\) is negative.

Condition (iii) is the geometric counterpart of the unique critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\). A small endpoint arising merely because the vectors \(m^{-s}\) themselves are short (\(\sigma>1/2\)) fails the relative test unless \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}+O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-\sigma})\), so truncation artifacts are no longer unrestricted closures.

Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Every balanced path-closure \(s=\sigma+it\in\mathcal{C}_N\) satisfies \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\) produces a balanced closure for all \(N\ge N_0(T)\), with \(N_0(T)\) as in Corollary 4.1.2. Every zero of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\) with real part \(\sigma\ge\varepsilon\) produces an unrestricted (relative) closure for all sufficiently large \(N\): Theorem 4.3 gives \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ll F_N(\rho)\), and \(F_N(\rho)/P_N(\rho)\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{-\sigma}\le N^{-\varepsilon}\) once \(N^{\sigma-\varepsilon}\ge 1+\lvert\gamma\rvert\). The nearest grid abscissa leaks a main term \(P_N O(N^{-\kappa})\) which, since \(\kappa=\varepsilon\), does not exceed the relative threshold.
On \(S_\delta\) one has \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1-\sigma}-1\). Condition (iii) therefore forces \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) for large \(N\). On the critical line, which belongs to \(\Sigma_N\), \(P_N\ge N^{1/2}-1\), so (iii) and (iv) reduce to Corollary 4.1.1. Local isolation (ii) holds for large \(N\) because the zeros of \(\Xi\) are isolated and \(N^{-\kappa}\) is eventually smaller than half the gap to the neighbouring zero. For an off-line zero \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\), Corollary 4.3.1 and \(P_N(\rho)\asymp N^{1-\sigma}\) give the relative inequality as soon as \(N^{\sigma-\varepsilon}\ge 1+\lvert\gamma\rvert\). At the nearest \(\sigma_N\in\Sigma_N\), \(\lvert\sigma_N-\sigma\rvert\le N^{-\kappa}\) and \(\zeta(\sigma_N+i\gamma)=O_\rho(N^{-\kappa})\) by analyticity, so the leaked main term is \(O_\rho(P_N N^{-\varepsilon})\). The implied constant depends on \(\rho\) and is absorbed in \(N\ge N_0(\rho)\).
Remark 5.3 (What the two families see).
The balanced family \(\mathcal{C}_N\) cannot see a zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\), because the prefactor is then too small. Unrestricted closures, being relative, see every zero with \(\sigma\ge\varepsilon\), including the right half-strip. An off-line zero in the band \(\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\le\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon/2\) still satisfies the uniform depth \(D_N\) by Corollary 4.3.1, hence is balanced as well. Identity L is precisely the statement that this last clause is false: \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) at such a zero is at least \(D_N\). The Riemann hypothesis is the assertion that \(\mathcal{U}_N\) and \(\mathcal{C}_N\) have the same limiting support; L would prove that.
Definition 5.4 (Relative sublevel set).
\[ \Omega_N=\bigl\{s\in S_\delta:\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\bigr\}. \] The unrestricted closures \(\mathcal{U}_N\) are the isolated \(t\)-minima of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) on the grid lines \(\Sigma_N\) that lie in \(\Omega_N\). Equivalently, one may take one representative from each connected component of \(\Omega_N\) that meets a grid line.
Lemma 5.5 (Rouché localisation of components).
Let \(\rho\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) in the interior of \(S_\delta\), of finite multiplicity. For all \(N\ge N_0(\rho)\) the disk \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert<N^{-\kappa}\) contains exactly as many zeros of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) as the multiplicity of \(\rho\), and meets \(\Omega_N\) in a single connected component. Extra components of \(\Omega_N\), if any, cannot accumulate at a point where \(\zeta\ne 0\).
On the circle \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert=N^{-\kappa}\), one has \(\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\asymp_\rho N^{-\kappa}\). Theorem 4.3 gives \(\bigl\lvert\eta-(1-N^{1-s})\zeta\bigr\rvert=O(F_N)\). The main term has size \(P_N\lvert\zeta\rvert\asymp_\rho N^{1-\sigma-\kappa}\). This dominates \(F_N\asymp(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\) once \(N^{\sigma-\kappa}\gg 1+\lvert\gamma\rvert\), which is the same threshold as Theorem 5.2 with \(\kappa=\varepsilon\). Rouché therefore equates the zeros of \(\eta\) and of \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta\) inside the disk. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has no zero in \(S_\delta\) (Lemma 2.2), so the count equals the multiplicity of \(\rho\). A holomorphic function has \(\lvert f\rvert\) small throughout a neighbourhood of its zeros and not elsewhere on the circle, so that disk meets \(\Omega_N\) in one component. Extra components would produce unrestricted closures whose limit points are non-zeros of \(\Xi\), contradicting Theorem 10.1. (Dirichlet polynomials may have additional zeros in \(S_\delta\); those zeros lie in \(\Omega_N\), but as \(N\to\infty\) they either escape every compact or approach a zero of \(\Xi\).)

6. Continuous-radius interpolation

The integer theory is the one used for the measures. The continuous filter of Definition 2.3 interpolates it. This section records the identities that survive that interpolation, and the local diagonal as it actually evaluates (not as a constant \(c_1=\frac12\)).

Lemma 6.1 (\(C^1\) regularity).
After smoothing the upper limit, \(R\mapsto\eta_R(R^2)(s)\) is \(C^1\) on \([2,\infty)\) for each fixed \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)\). Differentiation under the sum is justified by \[ \sum_{m\le R^2}\lvert\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})\rvert\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R. \]
The coefficients are smooth in \(R\). On the support of \(\varphi\), \(\lvert\partial_R c_m\rvert\ll 1+\lvert\varphi'\rvert_\infty(1+m/R)\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the majorant. For the sharp truncation the map is piecewise \(C^1\), with jumps of size \(2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\) when \(R^2\) is an integer (Definition 6.2).
Definition 6.2 (Splitting of \(\partial_R\eta\)).
On the critical line, \[ \partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R), \] where \(A=\sum_{m\le R^2}(\partial_R c_m)m^{-1/2-i\gamma}\) is the interior coefficient derivative, \(B=c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}(R^2)^{-1/2-i\gamma}\cdot 2R\) is the Leibniz boundary, and \(E\) is the difference between this sharp splitting and a smooth cutoff of width \(R^{2\theta}\), \(\theta\in(0,1/4)\). Since \(\lvert(R^2)^{-1/2}\rvert=R^{-1}\), one has \(\lvert B\rvert=2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\). For the integer filter, \(N\mid N^2\), so \(c_{N^2}=1-N\) and \(\lvert B\rvert=2(N-1)\). The integer theorems of Sections 4–5 and 8–12 do not use \(B\).
Lemma 6.3 (Local diagonal).
On the packet \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\), with \(u=m/R-k\) and local mass \(M_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}\lvert c_m(R)\rvert m^{-1/2}\), \[ \partial_R c_m=-\varphi(u)+(k+u)\varphi'(u), \] and the diagonal \[ D_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}c_m(R)\,(\partial_R c_m(R))\,m^{-1} \] satisfies \[ D_k=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)}{k+u}\,du+R\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)^2}{k+u}\,du+R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}. \] The \(\varphi'\) term integrates by parts to zero by compact support of \(\varphi\). Apostol’s remainder [12] is \(R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}=\int\overline{B}_1 f_k'\) with \(\lvert\overline{B}_1\rvert\le\tfrac12\). Summing in \(k\) gives \[ \sum_{k=1}^R D_k=R\Bigl(\int\varphi^2\Bigr)\log R+O_\varphi(R)+\sum_k R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}, \] which is not a multiple of \(R^{-1}\rho^2\). The evaluation \(\int(1-R\varphi)(-\varphi)\,du=-\tfrac12\) is false (that integral equals \(-1+R\int\varphi^2\)).
Substitute the expressions for \(c_m\) and \(\partial_R c_m\), change variables, and integrate the \(\varphi'\) term by parts. The harmonic sum \(\sum_{k\le R}k^{-1}=\log R+O(1)\) produces the displayed main term. No identification of \(\sum_k\lvert\eta^{(k)}\rvert^2\) with \(\rho^2\) is claimed.

The integer depth bound of Corollary 4.1.1 does not pass through this local form. For real \(R\), the same Euler–Maclaurin comparison as Theorem 4.1 applies to the sharp filter at \(\lfloor R\rfloor\), and Lemma 6.1 controls the increment from \(\lfloor R\rfloor\) to \(R\) on compact ranges of \(t\); the measures below are indexed by integers \(N\).

7. Mean-value estimates

Theorem 7.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, explicit constant).
For a finitely supported sequence \((a_m)\) and \(T\ge 1\), \[ \int_0^T\Bigl\lvert\sum_m a_m m^{-it}\Bigr\rvert^2 dt=\sum_m\lvert a_m\rvert^2 T+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_m m\lvert a_m\rvert^2, \] with \(\lvert\theta\rvert\le 1\) (Montgomery–Vaughan [13]; [7, §7.3]). On the critical line, \(a_m=c_m m^{-1/2}\) therefore gives \[ \int_0^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt=T\sum_{m\le N^2}\frac{\lvert c_m\rvert^2}{m}+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_{m\le N^2}\lvert c_m\rvert^2. \] The coefficient sums are \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\), because the spikes \(c_m=1-N\) dominate. Mean-zero cancellation is in \(\sum c_m m^{-s}\), not in \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\). In particular the diagonal is not \(O(1)\), and \[ \int_{-T}^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt\ll TN\log N+N^3. \]
Expand the square. Off-diagonal integrals are bounded by \(\min(T,1/\lvert\log(n/m)\rvert)\). Hilbert’s inequality as generalised in [13] produces the constant \(2\pi\). For the spikes: there are \(N\) multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^2\), each with \(\lvert c_m\rvert^2=(N-1)^2\), whence \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\).
Lemma 7.2 (Weighted second moment, exact).
The Fourier transform of \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) is \(\pi e^{-\lvert\xi\rvert}\), so \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2}{1+t^2}\,dt =\pi\sum_{m,n\le N^2}c_m\overline{c}_n\,(mn)^{-1/2}\frac{\min(m,n)}{\max(m,n)}. \] The diagonal is \(\pi\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp\pi N\log N\). There is no remainder of size \(N^3\).
Expand the square and integrate termwise. Each pair \((m,n)\) produces \(\pi\exp(-\lvert\log(m/n)\rvert)\).
Lemma 7.3 (Pointwise from local means).
For \(F\in C^1(\mathbb{R})\) and \(H>0\), \[ \lvert F(\gamma)\rvert^2\le\frac2H\int_0^H\lvert F(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du+2H\int_0^H\lvert F'(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du. \] If \(F(t)=\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\), then \(\lvert F'\rvert\le 2(\log N)\,F_{\log}\). Taking \(H=1/\log N\) costs two logarithms relative to the local \(L^2\) mean, and does not produce a power of \(N^{-1}\). Depth of closure is not taken from this lemma; it is Corollary 4.1.1.
Write \(F(\gamma)=\frac1H\int_0^H\bigl(F(\gamma+u)-\int_0^u F'(\gamma+v)\,dv\bigr)du\) and apply \(\lvert x+y\rvert^2\le 2\lvert x\rvert^2+2\lvert y\rvert^2\).

8. Construction of finite spectral measures

The measures live on the truncated strip \(S_\delta\), identified with a closed subset of \(\mathbb{C}\). A Borel measure on \(S_\delta\) is tempered when \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(s)<\infty\). Horizontal compactness of \([\delta,1-\delta]\) reduces tightness to control of the imaginary tails.

Definition 8.1 (Finite measures on the strip).
With \(\mathcal{C}_N\) and \(\mathcal{U}_N\) as in Definition 5.1, set \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{C}_N}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,\delta_s, \qquad \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{U}_N}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,\delta_s, \] and let \(\rho_N\) be the residual density on \(S_\delta\) \[ \rho_N(\sigma+it)=\frac1N\min\Bigl(1,\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it)\bigr\rvert^2\Bigr)\,(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}. \] Then \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) is the balanced measure and \(\nu_N=\nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) is the unrestricted measure. The factor \(1/N\) makes \(\int_{S_\delta}\rho_N=O(N^{-1})\), since \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\sigma\,dt\ll 1\). The atomic weights are independent of \(N\).

The isolation radius \(N^{-\kappa}\) separates distinct path-closure events on each grid line. Lemma 5.5 identifies those events with components of the relative sublevel set \(\Omega_N\), so the atomic part may equivalently be summed over components, one Dirac mass at a minimiser in each. The weight \((1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\) is the standard tempering factor. The original construction on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\) is the restriction of \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\) to the critical-line grid abscissa.

9. Tightness

Theorem 9.1 (Tightness).
The families \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) and \(\{\nu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) are tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures on \(S_\delta\).
The abscissa interval \([\delta,1-\delta]\) is compact, so it is enough to control imaginary tails. The residual satisfies \[ \int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_\delta^{1-\delta}d\sigma\int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}A^{-1}), \] uniformly in \(N\). For the atomic part, split at height \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). On an unrestricted closure with \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), Theorem 4.3 and the relative threshold give \[ P_N(s)\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}+O\bigl(F_N(s)\bigr), \] hence \(\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}+O\bigl((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-\sigma}\bigr)\). On this height range the second term is \(O(N^{1/2-\varepsilon-\sigma})\). For \(\sigma\ge\varepsilon\) this is \(O(N^{1/2-2\varepsilon})\), and in any case \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\to 0\) along any sequence with \(N\to\infty\) at fixed \(s\); for the uniform count, the zeros of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\) up to height \(T\) number \(O(T\log T)\), and \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>A\}\cap\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\bigr) \le\sum_{\lvert\gamma\rvert>A}(1+\lvert\rho\rvert^2)^{-1}=O\bigl((\log A)/A\bigr). \] The same bound applies to \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\subset\nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\) on \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). On a balanced closure one may also divide by the prefactor lower bound \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\): \[ \lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon/2}+O\bigl((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma-1/2+\varepsilon/2}\bigr). \] Splitting at \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) recovers \(O(N^{-\varepsilon/2})\) on the critical line, which is the comparison used for depth. On \(\lvert t\rvert>N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), isolation on each of the \(O(N^{\kappa})\) grid lines yields \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\}\bigr) \ll N^{2\kappa}\int_{N^{1/2-\varepsilon}}^\infty\frac{dt}{1+t^2}\ll N^{2\kappa-1/2+\varepsilon}=N^{3\varepsilon-1/2}. \] The exponent is negative. For any \(\eta>0\) choose \(A\) large enough that the zero-tail and the residual tail are each \(<\eta/3\); then choose \(N_1\) large enough that the high atomic mass is \(<\eta/3\) for \(N\ge N_1\); the finitely many measures with \(N<N_1\) are handled by enlarging \(A\). Prokhorov’s theorem [3] yields tightness of both families.

Markov applied to Theorem 7.1 on the sublevel \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) produces measure \(O(TN^{2\varepsilon}\log N+N^{2+2\varepsilon})\), which does not save a negative power of \(N\). Tightness is taken from Theorem 4.3 and isolation, not from that Markov bound.

10. Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass

Theorem 10.1 (No spurious atoms).
Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) or of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\). Balanced atoms additionally satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\).
Suppose a limit point of \(\{\nu_N\}\) carries an atom at \(s^*\in S_\delta\) with \(\zeta(s^*)\ne 0\). Along a subsequence \(N_j\to\infty\) there are unrestricted closures \(s_j\to s^*\) with \(\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(s_j)\rvert\le P_{N_j}(s_j)\,N_j^{-\varepsilon}\). Theorem 4.3 gives \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(s_j)\bigr\rvert \ge P_{N_j}(s_j)\bigl\lvert\zeta(s_j)\bigr\rvert-O\bigl(F_{N_j}(s_j)\bigr), \] hence \(\lvert\zeta(s_j)\rvert\le N_j^{-\varepsilon}+O\bigl((1+\lvert s^*\rvert)N_j^{-\sigma_j}\bigr)\to 0\). Continuity of \(\zeta\) off its zeros contradicts \(\zeta(s^*)\ne 0\). There is no second main term of comparable size: the polar terms cancel at \(M=N^2\). The same comparison with the additional bounds \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\) and \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) applies to \(\{\mu_N\}\). If \(\zeta(s^*)=0\), there is no contradiction: Theorem 5.2 predicts a relative closure. The abscissa bound for balanced atoms is Theorem 5.2.

11. Vanishing of residual continuous spectrum

Theorem 11.1 (Residual vanishing).
Along any weak-star convergent subsequence the residual densities \(\rho_{N_j}\,d\sigma\,dt\) converge to the zero measure on \(S_\delta\). Consequently every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) or of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is pure-point.
\[ \int_{S_\delta}\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\sigma\,dt=O(N^{-1}). \] Thus the total residual mass tends to zero. For every continuous test function \(f\) of compact support in \(S_\delta\), \(\int f\rho_N\to 0\). Any weak-star limit of \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) therefore coincides with the corresponding limit of \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\), and likewise for \(\nu_N\). Existence of the limits is Theorem 9.1.

12. Realisation of the limiting measure

Theorem 12.1 (Balanced spectral measure).
Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure on \(S_\delta\) whose support consists of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) with real part at most \(\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) is an atom of \(\mu\), with mass \((1+\lvert\rho\rvert^2)^{-1}\). There are no atoms at non-zeros.
Tightness is Theorem 9.1. Residual vanishing is Theorem 11.1, so the limit is pure-point. Theorem 10.1 identifies atoms with zeros of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\) and restricts balanced atoms to \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Conversely, if \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) is a critical-line zero of \(\Xi\), Corollary 4.1.2 produces a balanced path-closure for every \(N\ge N_0(\lvert\gamma\rvert)\), on the grid abscissa \(\sigma=\tfrac12\). For all large \(N\) this closure is the unique local minimum in its isolation interval. The atomic weight \((1+\lvert\rho\rvert^2)^{-1}\) is independent of \(N\), so \(\rho\) is an atom of every weak-star limit.
Theorem 12.2 (Unrestricted spectral measure).
Any weak-star limit point \(\nu\) of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure on \(S_\delta\) whose support consists of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\). Every zero of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\) with real part at least \(\varepsilon\) is an atom of \(\nu\), by the relative clause of Theorem 5.2. In particular an off-line zero appears in \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\), and if also \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) and Corollary 4.3.1 supplies the uniform depth, in \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\).
The identification of atoms with zeros is Theorem 10.1. Existence at a fixed zero of real part \(\ge\varepsilon\) is Theorem 5.2 (relative clause); the grid miss is controlled by \(\kappa=\varepsilon\). The comparison of the two supports is Remark 5.3.
Corollary 12.3 (Conversion of the Riemann hypothesis).
The following are equivalent.
  1. Every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) lies on the critical line (the Riemann hypothesis).
  2. Every unrestricted path-closure is balanced, for all sufficiently large \(N\): \(\mathcal{U}_N=\mathcal{C}_N\).
  3. Every weak-star limit of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is supported on the critical line.
  4. The balanced and unrestricted limiting measures coincide, and their common support is exactly the set of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\).
Theorem 12.1 places every critical-line zero in \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset\operatorname{supp}\nu\). Theorem 12.2 places every zero of real part \(\ge\varepsilon\) in \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\). An off-line zero with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho>\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) is unrestricted but not balanced. An off-line zero in the band \(\bigl[\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2,\tfrac12-\varepsilon/2\bigr]\) is balanced as well, unless Identity L holds. Either event separates \(\nu\) from \(\mu\). Conversely, if every zero lies on the line, then relative closures off the line can accumulate only at zeros, of which there are none, so Theorem 10.1 puts \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) on the critical line, and \(\mathcal{U}_N\) and \(\mathcal{C}_N\) have the same limiting support. Zeros with real part \(<\varepsilon\) are paired by \(\Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s)\) with zeros of real part \(>1-\varepsilon\), already visible to \(\nu_N\).
Theorem 12.4 (Identity L implies the Riemann hypothesis in this construction).
Assume Identity L. Then every weak-star limit of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is supported on the critical line, every weak-star limit of \(\{\nu_N\}\) has no balanced off-line atoms, and the four statements of Corollary 12.3 hold.
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a zero with \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\). Identity L on the tightness range gives \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ge c D_N\). Corollary 4.3.1 gives \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\). For \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) the upper bound is \(o(D_N)\), a contradiction. Thus no such zero exists. Taking \(\delta\le\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\), the complementary abscissae \(\sigma\le\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) pair under \(\Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s)\) with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac34-\varepsilon/2\), already excluded. Hence every non-trivial zero lies on the critical line, which is Corollary 12.3 (i). Proposition 4.6 then places \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\) on the line, and Theorem 12.2 with no off-line zeros places \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) there as well.

14. Conclusions

Euler–Maclaurin on the mean-zero filter at quadratic truncation yields \(\eta=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta+O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma})\) throughout the truncated strip. Unrestricted closures are relative to the main-term scale \(P_N\); balanced closures use the uniform DQPT depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) and require \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\). The measures \(\mu_N\) and \(\nu_N\) are tight; the residual vanishes by construction; every weak-star limit is pure-point, supported on zeros of \(\Xi\). Identity L is the lower bound of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) against \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) at off-line zeros on the tightness range; it is open, and would make Corollary 12.3 a theorem (Theorem 12.4).

The same geometric mechanism extends immediately to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) with the analogous mean-zero filters twisted by a Dirichlet character. The finite polygonal paths themselves supply elementary contours for zero-counting via the argument principle.

Addendum A. Quantum algorithms in geometric language

The classical polygonal construction admits a precise dictionary with existing quantum algorithms. Both the dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) systems of Wei et al. and the quantum algorithms for exponential sums may be expressed entirely in the analytic and geometric language already developed in the body of the paper.

A.1. DQPT systems as coherent polygonal paths

In the quantum systems of Wei et al. the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) generates a Loschmidt amplitude of the schematic form \[ \mathcal{L}(\beta,t)=\sum_n e^{-\beta\ln n}\,e^{-it\ln n}=\sum_n n^{-\beta-it}. \] At the critical inverse temperature \(\beta=1/2\) this reduces to a Dirichlet series on the critical line. The vanishing of \(\mathcal{L}(1/2,t)\) is the signature of a dynamical quantum phase transition.

The same object appears in the classical setting as the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path. After the mean-zero root-of-unity filter and the quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\), Theorem 4.1 identifies \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\) with \((1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it)\) up to \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Consequently:

  • the quantum Loschmidt amplitude vanishing is the analogue of a path-closure event of controlled depth for the classical polygonal sum;
  • the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) is the analytic counterpart of the geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • the discrete set of times at which the quantum amplitude vanishes is the support of the pure-point measure constructed from the classical path-closure ordinates.

The geometric measure \(\mu\) obtained in Theorem 12.1 is the classical shadow of the discrete spectrum of DQPT times. The two realisations—one unitary, one polygonal—encode the same cancellations.

A.2. Quantum evaluation of exponential sums as accelerated path endpoints

Any quantum algorithm that estimates weighted exponential sums \[ S(f,w,N)=\sum_{k}w_k\,e^{2\pi i f(k)} \] by amplitude estimation (Tyagi [14]) evaluates, under the choice \(f(k)=-(t/2\pi)\log k\) and suitable weights, the very Dirichlet polynomials that define the coherent polygonal paths. In geometric language the algorithm computes the Euclidean length of the endpoint vector of the path (or decides whether that length falls below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\)) with a quadratic improvement in the precision parameter relative to classical sampling.

The definition of a path-closure event, the isolation of local minima, the construction of the measures \(\mu_N\), and the weak-star analysis remain purely classical. The quantum routine supplies only a computational acceleration for the numerical detection of those events at large height. It does not alter the geometric or measure-theoretic content of the construction.

Addendum B. Refined quadratic asymptotic

Theorem 4.1 is already uniform in \(t\) with remainder \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is the input to depth-of-closure and to the exclusion of spurious atoms. For large \(\lvert t\rvert\) one may replace Euler–Maclaurin by the approximate functional equation, keeping the dual sums of lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). The following statement records the exponent that would follow from Bourgain’s subconvexity on those dual sums. It is not required for Theorems 9.1–12.2.

Refined remainder (optional).
There exist a positive exponent \(\delta=29/168-\eta\) and a range \(\delta'=1/3+1/42-\eta\) such that, for \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\), the remainder in Theorem 4.1 may be replaced by \(O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}(1+\lvert t\rvert)^\varepsilon\bigr)\) after inserting Bourgain’s bound \(\mu(1/2)=13/84\) and a van-der-Corput estimate of order 1 on the dual tails, provided an explicit constant in Bourgain’s bound is fixed.
On the range \(\lvert t\rvert\le 2\pi N\) both dual sums of the approximate functional equation are empty and Theorem 4.1 already gives \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is stronger. For \(2\pi N<\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\) write \[ D_x(s)=\zeta(s)-\chi(s)D_{\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi x)}(1-s)+R(x,s) \] and substitute \(x=N^2\) and \(x=N\) into \(\eta=D_{N^2}-N^{1-s}D_N\). The dual sums have lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). Estimate them by convexity or by Bourgain, and estimate the AFE remainders by van der Corput. Collecting exponents as in the previous seven-step sketch yields \(\delta=1/6+1/168-\eta\) from Weyl plus one van-der-Corput increment, or the displayed value from Bourgain. The dual terms vanish at critical-line zeros because they contain \(\zeta(1-s)\) or a short Dirichlet polynomial that is \(O(1)\) when the length is \(O(1)\).
