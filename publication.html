Geometric Dynamical Phase Transitions and the Spectral Measure of the Riemann Zeros

A self-contained geometric framework, with continuous-radius interpolation

Victor Geere · 20 August 2026

Abstract. We develop a purely geometric formulation of dynamical phase transitions associated with the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Starting from the depth-one finite Euler sums \(H_x^{(k)}=\sum_{n\le x}n^{-k}\) at complex weight \(k=s\), we introduce coherent polygonal partial sums whose successive vectors form a discrete path in the complex plane: the central object is the exact pair \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}=H_{N^2}^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}\), equivalently a Dirichlet polynomial carrying a mean-zero root-of-unity filter; no infinite series is truncated. When the outer Euler-sum length is linked to the filter period by the quadratic geometric relation \(M=N^2\), Euler–Maclaurin applied to each Euler sum separately supplies, throughout the truncated strip \(\delta\le\operatorname{Re}s\le 1-\delta\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+O\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr). \] On the critical line the error is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) and the prefactor has size \(N^{1/2}\). Path-closure events are located as isolated minima in the whole strip, at relative depth \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert N^{-\varepsilon}\). A closure is balanced when the prefactor has size at least \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\), \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), and \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) (slow passage). Balanced closures can occur only for \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\); every critical-line zero produces one once \(N\) exceeds an explicit threshold \(N_0(\gamma)\). The associated measures \(\mu_N\) (balanced) and \(\nu_N\) (unrestricted) live on the strip; both families are tight (Theorem 9.1, via the tail-localisation of Lemma 9.6); every weak-star limit is pure-point, supported on zeros of \(\Xi\). Unrestricted closures use the north-pole chart of the nested fibre: Marty speed \(\eta^{\#}=\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert/(1+\lvert\eta\rvert^2)\) is the spherical speed of the lift, and excludes the left half-strip (Lemma 4.8). Identity L is the Euclidean speed of last-chord cancellation at simple zeros with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\). A vanishing aperture with the jet of Lemma 4.5.3 places every balanced atom on the critical line (Theorem 12.6). The Riemann hypothesis is the coincidence of the two families, equivalently the absence of right-half atoms of \(\nu\). Addendum C runs the same construction on the Davenport–Heilbronn function, which has a functional equation and no Euler product: the framework transfers verbatim and correctly reports a false Riemann hypothesis at Spira's off-line zero, so the conversion is sharp — and no theorem provable from the toolbox used here can be the Riemann hypothesis.

1. Introduction and relation to Wei et al.

Wei, Zhai, Lu, Yang, Gao, Wei, Song, Nori, Xin and Long established a direct correspondence between the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function and dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs) in engineered quantum many-body systems [11]. In their construction the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) produces a Loschmidt amplitude whose vanishing signals a DQPT. The critical temperature of that construction is precisely \(\beta=1/2\), so that the occurrence of DQPTs at a unique temperature is equivalent to the statement that all non-trivial zeros lie on the critical line.

The present work supplies a purely classical, geometric counterpart of the same correspondence. The role of the Loschmidt amplitude is played by the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path built from depth-one finite Euler sums of weight \(s\) (Definition 3.0): \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=H_M^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_{\lfloor M/N\rfloor}^{(s)} =\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s},\qquad c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1&N\nmid m,\\1-N&N\mid m.\end{cases} \] A path-closure event of controlled depth \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\bigl(\tfrac12+it\bigr)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] is the geometric analogue of the vanishing of the Loschmidt amplitude. The quadratic link \(M=N^2\) between the two Euler-sum lengths is the geometric counterpart of the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\).

The relationship is made rigorous by the following dictionary:

  • Loschmidt amplitude \(\longleftrightarrow\) coherent polygonal endpoint \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\);
  • DQPT (vanishing of the amplitude) \(\longleftrightarrow\) path-closure event of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\);
  • critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) \(\longleftrightarrow\) quadratic geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • spectral support of the zeros on the critical line \(\longleftrightarrow\) pure-point support of the limiting tempered measure constructed from the balanced path-closures in the strip.

The measures of Sections 8–12 are built from path-closures located in the truncated strip, not a priori on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\). Balanced closures are confined to \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) by Theorem 5.2; the identification of their limit with zeros of \(\Xi\) is Theorem 12.1. The continuous-radius interpolation of the filter, previously a separate argument, is Section 6. Nested-sphere geometry is recorded in nested_spheres_RH.html.

2. Preliminaries

Definition 2.1 (Completed xi-function).
\[ \Xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),\qquad \Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s). \] The zeros of \(\Xi\) are precisely the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\).
Lemma 2.2 (Purification identity, integer filter).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) the coefficients \[ c_m^{(N)}=\begin{cases}1 & N\nmid m,\\1-N & N\mid m\end{cases} \] satisfy \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s),\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>1, \] with meromorphic continuation to the whole plane. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has zeros only on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\).
Write the Dirichlet series for \(\zeta(s)\) and subtract the terms with \(N\mid m\), which produces the factor \(N^{1-s}\zeta(s)\). Equivalently, \(c_m^{(N)}\) is the convolution of the constant sequence \(1\) with the finite geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Definition 2.3 (Continuous mean-zero filter).
For real \(R\ge 2\) and a fixed even bump \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) with \(\int\varphi=1\), \[ c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\bigl(m/R-k\bigr). \] When \(R=N\) is an integer, the sharp-bump limit recovers \(c_m^{(N)}\). The associated coherent sum is \(\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)m^{-s}\) with \(X=R^2\), and \(\rho(R,s)=\lvert\eta_R(R^2)(s)\rvert\).
Definition 2.4 (Tempered measure).
A positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is tempered if \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(t)<\infty. \] The same name is used for a positive Borel measure on the truncated strip \(S_\delta\subset\mathbb{C}\) of Definition 5.0 with \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(s)<\infty\). Every tempered measure admits a unique decomposition \(\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\) into pure-point, absolutely continuous and singular continuous parts. The residual continuous spectrum is \(\mu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\mu_{\mathrm{sc}}\).

3. Coherent polygonal partial sums

Definition 3.0 (Depth-one finite Euler sums).
For real \(x\ge 0\) and a complex weight \(k\), \[ H_x^{(k)}=\sum_{n\le x}n^{-k}, \] an empty sum being \(0\). At integer weight these are the generalised harmonic numbers, the depth-one case of the finite Euler sums of the multiple-harmonic-sum literature (Hoffman [16]), where the depth counts the nested summation indices and the weight the exponent. Throughout this paper the weight is the complex variable of the strip, \(k=s\); each \(H_x^{(s)}\) is then an entire function of \(s\) — a finite Dirichlet polynomial — and is the endpoint of the plain \(\lfloor x\rfloor\)-step polygon with steps \(m^{-s}\).
Definition 3.1 (Coherent polygonal sum).
For integers \(N\ge 2\) and \(M\ge 1\) set \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=H_M^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_{\lfloor M/N\rfloor}^{(s)}. \] The coherent sum is an exact, finite combination of two depth-one Euler sums of weight \(s\); no truncation of an infinite series occurs. The quadratic link \(M=N^2\) of Section 4 makes the inner length equal to the filter period, \(\lfloor N^2/N\rfloor=N\).
Lemma 3.2 (Filter form and the polygonal path).
With \(c_m^{(N)}\) as in Lemma 2.2, \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}. \] The associated polygonal path in the complex plane is the sequence of partial sums of the vectors \(c_m^{(N)}m^{-s}\); the endpoint of the path is precisely \(\eta_N^{(M)}(s)\), and the coefficients have mean zero over every complete block of \(N\) consecutive integers.
\(N^{1-s}H_{\lfloor M/N\rfloor}^{(s)}=N\sum_{j\le\lfloor M/N\rfloor}(Nj)^{-s}=N\sum_{m\le M,\;N\mid m}m^{-s}\), since the multiples of \(N\) not exceeding \(M\) are exactly \(Nj\) with \(j\le\lfloor M/N\rfloor\). Subtracting this from \(H_M^{(s)}=\sum_{m\le M}m^{-s}\) replaces the coefficient \(1\) by \(1-N\) at precisely those multiples.

The mean-zero property guarantees that the path has no systematic drift. Letting \(M\to\infty\) in Lemma 3.2 recovers the purification identity of Lemma 2.2: the pair of Euler sums is the finite form of \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\). The next section compares the pair at the quadratic link \(M=N^2\) to that identity, applying Euler–Maclaurin to each Euler sum separately.

4. Quadratic scaling and the explicit remainder

Each depth-one Euler sum is related to \(\zeta\) by the standard Euler–Maclaurin formula (Titchmarsh [1, §4.11]; Montgomery–Vaughan [7, Theorem 1.12]): for \(x\ge 1\), \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\ge\sigma_0>0\) and \(s\ne 1\), \[ H_x^{(s)}=\sum_{n\le x}n^{-s}=\zeta(s)+\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\frac12 x^{-s}+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,x^{-\sigma}\bigr). \] No auxiliary identity is required: Definition 3.1 already presents \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=H_{N^2}^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}\), and the formula is applied to each Euler sum separately.

Theorem 4.1 (Explicit remainder).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) and every real \(t\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) =(1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr)+O(1+\lvert t\rvert), \] with an absolute implied constant \(C_1\). In particular, at a critical-line zero one has \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le C_1(1+\lvert t\rvert)\).
Apply the displayed Euler–Maclaurin formula at \(\sigma=1/2\) to both Euler sums \(H_{N^2}^{(s)}\) and \(H_N^{(s)}\). The remainders are \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1})\) and \(O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-1/2})\) respectively. Multiplication of the second remainder by \(N^{1-s}=N^{1/2-it}\) produces \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). The polar terms cancel: \[ \frac{(N^2)^{1-s}}{1-s}=N^{1-s}\cdot\frac{N^{1-s}}{1-s}. \] The order-zero boundary terms \(\tfrac12 x^{-s}\) contribute \(\tfrac12 N^{-1-2it}\) from \(H_{N^2}^{(s)}\) and \(\tfrac12 N^{1-s}N^{-s}=\tfrac12 N^{-2it}\) from \(N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}\), both \(O(1)\). Collecting the main terms gives \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) on the critical line.
Corollary 4.1.1 (Depth of closure at critical-line zeros).
Let \(\gamma\) be a real number with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon} \] for all integers \(N\ge N_0(\gamma)\), where \(N_0(\gamma)=\bigl(C(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\bigr)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\) and \(C\) is the implied constant of Theorem 4.1. In particular \(\log N_0(\gamma)\ll\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\).
Theorem 4.1 and \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\) give \(\lvert\eta\rvert=O(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\). Compare with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Corollary 4.1.2 (Uniform threshold up to height \(T\)).
Let \(T\ge 2\) and \(N_0(T)=\bigl(C(1+T)\bigr)^{2/(1-2\varepsilon)}\), with \(C\) as in Corollary 4.1.1. Then every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\) produces a balanced path-closure for all \(N\ge N_0(T)\). The number of such zeros is \(O(T\log T)\) (Titchmarsh [1, §9.3]).
The map \(\gamma\mapsto N_0(\gamma)\) is increasing in \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\), so \(N_0(\gamma)\le N_0(T)\) throughout \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\). Riemann–von Mangoldt supplies the count.
Proposition 4.2 (Geometric distinction of the quadratic link).
Fix the filter period \(N\) and consider the one-parameter family of Euler-sum pairs \[ Q_\alpha(s)=H_{N^\alpha}^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)},\qquad\alpha>1. \] (i) \(Q_\alpha\) coincides with the coherent sum \(\eta_N^{(\lfloor N^\alpha\rfloor)}\) of Definition 3.1 for every \(N\ge 2\) precisely when \(\alpha=2\): the inner Euler sum removes the multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^\alpha\) exactly when \(\lfloor N^\alpha/N\rfloor=N\). (ii) The polar terms of the two Euler–Maclaurin expansions cancel if and only if \((N^\alpha)^{1-s}=N^{1-s}\cdot N^{1-s}\), that is \(\alpha=2\); otherwise the comparison with \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) carries the uncancelled pole term \(\bigl(N^{\alpha(1-s)}-N^{2(1-s)}\bigr)/(1-s)\). (iii) At \(\alpha=2\) the two remainders are \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{-2\sigma})\) and \(N^{1-\sigma}\,O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{-\sigma})\), so on \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) the error is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) while the prefactor of \(\zeta\) has size \(N^{1/2}\).
(i) \(N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}=N\sum_{j\le N}(Nj)^{-s}\) is the sum over the multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^2\) (Lemma 3.2); for \(\alpha>2\) the multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^\alpha\) are more numerous, for \(\alpha<2\) fewer, for every \(N\ge 2\). (ii) and (iii) are the displayed Euler–Maclaurin formula applied to each Euler sum, as in the proof of Theorem 4.1.
Theorem 4.3 (Remainder in the strip).
Let \(\sigma_0\in(0,1/2)\) and let \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\in[\sigma_0,1-\sigma_0]\) and \(s\ne 1\). For every integer \(N\ge 2\), \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr). \] In particular \[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr), \] and on \(\sigma=1/2\) this is Theorem 4.1.
Apply the Euler–Maclaurin formula of Section 4 to the Euler sums \(H_{N^2}^{(s)}\) and \(H_N^{(s)}\). The polar terms cancel as before. The order-zero boundary terms contribute \(\tfrac12 N^{-2s}-\tfrac12 N^{1-2s}=\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\). The remainder for \(H_{N^2}^{(s)}\) is \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{-2\sigma})\); multiplication of the remainder for \(H_N^{(s)}\) by \(N^{1-s}\) produces \(O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma})\).
Remark (Second-order pair remainder, unused).
Because the expansion is performed on each Euler sum separately, one further Bernoulli term in the classical formula (Apostol [12]) replaces the remainder \(O_{\sigma_0}((1+\lvert s\rvert)x^{-\sigma})\) by \(O_{\sigma_0}((1+\lvert s\rvert)^2x^{-\sigma-1})\), and hence the remainder of Theorem 4.3 by \(O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)^2N^{-2\sigma}\bigr)\) — smaller than the half-term \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\) by the factor \(N/(1+\lvert s\rvert)^2\), so that the half-term is an honest second asymptotic term whenever \(\lvert s\rvert=o(N^{1/2})\). For \(\lvert s\rvert\le N\) this sharpens the stated bound. Every statement below uses only the stated form.
Corollary 4.3.1 (Depth at zeros in the strip).
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\sigma\in[\sigma_0,1-\sigma_0]\), and fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma}. \] This is at most \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) as soon as \(N^{1-2\sigma}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). In particular, every zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) meets the depth threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\) (depending on \(\gamma\)). Whether such a zero is an unrestricted closure is decided by the Marty condition of Definition 5.1 (iii): it fails on the left of \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) (Lemma 4.8, Theorem 5.2).
Theorem 4.3 and \(\zeta(\rho)=0\). Compare \(N^{1-2\sigma}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Corollary 4.3.2 (Floor lower bound on the tightness range).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\). If \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) is a zero of \(\Xi\) in the truncated strip and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), then for all sufficiently large \(N\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ge\tfrac14\,N^{1-2\sigma}. \] Consequently such a zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac14-\varepsilon/2\) cannot satisfy \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). This is Identity L\(_0\) of gaps.html. The range \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) is the tightness window of Theorem 9.1; it is the same window in which the polygonal path, completed to a continuous curve over \(\mathbb{R}\), has its floor under control (Lemma 4.3.3). On that window \(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2/N\le N^{-2\varepsilon}\to 0\), so the next Euler–Maclaurin remainder is absorbed.
Retain the Bernoulli term \(B_2=1/6\) in Euler–Maclaurin: it contributes \(-\frac{s}{12}N^{-2s}(1-N^{-2})\) to \(\eta\), of size \(O(\lvert s\rvert N^{-2\sigma})\). The next remainder is \(O_{\sigma_0}(\lvert s\rvert^2 N^{-2\sigma})\). At a zero the explicit half-term is \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\), of size \(\tfrac12(N-1)N^{-2\sigma}\). The ratio of the remainder to the half-term is \(O(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2/N)\). On the tightness range \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) this is \(O(N^{-2\varepsilon})\), which is less than \(1/8\) for large \(N\), uniformly in \(\gamma\). The parenthesis is then at least \(N/4\). Comparing \(N^{1-2\sigma}\) with \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) yields the abscissa restriction. (The older bound \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le c_0 N^{1/2}\) with a small absolute \(c_0\) is weaker for large \(N\), since \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}=o(N^{1/2})\).)
Lemma 4.3.3 (Completed curve over \(\mathbb{R}\), tightness window).
For each fixed \(\sigma\in[\delta,1-\delta]\) the map \[ t\mapsto\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}, \] is a trigonometric polynomial, hence a real-analytic curve \(\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\). Completing the underlying polygonal path to a continuous fibre curve as in nested_spheres_RH.html (sampling error \(O(N^{-1})\) in Hausdorff distance on the fibre; interactive picture in viz.html) yields a continuous curve \(\gamma_{N,\sigma}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\) with \[ \bigl\lvert\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it)\bigr\rvert-\operatorname{dist}(0,\gamma_{N,\sigma}(t))\bigr\rvert\ll N^{-1}. \] The nested sequence of these curves, scaled by successive factors \(N^{-1}\), is the fractal geometry of the construction. Theorem 9.1 localises all but \(o(1)\) of the atomic mass to the window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\): that window is built into the fractal because isolation of minima at scale \(N^{-\kappa}\) and packing of the completed curve against the tempering measure \((1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\) produce a negative exponent only there. Consequently any lower bound for \(\operatorname{dist}(0,\gamma_{N,\sigma})\) on this window transfers to \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}\rvert\) up to \(O(N^{-1})\). On the same window the unmatched last chord of the completed curve — the order-zero boundary term \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\) — has size \(\tfrac12 N^{1-2\sigma}\) and is not cancelled by the next Bernoulli remainder, which is Corollary 4.3.2. The unique self-similar scale at which this chord is \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) while the prefactor is \(N^{1/2}\) remains \(\sigma=\tfrac12\).
The Dirichlet polynomial is a finite sum of characters \(t\mapsto m^{-it}\), hence real-analytic on \(\mathbb{R}\). Each edge of the polygonal path subtends an angle \(O(N^{-1})\) on a fibre of radius \(O(1)\), so the Hausdorff distance to a complete fibre curve is \(O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in \(t\) (nested_spheres_RH.html, Lemma 3.2). The endpoint of the polygon is \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\), so the displayed comparison of moduli follows. Tightness is Theorem 9.1: high-height packing on the completed curve contributes mass \(O(N^{3\varepsilon-1/2}\log N)\). Restricting to \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) therefore loses only \(o(1)\) mass, and is the range in which Corollary 4.3.2 applies. The last-chord identification is the order-zero Euler–Maclaurin boundary term already used there; \(O(N^{-1})\) is smaller than \(\tfrac14 N^{1-2\sigma}\) throughout \(S_\delta\).
Lemma 4.3.4 (Hilbert zoom; the envelope scale).
The nested completed curves of Lemma 4.3.3, with fibre radii scaled by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) and outer length \(M=N^2\), form a self-similar approximating sequence in the same sense as the dyadic polygonal approximations to a Hilbert curve: granularity \(N^{-1}\) tends to zero, and the parameter interval on which the approximations are packed is the tightness window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), playing the role of the unit interval. The unique rescaling that keeps the \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) pictures in a compact family is division by the DQPT depth \(D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). Write \[ \widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(t)=D_N^{-1}\gamma_{N,\sigma}(t),\qquad \lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] A point of the window is an envelope collapse of this zoom when \(\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(t)\to 0\) as \(N\to\infty\). Corollary 4.3.1 says every off-line zero in the band \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) is an envelope collapse: the last chord has Euclidean size \(N^{1-2\sigma}=o(D_N)\). The fractal therefore does not force \(\lvert\eta\rvert\ge c\,D_N\) off the line — that comparison is the envelope of \(C_1(1+\lvert t\rvert)\) over the whole window, too coarse at a single height. What the nested curves do detect is the speed of the cancellation that produces the collapse (Lemma 4.5.2).
The quadratic link \(M=N^2\) at \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) makes the prefactor \(P_N\asymp N^{1/2}\) and the Euler–Maclaurin remainder \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Dividing by \(D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) is the only power of \(N\) for which both the typical path (size \(P_N\lvert\zeta\rvert\)) and the isolation scale \(N^{-\kappa}\) stay bounded, up to \(N^{\pm\varepsilon}\), on the window of Theorem 9.1. Nested fibres of radius \(\asymp N^{-1}\) are the analogue of Hilbert cells of side \(2^{-n}\): each stage refines the previous by a linear factor \(N\), while the quadratic link \(M=N^2\) is the analogue of the fact that a Hilbert cell at level \(n\) is traversed by a curve of length \(2^n\). The zoomed modulus is \(\lvert\eta\rvert/D_N\) up to \(O(N^{-1}/D_N)\to 0\), so envelope non-collapse of \(\widetilde{\gamma}_{N,\sigma}(\gamma)\) would be \(\lvert\eta(\sigma+i\gamma)\rvert\ge c D_N\). The Euler–Maclaurin size of \(\eta\) at an off-line zero is not altered by the rescaling. That envelope comparison is therefore false at any such zero (Corollary 4.3.1). The quantity that does not collapse is the speed of the last-chord cancellation, Lemma 4.5.2.
Definition 4.4 (Three scales).
On \(s=\sigma+it\) in the truncated strip of Definition 5.0, write \[ P_N(s)=\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert,\qquad F_N(s)=(1+\lvert t\rvert)\,N^{1-2\sigma},\qquad D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] These are the main-term scale, the Euler–Maclaurin floor, and the DQPT depth. By Theorem 4.3, \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\rvert=P_N(s)\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert+O(F_N(s))\). The three scales are of the same order (up to \(1+\lvert t\rvert\)) if and only if \(\sigma=\tfrac12\). A relative closure is \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\). A balanced closure is a relative closure that is also a slow passage in the sense of Definition 5.1: \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le D_N\), \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\), and \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\).
Lemma 4.5 (Differentiated remainder).
On the truncated strip of Definition 5.0, for every integer \(N\ge 2\), \[ \partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s) =i(1-N^{1-s})\zeta'(s)+i(\log N)\,N^{1-s}\zeta(s) -i(\log N)(1-N)N^{-2s} +O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)(\log N)\,N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr). \]
Differentiate the Euler–Maclaurin expansion of Theorem 4.3 in the height. Each monomial \(m^{-s}\) contributes a factor \(-\log m\), and \(\log m\le 2\log N\) throughout \(m\le N^2\). The polar terms continue to cancel at \(M=N^2\). The order-zero boundary term differentiates to the displayed logarithmic multiple of \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\). The remainder of Theorem 4.3 is an Euler–Maclaurin integral of \(f'(x)\) against a periodic Bernoulli function; differentiating under that integral in \(t\) produces one extra logarithm and the same power of \(N\).
Lemma 4.5.1 (Speed at critical-line zeros).
Let \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\) with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\). Then \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr\rvert \le N^{1/2}\lvert\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)\rvert+O\bigl((1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\log N\bigr). \] In particular, for every fixed such \(\gamma\) one has \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).
Lemma 4.5 at \(\sigma=1/2\), with \(\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)=0\). The prefactor has size \(N^{1/2}\). The half-term derivative is \(O(\log N)\). Compare with \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\).
Lemma 4.5.2 (Speed of last-chord cancellation).
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a simple zero of \(\Xi\) in the interior of \(S_\delta\), and let \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\). There is \(N_1(\rho)\) such that for all \(N\ge N_1(\rho)\) and every \(s\) with \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}\), \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert \ge\tfrac12 N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta'(\rho)\rvert. \] If also \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\) with \(\delta>\varepsilon\), then for all large \(N\) this exceeds \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\).
Write \(s=\rho+h\) with \(\lvert h\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}\). Then \(\zeta(s)=\zeta'(\rho)h+O_\rho(h^2)\) and \(\zeta'(s)=\zeta'(\rho)+O_\rho(h)\). Substitute into Lemma 4.5. The leading term is \(i(1-N^{1-s})\zeta'(s)\), of size \(\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}+O(1)\bigr)\bigl(\lvert\zeta'(\rho)\rvert+O(N^{-\varepsilon})\bigr)\). The term \(N^{1-s}(\log N)\zeta(s)\) has size \(O_\rho\bigl(N^{1-\sigma-\varepsilon}\log N\bigr)\), which is \(o\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}\bigr)\). The half-term derivative has size \(O\bigl(N^{1-2\sigma}\log N\bigr)\), likewise \(o\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}\bigr)\). The Euler–Maclaurin remainder contributes \(O_\rho\bigl(N^{1-2\sigma}\log N\bigr)\). For large \(N\) the leading term dominates and the factor \(\tfrac12\) remains. Comparing \(N^{1-\sigma}\) with \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) uses \(1-\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\delta>\tfrac12+\varepsilon\).
Lemma 4.5.3 (Jet of order \(m\)).
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) of multiplicity \(m\ge 1\), so \(\zeta^{(\,j)}(\rho)=0\) for \(0\le j<m\) and \(\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\ne 0\). Let \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\). There is \(N_m(\rho)\) such that for all \(N\ge N_m(\rho)\) and every \(s\) with \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}\), \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t^{\,m}\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert \ge\tfrac12 N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\rvert. \] If \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\) with \(\delta>\varepsilon\), this exceeds \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}(2\log N)^m\) for all large \(N\). The case \(m=1\) is Lemma 4.5.2.
Leibniz on Lemma 4.5 iterated \(m\) times: \[ \partial_t^{\,m}\bigl[(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\bigr] =\sum_{j=0}^m\binom{m}{j}\partial_t^{\,j}(1-N^{1-s})\,\partial_t^{\,m-j}\zeta(s). \] For \(j\ge 1\), \(\partial_t^{\,j}(1-N^{1-s})=-( -i\log N)^j N^{1-s}\). At \(s=\rho+h\) with \(\lvert h\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}\), Taylor gives \(\zeta^{(m-j)}(s)=O_\rho(\lvert h\rvert^{j})\) for \(j\ge 1\), since the first \(m-1\) derivatives of \(\zeta\) vanish at \(\rho\). Those terms are therefore \[ O_\rho\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}(\log N)^j N^{-\varepsilon j}\lvert\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\rvert\bigr)=o\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\rvert\bigr), \] because \(N^{\varepsilon}/\log N\to\infty\). The \(j=0\) term is \((1-N^{1-s})i^m\zeta^{(m)}(s)\), of size \(\bigl(N^{1-\sigma}+O(1)\bigr)\bigl(\lvert\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\rvert+O(N^{-\varepsilon})\bigr)\). The half-term of Theorem 4.3 differentiates to size \(O\bigl(N^{1-2\sigma}(2\log N)^m\bigr)\), and the Euler–Maclaurin remainder to \(O_\rho\bigl((1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)(2\log N)^m N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr)\), both \(o(N^{1-\sigma})\). For large \(N\) the factor \(\tfrac12\) remains. Comparing \(N^{1-\sigma}\) with \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}(2\log N)^m\) uses \(1-\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\delta>\tfrac12+\varepsilon\) and \((2\log N)^m=N^{o(1)}\).
Identity L (slow passage; proved).
Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a simple zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\) and \(\delta>0\). Then for every \(\varepsilon\in(0,\min(\delta,1/6))\) and every integer \(N\) large enough that \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\ge\tfrac12 N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta'(\rho)\rvert> N^{1/2+\varepsilon} \] throughout the isolation disk \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}\). Geometrically: the last chord of Lemma 4.3.3 has size \(\tfrac12 N^{1-2\sigma}\) and is cancelled, in that disk, by the main-term slope \(P_N\zeta'\asymp N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta'\rvert\). The cancellation is a high-speed transit through a small neighbourhood of the origin, not a slow Hilbert-cell visit. Envelope non-degeneracy \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ge c\,D_N\) is false throughout the band \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) (Corollary 4.3.1) — and true below it, where the floor of Corollary 4.3.2 exceeds \(D_N\) — so it is not the identity. The remaining simple abscissae for a fixed construction with parameter \(\varepsilon\) are \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon<\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). For multiplicity \(m\ge 2\), Lemma 4.5.3 is the same comparison on \(\partial_t^{\,m}\eta\). See gaps.html.
Proposition 4.6 (L excludes off-line simple balanced closures).
Assume \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\). Let \(\rho\) be a simple zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\) and \(\delta>\varepsilon\). Then no balanced path-closure of generation \(N\), in the sense of Definition 5.1, can lie in the isolation disk of \(\rho\) once \(N\) is large and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). Consequently no such \(\rho\) is a persistent balanced atom.
A balanced closure \(s\) satisfies \(\lvert\partial_t\eta(s)\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) (Definition 5.1 (vi)). Identity L gives the opposite inequality throughout the isolation disk. At a non-zero, Theorem 10.1 already excludes a persistent atom. The isolation neighbourhood of a closure has radius \(N^{-\kappa}\); for large \(N\) this cannot jump from \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\) to the critical line.
Proposition 4.7 (Jet excludes multiple off-line balanced closures).
Assume \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) and \(K\in\mathbb{N}\). Let \(\rho\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) of multiplicity \(m\) with \(1\le m\le K\) and \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta\), \(\delta>\varepsilon\). Then no point of the isolation disk of \(\rho\) can satisfy \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t^{\,m}\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}(2\log N)^m \] for all large \(N\) with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). In particular, if balanced closures are required to obey this bound for every \(k=1,\dots,K\), no such \(\rho\) is a persistent balanced atom.
Lemma 4.5.3 gives the opposite inequality on \(\partial_t^{\,m}\eta\) throughout the disk. The case \(m=1\) is Proposition 4.6.
Lemma 4.8 (North-pole lift; Marty speed).
Identify the nested-sphere fibre with \(\mathbb{C}\) by stereographic projection from the north pole \(N=(0,0,1)\) onto the equatorial plane, as in projection.html: \[ \pi_N(x,y,z)=\frac{x+iy}{1-z},\qquad \pi_N^{-1}(w)=\Bigl(\frac{2w}{1+\lvert w\rvert^2},\;\frac{\lvert w\rvert^2-1}{1+\lvert w\rvert^2}\Bigr)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}. \] The south pole \(S\) is the origin of \(\mathbb{C}\); \(N\) is the point at infinity. The round metric on \(S^2\) pulls back to the spherical derivative (Marty) \[ \eta^{\#}(s)=\frac{\bigl\lvert\partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert}{1+\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert^2}. \] Near \(S\) one has \(\eta^{\#}\sim\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\). A Hilbert-cell visit on the fibre is a slow visit to \(S\), not a Euclidean-small value in the unbounded chart. Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a simple zero of \(\Xi\). If \(\sigma<\tfrac12\), then \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ge\tfrac14 N^{1-2\sigma}\to\infty\) (Corollary 4.3.2), so \(\pi_N^{-1}(\eta(\rho))\to N\): the endpoint at the zero itself tends to the projection centre, not to \(S\). At a local minimiser of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) in the isolation disk one has \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) small, hence \(\eta^{\#}\sim\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\), and Identity L gives \(\eta^{\#}>N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) once \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\). If \(\sigma>\tfrac12\), then \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\asymp N^{1-\sigma}\lvert\zeta'(\rho)\rvert=o(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) and \(\eta^{\#}\) passes the same cap.
The identities for \(\pi_N\) are the standard north-pole chart of \(S^2\); the spherical element is \(2\lvert dw\rvert/(1+\lvert w\rvert^2)\), whence the displayed formula for \(\eta^{\#}\). The limit \(\pi_N^{-1}(\eta(\rho))\to N\) is \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\to\infty\). At a minimiser, \(\lvert\eta\rvert\to 0\) along the \(\eta\)-zeros supplied by Lemma 5.5, so the denominator tends to \(1\). The comparison of \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\) with \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) is Lemma 4.5.2 on the left and the same expansion with \(1-\sigma<1/2\) on the right.

5. Geometric dynamical phase transitions

Definition 5.0 (Truncated strip).
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\). Write \[ S_\delta=\bigl\{s=\sigma+it:\delta\le\sigma\le 1-\delta\bigr\}. \] The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) vanishes only on \(\operatorname{Re}s=1\) (Lemma 2.2), hence is bounded away from zero on \(S_\delta\): \(\lvert 1-N^{1-s}\rvert\ge N^{1-\sigma}-1\).
Definition 5.1 (Path-closure events in the strip).
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) and set the isolation exponent \(\kappa=\varepsilon\). For each integer \(N\ge 2\) let \(\Sigma_N\) be the finite grid \[ \Sigma_N=\Bigl(\bigl\{\tfrac12\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\delta+j N^{-\kappa}(\log N)^{-1}:j\in\mathbb{N}_0\bigr\}\Bigr)\cap[\delta,1-\delta]. \] The critical line is always a grid abscissa, so Corollary 4.1.1 applies on \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) without interpolation. Any finite set of additional abscissae in \([\delta,1-\delta]\), independent of \(N\), may be included in \(\Sigma_N\). A point \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\in\Sigma_N\) is an unrestricted path-closure of generation \(N\) when
  1. \(\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\) (relative depth, Definition 4.4),
  2. \(t\) is a local minimum of \(u\mapsto\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+iu)\bigr\rvert\) in \((t-N^{-\kappa},t+N^{-\kappa})\),
  3. \(\eta^{\#}(s)\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) (Marty speed, Lemma 4.8: spherical speed of the north-pole lift).
It is a balanced path-closure when, in addition,
  1. \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\),
  2. \(\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) (uniform DQPT depth),
  3. \(\bigl\lvert\partial_t\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) (slow passage).
Write \(\mathcal{C}_N\) for the set of balanced closures and \(\mathcal{U}_N\) for the set of unrestricted closures. Both are discrete subsets of \(S_\delta\). On \(\sigma=\tfrac12\) one has \(P_N\asymp N^{1/2}\), so relative and uniform depths coincide, and Lemma 4.5.1 makes (vi) automatic at critical-line zeros for large \(N\). Near a local minimum with \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) small, (iii) and (vi) coincide. The choice \(\kappa=\varepsilon\) with spacing \(N^{-\kappa}(\log N)^{-1}\) makes a grid miss leak a main term \(P_N\,O(N^{-\kappa}/\log N)\), strictly below the relative threshold \(P_N N^{-\varepsilon}\) once \(N\) is large: the logarithm absorbs the implied constant. The grid-line count grows only by the same logarithm, and the tightness exponent \(2\kappa-1/2+\varepsilon=3\varepsilon-1/2\) is negative, so \(N^{3\varepsilon-1/2}\log N\to 0\). Marty speed and slow passage only remove transits; they do not add atoms, so the tightness tail of Theorem 9.1 is unchanged.

Condition (iv) is the geometric counterpart of the unique critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\). A small endpoint arising merely because the vectors \(m^{-s}\) themselves are short (\(\sigma>1/2\)) fails the relative test unless \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}+O((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-\sigma})\), so finite-length artefacts are no longer unrestricted closures. Condition (iii) is the fibre metric: Euclidean relative smallness without Marty speed is smallness in the unbounded chart, not a visit to the south pole.

Theorem 5.2 (Geometric DQPT criterion).
Every balanced path-closure \(s=\sigma+it\in\mathcal{C}_N\) satisfies \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le T\) produces a balanced closure for all \(N\ge N_0(T,\rho)\), with \(N_0\) as in Corollary 4.1.2 enlarged if necessary so that Lemma 4.5.1 supplies the slow-passage bound (if \(\zeta'(\rho)=0\) the speed is \(O((1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\log N)\), which is even smaller). Relative depth without Marty speed holds at every zero of real part \(\sigma\ge\varepsilon\). Marty speed (iii) holds at simple zeros with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) and fails with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) (Lemma 4.8). Thus an unrestricted fibre-closure occurs at critical-line zeros and at simple zeros in the right half-strip, not at simple zeros in the left half-strip.
On \(S_\delta\) one has \(P_N(s)\ge N^{1-\sigma}-1\). Condition (iv) therefore forces \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) for large \(N\). On the critical line, which belongs to \(\Sigma_N\), \(P_N\ge N^{1/2}-1\), so (iv) and (v) reduce to Corollary 4.1.1, and (iii) and (vi) are Lemma 4.5.1. Local isolation (ii) holds for large \(N\) because the zeros of \(\Xi\) are isolated and \(N^{-\kappa}\) is eventually smaller than half the gap to the neighbouring zero. For an off-line zero \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\), Corollary 4.3.1 and \(P_N(\rho)\asymp N^{1-\sigma}\) give the relative inequality as soon as \(N^{\sigma-\varepsilon}\ge 1+\lvert\gamma\rvert\). At the nearest \(\sigma_N\in\Sigma_N\), \(\lvert\sigma_N-\sigma\rvert\le N^{-\kappa}(\log N)^{-1}\) and \(\zeta(\sigma_N+i\gamma)=O_\rho(N^{-\kappa}/\log N)\) by analyticity, so the leaked main term is at most \(P_N N^{-\varepsilon}\) once \(N\ge N(\rho)\): the logarithm in the spacing absorbs the implied constant. Marty speed at the minimiser is Lemma 4.8: it fails on the left of \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) and holds on the right.
Remark 5.3 (What the two families see).
The balanced family \(\mathcal{C}_N\) cannot see a zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\), because the prefactor is then too small, nor a simple zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\), because the passage is too fast. Unrestricted fibre-closures use the north-pole chart of Lemma 4.8: the left half-strip fails Marty speed (a transit through the south pole, or else an endpoint tending to \(N\)); the right half-strip is slow and relatively small, hence visible to \(\mathcal{U}_N\) and not to \(\mathcal{C}_N\). The Riemann hypothesis is the assertion that \(\mathcal{U}_N\) and \(\mathcal{C}_N\) have the same limiting support, equivalently that \(\nu\) has no right-half atoms.
Definition 5.4 (Relative sublevel set).
\[ \Omega_N=\bigl\{s\in\operatorname{int}S_{\delta/2}:\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert< P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\bigr\} =\bigl\{s\in\operatorname{int}S_{\delta/2}:\lvert h_N(s)\rvert<N^{-\varepsilon}\bigr\}, \qquad h_N=\frac{\eta_N^{(N^2)}}{1-N^{1-s}}, \] an open set: components are taken in the half-margin strip \(S_{\delta/2}\), on which \(h_N\) is holomorphic (Lemma 2.2), while representatives lie on the grid in \(S_\delta\); the boundary case of equality carries no representative. The unrestricted closures \(\mathcal{U}_N\) are one representative from each connected component of \(\Omega_N\) that meets a grid line and is a slow lift: the minimiser of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) over the grid points of the component, subject to Definition 5.1 (ii) and (iii). The representative is required to be anchored: its component contains a zero of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) within unit distance of the representative — an exact closure of the polygon certifying the event. Lemma 5.5 supplies such a zero within \(N^{-\kappa}\) of every zero of \(\Xi\) once \(N\ge N_0(\rho)\), so anchoring removes no atom at a zero of \(\Xi\); distinct components contain distinct certifying zeros. A component of diameter \(O(N^{-\kappa})\) can meet several of the finer grid lines; the component convention counts it once, so a zero of \(\Xi\) carries a single Dirac mass (Lemma 5.5). The same convention applies to \(\mathcal{C}_N\).
Lemma 5.5 (Rouché localisation of components).
Let \(\rho\) be a zero of \(\Xi\) in the interior of \(S_\delta\), of finite multiplicity. For all \(N\ge N_0(\rho)\) the disk \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert<N^{-\kappa}\) contains exactly as many zeros of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) as the multiplicity of \(\rho\), and meets \(\Omega_N\) in a single connected component. Extra components of \(\Omega_N\) are Dirichlet-polynomial zeros; their tempered mass on the moderate range is Lemma 9.6, and they cannot accumulate at a fixed non-zero (Theorem 10.1).
On the circle \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert=N^{-\kappa}\), one has \(\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\asymp_\rho N^{-\kappa}\). Theorem 4.3 gives \(\bigl\lvert\eta-(1-N^{1-s})\zeta\bigr\rvert=O(F_N)\). The main term has size \(P_N\lvert\zeta\rvert\asymp_\rho N^{1-\sigma-\kappa}\). This dominates \(F_N\asymp(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\) once \(N^{\sigma-\kappa}\gg 1+\lvert\gamma\rvert\), the threshold of Theorem 5.2 with \(\kappa=\varepsilon\). Rouché therefore equates the zeros of \(\eta\) and of \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta\) inside the disk. The elementary factor \(1-N^{1-s}\) has no zero in \(S_\delta\) (Lemma 2.2), so the count equals the multiplicity of \(\rho\). A holomorphic function has \(\lvert f\rvert\) small throughout a neighbourhood of its zeros and not elsewhere on the circle, so that disk meets \(\Omega_N\) in one component. Extra components are Dirichlet-polynomial zeros, controlled globally by Lemma 9.6 rather than by a pointwise exclusion.

6. Continuous-radius interpolation

The integer theory is the one used for the measures. The continuous filter of Definition 2.3 interpolates it. This section records the identities that survive that interpolation, and the local diagonal as it actually evaluates (not as a constant \(c_1=\frac12\)).

Lemma 6.1 (\(C^1\) regularity).
After smoothing the upper limit, \(R\mapsto\eta_R(R^2)(s)\) is \(C^1\) on \([2,\infty)\) for each fixed \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)\). Differentiation under the sum is justified by \[ \sum_{m\le R^2}\lvert\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})\rvert\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R. \]
The coefficients are smooth in \(R\). On the support of \(\varphi\), \(\lvert\partial_R c_m\rvert\ll 1+\lvert\varphi'\rvert_\infty(1+m/R)\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the majorant. For the sharp cutoff at \(m\le R^2\) the map is piecewise \(C^1\), with jumps of size \(2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\) when \(R^2\) is an integer (Definition 6.2).
Definition 6.2 (Splitting of \(\partial_R\eta\)).
On the critical line, \[ \partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R), \] where \(A=\sum_{m\le R^2}(\partial_R c_m)m^{-1/2-i\gamma}\) is the interior coefficient derivative, \(B=c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}(R^2)^{-1/2-i\gamma}\cdot 2R\) is the Leibniz boundary, and \(E\) is the difference between this sharp splitting and a smooth cutoff of width \(R^{2\theta}\), \(\theta\in(0,1/4)\). Since \(\lvert(R^2)^{-1/2}\rvert=R^{-1}\), one has \(\lvert B\rvert=2\lvert c_{\lfloor R^2\rfloor}\rvert\). For the integer filter, \(N\mid N^2\), so \(c_{N^2}=1-N\) and \(\lvert B\rvert=2(N-1)\). The integer theorems of Sections 4–5 and 8–12 do not use \(B\).
Lemma 6.3 (Local diagonal).
On the packet \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\), with \(u=m/R-k\) and local mass \(M_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}\lvert c_m(R)\rvert m^{-1/2}\), \[ \partial_R c_m=-\varphi(u)+(k+u)\varphi'(u), \] and the diagonal \[ D_k=\sum_{m\in I_k}c_m(R)\,(\partial_R c_m(R))\,m^{-1} \] satisfies \[ D_k=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)}{k+u}\,du+R\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\varphi(u)^2}{k+u}\,du+R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}. \] The \(\varphi'\) term integrates by parts to zero by compact support of \(\varphi\). Apostol’s remainder [12] is \(R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}=\int\overline{B}_1 f_k'\) with \(\lvert\overline{B}_1\rvert\le\tfrac12\). Summing in \(k\) gives \[ \sum_{k=1}^R D_k=R\Bigl(\int\varphi^2\Bigr)\log R+O_\varphi(R)+\sum_k R_{\mathrm{EM}}^{(k)}, \] which is not a multiple of \(R^{-1}\rho^2\). The evaluation \(\int(1-R\varphi)(-\varphi)\,du=-\tfrac12\) is false (that integral equals \(-1+R\int\varphi^2\)).
Substitute the expressions for \(c_m\) and \(\partial_R c_m\), change variables, and integrate the \(\varphi'\) term by parts. The harmonic sum \(\sum_{k\le R}k^{-1}=\log R+O(1)\) produces the displayed main term. No identification of \(\sum_k\lvert\eta^{(k)}\rvert^2\) with \(\rho^2\) is claimed.

The integer depth bound of Corollary 4.1.1 does not pass through this local form. For real \(R\), the same Euler–Maclaurin comparison as Theorem 4.1 applies to the sharp filter at \(\lfloor R\rfloor\), and Lemma 6.1 controls the increment from \(\lfloor R\rfloor\) to \(R\) on compact ranges of \(t\); the measures below are indexed by integers \(N\).

7. Mean-value estimates

Theorem 7.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, explicit constant).
For a finitely supported sequence \((a_m)\) and \(T\ge 1\), \[ \int_0^T\Bigl\lvert\sum_m a_m m^{-it}\Bigr\rvert^2 dt=\sum_m\lvert a_m\rvert^2 T+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_m m\lvert a_m\rvert^2, \] with \(\lvert\theta\rvert\le 1\) (Montgomery–Vaughan [13]; [7, §7.3]). On the critical line, \(a_m=c_m m^{-1/2}\) therefore gives \[ \int_0^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt=T\sum_{m\le N^2}\frac{\lvert c_m\rvert^2}{m}+\theta\cdot 2\pi\sum_{m\le N^2}\lvert c_m\rvert^2. \] The coefficient sums are \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\), because the spikes \(c_m=1-N\) dominate. Mean-zero cancellation is in \(\sum c_m m^{-s}\), not in \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\). In particular the diagonal is not \(O(1)\), and \[ \int_{-T}^T\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2 dt\ll TN\log N+N^3. \]
Expand the square. Off-diagonal integrals are bounded by \(\min(T,1/\lvert\log(n/m)\rvert)\). Hilbert’s inequality as generalised in [13] produces the constant \(2\pi\). For the spikes: there are \(N\) multiples of \(N\) up to \(N^2\), each with \(\lvert c_m\rvert^2=(N-1)^2\), whence \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2\asymp N^3\) and \(\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp N\log N\).
Lemma 7.2 (Weighted second moment, exact).
The Fourier transform of \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) is \(\pi e^{-\lvert\xi\rvert}\), so \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\bigr\rvert^2}{1+t^2}\,dt =\pi\sum_{m,n\le N^2}c_m\overline{c}_n\,(mn)^{-1/2}\frac{\min(m,n)}{\max(m,n)}. \] The diagonal is \(\pi\sum\lvert c_m\rvert^2/m\asymp\pi N\log N\). There is no remainder of size \(N^3\).
Expand the square and integrate termwise. Each pair \((m,n)\) produces \(\pi\exp(-\lvert\log(m/n)\rvert)\).
Lemma 7.3 (Pointwise from local means).
For \(F\in C^1(\mathbb{R})\) and \(H>0\), \[ \lvert F(\gamma)\rvert^2\le\frac2H\int_0^H\lvert F(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du+2H\int_0^H\lvert F'(\gamma+u)\rvert^2\,du. \] If \(F(t)=\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\), then \(\lvert F'\rvert\le 2(\log N)\,F_{\log}\). Taking \(H=1/\log N\) costs two logarithms relative to the local \(L^2\) mean, and does not produce a power of \(N^{-1}\). Depth of closure is not taken from this lemma; it is Corollary 4.1.1.
Write \(F(\gamma)=\frac1H\int_0^H\bigl(F(\gamma+u)-\int_0^u F'(\gamma+v)\,dv\bigr)du\) and apply \(\lvert x+y\rvert^2\le 2\lvert x\rvert^2+2\lvert y\rvert^2\).

8. Construction of finite spectral measures

The measures live on the truncated strip \(S_\delta\), identified with a closed subset of \(\mathbb{C}\). A Borel measure on \(S_\delta\) is tempered when \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(s)<\infty\). Horizontal compactness of \([\delta,1-\delta]\) reduces tightness to control of the imaginary tails.

Definition 8.1 (Finite measures on the strip).
With \(\mathcal{C}_N\) and \(\mathcal{U}_N\) as in Definition 5.1, set \[ \mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{C}_N}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,\delta_s, \qquad \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{U}_N}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,\delta_s, \] and let \(\rho_N\) be the residual density on \(S_\delta\) \[ \rho_N(\sigma+it)=\frac1N\min\Bigl(1,\bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\sigma+it)\bigr\rvert^2\Bigr)\,(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}. \] Then \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) is the balanced measure and \(\nu_N=\nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) is the unrestricted measure. The factor \(1/N\) makes \(\int_{S_\delta}\rho_N=O(N^{-1})\), since \(\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\sigma\,dt\ll 1\). The atomic weights are independent of \(N\).

The isolation radius \(N^{-\kappa}\) separates distinct path-closure events on each grid line. Lemma 5.5 identifies those events with components of the relative sublevel set \(\Omega_N\); the atomic part is summed over components, one Dirac mass at a minimiser in each (Definition 5.4), so the per-line minima description overcounts a component by at most its bounded number of grid lines and no limit statement uses it. The weight \((1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\) is the standard tempering factor. The original construction on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\) is the restriction of \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\) to the critical-line grid abscissa.

9. Tightness

Tightness of the atomic parts rests on two elementary counts. Below polylogarithmic heights every closure is trapped near a zero of \(\Xi\), because the relative threshold \(N^{-\varepsilon}\) dives beneath the classical floor for \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\) off its zeros. Above them, the anchoring of Definition 5.4 lets Jensen's formula count atoms by exact closures of the polygon. No value-distribution input is used.

Lemma 9.2 (Zeros of \(\eta\) per unit height).
For every integer \(N\ge 2\) and every \(T\in\mathbb{R}\), the number of zeros of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) in the rectangle \(\{-1\le\sigma\le 2,\ \lvert t-T\rvert\le 3/2\}\) is at most \(20\log N+2\).
\(\eta\) is entire. At \(z_0=3+iT\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(z_0)\bigr\rvert\ge 1-\sum_{m\ge 2}m^{-3}-(N-2)\sum_{j\ge 1}(jN)^{-3} \ge 1-(\zeta(3)-1)-\frac{(N-2)\,\zeta(3)}{N^{3}}\ge\tfrac34, \] uniformly in \(N\ge 2\) and \(T\). Every point of the rectangle lies within distance \(\sqrt{16+9/4}<4.3\) of \(z_0\). On \(\lvert z-z_0\rvert\le 8.6\) one has \(\sigma\ge-5.6\), so \[ \lvert\eta\rvert\le\sum_{m\le N^2}\lvert c_m^{(N)}\rvert\,m^{5.6}\le (N^2)^{6.6}+(N-1)\sum_{j\le N}(jN)^{5.6}\le 2N^{13.2}. \] Jensen's formula (Ahlfors [4]) on the disks of radii \(4.3\) and \(8.6\) about \(z_0\) bounds the zero count in the inner disk by \[ n(4.3)\le\frac{\log\bigl(2N^{13.2}\big/\tfrac34\bigr)}{\log 2}\le 20\log N+2. \]
Lemma 9.3 (\(\zeta\) at relative closures).
There are \(C_\delta\) and \(N(\delta)\) such that for \(N\ge N(\delta)\) and every \(s\in S_{\delta/2}\) with \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\rvert\le P_N(s)\,N^{-\varepsilon}\), \[ \lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}+C_\delta\,(2+\lvert t\rvert)\,N^{-\sigma}. \] At a zero \(s_0\) of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) in \(S_{\delta/2}\) the first term is absent.
Theorem 4.3 with \(\sigma_0=\delta/4\): \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta=\eta-\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}+O_\delta\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr)\). Divide by \(P_N\ge N^{1-\sigma}-1\ge\tfrac12 N^{1-\sigma}\), valid once \(N^{\delta/2}\ge 2\): the half-term and the remainder together contribute \(C_\delta(2+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-\sigma}\).
Lemma 9.4 (Classical floor off the zeros).
There is an absolute \(C_2\) such that for every \(\omega\in(0,\tfrac14]\) and every \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(-1\le\sigma\le 2\) and \(\lvert t\rvert\ge 2\) whose distance from every zero of \(\zeta\) is at least \(\omega\), \[ \lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\ge(2+\lvert t\rvert)^{-C_2(1+\log(1/\omega))}. \]
Titchmarsh [1, §9.6]: \(\log\zeta(s)=\sum_{\lvert t-\gamma\rvert\le 1}\log(s-\rho)+O(\log\lvert t\rvert)\), uniformly in \(-1\le\sigma\le 2\) away from the zeros. The number of terms is \(O(\log\lvert t\rvert)\) (Riemann–von Mangoldt [1, §9.3]), and each satisfies \(\log\omega\le\log\lvert s-\rho\rvert\le\log 4\). Take real parts.
Lemma 9.5 (Localisation, trapping, exact counts below polylogarithmic heights).
Set \(\omega(t)=c_\delta/\log(2+\lvert t\rvert)\) with \(c_\delta\) small. There is \(N_1(\delta,\varepsilon)\) such that for all \(N\ge N_1\):
  1. every \(s\in S_{\delta/2}\) with \(\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\rvert\le P_N(s)N^{-\varepsilon}\) and \(2\le\lvert t\rvert\le(\log N)^2\) lies within \(\omega(t)<1\) of a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of \(\Xi\);
  2. for each such \(\rho\) there is \(r\in\bigl(10\,\omega(\gamma),20\,\omega(\gamma)\bigr)\) with \(\lvert\eta\rvert\ge P_N\,\kappa_N\) on the circle \(\lvert s-\rho\rvert=r\), where \(\kappa_N=\exp\bigl(-C_\delta(\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)\bigr)>2N^{-\varepsilon}\); consequently every component of \(\Omega_N\) meeting \(D(\rho,\omega)\) is contained in \(D(\rho,r)\), and each such component contains a zero of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) (minimum principle);
  3. the number of zeros of \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) in \(D(\rho,r)\) equals the number of zeros of \(\Xi\) there, with multiplicity (Rouché), and is at most \(C\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\).
(i) Lemma 9.3 gives \(\lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon}+C_\delta\bigl(2+(\log N)^2\bigr)N^{-\delta/2}\le 2N^{-\varepsilon_2}\) with \(\varepsilon_2=\min(\varepsilon,\delta/4)\), for \(N\) large. Were the distance to every zero at least \(\omega(t)\), Lemma 9.4 would give \[ \lvert\zeta(s)\rvert\ge(2+\lvert t\rvert)^{-C_2(1+\log(1/\omega))}\ge\exp\bigl(-C_\delta(\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)\bigr), \] which exceeds \(2N^{-\varepsilon_2}\) for all large \(N\), since \((\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)=o(\log N)\). For \(\lvert t\rvert\ge 2\) the zeros of \(\zeta\) here are the zeros of \(\Xi\); \(\beta\ge\sigma-\omega\ge\delta/4\), and symmetrically on the right. (ii) Let \(n\le C\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) be the number of zeros in \(D(\rho,25\,\omega)\), with multiplicity (Riemann–von Mangoldt). They exclude annuli of total width \(\omega/2\) from the range of \(r\), so some \(r\) keeps the circle at distance \(\ge\omega/(4n)\) from every zero, and Lemma 9.4 with \(\omega/(4n)\) gives \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\ge\kappa_N\) there, because \(\log(4n/\omega)=O(\log\log\log N)\) at heights \(\le(\log N)^2\). Once \(c_\delta\) is small the circle stays in \(\delta/4\le\sigma\le 1-\delta/4\): the zero-free region of de la Vallée Poussin (Titchmarsh [1, §3.8]) keeps \(\beta\le 1-c/\log\lvert\gamma\rvert\), and \(20\,\omega(\gamma)<c/\log\lvert\gamma\rvert\) once \(c_\delta<c/20\), symmetrically on the left. By Theorem 4.3, on the circle \[ \frac{\lvert\eta\rvert}{P_N}\ge\lvert\zeta\rvert-C_\delta(2+\lvert t\rvert)N^{-\sigma}\ge\kappa_N-C_\delta(\log N)^2N^{-\delta/4}\ge\tfrac{\kappa_N}{2}>N^{-\varepsilon}, \] the last comparison because \((\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)=o(\log N)\). A component of \(\Omega_N\) has \(\lvert\eta\rvert<P_NN^{-\varepsilon}\) throughout, cannot meet the circle, and is connected, hence is trapped in \(D(\rho,r)\subset\operatorname{int}S_{\delta/8}\), where \(h_N\) of Definition 5.4 is holomorphic. Were a trapped component free of zeros of \(\eta\), the maximum principle applied to \(1/h_N\) — boundary values of modulus \(N^{\varepsilon}\) — would force \(\lvert h_N\rvert\ge N^{-\varepsilon}\) inside, contradicting openness. Distinct components contain distinct zeros. (iii) On the circle, \(\lvert\eta-(1-N^{1-s})\zeta\rvert\le C_\delta(2+\lvert t\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\le\tfrac12 P_N\kappa_N\le\bigl\lvert(1-N^{1-s})\zeta\bigr\rvert\) for large \(N\), and Rouché equates the zero counts of \(\eta\) and of \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta\); the elementary factor has no zero for \(\sigma<1\) (Lemma 2.2). The count is \(n\).
Lemma 9.6 (Tail localisation).
There are \(C(\delta,\varepsilon)\) and \(N_1(\delta,\varepsilon)\) such that for all \(N\ge N_1\) and every \(A\ge 3\), \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{A\le\lvert t\rvert\le(\log N)^2\}\bigr)\le C\,\frac{(\log A)^2}{A}, \qquad \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>(\log N)^2\}\bigr)\le\frac{C}{\log N}. \] The same bounds hold for \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\).
Every atom with \(A\le\lvert t\rvert\le(\log N)^2\) lies within \(\omega(t)<1\) of a zero \(\rho\) of \(\Xi\) (Lemma 9.5 (i)); its component is trapped in \(D(\rho,r)\) and contains its own zero of \(\eta\) (Lemma 9.5 (ii)); distinct components contain distinct zeros, so the number of atoms attached to \(\rho\) is at most the number of zeros of \(\eta\) in \(D(\rho,r)\), which is at most \(C\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) (Lemma 9.5 (iii)). Riemann–von Mangoldt gives at most \(C\log(2+k)\) zeros with \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\in[k,k+1]\), whence \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{A\le\lvert t\rvert\le(\log N)^2\}\bigr) \le\sum_{k\ge A-1}\frac{C\bigl(\log(2+k)\bigr)^2}{1+k^2}\le C\,\frac{(\log A)^2}{A}. \] Above height \((\log N)^2\), the anchoring of Definition 5.4 attaches to each atom a zero of \(\eta\) in its component within unit distance, distinct for distinct atoms, so Lemma 9.2 bounds the number of atoms with \(\lvert t-T\rvert\le\tfrac12\) by \(3(20\log N+2)\), and \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>(\log N)^2\}\bigr)\ll\log N\sum_{k\ge(\log N)^2-1}\frac{1}{1+k^2}\ll\frac{1}{\log N}. \] Balanced closures are unrestricted closures with further conditions, so both bounds hold for \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\). (Isolation packing alone gives the cruder \(O(N^{3\varepsilon-1/2}\log N)\) beyond the window \(\lvert t\rvert>N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), the form quoted in Lemma 4.3.3.)
Remark 9.7 (Universality is outrun, then outcounted).
Voronin universality [15] supplies discs at arbitrarily large height in \(\tfrac12<\sigma<1\) on which \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\) is below any fixed threshold with no zero of \(\Xi\) nearby, so no pointwise principle can identify closures with zeros uniformly in height. The proof above does not attempt one. Below height \((\log N)^2\) the closure threshold \(N^{-\varepsilon}\) is smaller than the classical floor of Lemma 9.4, so universality discs cannot host closures there. Above that height no identification is claimed: the anchored count of Lemma 9.2 bounds the atoms any disc can carry by the exact closures it contains, and the tempering weight integrates that to \(O(1/\log N)\).
Theorem 9.1 (Tightness).
The families \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) and \(\{\nu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) are tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures on \(S_\delta\).
The abscissa interval \([\delta,1-\delta]\) is compact, so it is enough to control imaginary tails. The residual satisfies \[ \int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_\delta^{1-\delta}d\sigma\int_{\lvert t\rvert>A}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt=O(N^{-1}A^{-1}), \] uniformly in \(N\). For the atomic part, Lemma 9.6 gives, for all \(N\ge N_1(\delta,\varepsilon)\) and \(A\ge 3\), \[ \nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\bigl(\{\lvert t\rvert>A\}\bigr)\le C\,\frac{(\log A)^2}{A}+\frac{C}{\log N}, \] and the same for \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\subset\nu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\). Given \(\eta>0\), choose \(A\) so that \(C(\log A)^2/A\) and the residual tail are each \(<\eta/3\); then choose \(N_2\ge N_1\) with \(C/\log N<\eta/3\) for \(N\ge N_2\); the finitely many measures with \(N<N_2\) are handled by enlarging \(A\), each \(\nu_N\) being a finite measure. Prokhorov’s theorem [3] then yields tightness of both families. (On a balanced closure one may in addition divide by the prefactor lower bound \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\), recovering \(\lvert\zeta\rvert\le N^{-\varepsilon/2}+O\bigl((1+\lvert t\rvert)N^{1/2+\varepsilon/2-2\sigma}\bigr)\) on the window — the comparison used for depth; it is not needed for tightness.)

Markov applied to Theorem 7.1 on the sublevel \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) produces measure \(O(TN^{2\varepsilon}\log N+N^{2+2\varepsilon})\), which does not save a negative power of \(N\). Tightness is taken from Theorem 4.3 and isolation, not from that Markov bound.

10. Exclusion of spurious pure-point mass

Theorem 10.1 (No spurious atoms).
Every pure-point atom of any weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) or of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is a non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\). Balanced atoms additionally satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\).
Suppose a limit point of \(\{\nu_N\}\) carries an atom at \(s^*\in S_\delta\) with \(\zeta(s^*)\ne 0\). Along a subsequence \(N_j\to\infty\) there are unrestricted closures \(s_j\to s^*\) with \(\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(s_j)\rvert\le P_{N_j}(s_j)\,N_j^{-\varepsilon}\). Theorem 4.3 gives \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_{N_j}^{(N_j^2)}(s_j)\bigr\rvert \ge P_{N_j}(s_j)\bigl\lvert\zeta(s_j)\bigr\rvert-O\bigl(F_{N_j}(s_j)\bigr), \] hence \(\lvert\zeta(s_j)\rvert\le N_j^{-\varepsilon}+O\bigl((1+\lvert s^*\rvert)N_j^{-\sigma_j}\bigr)\to 0\). Continuity of \(\zeta\) off its zeros contradicts \(\zeta(s^*)\ne 0\). There is no second main term of comparable size: the polar terms cancel at \(M=N^2\). The same comparison with the additional bounds \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\) and \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) applies to \(\{\mu_N\}\). If \(\zeta(s^*)=0\), there is no contradiction from relative depth alone; Marty speed (Lemma 4.8) still excludes a persistent unrestricted atom on the left of \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon\). The abscissa bound for balanced atoms is Theorem 5.2.

11. Vanishing of residual continuous spectrum

Theorem 11.1 (Residual vanishing).
Along any weak-star convergent subsequence the residual densities \(\rho_{N_j}\,d\sigma\,dt\) converge to the zero measure on \(S_\delta\). Consequently every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) or of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is pure-point.
\[ \int_{S_\delta}\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\le N^{-1}\int_{S_\delta}(1+\lvert s\rvert^2)^{-1}\,d\sigma\,dt=O(N^{-1}). \] Thus the total residual mass tends to zero. For every continuous test function \(f\) of compact support in \(S_\delta\), \(\int f\rho_N\to 0\). Any weak-star limit of \(\mu_N=\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}+\rho_N\,d\sigma\,dt\) therefore coincides with the corresponding limit of \(\mu_N^{\mathrm{pp}}\), and likewise for \(\nu_N\). Existence of the limits is Theorem 9.1.

12. Realisation of the limiting measure

Theorem 12.1 (Balanced spectral measure).
Any weak-star limit point \(\mu\) of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure on \(S_\delta\) whose support consists of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) with real part at most \(\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\) is an atom of \(\mu\), with mass \((1+\lvert\rho\rvert^2)^{-1}\). There are no atoms at non-zeros. There are no simple atoms with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\).
Tightness is Theorem 9.1. Residual vanishing is Theorem 11.1, so the limit is pure-point. Theorem 10.1 identifies atoms with zeros of \(\Xi\) in \(S_\delta\) and restricts balanced atoms to \(\operatorname{Re}s\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Simple atoms with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) are excluded by Proposition 4.6. Conversely, if \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) is a critical-line zero of \(\Xi\), Corollary 4.1.2 produces a path-closure of depth \(D_N\) for every \(N\ge N_0(\lvert\gamma\rvert)\), on the grid abscissa \(\sigma=\tfrac12\); Lemma 4.5.1 makes it slow for all large \(N\), hence balanced. For all large \(N\) this closure is the unique local minimum in its isolation interval. The atomic weight \((1+\lvert\rho\rvert^2)^{-1}\) is independent of \(N\), so \(\rho\) is an atom of every weak-star limit.
Theorem 12.2 (Unrestricted spectral measure).
Any weak-star limit point \(\nu\) of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure on \(S_\delta\) whose support consists of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\). Every critical-line zero is an atom of \(\nu\). A simple zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) is not: Marty speed fails (Lemma 4.8), so the left half-strip is absent from both \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) and \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\). A simple zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) is an unrestricted atom and not a balanced one.
The identification of atoms with zeros is Theorem 10.1. Existence at a critical-line zero is Theorem 5.2. Existence on the right and exclusion on the left are Lemma 4.8 together with the relative clause of Theorem 5.2; the grid miss is controlled by \(\kappa=\varepsilon\) and the logarithmic spacing refinement of Definition 5.1. The comparison of the two supports is Remark 5.3.
Corollary 12.3 (Conversion of the Riemann hypothesis).
The following are equivalent.
  1. Every non-trivial zero of \(\Xi\) lies on the critical line (the Riemann hypothesis).
  2. Every unrestricted path-closure is balanced, for all sufficiently large \(N\): \(\mathcal{U}_N=\mathcal{C}_N\).
  3. Every weak-star limit of \(\{\nu_N\}\) is supported on the critical line.
  4. The balanced and unrestricted limiting measures coincide, and their common support is exactly the set of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\).
Theorem 12.1 places every critical-line zero in \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset\operatorname{supp}\nu\). Theorem 12.2, via the Marty bound of Lemma 4.8, excludes the left half-strip from \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) and keeps the right half-strip: a simple zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) is unrestricted and not balanced, which separates \(\nu\) from \(\mu\). Pairing \(\Xi(s)=\Xi(1-s)\) converts a left-half zero into a right-half zero already visible to \(\nu_N\). Conversely, if every zero lies on the line, then fibre-closures off the line can accumulate only at zeros, of which there are none, so Theorem 10.1 puts \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) on the critical line and \(\mathcal{U}_N=\mathcal{C}_N\) in the limit. The vanishing-aperture jet of Theorem 12.6 removes multiple left-half atoms from \(\mu\); the same Marty comparison at the minimiser removes them from \(\nu\) once \(K_N\ge m\).
Theorem 12.4 (Identity L places simple balanced atoms in an \(\varepsilon\)-neighbourhood of the line).
Identity L holds. Consequently every simple atom of a weak-star limit of \(\{\mu_N\}\) satisfies \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon<\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Every weak-star limit of \(\{\nu_N\}\) still charges every zero with \(\sigma\ge\tfrac12-\varepsilon\); a simple zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) fails Marty speed and is absent (Lemma 4.8, Theorem 12.2). The four statements of Corollary 12.3 remain equivalent to one another, and are not a theorem.
Proposition 4.6 excludes every simple zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\) from the balanced family for all large \(N\). The prefactor test excludes \(\sigma>\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\). Theorem 12.1 therefore confines simple atoms of \(\mu\) to the displayed interval. Multiple zeros at fixed \(\varepsilon\) with only the first-derivative test are handled by the jet of Definition 12.5: Theorem 12.6 places every balanced atom of the vanishing-aperture family on the critical line. Theorem 12.2 is unchanged: relative depth does not use the jet, so a right-half zero of any multiplicity remains an unrestricted atom; the left half-strip is removed at the minimiser by Marty speed (Lemma 4.8), the same comparison as the jet. Envelope comparison of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) with \(D_N\) at the zero itself contradicts Euler–Maclaurin in the band \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) (Corollary 4.3.1) and is not invoked.
Definition 12.5 (Vanishing aperture).
A sequence \(\varepsilon_N\in(0,1/6)\) is a vanishing aperture when \(\varepsilon_N\to 0\) and \(\varepsilon_N\log N\to\infty\). (For instance \(\varepsilon_N=(\log N)^{-1/2}\) for all large \(N\).) For the tightness constants of Lemma 9.6, require also \(\varepsilon_N\log N\,/\,\bigl((\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)\bigr)\to\infty\), which the example satisfies. The second condition makes \(N^{-\varepsilon_N}\to 0\), so isolation still shrinks, and \(N^{\varepsilon_N}\to\infty\), so the slow-passage cap \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon_N}\) still admits every fixed critical-line zero (Lemma 4.5.1). Write \(K_N=\max\bigl(1,\lfloor\log\log(N+16)\rfloor\bigr)\). The measures of Definition 8.1 may be formed with the parameter \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_N\) at generation \(N\), with the additional jet condition that a balanced closure \(s\) satisfies \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t^{\,k}\eta_N^{(N^2)}(s)\bigr\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon_N}(2\log N)^k \qquad\text{for every integer }k=1,\dots,K_N. \] The \(k=1\) clause is Definition 5.1 (vi), relaxed by the factor \(2\log N\). Every zero of \(\Xi\) has finite multiplicity, and \(K_N\to\infty\), so for each fixed zero one has \(K_N\ge m(\rho)\) for all large \(N\).
Theorem 12.6 (Balanced atoms on the line).
Let \(\varepsilon_N\) be a vanishing aperture, and let \(\{\mu_N\}\) be the balanced jet measures of Definition 12.5. Then every atom of any weak-star limit of \(\{\mu_N\}\) lies on the critical line. Every critical-line zero of \(\Xi\), of any finite multiplicity, remains an atom. Marty speed (Lemma 4.8) likewise excludes the left half-strip from \(\{\nu_N\}\). A right-half zero remains an unrestricted atom, so Corollary 12.3 is the conversion of the Riemann hypothesis, not a theorem.
The estimates of Theorems 9.1–11.1 use \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) only through \(N^{-\varepsilon}\to 0\) and the negative tightness exponent \(3\varepsilon-1/2\). Both persist along a vanishing aperture: \(N^{3\varepsilon_N-1/2}\log N\to 0\). Lemma 9.6 uses the aperture only through \(N^{-\varepsilon_N}\le\kappa_N/2\), i.e. \(\varepsilon_N\log N\gg(\log\log N)(\log\log\log N)\); the example \(\varepsilon_N=(\log N)^{-1/2}\) of Definition 12.5 satisfies this, so the moderate-range mass remains \(o(1)\). The jet condition only removes closures, so tightness is unchanged. Theorem 10.1 is unchanged. Let \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) be a zero of multiplicity \(m\ge 1\), off the line, and write \(\delta=\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert>0\). If \(\sigma>\tfrac12\), the prefactor test of Theorem 5.2 requires \(\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon_N/2\) for a balanced closure; since \(\varepsilon_N\to 0\), this fails for all large \(N\). If \(\sigma<\tfrac12\), then \(\varepsilon_N<\delta\) and \(K_N\ge m\) for all large \(N\), so \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\delta<\tfrac12-\varepsilon_N\), and Proposition 4.7 with \(K=K_N\) and Lemma 4.5.3 exclude \(\rho\). Thus no off-line zero is a persistent balanced atom. Conversely, if \(\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma\) is a critical-line zero of multiplicity \(m\), Corollary 4.1.1 gives \(\lvert\eta\rvert\le C_1(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon_N}\) once \(N\) is large. For each fixed \(k\le m\), Leibniz as in Lemma 4.5.3 and \(\zeta^{(\,j)}(\rho)=0\) for \(j<m\) give \[ \bigl\lvert\partial_t^{\,k}\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert \le N^{1/2}\lvert\zeta^{(k)}(\rho)\rvert+O_\rho\bigl((1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)(2\log N)^k\bigr). \] (The bound extends to \(k\le K_N\) with a constant depending on \(\rho\) and \(k\), by Cauchy’s estimates for \(\zeta^{(k)}(\rho)\) on a fixed disk about \(\rho\).) The condition \(\varepsilon_N\log N\to\infty\) yields \(N^{\varepsilon_N}\to\infty\), so for this fixed \(\rho\) the jet inequalities hold for all \(k\le K_N\) once \(N\) is large. The height \(\gamma\) enters the tightness window once \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon_N}\ge\lvert\gamma\rvert\). Hence \(\rho\) is a balanced jet-closure for all large \(N\), and is an atom of every weak-star limit. Marty speed for \(\nu_N\) (Lemma 4.8) excludes the left half-strip at the minimiser, the same comparison as the jet. A right-half zero remains unrestricted and not balanced, so \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) meets the right half-strip if such a zero exists, and Corollary 12.3 remains the conversion.

14. Conclusions

Euler–Maclaurin on the depth-one Euler-sum pair \(H_{N^2}^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}\) at the quadratic link yields \(\eta=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta+O((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma})\) throughout the truncated strip. Unrestricted closures are relative to the main-term scale \(P_N\); balanced closures are slow passages of uniform DQPT depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) with \(P_N\ge N^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}\) and \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\le N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\). The measures \(\mu_N\) and \(\nu_N\) are tight (Theorem 9.1, Lemma 9.6); the residual vanishes by construction; every weak-star limit is pure-point, supported on zeros of \(\Xi\). Identity L is the lower bound of \(\lvert\partial_t\eta\rvert\) against \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) throughout an isolation disk of a simple zero with \(\sigma\le\tfrac12-\varepsilon\); it is Lemma 4.5.2. With a fixed aperture it places every simple atom of \(\mu\) in \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon<\sigma\le\tfrac12+\varepsilon/2\) (Theorem 12.4). A vanishing aperture \(\varepsilon_N\to 0\) with \(\varepsilon_N\log N\to\infty\), with the jet of Lemma 4.5.3, places every balanced atom on the critical line (Theorem 12.6), of any finite multiplicity. Envelope comparison of \(\lvert\eta\rvert\) with \(D_N\) contradicts Euler–Maclaurin in the band \(\sigma>\tfrac14+\varepsilon/2\) and is not used. Corollary 12.3 is a self-contained conversion of the Riemann hypothesis: Marty speed (Lemma 4.8, the north-pole fibre metric) removes the left half-strip from both families; a right-half zero remains visible to \(\nu\) and not to \(\mu\). Thus \(\operatorname{supp}\mu=\operatorname{supp}\nu\) if and only if every non-trivial zero lies on the line.

The same geometric mechanism extends immediately to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions by replacing the coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\) with the analogous mean-zero filters twisted by a Dirichlet character. The finite polygonal paths themselves supply elementary contours for zero-counting via the argument principle.

Addendum A. Quantum algorithms in geometric language

The classical polygonal construction admits a precise dictionary with existing quantum algorithms. Both the dynamical quantum phase transition (DQPT) systems of Wei et al. and the quantum algorithms for exponential sums may be expressed entirely in the analytic and geometric language already developed in the body of the paper.

A.1. DQPT systems as coherent polygonal paths

In the quantum systems of Wei et al. the logarithmic Hamiltonian \(\mathcal{H}_0=\sum_n(\ln n)\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert\) generates a Loschmidt amplitude of the schematic form \[ \mathcal{L}(\beta,t)=\sum_n e^{-\beta\ln n}\,e^{-it\ln n}=\sum_n n^{-\beta-it}. \] At the critical inverse temperature \(\beta=1/2\) this reduces to a Dirichlet series on the critical line. The vanishing of \(\mathcal{L}(1/2,t)\) is the signature of a dynamical quantum phase transition.

The same object appears in the classical setting as the endpoint of a coherent polygonal path. After pairing the depth-one Euler sums at the quadratic link \(M=N^2\) — equivalently, after the mean-zero root-of-unity filter — Theorem 4.1 identifies \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1/2+it)\) with \((1-N^{1/2-it})\zeta(1/2+it)\) up to \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\). Consequently:

  • the quantum Loschmidt amplitude vanishing is the analogue of a path-closure event of controlled depth for the classical polygonal sum;
  • the critical temperature \(\beta=1/2\) is the analytic counterpart of the geometric scaling \(M=N^2\);
  • the discrete set of times at which the quantum amplitude vanishes is the support of the pure-point measure constructed from the classical path-closure ordinates.

The geometric measure \(\mu\) obtained in Theorem 12.1 is the classical shadow of the discrete spectrum of DQPT times. The two realisations—one unitary, one polygonal—encode the same cancellations.

A.2. Quantum evaluation of exponential sums as accelerated path endpoints

Any quantum algorithm that estimates weighted exponential sums \[ S(f,w,N)=\sum_{k}w_k\,e^{2\pi i f(k)} \] by amplitude estimation (Tyagi [14]) evaluates, under the choice \(f(k)=-(t/2\pi)\log k\) and suitable weights, the very Dirichlet polynomials that define the coherent polygonal paths. In geometric language the algorithm computes the Euclidean length of the endpoint vector of the path (or decides whether that length falls below the threshold \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\)) with a quadratic improvement in the precision parameter relative to classical sampling.

The definition of a path-closure event, the isolation of local minima, the construction of the measures \(\mu_N\), and the weak-star analysis remain purely classical. The quantum routine supplies only a computational acceleration for the numerical detection of those events at large height. It does not alter the geometric or measure-theoretic content of the construction.

Addendum B. Refined quadratic asymptotic

Theorem 4.1 is already uniform in \(t\) with remainder \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is the input to depth-of-closure and to the exclusion of spurious atoms. For large \(\lvert t\rvert\) one may replace Euler–Maclaurin by the approximate functional equation, keeping the dual sums of lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). The following statement records the exponent that would follow from Bourgain’s subconvexity on those dual sums. It is not required for Theorems 9.1–12.2.

Refined remainder (optional).
There exist a positive exponent \(\delta=29/168-\eta\) and a range \(\delta'=1/3+1/42-\eta\) such that, for \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\), the remainder in Theorem 4.1 may be replaced by \(O\bigl(N^{1/2-\delta}(1+\lvert t\rvert)^\varepsilon\bigr)\) after inserting Bourgain’s bound \(\mu(1/2)=13/84\) and a van-der-Corput estimate of order 1 on the dual tails, provided an explicit constant in Bourgain’s bound is fixed.
On the range \(\lvert t\rvert\le 2\pi N\) both dual sums of the approximate functional equation are empty and Theorem 4.1 already gives \(O(1+\lvert t\rvert)\), which is stronger. For \(2\pi N<\lvert t\rvert\le N^{\delta'}\) write \[ H_x^{(s)}=\zeta(s)-\chi(s)H_{\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi x)}^{(1-s)}+R(x,s) \] and substitute \(x=N^2\) and \(x=N\) into \(\eta=H_{N^2}^{(s)}-N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}\). The dual sums have lengths \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N^2)\) and \(\lvert t\rvert/(2\pi N)\). Estimate them by convexity or by Bourgain, and estimate the AFE remainders by van der Corput. Collecting exponents as in the previous seven-step sketch yields \(\delta=1/6+1/168-\eta\) from Weyl plus one van-der-Corput increment, or the displayed value from Bourgain. Addendum C. The Davenport–Heilbronn twist: sharpness of the conversion

The construction of Sections 3–12 never mentions the Euler product. This addendum makes that observation into a theorem rather than a caution, by running the entire framework on a function that has every feature of \(\zeta\) used above and no Euler product. The vehicle is the Davenport–Heilbronn function [17], and the bridge to it is the depth-one Euler-sum basis of Definition 3.0. The outcome is twofold: the conversion of Corollary 12.3 is sharp — it correctly reports a false Riemann hypothesis when there is one — and no result provable from the present toolbox can be the Riemann hypothesis.

C.1. The additive-character basis

Definition C.1 (Level-\(q\) depth-one Euler sums).
For \(\omega\in\mathbb{C}\) with \(\omega^q=1\) and real \(x\ge 0\), \[ H_x^{(s)}[\omega]=\sum_{n\le x}\omega^{n}n^{-s}, \] the depth-one finite Euler sum of weight \(s\) at level \(q\) (a truncated Lerch value). For a \(q\)-periodic sequence \(a=(a_r)\) put \(H_x^{(s)}[a]=\sum_{n\le x}a_n n^{-s}\) and \(\widehat{a}_j=q^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{q}a_r e(-jr/q)\).
Lemma C.2 (Every mean-zero periodic polygon is a level-\(q\) Euler-sum combination).
Let \(a\) be \(q\)-periodic. Then \(\sum_{r=1}^q a_r=0\) if and only if \(\widehat{a}_0=0\), and in that case \[ H_x^{(s)}[a]=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\widehat{a}_j\,H_x^{(s)}[\zeta_q^{\,j}],\qquad \zeta_q=e^{2\pi i/q}. \] For the filter of Lemma 2.2 one has \(\widehat{c^{(N)}}_j=-1\) for every \(j\not\equiv 0\), so \[ \eta_N^{(M)}(s)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}H_M^{(s)}\bigl[\zeta_N^{\,j}\bigr]. \]
Finite Fourier inversion \(a_n=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\widehat{a}_j e(jn/q)\) applied termwise; \(\widehat{a}_0\) is the mean. For \(c^{(N)}\), \(\widehat{c}_j=N^{-1}\bigl(\sum_{r=1}^{N}e(-jr/N)-N\bigr)=-1\) when \(j\not\equiv0\). The displayed identity is then Lemma 3.2 read backwards, and is the precise form of the remark in the proof of Lemma 2.2 that \(c^{(N)}\) is the geometric sum over the non-trivial \(N\)-th roots of unity.

Lemma C.2 identifies the ambient space: the coherent polygon of Definition 3.1 is a \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear combination of depth-one Euler sums twisted by additive characters \(n\mapsto e(jn/q)\), and so is every mean-zero periodic Dirichlet polynomial. The Davenport–Heilbronn function lives in the same space at \(q=5\).

C.2. The twist

Definition C.3 (Davenport–Heilbronn coefficients).
Let \[ \xi=\frac{\sqrt{10-2\sqrt5}-2}{\sqrt5-1}=0.2840790438\ldots, \] and let \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) be the sequence of period \(5\) with \((a_1,\dots,a_5)=(1,\xi,-\xi,-1,0)\). Write \(f(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}a^{\mathrm{DH}}_n n^{-s}\) for its Dirichlet series, entire because the coefficients have mean zero.
Lemma C.4 (Self-duality; what \(f\) has and lacks).
(i) \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) is odd, \(a_{5-r}=-a_r\), has mean zero, and is a fixed vector of the normalised finite Fourier transform: \((i\sqrt5)^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{5}a_r e(rk/5)=a_k\) for every \(k\). Consequently \(f\) satisfies a Riemann-type functional equation relating \(f(s)\) to \(f(1-s)\), with the same gamma factor as an odd Dirichlet \(L\)-function of conductor \(5\). (ii) \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) is supported on the units mod \(5\), so \(f=\sum_{\chi\bmod 5}b_\chi L(s,\chi)\); numerically the principal and quadratic characters drop out and \[ f(s)=b\,L(s,\chi)+\overline{b}\,L(s,\overline{\chi}),\qquad b=\tfrac12-0.14203952\ldots\,i, \] with \(\chi\) an odd primitive character mod \(5\). Thus \(f\) is a \(\mathbb{C}\)-combination of two primitive \(L\)-functions and has no Euler product. (iii) \(f\) has infinitely many zeros on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\) and also zeros off that line, including in \(\sigma>1\) [17]. An off-line zero inside the strip is \[ \rho_{\mathrm{DH}}=0.8085171824566374\ldots+85.69934848537759\ldots\,i \] (Spira [18]), verified here to \(\lvert f(\rho_{\mathrm{DH}})\rvert<4\cdot10^{-21}\). It lies in \(S_\delta\) for every \(\delta\le 0.19\).
Definition C.5 (The twisted Euler-sum pair).
For an integer \(N\ge2\), \[ \eta_N^{\mathrm{DH},(N^2)}(s)=H_{N^2}^{(s)}\bigl[a^{\mathrm{DH}}\bigr]-N^{1-s}H_{N}^{(s)}\bigl[a^{\mathrm{DH}}\bigr], \] the pair of Definition 3.1 with the plain Euler sums replaced by their level-\(5\) twists. By Lemma C.2 it is again a combination of depth-one Euler sums, now at level \(5N\).
Lemma C.6 (Exact filter form).
If \(N\equiv1\pmod 5\) then \[ \eta_N^{\mathrm{DH},(N^2)}(s)=\sum_{m\le N^2}c_m^{(N)}a^{\mathrm{DH}}_m\,m^{-s}, \] so the twisted pair is literally the coherent polygon of Section 3 with steps reweighted by \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\). For \(N\not\equiv1\pmod5\) the identity fails.
\(N^{1-s}H_N^{(s)}[a]=N\sum_{j\le N}a_j(Nj)^{-s}\), and for \(m=Nj\le N^2\) one has \(m\equiv j\pmod 5\) exactly when \(N\equiv1\pmod 5\), whence \(a_j=a_m\) and the subtracted sum is \(N\sum_{N\mid m,\;m\le N^2}a_m m^{-s}\). Otherwise the two indexings disagree. Numerically the discrepancy is \(0\) to \(10^{-21}\) at \(N=11,16,21\) and of size \(0.77\)–\(4.9\) at \(N=12,13,14\).
Theorem C.7 (Theorem 4.3 for the twist).
Let \(\sigma_0\in(0,1/2)\) and \(s=\sigma+it\) with \(\sigma\in[\sigma_0,1-\sigma_0]\). For every integer \(N\ge2\), \[ \eta_N^{\mathrm{DH},(N^2)}(s)=(1-N^{1-s})f(s)+O_{\sigma_0}\bigl((1+\lvert s\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}\bigr), \] with an absolute implied constant — the period \(5\) does not grow with \(N\). There is no half-term: the analogue of \(\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\) is absorbed by the mean-zero cancellation.

Remark C.11 (Additive basis, multiplicative question).
Corollary C.10 is not a defect of the present construction but a description of the space it lives in. The two functions are complementary in exactly the features the framework does not use: \(f\) has a clean functional equation and no product, while the limit \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) has a product but only the twisted symmetry \(F(s)=\chi(s)\bigl[(1-N^{1-s})/(1-N^{s})\bigr]F(1-s)\). The conversion of Corollary 12.3 is therefore exactly as strong as it should be, and the missing input is identified sharply: an admissible additional hypothesis must be one that fails for \(f\). The cleanest such separator is the absence of zeros in \(\sigma>1\), true for \(\zeta\) by the Euler product and false for \(f\) by the theorem of Davenport and Heilbronn. This is the same doorway named in §14 and in tensor.html: arithmetic positivity, which is a statement about primes and not about additive characters.
