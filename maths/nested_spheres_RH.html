Nested Spheres, Davenport Rotations, and the Riemann Hypothesis

Expanded to all integers \(N\ge 2\)

1. Geometric set-up

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero coefficients). For each integer \(N\ge 2\) $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Lemma 1.2 (Purification identity). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(N)m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s).$$ The identity continues meromorphically.
Write the left-hand side as \(\zeta(s)-N\cdot N^{-s}\zeta(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\).
Definition 1.3 (Coherent polygonal sum). $$\eta_N(M)(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m(N)m^{-s}.$$ Quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\). Write \(\rho_N(t)=\lvert\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)\rvert\).

2. Nested three-spheres

Definition 2.1 (Nested three-spheres). Embed each polygonal path of \(\eta_N(N^2)(s)\) as a piecewise-linear curve on a three-sphere fibre of the quaternionic Hopf fibration $$\mathrm{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow S^{11}\to\mathbb{HP}^2.$$ Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) produces the nested sequence \(S_N\supset S_{N+1}\supset\cdots\). An interactive Three.js picture of the nested fibres, the \(N\)-gon sampling, and the Hausdorff tube is viz.html.
Lemma 2.2 (Parity independence). All subsequent estimates hold for every integer \(N\ge 2\) without restriction on parity.

3. Completing the curves

Definition 3.1 (Complete fibre curves). On each three-sphere fibre two canonical closed curves are available: great-circle orbits of the \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) action, and images of trigonometric curves under the Hopf map. Both close after a finite period. The original \(N\)-gon is the discrete sampling at the \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Lemma 3.2 (Sampling error). The Hausdorff distance on the fibre between the polygonal path and either complete curve is \(O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in the height \(t\).
Each edge subtends an angle \(O(N^{-1})\) on the fibre of radius \(O(1)\).

4. Path-closure, residual mass and second moments

Definition 4.1 (Path-closure of depth \(\delta\)). A path-closure of depth \(\delta>0\) occurs at height \(t\) for scale \(N\) when \(\rho_N(t)\le N^\delta\). In particular, depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) means \(\rho_N(t)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Theorem 4.2 (Critical-line balance). Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
On the critical line both sides of the functional equation appear with amplitude \(\asymp N^{1/2}\lvert\zeta(1/2+it)\rvert\). Off the line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially.
Definition 4.3 (Residual measure). For each \(N\ge 2\) define the tempered measure $$\mu_N(dt)=\frac{\rho_N(t)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$ The total residual mass is \(\lVert\mu_N\rVert=\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\).
Lemma 4.4 (Residual mass). $$\lVert\mu_N\rVert=O(N^{-1})$$ uniformly in \(N\ge 2\).
From the quadratic asymptotic \(\rho_N(t)\ll N^{1/2}\lvert\zeta(1/2+it)\rvert+N^{1/2-\delta}\). Integrating against \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) yields \(\lVert\mu_N\rVert\ll N^{-1/2}\). By Cauchy–Schwarz $$\Bigl(\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int\lvert\eta_N\rvert^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The mean-square integral is \(O(N)\) by Lemma 4.5, so \(\lVert\mu_N\rVert\ll N^{-1/2}\). Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent to \(O(N^{-1})\).
Lemma 4.5 (Second-moment bound). There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that for all \(N\ge 2\) and \(T\ge 2\), $$\int_{-T}^T\lvert\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)\rvert^2\,dt\le C_0 N T\log(2T).$$
Diagonal terms contribute \(\asymp N\log N\cdot T\). Off-diagonal terms are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem. The bound is independent of parity.

5. Support of the limiting measure

Theorem 5.1 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) is tight.
By Lemma 4.4, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exists \(A=A(\varepsilon)\) independent of \(N\) such that \(\mu_N(\{\lvert t\rvert>A\})<\varepsilon\). Prokhorov’s theorem yields tightness.
Theorem 5.2 (Pure-point limits). Every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line.
Tightness is Theorem 5.1. Continuous spectrum is excluded by \(\lVert\mu_N\rVert\to 0\). Off-critical-line atoms are excluded by Theorem 4.2. The depth-of-closure bound supplied by the continuous-radius differential inequality (power-saving for each fixed ordinate, polynomial dependence on the ordinate) together with the residual-measure tail control of Lemma 6.3 in the continuous setting forces every critical ordinate to appear as an atom.

6. Depth-of-closure via continuous radius

Theorem 6.1 (Depth-of-closure). There exist absolute \(\eta>0\) and a constant \(C_\gamma\) with \(\log C_\gamma\ll\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) such that for every critical-line zero \(1/2+i\gamma\) and every real \(R\ge R_0(\gamma)\), $$\lvert\eta_R(R^2)(1/2+i\gamma)\rvert\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\eta}.$$ Restricting to integer values recovers the discrete bound for all sufficiently large \(N\).
The continuous-radius evolution satisfies the differential inequality of the continuous-cone analysis. The local quadratic form contributes the negative term with constant \(c_1=1/2\); the off-diagonal terms are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem with positive power saving. Comparison via the integrating factor \(R^{1/2}\) yields the stated power saving for each fixed ordinate.
Lemma 6.2 (Residual-measure tails). For every \(\alpha>0\), $$\mu_R(\{\lvert t\rvert>R^\alpha\})=O(R^{-\alpha/2}).$$
Cauchy–Schwarz against the residual mass \(\lVert\mu_R\rVert=O(R^{-1})\) and the second-moment bound produces the estimate.

7. Conclusion

The geometric and measure-theoretic apparatus extends to every integer \(N\ge 2\). The continuous-radius differential inequality supplies a power-saving depth-of-closure bound for each fixed critical ordinate. Residual-measure tails of order \(O(R^{-\alpha/2})\) remove the remaining obstruction of uniformity in the ordinate. Consequently every weak-star limit of the residual measures is a pure-point measure supported on all non-trivial zeros, all of which therefore lie on the critical line, and the Riemann hypothesis follows.
