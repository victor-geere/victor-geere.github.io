1. Geometric set-up (valid for every \(N\geq 2\))

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter). For each integer \(N\geq 2\), even or odd, \[ c_m(N)=\begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }N\nmid m,\\ 1-N & \text{if }N\mid m. \end{cases} \]
Lemma 1.2 (Purification identity). For every integer \(N\geq 2\) and \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), \[ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(N)m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s). \] The identity continues meromorphically to the whole plane.
Write the left-hand side as \(\zeta(s)-N\cdot N^{-s}\zeta(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\). The same algebraic identity holds after analytic continuation.
Definition 1.3 (Coherent polygonal sum). \[ \eta_N(M)(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m(N)\,m^{-s}. \] Quadratic truncation means \(M=N^2\).

2. From polygons to nested spheres (all \(N\))

Definition 2.1 (Nested three-spheres). Embed each polygonal path \(\eta_N(N^2)(s)\) as a piecewise-linear curve on a three-sphere fibre of the quaternionic Hopf fibration \[ \mathrm{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow S^{11}\to\mathbb{HP}^2. \] Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) produces the nested sequence \[ S_N\supset S_{N+1}\supset S_{N+2}\supset\cdots \] whose radii shrink geometrically toward the origin of the fibre.
Lemma 2.2 (Parity independence). All subsequent estimates hold for every integer \(N\geq 2\) without restriction on parity.
The coefficients \(c_m(N)\), the purification identity, the quadratic asymptotic and the second-moment bound never use the parity of \(N\). The geometric distinction (balanced amplitudes if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\)) is likewise parity-free.

3. Completing the curves (uniformly in \(N\))

Definition 3.1 (Complete fibre curves). On each three-sphere fibre two canonical closed curves are available:
  • great-circle orbits of the \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) action;
  • images of the trigonometric curves \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) under the Hopf map.
Both are equivariant under the residual circle action and close after a finite period. The original \(N\)-gon is the discrete sampling of either curve at the \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Lemma 3.2 (Sampling error). The Hausdorff distance on the fibre between the polygonal \(N\)-gon and its smooth completion is \(O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in the base point.
Each side of the \(N\)-gon subtends an angle \(2\pi/N\). The chord-to-arc discrepancy on a sphere of bounded radius is \(O((2\pi/N)^2)=O(N^{-2})\) per side and therefore \(O(N^{-1})\) globally.

4. Path-closure and residual mass (all \(N\))

Definition 4.1 (Path-closure of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\)). A path-closure occurs at height \(t\) when \[ \bigl|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]
Theorem 4.2 (Critical-line balance). Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
On the critical line the quadratic asymptotic (continuous or discrete) shows that both sides of the functional equation appear with amplitude \(\asymp N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+it)|\). Off the line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially, destroying the balance. See Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 5.2 of the original polygonal paper; the argument is unchanged for odd \(N\).
Lemma 4.3 (Residual mass). \[ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_N(t)\,dt=O(N^{-1}) \] uniformly in \(N\geq 2\).
By definition \(\rho_N(t)\le N^{-1}\min\bigl(1,|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|^2\bigr)(1+t^2)^{-1}\). Integrating the weight \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) yields a constant; the factor \(N^{-1}\) remains.
Lemma 4.4 (Second-moment bound). There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that for all \(N\geq 2\) and \(T\geq 2\), \[ \int_{-T}^T\bigl|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)\bigr|^2\,dt\le C_0\,N\,T\log(2T). \]
Expand the square. Diagonal terms contribute \(\asymp N\log N\cdot T\) because \(\sum_{m\le N^2}|c_m(N)|^2 m^{-1}\asymp N\log N\). Off-diagonal terms are estimated by the Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem for Dirichlet polynomials. The mean-zero condition improves the constant but is not essential. The bound is independent of the parity of \(N\).

5. Support of the limiting measure

Theorem 5.1 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\geq 2}\) is tight in the weak-star topology of tempered measures.
The second-moment bound of Lemma 4.4 together with a Markov inequality and the isolation of local minima (Definition 6.1 of the polygonal paper) show that for every \(\varepsilon>0\) a fixed compact interval already carries mass \(1-\varepsilon\) uniformly in \(N\). Prokhorov’s theorem supplies tightness.
Theorem 5.2 (Pure-point limits). Every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line.
Residual continuous mass vanishes by Lemma 4.3. Spurious atoms are excluded by the phase-density argument of the original Theorem 8.1 (the same argument works for odd \(N\)). The geometric distinction of Theorem 4.2 forces the support onto the critical line.

6. The missing lower bound (still open for all \(N\))

Conjecture 6.1 (Uniform depth-of-closure). There exist \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(N_0\) such that for every critical-line zero \(1/2+i\gamma\) and every integer \(N\geq N_0\), \[ \bigl|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \]

The nested-sphere picture makes the conjecture plausible: once a closure occurs at scale \(N_0\), self-similar scaling should force closures at every larger scale. The continuous-cone differential inequality developed in the companion note supplies a route to the conjecture; until that inequality is fully verified, the uniform bound remains open for both even and odd \(N\).

7. Conclusion

The entire geometric and measure-theoretic apparatus—nested three-spheres, Davenport–Heilbronn rotations, complete curves, residual vanishing, tightness, and support on the critical line—extends verbatim to every integer \(N\geq 2\). The single missing analytic step is the uniform depth-of-closure bound of Conjecture 6.1. Once that bound is established (for example via the continuous-cone differential inequality), the construction yields a pure-point spectral measure supported on all critical-line zeros and thereby proves the Riemann hypothesis.
