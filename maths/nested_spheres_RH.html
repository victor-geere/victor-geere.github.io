Expanded to all integers \(N\ge 2\)

1. Geometric set-up

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero coefficients). For each integer \(N\ge 2\) $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Lemma 1.2 (Purification identity). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(N)m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s).$$ The identity continues meromorphically.
Write the left-hand side as \(\zeta(s)-N\cdot N^{-s}\zeta(s)=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\).
Definition 1.3 (Coherent polygonal sum). $$\eta_N(M)(s)=\sum_{m=1}^M c_m(N)m^{-s}.$$ Quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\). Write \(\rho_N(t)=|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|\).

2. Nested three-spheres

Definition 2.1 (Nested three-spheres). Embed each polygonal path of \(\eta_N(N^2)(s)\) as a piecewise-linear curve on a three-sphere fibre of the quaternionic Hopf fibration $$\mathrm{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow S^{11}\to\mathbb{HP}^2.$$ Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) produces the nested sequence \(S_N\supset S_{N+1}\supset\cdots\).
Lemma 2.2 (Parity independence). All subsequent estimates hold for every integer \(N\ge 2\) without restriction on parity.

3. Completing the curves

Definition 3.1 (Complete fibre curves). On each three-sphere fibre two canonical closed curves are available: great-circle orbits of the \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) action, and images of trigonometric curves under the Hopf map. Both close after a finite period. The original \(N\)-gon is the discrete sampling at the \(N\)-th roots of unity.
Lemma 3.2 (Sampling error). The Hausdorff distance on the fibre between the polygonal path and either complete curve is \(O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in the height \(t\).
Each edge subtends an angle \(O(N^{-1})\) on the fibre of radius \(O(1)\).

4. Path-closure, residual mass and second moments

Definition 4.1 (Path-closure of depth \(\delta\)). A path-closure of depth \(\delta>0\) occurs at height \(t\) for scale \(N\) when \(\rho_N(t)\le N^\delta\). In particular, depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) means \(\rho_N(t)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\).
Theorem 4.2 (Critical-line balance). Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
On the critical line both sides of the functional equation appear with amplitude \(\asymp N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+it)|\). Off the line the prefactor \(1-N^{1-\sigma-it}\) grows or decays exponentially.
Definition 4.3 (Residual measure). For each \(N\ge 2\) define the tempered measure $$\mu_N(dt)=\frac{\rho_N(t)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$ The total residual mass is \(\|\mu_N\|=\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\).
Lemma 4.4 (Residual mass). $$\|\mu_N\|=O(N^{-1})$$ uniformly in \(N\ge 2\).
From the quadratic asymptotic \(\rho_N(t)\ll N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+it)|+N^{1/2-\delta}\). Integrating against \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) yields \(\|\mu_N\|\ll N^{-1/2}\). By Cauchy–Schwarz $$\Bigl(\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int|\eta_N|^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The mean-square integral is \(O(N)\) by Lemma 4.5, so \(\|\mu_N\|\ll N^{-1/2}\). Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent to \(O(N^{-1})\).
Lemma 4.5 (Second-moment bound). There exists an absolute constant \(C_0\) such that for all \(N\ge 2\) and \(T\ge 2\), $$\int_{-T}^T|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|^2\,dt\le C_0 N T\log(2T).$$
Diagonal terms contribute \(\asymp N\log N\cdot T\). Off-diagonal terms are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem. The bound is independent of parity.

5. Support of the limiting measure

Theorem 5.1 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}\) is tight.
By Lemma 4.4, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exists \(A=A(\varepsilon)\) independent of \(N\) such that \(\mu_N(\{|t|>A\})<\varepsilon\). Prokhorov’s theorem yields tightness.
Theorem 5.2 (Pure-point limits). Every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_N\}\) is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line, provided a uniform depth-of-closure bound \(\rho_N(\gamma)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) holds for every critical ordinate \(\gamma\) and all sufficiently large \(N\).
Tightness is Theorem 5.1. Continuous spectrum is excluded by \(\|\mu_N\|\to 0\). Off-critical-line atoms are excluded by Theorem 4.2. The depth-of-closure hypothesis forces every critical ordinate to appear as an atom.

6. The missing lower bound

Conjecture 6.1 (Uniform depth-of-closure). There exist \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(N_0\) such that for every critical-line zero \(1/2+i\gamma\) and every integer \(N\ge N_0\), $$|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+i\gamma)|\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}.$$

The nested-sphere picture makes the conjecture plausible. The continuous-cone differential inequality supplies a route to the conjecture.

7. Conclusion

The geometric and measure-theoretic apparatus extends to every integer \(N\ge 2\). The single missing analytic step is the uniform depth-of-closure bound of Conjecture 6.1. Once that bound is established the construction yields a pure-point spectral measure supported on all critical-line zeros and thereby proves the Riemann hypothesis.
