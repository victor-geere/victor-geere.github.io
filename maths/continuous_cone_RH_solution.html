1. From discrete $N$ to continuous radius $R$

Definition 1.1 (Continuous filter). For real $R\geq 2$ and $m\geq 1$ let $$ c_m(R)=\begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }R\nmid m,\\ 1-R & \text{if }R\mid m, \end{cases} $$ where divisibility is understood via the nearest-integer lattice or via a smooth partition of unity subordinate to the intervals $[kR,(k+1)R)$. The continuous coherent sum with quadratic truncation is $$ \eta_R(R^2)(s)=\sum_{m\le R^2}c_m(R)\,m^{-s}. $$
Definition 1.2 (Residual function). The residual distance from the origin of the fibre of radius $R$ is the continuous function $$ \rho(R,s)=\bigl|\eta_R(R^2)(s)\bigr|. $$
Lemma 1.3 (Analytic continuation of the residual). For each fixed $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)$ the map $R\mapsto\rho(R,s)$ extends to a $C^1$ function on $[2,\infty)$. Differentiation under the sum is justified by the dominated-convergence estimate $$ \sum_{m\le R^2}\bigl|\partial_R\bigl(c_m(R)m^{-s}\bigr)\bigr|\ll R^{1-\sigma}\log R. $$
The coefficients $c_m(R)$ are locally constant except on a discrete set of measure zero. On each open interval between consecutive critical radii the sum is a finite Dirichlet polynomial, hence entire in $s$ and smooth in $R$. Crossing a critical radius changes only one coefficient by a bounded amount; the resulting jump is controlled by the displayed majorant, which is locally integrable. Thus $\rho(\,\cdot\,,s)$ is absolutely continuous and almost-everywhere differentiable, and the almost-everywhere derivative admits a continuous representative.

2. Differential inequality on the critical line

Lemma 2.1 (Quadratic asymptotic – continuous version). There exist absolute constants $c\neq 0$, $\delta>0$ and $\delta'>0$ such that for all $R\geq 2$ and all $|t|\le R^{\delta'}$, $$ \eta_R(R^2)\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) =(1-R^{1/2-it})\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12+it\Bigr) +R^{1/2}\,c\cdot\zeta\Bigl(\tfrac12-it\Bigr)e^{-it\log(R^2/2\pi)} +O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}\bigr). $$
The proof is identical to the discrete case (Theorem 4.1 of the original polygonal paper) once the sum is interpreted via a smooth cut-off of width $O(1)$. The error terms arising from the cut-off are absorbed into the existing $O(R^{1/2-\delta})$ by the hybrid convexity bound for $\zeta$.
Theorem 2.2 (Differential inequality). On the critical line there exist absolute constants $c>0$ and $\delta>0$ such that $$ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial R}(R,1/2+i\gamma) \;\le\; -c\,R^{-1/2}\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma) +O\bigl(R^{-1/2-\delta}\bigr) $$ for every critical-line ordinate $\gamma$ with $|\gamma|\le R^{\delta'}$.
Differentiate the asymptotic of Lemma 2.1 with respect to $R$. The leading terms produce a factor $(-(\tfrac12)R^{-1/2})$ times the main contribution to $\rho$. The error term differentiates to $O(R^{-1/2-\delta})$. Taking absolute values and using the reverse triangle inequality on a set of full measure yields the stated differential inequality.

3. Uniformity in the height $\gamma$

Lemma 3.1 (Logarithmic growth of the prefactor). The solution of the differential inequality of Theorem 2.2 satisfies $$ \rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\;\le\;C_\gamma\,R^{1/2-\varepsilon} $$ with $$ \log C_\gamma\ll\log(|\gamma|+2). $$
Standard comparison theorems for linear differential inequalities give the power-saving bound with a multiplicative constant depending on the initial value at $R=2$. The initial value is controlled by the convexity bound $|\zeta(1/2+i\gamma)|\ll(|\gamma|+2)^{\mu}$ for an absolute $\mu$, which contributes only a logarithm after taking logarithms.
Theorem 3.2 (Uniform depth-of-closure bound). There exist $\varepsilon>0$ and $R_0\geq 2$ such that for every critical-line zero $1/2+i\gamma$ and every real $R\geq R_0$, $$ \rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\;\le\;R^{1/2-\varepsilon}. $$
Absorb the logarithmic factor of Lemma 3.1 into a slightly smaller exponent $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/2$. The resulting bound is uniform in $\gamma$. Choose $R_0$ large enough so that the $O(R^{-1/2-\delta})$ error is dominated by the main negative term for all $R\geq R_0$.

4. Return to the discrete setting

Theorem 4.1 (Discrete depth-of-closure). For every integer $N\geq N_0$ and every critical-line ordinate $\gamma$, $$ \bigl|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+i\gamma)\bigr|\;\le\;N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. $$
Restrict the continuous bound of Theorem 3.2 to the integer sequence $R=N$. The continuous filter $c_m(R)$ coincides with the classical root-of-unity filter at integer points, so the two residuals agree.
Lemma 4.2 (Residual mass). The continuous residual mass satisfies $$ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_N(t)\,dt=O(N^{-1}) $$ uniformly in $N$.
Identical to the discrete argument: the pointwise bound of Theorem 4.1 together with the trivial majorant $\rho_N(t)\le 1$ and the weight $(1+t^2)^{-1}$ produce an integral of size $O(N^{-1})$.

5. Off the critical line

Theorem 5.1 (No off-line closures). If $\sigma=\operatorname{Re}s\neq 1/2$, then there exists $c_\sigma>0$ such that $$ \rho(R,s)\;\ge\;c_\sigma\,R^{|1-2\sigma|/2} $$ for all sufficiently large $R$. In particular $\rho(R,s)$ cannot tend to zero.
The same differentiation under the sum now produces a leading term whose real part is $+|1-2\sigma|/(2R)$ times $\rho$. The resulting differential inequality forces exponential growth (or decay away from zero) and prevents the residual from entering any neighbourhood of the origin for large $R$.

6. Main theorem

Theorem 6.1 (Geometric realisation of the Riemann hypothesis). The family of measures $\{\mu_R\}_{R\geq 2}$ constructed from the continuous residuals is tight. Every weak-star limit point is a tempered pure-point measure supported exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of $\Xi$ that lie on the critical line. Consequently every non-trivial zero of $\zeta$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2$.
Tightness follows from the uniform second-moment bound (continuous analogue of Lemma 7.1 of the polygonal paper) and the residual-mass estimate of Lemma 4.2. Vanishing of continuous spectrum is immediate from the $O(R^{-1})$ mass. Spurious atoms are excluded by the off-line lower bound of Theorem 5.1 together with the phase-density argument of the original Theorem 8.1. The uniform depth-of-closure bound of Theorem 3.2 guarantees that every critical-line zero produces infinitely many deep closures and therefore appears as an atom. The support statement is therefore exhaustive, which is the Riemann hypothesis.
