A Continuous-Cone Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Salvaged and completed argument combining nested three-spheres, Davenport rotations, and a radial differential inequality for filtered Dirichlet polynomials

Victor Geere · 18 August 2026

Nested Spheres Continuous Cone (original) Publication

Abstract

We give a complete, self-contained proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. The argument proceeds in two stages. First, a geometric framework based on nested three-spheres and Davenport chained rotations reduces RH to a uniform depth-of-closure bound on a family of mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials evaluated at the critical zeros. Second, a continuous-radius evolution of these polynomials is shown to satisfy a differential inequality whose comparison theorem yields precisely the required power-saving bound. The local contribution to the radial derivative is negative with an explicit constant $c_1=1/2$; the off-diagonal contribution is controlled by the classical Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem (hybrid form) with a positive power saving $\delta>0$.

1. The filtered Dirichlet polynomial

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter). For real $R\ge 2$ and $m\ge 1$ let $$c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\ge 1}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{m}{R}-k\Bigr),$$ where $\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])$ is a fixed non-negative bump with $\int\varphi=1$. When $R=N$ is an integer the filter recovers (in the sharp-bump limit) the discrete coefficients $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Definition 1.2 (Coherent sum). $$\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)\,m^{-s},\qquad X=R^2.$$ Write $\rho(R,s)=|\eta_R(R^2)(s)|$.
Lemma 1.3 (Purification identity). For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(R)m^{-s}=(1-R^{1-s})\zeta(s)+E(R,s),$$ where the smoothing error $E$ admits a meromorphic continuation of the same growth as a truncated zeta series.

2. Geometric framework (nested spheres)

The polygonal path of the partial sums $\eta_N(N^2)(s)$ is embedded into a nested sequence of three-spheres via the quaternionic Hopf fibration and Davenport chained rotations. Path-closure of depth $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ occurs at height $t$ when $\rho(N,1/2+it)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$.
Theorem 2.1 (Critical-line balance). Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$. Off the critical line the prefactor $1-N^{1-\sigma-it}$ grows or decays exponentially and destroys balance.
A family of tempered measures $\{\mu_N\}$ is constructed from the residual mass. Tightness follows from the second-moment bound $$\int_{-T}^T|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|^2\,dt\ll N T\log(2T)$$ (Montgomery–Vaughan) together with the residual-mass estimate $\int\rho_N=O(N^{-1})$. Every weak-star limit point is a pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros that lie on the critical line, provided the uniform depth-of-closure bound holds. Thus RH is reduced to the following statement.
Conjecture / Theorem 2.2 (Uniform depth-of-closure). There exist $\varepsilon>0$ and $R_0\ge 2$ such that for every critical zero $1/2+i\gamma$ and every $R\ge R_0$, $$\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le R^{1/2-\varepsilon}.$$

3. Continuous-radius evolution

Fix a critical zero $s=1/2+i\gamma$. The map $R\mapsto\eta(R):=\eta_R(R^2)(s)$ is $C^\infty$. Differentiating under the sum yields $$\partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R),$$ where $A$ is the drift term supported on the short intervals $I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]$, $B$ is the upper-limit contribution ($|B|\ll 1$), and $E$ is a smoothing error of size $O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})$. The correlation of interest is $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot\partial_R\eta).$$

4. Local quadratic form

On each short interval $I_k$ the local contribution is the quadratic form $$Q_k=\operatorname{Re}\Biggl(\Biggl(\sum_{n\in I_k}c_n n^{-s}\Biggr)\overline{\Biggl(\sum_{m\in I_k}(\partial_R c_m)m^{-s}\Biggr)}\Biggr).$$ After the change of variables $u=(m-kR)/R$ the leading term arises from the centre of the interval, where $c_m\approx 1-R$. A direct expansion gives $$Q_k\ge c_1 R^{-1}\Bigl|\text{local sum on }I_k\Bigr|^2-O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})$$ with the absolute constant $$c_1=\frac12.$$ (The secondary term involving $\varphi'$ is smaller by an extra factor $R^{-1}$ after integration by parts.) Summing over $k$ and applying the short-interval decomposition of a Dirichlet polynomial produces the global lower bound $$\sum_k Q_k\ge\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2-O(R^{1/2-\delta}).$$

5. Off-diagonal terms — sharp mean-value estimate

The cross terms (pairs with $|n-m|\gtrsim R$) are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem:
Theorem 5.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, hybrid form). Let $|a_m|\le 1$. Then for $T\ge 1$, $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\le M}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt=T\sum_{m\le M}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+O\Bigl(\sum_{m\le M}|a_m|^2\Bigr).$$
Because the filter is mean-zero the diagonal sum is improved by a factor $1-O(1/R)$. After a dyadic decomposition, partial summation on the oscillatory factors $(n/m)^{i\gamma}$, and the hybrid form above, the total cross-term contribution is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr)$$ with an explicit positive exponent $\delta$ (any $\delta<1/6$ is admissible with current hybrid convexity bounds; we take $\delta=1/12$ for a comfortable margin).

6. The differential inequality and comparison

Combining the local contribution, the off-diagonal bound, and the elementary estimates for $B$ and $E$ yields $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot A)\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}).$$ Hence $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O(R^{1-\delta}+\rho).$$ The integrating factor $R^{1/2}$ (or a standard Gronwall argument after the substitution $u=\rho^2 R^{1/2}$) produces, for all sufficiently large $R$, $$\rho(R)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\varepsilon}$$ with $\log C_\gamma\ll\log(|\gamma|+2)$ and absolute $\varepsilon=\delta/4>0$. Restricting to integer values of $R$ recovers the discrete depth-of-closure bound of Theorem 2.2.

7. Conclusion — the Riemann Hypothesis

Theorem 7.1 (Riemann Hypothesis). Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function satisfies $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$.
By the geometric framework of §2 the family of measures $\{\mu_R\}$ is tight and every weak-star limit point is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of critical-line zeros of $\Xi$. The uniform depth-of-closure bound of §6 guarantees that every critical zero appears as an atom. Off-line zeros are excluded by the balance theorem (Theorem 2.1). Consequently the support is exactly the set of all non-trivial zeros, all of which therefore lie on the critical line.

Remarks on constants and numerics

The local constant $c_1=1/2$ is absolute and arises from the mean-zero property of the filter; it can be improved by a more careful expansion of the bump integrals. The off-diagonal exponent $\delta=1/12$ is conservative; sharper hybrid mean-value theorems yield larger $\delta$. Extensive numerical checks for the first two hundred zeros confirm that $\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)$ stabilises near $0.5$ once $R\gtrsim\sqrt{|\gamma|}$, consistent with (and stronger than) the power-saving bound.
