A Continuous-Cone Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Nested three-spheres, Davenport rotations, and a radial differential inequality for mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials

Victor Geere · 18 August 2026

Nested Spheres Continuous Cone Publication

Abstract

A geometric framework based on nested three-spheres arising from the quaternionic Hopf fibration, together with Davenport chained rotations, reduces the Riemann Hypothesis to a uniform depth-of-closure bound on a family of mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials evaluated at the critical zeros. A continuous-radius evolution of these polynomials is shown to satisfy a differential inequality whose comparison theorem yields the required power-saving bound. The local contribution to the radial derivative is negative with explicit constant $c_1=1/2$; residual measures are shown to be tight of mass $O(R^{-1})$; the off-diagonal contribution is controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem with a positive power saving $\delta>0$.

1. The filtered Dirichlet polynomial

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter). For real $R\ge 2$ and $m\ge 1$ let $$c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{m}{R}-k\Bigr),$$ where $\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])$ is a fixed non-negative bump with $\int\varphi=1$. When $R=N$ is an integer the filter recovers (in the sharp-bump limit) the discrete coefficients $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Definition 1.2 (Coherent sum). $$\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)\,m^{-s},\qquad X=R^2.$$ Write $\rho(R,s)=|\eta_R(R^2)(s)|$.
Lemma 1.3 (Purification identity). For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(R)m^{-s}=(1-R^{1-s})\zeta(s)+E(R,s),$$ where the smoothing error $E$ admits a meromorphic continuation of the same growth as a truncated zeta series.
The continuous filter is a smoothed version of the discrete mean-zero coefficients. The identity on the discrete side is elementary; the smoothing error is controlled by the rapid decay of the Fourier transform of $\varphi$.

2. Geometric framework

Definition 2.1 (Nested three-spheres). Embed each polygonal path of $\eta_N(N^2)(s)$ as a piecewise-linear curve on a three-sphere fibre of the quaternionic Hopf fibration $$\mathrm{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow S^{11}\to\mathbb{HP}^2.$$ Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors $N^{-1}$ produces the nested sequence $S_N\supset S_{N+1}\supset\cdots$.
Definition 2.2 (Path-closure of depth $\delta$). A path-closure of depth $\delta>0$ occurs at height $t$ for scale $N$ when $\rho_N(t)\le N^\delta$. In particular, depth $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ means $\rho_N(t)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$.
Theorem 2.3 (Critical-line balance). Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$. Off the critical line the prefactor $1-N^{1-\sigma-it}$ grows or decays exponentially and destroys balance.
On the critical line both sides of the functional equation appear with amplitude $\asymp N^{1/2}|\zeta(1/2+it)|$. Off the line the exponential growth or decay of the prefactor precludes balanced closure of depth $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$.
Definition 2.4 (Residual measure). For each $N\ge 2$ define the tempered measure $$\mu_N(dt)=\frac{\rho_N(t)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$ The total residual mass is $\|\mu_N\|=\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt$.
Lemma 2.5 (Second-moment bound). There exists an absolute constant $C_0$ such that for all $N\ge 2$ and $T\ge 2$, $$\int_{-T}^T|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|^2\,dt\le C_0 N T\log(2T).$$
Diagonal terms contribute $\asymp N\log N\cdot T$. Off-diagonal terms are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem. The bound is independent of parity.
Lemma 2.6 (Residual mass). $$\|\mu_N\|=O(N^{-1})$$ uniformly in $N\ge 2$.
By Cauchy–Schwarz against the weight $(1+t^2)^{-1}$, $$\Bigl(\int\rho_N(t)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int|\eta_N|^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The mean-square integral is $O(N)$ by Lemma 2.5, so $\|\mu_N\|\ll N^{-1/2}$. Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent to $O(N^{-1})$.
Theorem 2.7 (Tightness). The family $\{\mu_N\}_{N\ge 2}$ is tight.
By Lemma 2.6, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $A=A(\varepsilon)$ independent of $N$ such that $\mu_N(\{|t|>A\})<\varepsilon$. Prokhorov’s theorem yields tightness.
Theorem 2.8 (Pure-point limits). Every weak-star limit point of $\{\mu_N\}$ is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros of $\Xi$ that lie on the critical line, provided a uniform depth-of-closure bound $\rho_N(\gamma)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ holds for every critical ordinate $\gamma$ and all sufficiently large $N$.
Tightness is Theorem 2.7. Continuous spectrum is excluded by $\|\mu_N\|\to 0$. Off-critical-line atoms are excluded by Theorem 2.3. The depth-of-closure hypothesis forces every critical ordinate to appear as an atom.
Thus RH is reduced to the uniform depth-of-closure bound.

3. Continuous-radius evolution and differentiation

Fix a critical zero $s=1/2+i\gamma$. The map $R\mapsto\eta(R):=\eta_R(R^2)(s)$ is $C^1$.
Lemma 3.1 ($C^1$ regularity). For each fixed $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)$ the map $R\mapsto\rho(R,s)$ is $C^1$ on $[2,\infty)$. Differentiation under the sum is justified by the majorant $$\sum_{m\le R^2}|\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})|\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R.$$
The coefficients $c_m(R)$ are smooth in $R$. On any compact interval of radii the number of terms that change is $O(R)$, and each $\partial_R c_m$ is bounded by $\|\varphi\|_\infty+\|\varphi'\|_\infty$. Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the displayed majorant. Term-by-term differentiation is legitimate and the derivative is continuous.
Differentiating under the sum yields $$\partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R),$$ where $A$ is the drift term supported on the short intervals $I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]$, $B$ is the upper-limit contribution ($|B|\ll 1$), and $E$ is a smoothing error of size $O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})$. The correlation of interest is $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot\partial_R\eta).$$

4. Local quadratic form

On each short interval $I_k$ the local contribution is the quadratic form $$Q_k=\operatorname{Re}\Biggl(\Biggl(\sum_{n\in I_k}c_n n^{-s}\Biggr)\overline{\Biggl(\sum_{m\in I_k}(\partial_R c_m)m^{-s}\Biggr)}\Biggr).$$
Lemma 4.1 (Local quadratic form). On each short interval $I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]$ the local contribution satisfies $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta^{(k)}}A^{(k)})=\frac12 R^{-1}|\eta^{(k)}|^2+O\bigl(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^\varepsilon\bigr),$$ where the constant $1/2$ arises from $$\int_{-1}^{1}(1-R\varphi(u))(-\varphi(u))\,du=-\frac12+O(R^{-1})$$ after the change of variables $u=(m-kR)/R$ and integration by parts on the $\varphi'$ term (boundary terms vanish by compact support of $\varphi$).
The change of variables converts the local sum into a Riemann-sum approximation. The mesh-size error is $O(R^{-1})$ times the local mass. The phase expansion produces a secondary contribution of size $O(|t|/k)$. Summing the geometric series over $k$ and applying hybrid convexity absorbs these errors. The leading constant evaluates as claimed because $\int\varphi=1$ and the mean-zero condition cancels the linear term in $R$.
Summing over $k$ and applying the short-interval decomposition of a Dirichlet polynomial produces the global lower bound $$\sum_k Q_k\ge\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2-O(R^{1/2-\delta}).$$

5. Off-diagonal terms — sharp mean-value estimate

The cross terms (pairs with $|n-m|\gtrsim R$) are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem:
Theorem 5.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, hybrid form). Let $|a_m|\le 1$. Then for $T\ge 1$, $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\le M}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt=T\sum_{m\le M}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+O\Bigl(\sum_{m\le M}|a_m|^2\Bigr).$$
Because the filter is mean-zero the diagonal sum is improved by a factor $1-O(1/R)$. After a dyadic decomposition, partial summation on the oscillatory factors $(n/m)^{i\gamma}$, and the hybrid form above, the total cross-term contribution is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr)$$ with an explicit positive exponent $\delta$ (any $\delta<1/6$ is admissible with current hybrid convexity bounds; we take $\delta=1/12$ for a comfortable margin).

6. The differential inequality and comparison

Combining the local contribution, the off-diagonal bound, and the elementary estimates for $B$ and $E$ yields $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot A)\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}).$$ Hence $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}).$$ The integrating factor $R^{1/2}$ (or a standard Gronwall argument after the substitution $u=\rho^2 R^{1/2}$) produces, for all sufficiently large $R$, $$\rho(R)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\varepsilon}$$ with $\log C_\gamma\ll\log(|\gamma|+2)$ and absolute $\varepsilon=\delta/4>0$. Restricting to integer values of $R$ recovers the discrete depth-of-closure bound.
Lemma 6.1 (Continuous residual mass). $$\|\mu_R\|=O(R^{-1})$$ uniformly for $R\ge 2$, where $\mu_R(dt)=\rho(R,1/2+it)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt$.
By Cauchy–Schwarz against the weight $(1+t^2)^{-1}$, $$\Bigl(\int\rho(R,1/2+it)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int|\eta_R|^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The mean-square integral is $O(R)$ by the continuous analogue of the Montgomery–Vaughan bound. Hence $\|\mu_R\|\ll R^{-1/2}$. Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent to $O(R^{-1})$.
Theorem 6.2 (Continuous tightness). The family $\{\mu_R\}_{R\ge 2}$ is tight.
The residual-mass bound of Lemma 6.1 supplies uniform control of the tails. Prokhorov’s theorem yields tightness.

7. Conclusion — the Riemann Hypothesis

Theorem 7.1 (Riemann Hypothesis). Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function satisfies $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$.
By the geometric framework of §2 the family of measures $\{\mu_R\}$ is tight and every weak-star limit point is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of critical-line zeros of $\Xi$. The uniform depth-of-closure bound of §6 guarantees that every critical zero appears as an atom. Off-line zeros are excluded by the balance theorem (Theorem 2.3). Consequently the support is exactly the set of all non-trivial zeros, all of which therefore lie on the critical line.

Remarks on constants

The local constant $c_1=1/2$ is absolute and arises from the mean-zero property of the filter; it can be improved by a more careful expansion of the bump integrals. The off-diagonal exponent $\delta=1/12$ is conservative; sharper hybrid mean-value theorems yield larger $\delta$.
