## Statement

\(i\) The limit of the present construction is on the multiplicative side: \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)=P(s)L(s,\chi_0)\) with \(P(s)=1-N^{1-s}\) and \(\chi_0\) principal. Its non-vanishing in \(\sigma>1\) is precisely the Euler product for \(\zeta\). (ii) The Davenport–Heilbronn function is not: by Lemma C.4(ii) it is \(bL(s,\chi)+\overline{b}L(s,\overline\chi)\) with both coefficients non-zero, hence not a Dirichlet polynomial times a single \(L\)-function; accordingly it has zeros in \(\sigma>1\) [\[17\]](publication.html#ref-dh), besides the in-strip off-line zero of Lemma C.4(iii). (iii) Therefore adjoining to the framework the hypothesis \[
\textbf{(M)}\qquad\text{the limit function has no zeros in }\sigma>1
\] is exactly adjoining multiplicativity. By Theorem C.12 it confines the limit to \(P(s)L(s,\chi)\); by Lemma 2.2 the elementary factor has no zeros in \(S_\delta\); so inside \(S_\delta\) the zeros of the limit are precisely the non-trivial zeros of one Dirichlet \(L\)-function.
