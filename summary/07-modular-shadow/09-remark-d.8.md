## Statement

Three consequences and one non-consequence.

- *An exact invariant.* Theorem D.4 and Corollary D.6 replace an asymptotic closure statement by an identity in \(\mathbb{F}_p\), valid for every admissible \(p\) with no error term. The whole classical apparatus of Fermat-quotient congruences becomes available for the filter.
- *A second, independent separation.* The filter and the Davenport–Heilbronn twist are separated here by which residue class carries the coefficient weight — \(c^{(N)}\) is a constant plus a multiple of the indicator of the subgroup class, while \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) vanishes on exactly that class. This is unrelated to the separation in Addendum C, which is the presence or absence of an Euler product.
- *Relations transfer one way.* Stuffle relations among depth-one Euler sums, and the shuffle relations available in \(\mathcal{A}\) but not at finite truncation, yield congruences among the modular polygons of different weights through Theorem D.4.
- *No detection of the off-line zeros.* The modular objects live at positive integer weight \(k\); the zeros of Addendum C live at complex \(s\) and are properties of an analytic continuation that the finite sums \(\sum_{n<p}a_n n^{-k}\) do not possess. There is no transfer between them, and none is claimed. Lemma D.2.1(iii) says why no transfer can be manufactured: the shadow is an invertible relabelling of \((a_1,\dots,a_q)\), so it constrains the zeros of \(\sum_n a_nn^{-s}\) exactly as much as the coefficients do and not one bit more. The Davenport–Heilbronn coefficients do not exclude Spira’s zero, so neither does the shadow. Note also that the objects a zero-removal argument would have to compare — \(\xi\), a Hadamard product over a subset of its zeros, or their quotient — are not Dirichlet series with periodic coefficients, hence lie outside the domain of \(F_{\mathcal{A}}\) altogether. The observed vanishing set of \(F_{\mathcal{A}}[a^{\mathrm{DH}}]\) is statistically indistinguishable from coincidence (Theorem D.7(iii)), so it carries no arithmetic fingerprint of anything. Likewise, closed forms for \(\eta(k)\) at integer \(k\) are values of the analytic function and admit no reduction modulo \(p\); the correct analogue is the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture \(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{A}}\cong\mathcal{Z}/\zeta(2)\mathcal{Z}\), which is open.
