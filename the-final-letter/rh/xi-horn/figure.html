The Ξ horn

The completed zeta function as a two-sided surface: the integers drawn as nested shells, the functional equation as a mirror, and closure replaced by a positivity margin. Computed live, in double precision, with no external dependency.

Abstract. rh3 draws the Dirichlet eta numeral as a helix and cannot decide anything from it: the chain's remaining travel is always half the last gear's radius, so 1031 gears are needed to certify a closure. This page draws the completed function instead. Riemann's weight Φ is even, and its evenness is the functional equation, so the surface has two mirror-congruent sheets meeting at a throat; the whole object lives in |u| ≤ 1.5 and closure is decidable from the entire picture. Φ is a sum over the integers, so the horn is literally built out of them — shell 1 carries 99.78% of the throat, shell 2 carries 0.217%, shell 3 carries 1.8×10−9 — and switching the shells on peels them apart. On the mirror side those same shells annihilate to twenty-three digits, which is the theta functional equation being enforced. Finally the phase can be modded out entirely: the moments of the silhouette carry the whole question as one explicit inequality, and the readout shows it. Nothing here is new mathematics and nothing here is a step towards the hypothesis.

1 · The construction

Riemann's weight, a sum over the integers:

Φ(u) = ∑n≥1 φn(u),    φn(u) = (2π2n4e9u/2 − 3πn2e5u/2)·e−πn2e2u

Φ is even — that is θ(1/x) = √x·θ(x), the functional equation, already installed — positive, and double-exponentially small. The horn is the surface of revolution with depth u and radius Φ(u), drawn on a logarithmic radial axis so that thirty decades fit. Its value is

Ξ(t) = ξ(½ + it) = 2(S+ + S−),    S± = ∫0∞ Φ(u)·e±itu du

For real t the two are conjugate, so Ξ = 4 Re S+ is real and a zero is where the two dual endpoints are purely imaginary and annihilate. Letting t = tr + i ti tilts the two sheets by e∓tiu and breaks the mirror; |ti| < ½ is exactly the critical strip, and ti = 0 is the critical line. RH, here: the horn only closes when it is untilted.

2 · The mirror, measured

On u ≥ 0 the shells are hierarchical and never cancel. On u < 0 the same shells annihilate: at u = −1.5 they sum to 0.95 in absolute value and produce 6.5×10−24. The readout above prints that condition number ∑|φn| / |Φ| at the current extent.

What the cancellation is

It is not a numerical misfortune. The twenty-three-digit collapse on the mirror side is the theta functional equation being enforced — the integers conspiring to reproduce, exactly, the mirror of the smooth positive side. Its magnitude is the content, and it is also why one must never evaluate Φ at negative u: use Φ(|u|), and double precision is sufficient everywhere.

3 · Modding out the phase

Expand the transform instead of evaluating it. With γ(n) = 4∫0∞u2nΦ(u)du — the even moments of the silhouette — ξ(½+z) = ∑ γ(n)z2n/(2n)!, and t disappears entirely. The moments themselves are forced log-convex by Cauchy–Schwarz, so they carry no information; all the content is in the factorial division. Splitting the Turán ratio exactly:

Δ2log γ(n)  <  log[(2n+3)(2n+4) / ((2n+1)(2n+2))]   ≈ 2/n

— the horn's Cauchy–Schwarz defect against the factorial gain. Positivity holds when the ratio of the two is below 1. Computed live from Φ at page load:

What this is not

Nothing on this page is new. Ξ as the transform of Φ is 1859; the Turán inequalities for ξ are a theorem (Csordas–Norfolk–Varga, 1986); the Jensen / Laguerre–Pólya criterion is classical and the Hermite limit is 2019. This page computes and draws; it discovers nothing. The margin table is a finite number of d = 2 conditions, and RH needs all degrees and all n — the open part is uniformity in the degree, which no margin table can see.
