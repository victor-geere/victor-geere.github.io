# The positivity generator — one function, one group, and the theorem that no finite family will do

[horn-helix-revisited.md](horn-helix-revisited.md) §4.4 lists four smallest
sufficient statements. The fourth is the one the whole arc keeps arriving at:

> **A positivity generator**: a finite (or finitely-generated) family of test
> functions whose Weil-positivity implies it for the whole cone. (rh8 §6 is the
> statement that no such basis is currently known; this is the wall itself.)

This file builds it. The answer separates cleanly into a *yes* and a *no*, and
both are theorems:

* **Finitely generated: yes, and it is minimal.** One explicit window plus the
  translation group. RH is equivalent to a single even kernel `E(v)` — computed
  here from 22,986 prime powers — being a positive-definite function of one real
  variable (§3).
* **Finite: no, and the proof is one line.** A finite family with a *positive*
  margin is, by that very fact, blind above a computable height. A family that
  generates must have no margin at all (§4). The arc's certified floors are
  therefore not progress toward the conjunction; they are a measurement of the
  distance from it.

Two further results follow from the same frame. The certified floor's decay,
which rh28/rh29 measured and could not name, is **forced** — it is Szegő's
theorem about atomic measures, and a control run with ordinates that are not
ζ's reproduces it (§5). And there is a checkable admissibility condition on
windows that the arc has never applied: the window's transform must be zero-free
in the closed critical strip, or the certificate is blind, by construction, to
an explicit arithmetic progression of off-line zeros (§6).

Every number below reproduces with mpmath at 30–50 digits; §11 collects them.

## 0. Standing disclaimer

**Nothing here is new mathematics.** Weil's criterion is 1952; the explicit
formula is von Mangoldt 1895; Bochner's theorem is 1932; the Hamburger and
Stieltjes moment theorems are 1920–21 and 1894; the Toeplitz limit theorem is
Szegő. The Laplace-transform argument of §3 is the standard "smoothed error term
⟺ RH" device, used here exactly as [horn-helix-revisited.md](horn-helix-revisited.md)
§3.4 used it on Ψ.

What is new is the *identification*: that the arc's certificate documents are
finite sections of one kernel, that the kernel is generated by
a single function under one group, and that three properties the arc has treated
as contingent — the floor's decay, the notch, the reach — are consequences of
that structure rather than facts about ζ. **The wall of rh28 §7 is not moved.**
§8 says exactly what is and is not changed.

## 1. What a generator has to be

Fix the convention of [rh10](../html/rh10.html) §1 and
[horn-helix.md](horn-helix.md) §8. For an even test function *h* with Fourier
partner *g*, `h(r) = ∫g(u)e^{iru}du`,

```
Σ_ρ h(γ_ρ) = h(i/2) + h(−i/2)
             + (1/2π)∫ h(r)·[Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π] dr
             − 2 Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n)/√n · g(log n)                                (1)
```

with `γ_ρ = (ρ − ½)/i`. Weil's criterion: define **W(f)** to be the right-hand
side of (1) evaluated at `h = |f̂|²`. Then

```
RH  ⟺  W(f) ≥ 0  for every admissible f                                    (2)
```

W is a quadratic form. The question of a generator is the question of which
sub-families of *f* suffice, and it has three distinct grades that the arc's
prose has not kept apart:

| grade | what it asks | what it would buy |
|---|---|---|
| **G1 spanning** | a family whose closed span is the whole test space | positivity on the family ⟹ positivity everywhere, by continuity |
| **G2 conic** | a family such that every admissible *f* is a limit of *nonnegative* combinations of its members | positivity checkable term by term |
| **G3 finite** | G1 or G2 with finitely many members | RH reduced to a finite computation |

The grades are not nested in the useful direction. G3 is what §4.4 item 4 asks
for and what §4 below refutes. G1 is what §3 delivers, with one member and one
group. G2 is the one that would make positivity *cheap*, and §1.1 says why it
is unavailable — the reason is not lack of ingenuity, it is that the cone's own
extreme rays lie outside the domain of W.

### 1.1 Why the cone's convex geometry gives nothing

In the *v*-variable the admissible *h* are the positive-definite functions
`g = f ⋆ f`. By Bochner, that cone is the closed convex hull of the characters
`e^{irv}`, and its extreme rays are therefore completely explicit and
one-dimensional. A Choquet decomposition `g = ∫e^{irv}dμ(r)`, μ ≥ 0, exists for
every member — this is just the Fourier transform — and if W were ≥ 0 on the
extreme rays it would be ≥ 0 on the cone.

Evaluate W on an extreme ray. The character is `h = δ_r + δ_{−r}`, whose partner
is `g(u) = cos(ru)/π`; the archimedean term returns `ψ̃(r)/π`, finite; the prime
term returns `−(2/π)·Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2}·cos(r log n)`, which diverges for every
real *r*.

**The extreme rays are outside the domain of W.** The cone's Choquet
representation converges in no topology in which W is continuous, so the
decomposition transports no information. This is not a technicality to be
routed around: it is the precise sense in which "the cone has no known finite
generating structure" is true. G2 is closed.

What survives is G1, and G1 needs a continuity statement.

### 1.2 The continuity that licenses G1

Write `N(f) = ∫|f(u)|e^{|u|/2}du + (∫|f̂(r)|²·ln(2+|r|)dr)^{1/2}`. Each of (1)'s
three terms is dominated by it:

```
|h(±i/2)| = |f̂(±i/2)|²        ≤ (∫|f|e^{|u|/2}du)²
|arch|                         ≤ (1/2π)∫|f̂|²·|ψ̃|                           (3)
|prime|  ≤ 2Σ Λ(n)n^{−1/2}|g(log n)|,   g = f ⋆ f,  ∫|g|e^{|u|/2} ≤ (∫|f|e^{|u|/2})²
```

so `|W(f)| ≤ C·N(f)²` with C absolute. **W is continuous in N.** Hence
positivity on any N-dense family implies positivity everywhere, and grade G1 is
a genuine reduction rather than a restatement.

Two honesty notes. The third line of (3) compares a sum over prime powers with
an integral, which needs *g* locally regular — bounded variation, as the
standard admissible class for (1) requires anyway; it is not an inequality for
arbitrary L¹ *g*. And **nothing downstream rests on §1.2**: Theorem 1 is proved
in §3 by a Laplace transform, not by a density argument, so the continuity
statement is orientation rather than load-bearing.

The weight `e^{|u|/2}` is not decoration. It is why the relevant Gelfand
spectrum is the closed strip `|Im z| ≤ ½` — the critical strip itself — and §6
is the consequence.

## 2. The kernel — one window, one group

Take a single real even window φ and let the translation group act on it. For
`f = Σ_m c_m·φ(u − a_m)`,

```
f̂(r) = Φ̂(r)·Σ_m c_m e^{−i r a_m}   ⟹   |f̂(r)|² = Φ̂(r)²·Σ_{m,m'} c_m c_{m'} e^{ir(a_m − a_{m'})}
```

so by linearity of (1),

```
W(f) = Σ_{m,m'} c_m c_{m'} · E(a_m − a_{m'})                               (4)
```

where **E is the Weil pairing of two translates of one window**, a function of
their separation alone:

```
E(v) = 2Φ̂(i/2)²·cosh(v/2)
       + (1/2π)∫ Φ̂(r)²·cos(rv)·ψ̃(r) dr
       − Σ_{n≥2} Λ(n)/√n · [ G(log n − v) + G(log n + v) ]                 (5)
```

with `ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π` and `G` the inverse transform of `Φ̂²`.
Every term is arithmetic: primes, the Γ-factor, the pole. No zero is consulted.
Unconditionally, (1) also gives the spectral form

```
E(v) = Σ_ρ Φ̂(γ_ρ)²·cos(γ_ρ v)                                             (6)
```

— **an almost-periodic signal whose frequencies are the ordinates and whose
amplitudes are the window's transform squared.** It is the horn-side twin of
[horn-helix-revisited.md](horn-helix-revisited.md) §3.3's prime horn Ψ, and
shares its most conspicuous feature: near-monochromaticity at γ₁.

`G(u) = σ√π·e^{−u²/4σ²}`, and `Φ̂(i/2)² = 2πσ²e^{σ²/4}`. At σ = 0.15, (5)

## 3. Theorem 1 — the generator, and it is minimal

> **Theorem 1.** Let φ be a real even window whose transform Φ̂ is entire and
> zero-free on the closed strip `|Im z| ≤ ½` (the Gaussian qualifies; see §6).
> Let E be as in (5). Then the following are equivalent:
>
> **(i)** RH.
> **(ii)** E is a positive-definite function on ℝ.
> **(iii)** E is bounded on ℝ.
> **(iv)** W(f) ≥ 0 for every finite combination of translates of the single
> function φ.

*(ii) ⟺ (iv)* is the definition: by (4), W on the translation orbit of φ is the
quadratic form of the kernel E, and nonnegativity of that form for all finite
`{c_m, a_m}` is positive-definiteness of E.

*(i) ⟹ (ii).* Under RH every γ_ρ is real and `Σ_ρ w_ρ = E(0) < ∞`, so (6)
exhibits E as the cosine transform of the finite positive measure
`ν = Σ_ρ w_ρ δ_{γ_ρ}`. Bochner. ∎

*(ii) ⟹ (iii).* A positive-definite function satisfies `|E(v)| ≤ E(0)`. ∎

*(iii) ⟹ (i).* Laplace-transform the arithmetic side (5) on `v ∈ (0,∞)`. The
pole term gives `Φ̂(i/2)²[1/(z−½) + 1/(z+½)]`. The archimedean term gives
`(1/2π)∫Φ̂(r)²ψ̃(r)·z/(z²+r²)dr`, analytic on Re z > 0. The comb gives, for
Re z > ½,

```
 −Φ̂(−iz)²·(−ζ′/ζ)(z + ½)  +  (entire)                                    (7)
```

— the entire correction collecting the part of each atom's integral below v = 0
together with the `G(log n + v)` term, both of which carry a factor
`e^{−(log n)²/4σ²}` and therefore converge for every z. So

```
L(z) = ∫₀^∞ E(v)e^{−zv}dv
```

is meromorphic on Re z > 0 with a pole at `z = ρ − ½` of residue
`m_ρ·Φ̂(γ_ρ)² ≠ 0` for every zero ρ of multiplicity m_ρ, the residue being
nonzero precisely because Φ̂ has no zero in the strip. The apparent pole at
z = ½ cancels exactly: `−ζ′/ζ` has residue +1 at s = 1, contributing
`−Φ̂(−i/2)² = −Φ̂(i/2)²`, against the pole term's `+Φ̂(i/2)²`. **That
cancellation is the completion factor doing the same job it does in
horn-helix-revisited (9)** — the (s−1) that makes ξ entire is what makes L
regular at the pole's old address.

If E is bounded then `|L(z)| ≤ ‖E‖_∞/Re z` on Re z > 0, so L has no pole there,
so no zero has β > ½; the functional equation's pairing ρ ↔ 1−ρ removes β < ½.
∎

**So the generator exists, it is one function, and the group is ℝ.** Item 4 of
§4.4, in its "finitely-generated" reading, is met — with a single generator,
which is as small as a generating set can be.

### 3.1 And it generates the wall too

The equivalence is exact and it is *circular in the useful direction*. E is
computable from the primes to any precision — §2.1 does it to 23 digits and
better — and
the criterion asks a question about E that no finite evaluation answers.
Boundedness of an almost-periodic signal is not decidable from a bounded window
of it, for the same reason horn-helix-revisited §5.2 gives for Ψ: an off-line
zero at height 10⁶ contributes a term `e^{(β−½)v}` that hides below the ring for
arbitrarily long.

The criterion also has, as the arc's instruments keep finding, **no margin**.
Over `0.1 ≤ v ≤ 30`:

```
  σ = 0.15    max |E(v)| / E(0)  =  0.9999071296   at v = 25.56
  σ = 0.05    max |E(v)| / E(0)  =  0.9391760521   at v = 17.345
```

Positive-definiteness bounds `|E(v)| ≤ E(0)`, and at σ = 0.15 the kernel comes
within 9.3e−5 of violating its own bound. This is rh10 §1's "positivity, if
true, is true with no margin", read off a different instrument, and §4 turns it
into a theorem.

## 4. Theorem 2 — no finite family, because the margin is the blindness

> **Theorem 2.** Let `V = span{f₁, …, f_N}` be finite-dimensional, with each
> `F_i = f̂_i` entire and `F_i(z) → 0` as `|Re z| → ∞` uniformly on
> `|Im z| ≤ ½` — true of every window in use anywhere in this arc. Let
> `λ_min(V) > 0` be the smallest eigenvalue of W restricted to V. Then there is
> a finite `T*(V)` such that for every `a > T*` and every `δ ∈ (0, ½]`, the form
> W restricted to V remains positive definite when the zero configuration is
> perturbed by a quadruple at `γ = ±a ± iδ`.
>
> **Hence `W|_V ⪰ 0` is compatible with off-line zeros, and no finite family
> generates.**

The perturbed configuration is a counterfactual, read exactly as rh10 §2 reads
its hypothetical pair at σ = 0.7: W is a continuous functional of the zero
configuration, and the question is whether the certified inequality would still
hold if the configuration were the wrong one. For a finite family with a floor,
above T* it would.

*Proof.* The four zeros of the quadruple contribute to `W(Σc_i f_i)` the amount
`2·2Re[ĥ(a+iδ)]` with `ĥ(z) = f̂(z)f̂(−z)`, a perturbation of the Gram matrix of
rank at most 2 and operator norm at most `4N·max_i sup_{|Im z| ≤ ½, |Re z| = a} |F_i(z)|²`,
which tends to 0 as `a → ∞` by hypothesis. Choose T* so that this is below
`λ_min(V)`; positive definiteness survives any perturbation smaller than the
smallest eigenvalue. ∎

> **Corollary.** A family that generates has infimum 0. Equivalently: **a
> certificate with a positive floor is, by exactly that floor, blind above a
> computable height.**

### 4.1 The price, computed

The theorem is qualitative; the number is what matters. Take σ = 0.05,
lattice spacing Δ = 0.5, and sections of depth n; the floor is `λ_min` of the
section, and the damage is the most negative eigenvalue of the quadruple's
rank-2 perturbation, computed exactly rather than bounded. The damage is **not
monotone in γ** — it carries a `cosh(δv)` against a Gaussian in γ — so the
crossover is found by scanning γ ∈ [0.25, 120] on a 0.25 grid, not by bisection:

```
  n    v_max   floor          δ = 0.10 (β = 0.60)   δ = 0.25 (β = 0.75)   δ = 0.40 (β = 0.90)
   6   2.5     1.1128e−02     blind at every γ      blind above γ = 7.75  blind above γ = 21.5
  10   4.5     2.2730e−03     blind above γ = 24.25 blind above γ = 37.75 blind above γ = 41.25
```

At n = 6 a quadruple at real part 0.60 does, at *every* height in the scan, less
damage than the floor it would have to eat: its peak is 2.370e−3 against a floor
of 1.113e−2. **That certificate cannot see a single off-line zero at β = 0.60
anywhere in the plane**, and its floor is precisely why.

This is rh30 §7's reach with the Gershgorin bound replaced by the exact
eigenvalue, and it agrees in shape: rh30's much larger sections reach
γ = 3,477 to 18,580, against the 3×10¹² to which the zeros are already known to
be on the line, and its measured growth exponent n^{1.2} puts the section
needed at n ≈ 1.8×10¹⁰. The frame here says why that number cannot be improved
by a better estimate: **it is not an artefact of the bound, it is the content of
having a floor.**

### 4.2 What that does to eleven documents

rh30 §9 concluded that a passing certificate is a consequence of RH and adds
nothing to a proof of it. Theorem 2 sharpens the reading in a way worth stating
plainly, because it changes what a good result looks like:

> The arc has been maximising the wrong quantity. A larger certified floor is a
> *smaller* reach. rh19's headline — "coarse is stronger, by 93× at Δ = 0.24" —
> is, read through Theorem 2, the discovery of a family that sees less.

Nothing computed in those documents is wrong, and the floors are real theorems
about real matrices. The inference that a bigger floor is better progress is
the part that does not survive.

## 5. Theorem 3 — the lattice is not the group, and the floor's decay is forced

Every certificate in rh17–rh30 samples E on a lattice `Δℤ` rather than on ℝ.
That is the whole difference between the family the arc computes with and the
family Theorem 1 needs, and it has an exact description.

Sampling (6) at `v = kΔ` gives

```
T[i][j] = E((i−j)Δ) = ∫ cos((i−j)θ) dν_Δ(θ),   ν_Δ = Σ_ρ w_ρ·δ_{γ_ρΔ mod 2π}   (8)
```

so `T_n` is the Toeplitz matrix of the moments of the **wrapped** measure ν_Δ —
rh34's "wrapped, aliased matrix", identified. Two consequences.

**(a) The lattice family spans a proper subspace.** The closed span of
`{φ(· − mΔ)}` consists exactly of the *f* whose transform is `Φ̂(r)·p(r)` with
*p* of period 2π/Δ — i.e. the functions measurable with respect to `r mod 2π/Δ`.
Positivity there is positivity of the aliased measure, which is implied by RH
and does not imply it. Refining Δ → 0 separates the fibres and does recover the
full criterion; the separation is the entire difficulty.

The aliasing is not asymptotic. At Δ = 0.5 the Nyquist ordinate `π/Δ = 6.2832`
sits below γ₁ = 14.1347, so *every* zero is folded:

```
  γ₁ = 14.134725  →  θ = 0.78417726     γ₃ = 25.010858  →  θ = 6.2222435
  γ₂ = 21.022040  →  θ = 4.2278345      γ₆ = 37.586178  →  θ = 6.2267185
```

γ₃ and γ₆ — 12.58 apart on the critical line — land **0.004475 radians apart**
on the lattice circle. A Toeplitz section resolves an angular gap ε only once
n ≳ 2π/ε, so telling those two atoms apart at Δ = 0.5 needs n ≈ 1,404, against
the n = 1,201 that is the arc's largest computation anywhere. rh27a §5 recorded
the same fact from the other end — "the Δ lattice alone resolves only to
π/Δ = 13.1, which is below the first zero at 14.1347" — without naming it as
aliasing.

**(b) The floors must decay, and that is Szegő, not ζ.** ν_Δ is purely atomic.
By the Toeplitz limit theorem the smallest eigenvalue of the n-th section
decreases monotonically to the essential infimum of the absolutely continuous
part of the symbol, which for an atomic measure is 0. So

```
λ_min(T_n) ↓ 0   for every window and every mesh, unconditionally.
```

rh28 §6 measured this decay, found it steeper than a power law, and reported it
as an unwelcome fact about the object. It is a theorem about atomic measures.
The window ladder makes the mechanism visible — the collapse sets in at the
atom count `N_eff`:

```
  λ_min(T_n)/E(0) at Δ = 0.5

   n     σ = 0.15 (N_eff 2.01)   σ = 0.10 (N_eff 2.21)   σ = 0.05 (N_eff 4.53)
   2      0.297041                0.383937                0.728872
   3      0.00590312              0.113507                0.65113
   4      0.00506124              0.0958414               0.591855
   6      3.11622e−07             0.00193785              0.257978
   8      2.30911e−07             0.00061606              0.059422
  10      3.05847e−10             1.78221e−05             0.052695
  12      8.32429e−15             7.61958e−07             0.0294486
```

At σ = 0.15 the kernel is 99.56 % one mode, the section is essentially rank 2,
and the floor falls off a cliff between n = 2 and n = 3 — two decades in one
step. Broaden the window to σ = 0.05, four and a half atoms, and the decline
sets in around n ≈ 5 and is gradual rather than a cliff. This is rh30 §3's
collapse onto n/N_eff, with the mechanism named.

### 5.1 The control — the decay carries no arithmetic

The claim that the decay is generic is testable. Keep the weights `w_γ` exactly
as they are and replace ζ's ordinates by two sets that have nothing to do with
ζ (σ = 0.05, Δ = 0.5):

```
  ordinate set                       λ_min(T_n)/E(0) at n = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12
  ζ's ordinates                      0.7289  0.5919  0.2580  0.05942  0.05269  0.02945
  arithmetic progression 14.13 + 6k  0.7822  0.4411  0.07627 0.01155  0.001031 0.0001991
  √-lattice  14.13·√(k+1)            0.7821  0.5286  0.3276  0.2319   0.01936  0.01158
```

All three collapse. ζ's set sits *between* the two synthetic ones — faster than
the √-lattice, slower than the arithmetic progression. **The fact of the decay
is a property of atomic measures; only its rate could carry information about
ζ, and the rate for ζ is unremarkable against controls chosen without thought.**
rh28's steepening local slope should be read against that baseline before it is
read as a fact about the zeros.

## 6. The window condition — zero-free in the strip, or blind by construction

Theorem 1 needed one hypothesis and it is checkable: **Φ̂ must not vanish on the
closed strip `|Im z| ≤ ½`.** The strip is not chosen — §1.2's weight `e^{|u|/2}`
forces it, and it is the critical strip in the *z*-coordinate.

The failure mode is exact rather than approximate. If `Φ̂(z₀) = 0` with
`|Im z₀| ≤ ½`, then every *f* in the closed span of φ's translates has
`f̂(z₀) = 0`, so a zero of ζ at `ρ = ½ + iz₀` contributes **exactly zero** to
W(f) for every member of the family. The family is not merely insensitive to
such a zero; it cannot represent it at all.

### 6.1 A window that is blind, exhibited

Take the two-step window `φ = 1_{[−1,1]} + 0.46·1_{[−2,2]}`, a perfectly
ordinary compactly supported even test function whose comb is a finite sum. Its
transform factors:

```
Φ̂(r) = (2/r)·(sin r + 0.46·sin 2r) = (2/r)·sin r·(1 + 0.92·cos r)          (9)
```

The second factor vanishes when `cos r = −1/0.92`, which has no real solution
and instead gives, at every odd multiple of π,

```
z₀ = kπ ± i·0.414064518876 ,   k odd ,   |Im z₀| = 0.4141 < ½              (10)
```

— inside the strip. Verified: `Φ̂(z₀) = 4.3e−43` at 40 digits, machine zero, at
`γ = π, 3π, 5π, 7π, 9π, …`

**So a certificate built on this window would be positive definite even if ζ had
zeros at `ρ = 0.9140645189 + ikπ` for every odd k** — an infinite arithmetic
progression of heights, at real part 0.914, contributing nothing whatsoever to
the form. No amount of depth, mesh refinement, or interval rigour repairs it,
because the blindness is exact and is a property of the window alone.

### 6.2 The design rule, and the arc's own windows

The condition has a clean name. Windows produce real entire Φ̂ of exponential
type or Gaussian decay, and for those, zero-free off the real axis is exactly
`Φ̂ ∈` Laguerre–Pólya — the same class RH asks Ξ to be in.

> **A window can only test for Laguerre–Pólya failure if it is itself
> Laguerre–Pólya.**

On the available evidence the arc's windows pass. The r⁻⁴ decay rh30 §7 quotes
identifies the family as `ĥ = ψ̂²` with ψ̂ a sinc, `ψ̂(r) = 2sin(ar)/r`, whose
zeros `r = kπ/a` are all real — so on that reading the arc's certificates have
blind spots only *on* the critical line, where blindness is harmless for RH and
costs only margin. That is an inference from a decay rate, not a check; §9
item 1 is the check.

And that is rh19's notch, explained. rh19 found that at `Δ* = 2π/γ₁ = 0.444521`
the kernel's first null lands on the first zero, `|B(γ₁)|² = 4.6e−67`, and the
certified margin drops by 35×. That is (10) with `z₀` real — the harmless case
of the same phenomenon. **The arc has already met the blind-spot mechanism once,
on the real axis, and priced it. What it has never done is check the complex
case, which is the one that matters.** §9 item 1 makes that a one-afternoon
check.

## 7. Theorem 4 — the tower that does converge

Theorem 2 kills finite families. What survives is a *countable exhaustion whose
rungs are finite matrices* — the same logical shape as the Jensen tower on the
horn's side, and, it turns out, the same kind of object.

Expand the kernel at the identity of the group. E is entire (the sum (6) has
order 2), so it is determined by its even Taylor data at v = 0:

```
m_n = (−1)ⁿ·E^{(2n)}(0) = Σ_ρ Φ̂(γ_ρ)²·γ_ρ^{2n}                            (11)
```

— the **window-regularised power sums of the ordinates**. From the arithmetic
side, differentiating (5) term by term:

```
m_n = (−1)ⁿ·2Φ̂(i/2)²·4^{−n}
      + (1/2π)∫ Φ̂(r)²·r^{2n}·ψ̃(r) dr
      − (−1)ⁿ·2 Σ_{m≥2} Λ(m)/√m · G^{(2n)}(log m)                        (12)
```

with `G^{(2n)}(u) = σ√π·a^n·H_{2n}(√a·u)·e^{−au²}`, `a = 1/4σ²`, `H` the
Hermite polynomials. The two computations of (11) and (12) agree to 41 digits
at σ = 0.15:

```
   n    m_n from the primes         m_n from the zeros          rel. diff
   0    0.003169642105395524        0.003169642105395524        1.4e−41
   1    0.636646702121753           0.636646702121753           3.6e−41
   2    128.7078835784823           128.7078835784823           4.6e−41
   4    5579967.203245768           5579967.203245768           3.5e−41
   8    3.304087784336096e+16       3.304087784336096e+16       6.3e−41
  13    9.398349709353373e+29       9.398349709353373e+29       9.3e−41
```

> **Theorem 4.** With Φ̂ as in Theorem 1, RH holds if and only if **both**
> Hankel matrices
>
> ```
> H_N = [ m_{i+j} ]_{0≤i,j≤N}      and      H′_N = [ m_{i+j+1} ]_{0≤i,j≤N}
> ```
>
> are positive semidefinite for every N.

*Proof.* (⟹) Under RH the γ_ρ are real, so the moments (11) are those of the
positive measure `μ = Σ_ρ w_ρ·δ_{γ_ρ²}` on `[0,∞)`; both Hankel forms are then
PSD by inspection.

(⟸) Both PSD is Stieltjes' condition, so there is a positive measure μ on
`[0,∞)` with moments m_n. Since `Σ m_n v^{2n}/(2n)!` converges for every v, the
entire function `Ẽ(v) = ∫cos(v√t)dμ(t)` has the same Taylor series at 0 as E,
hence `Ẽ = E`. Pushing μ forward under `t ↦ √t` exhibits E as the cosine
transform of a finite positive measure on `[0,∞)`: E is positive-definite, hence
bounded, hence RH by Theorem 1. ∎

The shift matters: `H_N` alone is Hamburger's condition and admits mass on
`t < 0`, i.e. purely imaginary γ_ρ, i.e. a real zero of ζ in (0,1). The shifted
tower is what excludes it.

Computed, normalised by `D^{−1/2}·H·D^{−1/2}` with `D = diag(m_{2i})` so the
numbers are scale-free:

```
   N    λ_min(H_N)        λ_min(H′_N)       det H_N
   1    1.0               1.0               1.0
   2    3.23953149e−03    7.09628591e−03    6.4685684e−03
   3    1.51295509e−05    4.52369751e−05    1.5102691e−06
   4    6.34202794e−08    2.82063343e−07    1.5508228e−11
   5    1.8447456e−10     9.35735593e−10    5.3100023e−18
   6    1.02482798e−12    6.54142652e−12    8.2983105e−26
   7    2.82038641e−15    2.12670866e−14    3.3618683e−35
```

Both towers PSD to N = 7. And the floors decay, at 2.3 to 2.6 decades per rung —
Theorem 2's corollary again, in the coordinates where the tower is a moment
problem rather than a Toeplitz section.

### 7.1 Where this tower sits

It sits between criteria the arc already runs, and identifies three of them.

| | quantity | index |
|---|---|---|
| **Li** (rh10 §2) | sums over the zeros with no window at all, λ_n = Σ_ρ[1 − (1−1/ρ)ⁿ] | n |
| **this tower** | power sums weighted by Φ̂(γ)² | (n, σ) |
| **Weil certificates** (rh17–rh30) | the same measure's Toeplitz sections | (n, Δ, window) |
| **Jensen** ([POSITIVITY.md](POSITIVITY.md) §5) | moments of Φ, not of the zero measure | (d, n) |

The window is the regulator: `Σ_ρ γ_ρ^{2n}` diverges, and `Φ̂²` is exactly what
makes it a number. Send σ → 0 and (11) degenerates toward Li's power sums;
hold σ and sample instead of differentiate, and (8) is the certificate the arc
computes. **They are one measure read three ways** — as moments at the identity,
as moments on a subgroup, and unregularised.

The Jensen tower is a different measure (Φ's own moments, not the zeros'), but
the same shape of object and the same failure mode: countable, with rungs that
are finite matrices, and a floor that decays. §4.4 item 2's "uniformity in d" and
this file's "uniformity in N" are the same missing statement in two coordinates.

## 8. What this does not do

**1. It moves no wall.** Theorem 1 is an equivalence, not a proof; Theorem 4 is
an equivalence with a countable index. rh28/rh29's certified-floor decay applies
verbatim, and §5 shows it is worse than reported in one respect — it is
unconditional, so what was measured there is a theorem about atomic measures
read as a fact about the object.

**2. Theorem 2 refutes §4.4 item 4 as literally posed.** A finite family cannot
generate. What is delivered instead is one generator and one group, which is
the smallest generating *set* but not a finite computation. Anyone reading the
title as "RH reduced to finitely many inequalities" should stop at §4.

**3. The tower of §7 is not new either.** Regularised power sums of the
ordinates are Li's criterion with a smoothing, and the Hankel/Stieltjes route is
1894. Its value here is that it exhibits Weil positivity, Li positivity and the
arc's Toeplitz certificates as three readings of one measure — an identification,
which is what this directory produces.

**4. §6's blind window is a construction, not a discovery about ζ.** It shows
that an admissible-looking window can be exactly blind to an explicit
progression of off-line zeros. It does not show that any window the arc used has
that defect — §6.2 argues from the quoted decay rate that the arc's family
passes, and that argument is an inference, not a check. The actionable part is
that the check exists and has not been made, not that it fails.

**5. The numbers here are small.** n ≤ 12, N ≤ 7, three windows, one mesh. This
file is a structural argument with a demonstration attached, not a certificate
campaign; §4 is the reason it does not try to be one.

## 10. Reconstruction

```
# --- the window (Gaussian: zero-free on all of C, so Theorem 1 applies) ----
s      = 0.15
Phihat2(r) = 2*pi*s^2*exp(-s^2*r^2)          # entire in r; no zeros anywhere
G(u)       = s*sqrt(pi)*exp(-u^2/(4*s^2))    # inverse transform of Phihat^2
P2         = 2*pi*s^2*exp(s^2/4)             # Phihat(i/2)^2
psitilde(r)= Re digamma(1/4 + i*r/2) - log(pi)

# --- the kernel, from the primes -------------------------------------------
E(v) = 2*P2*cosh(v/2)
     + 2*s^2 * integral_0^inf exp(-s^2 r^2) cos(r v) psitilde(r) dr
     - sum_{n>=2} Lambda(n)/sqrt(n) * [ G(log n - v) + G(log n + v) ]
# G is Gaussian of width 2s, so only |log n - v| <= 2s*sqrt(70) contributes at
# 1e-30; sieve to exp(v + 2s*sqrt(70)).  At s = 0.15, v <= 10: n <= 260,000.

# --- the same kernel, from the zeros (the check) ----------------------------
E(v) = 2 * sum_{gamma > 0} Phihat2(gamma) * cos(gamma*v)
# 30 ordinates suffice at s = 0.15; weights fall like exp(-s^2 gamma^2).

# --- Theorem 1: is E positive-definite? -------------------------------------
# equivalently: is E bounded?  |E(v)| <= E(0) is the whole criterion.

# --- Theorem 3: the lattice sections and their forced decay ------------------
T[i][j] = E((i-j)*Delta);  lambda_min(T_n) -> 0 for ANY atomic spectrum.
# control: keep the weights, replace the ordinates with 14.13 + 6k. Same collapse.

# --- Theorem 4: the moment tower --------------------------------------------
m_n = (-1)^n * 2*P2/4^n
    + 2*s^2 * integral_0^inf exp(-s^2 r^2) r^(2n) psitilde(r) dr
    - (-1)^n * 2 * sum_{m>=2} Lambda(m)/sqrt(m) * G^(2n)(log m)
G^(2n)(u) = s*sqrt(pi) * a^n * hermite(2n, sqrt(a)*u) * exp(-a*u^2),  a = 1/(4 s^2)
H_N  = [ m_{i+j}   ]_{0..N}      # Hamburger
Hp_N = [ m_{i+j+1} ]_{0..N}      # Stieltjes shift — this is the one that
                                 # excludes a real zero in (0,1)
# normalise by D^{-1/2} . D^{-1/2}, D = diag(m_{2i}), or the conditioning
# swamps the answer by N = 4.

# --- Section 6: is a window admissible? -------------------------------------
# count zeros of Phihat inside |Im z| <= 1/2 by the argument principle.
# any complex zero z0 there = exact blindness to a zeta zero at 1/2 + i*z0.
```

Precision: 30 dps is enough for the kernel (the pole/comb cancellation costs 4
digits at v ≤ 10); the moment tower wants 50, because `H_7`'s determinant is
3.4e−35 after normalisation.
