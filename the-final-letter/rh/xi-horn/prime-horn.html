The prime horn

Take the prime helix’s gear measure — atoms at log n, weights Λ(n)·n−1/2 — and smear it with the horn’s own bump. The main term cancels itself, and what is left is a nearly monochromatic sinusoid at γ₁ = 14.134725…, computed from the primes alone. Live, in double precision, with no external dependency.

Abstract. figure.html draws Φ, the horn built from the integers. This page runs the same recipe on the primes: replace the weight n−1/2 by Λ(n)·n−1/2 and keep the bump. Three things then happen, all of them visible here. The main term is silent — the prime number theorem loads ev/2 of mass under the bump at depth v and it cancels to nothing, because the completion factor C(s) carries the very (s−1) Riemann inserted to kill ζ’s pole. What survives is a ring: a bounded almost-periodic signal whose frequencies are exactly the horn’s closure ordinates, 99.06 % of it carried by the first. And the near field decays exactly, at (3πv + b)e−5v/2 with b a closed form in ζ′(−2), ζ″(−2) and γE — the residue of a double pole, not a fit. By v = 9 the sieve reproduces the ten-zero prediction to eight decimals. Nothing here is new mathematics, nothing here is evidence for the hypothesis, and §4 says exactly why a picture like this cannot be.

1 · The construction

The horn’s shells are one bump translated to the gear positions and weighted by the critical taper (revisited (6)–(7)): φn(u) = n−1/2φ₁(u + log n), so Φ(u) = Σn≥1 n−1/2φ₁(u + log n). Run the same recipe with Λ in place of 1:

Ψ(u) = Σn≥2 Λ(n)·n−1/2·φ₁(u + log n),  φ₁(u) = (2π²e9u/2 − 3πe5u/2)·e−πe2u

It is violently asymmetric: on u > 0 every atom is attenuated through the throat (Ψ(+2) ≈ 1.9×10−292), so everything happens on u < 0. Write u = −v and watch as v grows. Two facts organise the picture, both classical, both computed live below.

The middle term is stated more sharply here than in §3.3, which only claimed consistency with a shape O(v e−5v/2). Both constants are residues, so both are closed forms: Γ(s/2) has a pole at s = −2 and ζ has its first trivial zero there, so the integrand has a double pole, and

a = 3π = 9.4247779608,  b = 3π·ζ″(−2)/(2ζ′(−2)) − (3π/2)(γE + 2/3 + log π) = −1.0781069034

The figure divides the measured residual by that curve. The quotient is 1.000000 at v = 9 — which is what makes the difference column of §3.3 a predicted quantity rather than a leftover.

4 · What this figure does not show

read this before believing the picture

It is not evidence. RH ⇔ sup|Ψ| < ∞ is a statement about all v. An off-line zero at height 10⁶ contributes e(β−½)v with an amplitude of order e−π·10⁶/4 — it would leave every pixel of this figure, and every digit of every table on it, untouched. A finite scan of an almost-periodic signal decides nothing, and this one is finite.

The agreement is consistency, not confirmation. The purple curve and the amber curve are two evaluations of one classical identity. Their agreement to 1.4×10−8 at v = 9 tests the arithmetic of this page, not the hypothesis.

The right edge is real. Past v = log N − 1.5 the sieve is missing the atoms that dominate the sum and the purple curve is simply wrong. It is drawn into the shaded band rather than clipped, so the failure is legible instead of hidden.

What the figure is for: it makes one sentence checkable by eye. The primes, smeared by the horn’s own bump, oscillate at the first zero’s ordinate, at an amplitude equal to the first shell’s transform at that closure — a frequency and a constant that the Λ-sieve has no visible access to, and that this directory’s other figure already computed for its own reasons. Everything in the picture is an object defined elsewhere in this arc; nothing in it is fitted.

Self-contained: no network, no dependency, no stored data. The Λ-sieve, the complex Γ (Lanczos, g = 7), the twenty ordinates and every number on the page are computed in the browser at load.
