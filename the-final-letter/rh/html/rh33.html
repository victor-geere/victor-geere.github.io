rh33 · the loss

rh32 ordered one analytic step: prove that an atomic measure with no wide empty arc dominates its own smoothed density, with a loss controlled by the gaps. This document runs it and the step fails — not for want of effort, but because the hypothesis is about the wrong thing. Two measures on the same atoms, whose emptiest arcs are equally full at every width a gap statistic can resolve, differ by a factor 1.49 in the constant. What does control the loss is the measure’s Toeplitz moments; and those, for the wrapped zeta ordinates, are the explicit formula. Verified here against the arc’s own certified rows to 8.4×10⁻¹⁶ and against 22,491 zero ordinates to 7.2×10⁻⁸ of the mass, they are a sum over prime powers.

1 · Where this stands

rh32 left three successors. (a) The quadrature lemma: “for mirrored atomic measures whose emptiness profile stays above depth q at all widths beyond K kernel units, prove the atom sum dominates a smoothed density on degree-n forms with loss a function of (q, K) alone.” (b) The texture inside the safe zone: find the two-parameter law, or show by construction that no low-dimensional statistic can. (c) No new certificates.

This document runs (a) and (b) with one engine, because they are the same question asked twice — does a low-dimensional gap statistic decide the loss? — and the answer is no, with a construction so simple it is embarrassing that rh32 proposed the hypothesis. Leave the atoms exactly where the crystal puts them and modulate their weights at a frequency just below the polynomial degree. Every arc wider than one kernel width averages the modulation away, so the emptiness profile hardly moves; the Toeplitz matrix resolves precisely that frequency, so the loss constant falls to ½. A thinned arc with the same profile floor keeps 0.817. And the ordering reverses outright: a measure whose emptiest arc holds 78% of its fair mass loses more than one whose emptiest arc holds 60%. No monotone function of the profile can bound the loss.

What replaces the hypothesis is better than what it was reaching for. The loss is a function of the measure’s Toeplitz moments — it has to be, since the matrix is built from nothing else — and for the wrapped ordinates those moments are not a model of anything. They are the arc’s certified row: pole plus archimedean integral minus the prime comb, the object rh17 built and every document since rh19 has bounded. §6 checks that both ways to the same digit. The consequence for the programme is stated in §7 and it is not comfortable: the quadrature step rh32 asked for was never an inequality waiting to be proved. It was an identity the arc had held since rh17, and the geometry of rh29–rh32 was a picture of the matrix rather than a route to its smallest eigenvalue.

2 · The loss constant

Fix a mirrored atomic measure μ — atoms at ±tj with weights wj — and a degree n. Its Toeplitz matrix is Tn[j][k] = E(j − k) with E(k) = ∑j wj eiktj summed over all atoms, and the number this document is about is

ρ(μ) = λmin(Tn) / mass = the largest c with ∑j wj |p(tj)|2 ≥ c · mass · ‖p‖2 for every trigonometric polynomial p of degree < n.

This is the one-sided Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund constant: the exact quantity rh32 §8 (a) proposed to bound below. It is 1 when the atoms integrate degree-n polynomials exactly, which is rh32 §2’s identity — on the mirrored crystal Tn = mass · I, and ρ = 1 to 9.9×10⁻¹⁵. Puncture the crystal and ρ is rh32’s death curve in new clothes: ρ = c(K)·Cn/mass, checked against rh32’s stored numbers at K = 1.5, 2.1 and 3.1 and equal to the last bit. Everything below is a measurement of ρ.

3 · The crystal is not fragile

Before attacking the lemma it is worth knowing whether the crystal's exactness is a knife edge. Jitter each atom independently by up to α of a spacing and measure ρ. The classical expectation is Kadec’s quarter — perturb a lattice by less than a quarter of its spacing and the exponentials stay a Riesz basis; beyond it, no guarantee.

Nothing happens at a quarter. ρ falls in a straight line from 1, reaching 0.978 at α = ¼ and 0.957 at α = 0.49, where neighbouring atoms are nearly on top of each other, and the fall is linear to 0.3% of the total loss across the whole range. Three seeds agree to 0.0021. A destroyed lattice costs 4.3% of the constant.

The slope of that line depends on the grid only through n/M — the inverse of the oversampling — once M is large enough: at n/M = 0.1 the (M = 600, n = 60) and (M = 1200, n = 120) grids agree to 0.6%, and at n/M = 0.2 the (600, 120) and (1200, 240) grids agree to 0.3%. The smaller grids sit below the limit — M = 150 is 35% under at the same n/M — so the drift with M is finite size and not a second variable. The collapsed curve is not a power: its local exponent slides from 1.86 between n/M = 0.05 and 0.1, through 1.67, to 1.22 between 0.2 and 0.4. The tidy guess (n/M)3/2 is an average over that range, not a law.

The reading is that oversampling is what protects the constant, and it protects it robustly: positions can be wrong by half a spacing and the loss is a few percent, provided there are enough atoms per kernel width. That is a good sign for a lemma with gap hypotheses — and §4 is where it stops being one.

4 · The refutation

Leave the atoms where they are. Modulate the weights:

wj = 1 + ε·cos((n − 1)·tj) on the crystal ⇒ ρ = 1 − ε/2, exactly.

Exactly: 0.550 at ε = 0.9 and 0.500 at ε = 1, to machine zero. The mechanism is transparent — the modulation is a single Fourier mode, it lands on the single Toeplitz moment E(n − 1), and ρ = 1 − max|E|/mass for a rank-two perturbation of the identity.

Now look at what the emptiness profile sees. Write q1 for the profile floor beyond one kernel width: the least share of its fair mass that any arc wider than 2π/n holds. This is exactly the quantity rh32 §8 (a) proposed to price the loss with. For the modulated crystal at ε = 0.9, q1 = 0.803 — because one period of the modulation fits inside one kernel width, so every arc the profile looks at averages it away.

Now the control — and it is exactly the construction rh32 §8 (b) asked for by name. Take the same crystal and thin the atoms in a half-kernel-width arc to half density. Its floor comes out at q1 = 0.803 as well, matching the modulated measure’s to 5.9×10⁻⁴ — three digits. At that identical floor the two losses differ by a factor 1.49: the thinned arc keeps ρ = 0.817, the modulation 0.550.

That pair alone answers (b): no function of the profile floor, monotone or not, can be the loss. And the ordering reverses too. The strongest pair in the constructed set is a modulated measure whose emptiest arc beyond one kernel width holds 78% of its fair mass losing more than a thinned one whose emptiest arc holds 60% — ρ = 0.500 against 0.704, a factor 1.41 the wrong way. That kills (a): no monotone f can satisfy ρ ≥ f(q1). Across the whole constructed set — thirty-two measures: weights modulated at three frequencies and six depths, one arc thinned at five widths and four densities, two jittered lattices — 37 of the 446 ordered pairs whose floors differ by at least 0.05 have the fuller measure losing more, and within the band 0.35 ≤ q1 ≤ 0.85 the losses spread over a factor 1.75.

rh32 §8 (b) called this the cheaper outcome — “build two measures with identical (K, depth) profiles and measurably different ratios” — and it is, but it generalises past the safe zone it was asked about: the profile is a statement about how empty the measure looks at kernel scale, and ρ is a statement about what a degree-n polynomial can do with it. A polynomial of degree n resolves frequency n − 1; an arc of width 2π/n does not. Any statistic built from arc masses is blind in exactly the band where the matrix is most sensitive.

5 · What the loss is made of

The honest replacement is immediate and almost tautological: Tn is built from the moments E(0)…E(n − 1) and nothing else, so ρ is a function of the moments. For the modulated crystal that function is one line — ρ = 1 − max|E|/mass, verified to machine zero — because only one moment is on.

It is worth saying plainly why this does not immediately give a theorem. The elementary bound in the moments is ρ ≥ 1 − 2∑k≥1|E(k)|/mass, and on the living material at mesh Δ = 0.12, n = 61 that sum is 1.87 — the bound reads ρ ≥ −2.7 and says nothing, while the measured ρ is 0.514. The moments are large and they cancel. Any true statement about the loss has to know their signs, not just their sizes. That is the real difficulty, and it was never the difficulty rh32 named.

6 · The moments are a prime sum

Here the two halves of the arc meet. For a window ĥ(r) = Ûc(r)·A(r) with autocorrelation Ch = the inverse transform of ĥ2, the explicit formula gives, for every v,

E(v) = 2·ĥ(i/2)2·cosh(v/2) + arch(v) − ∑m≥2 Λ(m)·m−1/2 [Ch(ln m − v) + Ch(ln m + v)]

and the left-hand side at v = kΔ is precisely the Toeplitz moment of the wrapped-ordinate measure, E(kΔ) = 2∑γ ĥ(γ)2 cos(kΔγ). Two checks, from opposite directions:

  • Built from the primes, the row reproduces rh17’s certified row (Δ = 0.06, M = 1, n = 199) to 8.4×10⁻¹⁶ — so the assembly here is the arc’s own, not a re-derivation that might differ by a convention.
  • Built from the zeros — all 22,491 ordinates below 20000 — it agrees with the prime-side row to 7.2×10⁻⁸ of the mass at mesh 0.12, 1.4×10⁻⁷ at 0.06 and 5.7×10⁻⁹ at 0.24, the coarsest support the arc has certified. The loss constants agree to six digits.

So the wrapped measure of rh29–rh32 is not a model of the certified row. It is the certified row, evaluated on the other side of the explicit formula. And the arithmetic is visible in the moments: the six largest at mesh 0.12 each sit within 0.0109 in log of a prime power, on a grid whose spacing is 0.12 — k = 20 ↔ 11, 28 ↔ 29, 30 ↔ 37, 34 ↔ 59, 39 ↔ 107, 53 ↔ 577. The largest of all, |E|/mass = 0.146, sits at k = 20, where e2.4 = 11.02.

The tempting next sentence — so a handful of primes decides the certificate — is false, and the right panel is the disproof. Switch the comb off entirely and the row still has ρ = 0.762, the archimedean part alone. The twenty heaviest prime powers carry it past the truth, down to 0.458. The next sixty bring it back to 0.524. The loss is a cancellation across the whole comb, in the same way §5 found the moments cancelling — which is the same phenomenon seen twice, since the moments are the comb.

7 · Where this leaves the conjunction

rh32 ended with a two-parameter conjecture and one analytic gap, and called the gap “smaller and sharper than anything previously called the missing lemma”. Both parts need correcting.

The gap as stated does not exist, in two different senses. Its hypothesis class is wrong (§4): gap statistics are blind at the frequency that matters. And its conclusion was never needed (§6): the quantity it wanted to bound below by a domination inequality is equal, by the explicit formula, to the row the arc has been computing since rh17. There is no quadrature step between the atoms and the primes. There is an identity, and it has been in the code for fourteen documents.

What is actually left is what was left before rh29: a lower bound for λmin of the Toeplitz matrix of the prime comb, for a support Δ and a degree n beyond the certified nine. rh29–rh32 did not move that, and this document’s honest verdict on its own three predecessors is that they measured, explained and mapped an object they could not reach — the void law of rh32 is true, sharp and unconditional, and it prices the loss for measures the zeros never build. The programme’s own diagnosis from rh30 stands and gets sharper: as a route to RH this arc is a certified-computation programme, and the only thing that extends it is more certified computation.

One thing is genuinely new, and it is small but real. The arc now knows where in the row the difficulty lives. §6’s moment spectrum says the certificate at (Δ, n) is decided by prime powers m with |ln m − kΔ| < 2Δ, k < n — the comb sampled at the grid points — and §5 says what is needed is control of their signed sum, not their size. That is a Chebyshev-type question about prime powers in short intervals of the log scale, weighted by a fixed even window. It is not a new tool and this document does not have it. But it is the first time the arc’s floor question has been reduced to a statement with no zeros in it at all.

8 · What this document leaves

(a) the signed comb
§5’s obstruction, stated as a target: for the arc’s windows, bound λmin of the Toeplitz matrix whose row is the signed prime comb, using the cancellation and not the triangle inequality. The elementary bound is off by a factor of five at Δ = 0.12 and the certified computation is exact; between them is everything. Concretely: is there a Fejér- or Riesz-type kernel identity that makes the comb row’s quadratic form a sum of squares plus a small remainder? The arc has a positive-definite generator (rh25’s Uc) and never asked this question of the row.
(b) the moment hypothesis, if anyone still wants a lemma
§4 kills gap hypotheses but not all hypotheses. The honest replacement to try is a bound on ρ in terms of the measure’s normalised moments at frequencies near n, since that is where §4’s counterexample lives and where §3’s jitter law says the damage accumulates. The failure branch, again named in advance: a family with uniformly small individual moments and ρ → 0, which would show that even the moments have to be read jointly.
(c) the certified set stays closed
Standing order, renewed a fourth time — and after §7 it should be read differently. It is no longer idling until a lemma arrives; on this document’s reading no lemma is coming, and the only way the certified set grows is by paying for it. The next document that moves the conjunction will be one that certifies a point, not one that explains one.
