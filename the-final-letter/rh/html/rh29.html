rh29 · the symbol is a measure

rh28 ruled out an L1 symbol and read that as closing a route. It closes half of one. Under RH the arc’s Toeplitz row is the moment sequence of an explicit atomic measure on the circle — atoms at the zeros’ ordinates, weights the window’s own transform — and once that is written down, rh28’s three open items are one calculation each and two of the answers are not the ones it expected.

60 named checks, 264 s · four computed figures · zero table 22,491 ordinates to T = 20,000 · the twenty-eighth in the series, after rh28

What is conditional and what is not. §2 is a two-sided check: the same row, computed from 4.9 million prime powers and from 22,491 zero ordinates, with nothing assumed. Everything after it uses the zero side, and the zero side is RH — it assumes every ordinate is real. So §§3–7 say what RH predicts about the arc’s objects, not what the arc can certify. That is worth having: it tells you the shape the certificates are chasing. It is not a certificate, and it is not written as one.

1 · Where this stands

rh28 finished with three items and a recommendation. The recommendation was that (b), the depth curve, should come next, being “the cheapest thing in the arc that bears directly on the conjunction”; it costed the job as a prime stream to e24, about eleven times rh27c’s gear count, for a third of a decade more depth. Both figures are wrong — π(e24) against π(e18.25) is about 240, and v from 18 to 24 is an eighth of a decade, not a third — and the whole expense is avoidable, because there is a second way to compute the row and nobody in the arc had used it.

The way in is rh28’s own §5. It observed that the pole term and the comb each diverge and only their difference is bounded, concluded there is no L1 symbol, and stopped. But a Toeplitz form has a symbol whenever its row is a moment sequence, and the symbol need not be a function. Herglotz gives a measure; rh18’s equivalence chain already had an arrow called Bochner, which is exactly the assertion that such a measure exists and is positive. What rh18 could not do was write it down. Under RH one can.

2 · The explicit formula, read from right to left

The arc builds Eh(v) = pole + arch − comb and treats the left-hand side of the explicit formula as the thing being computed. Read the same identity the other way. For the test function Hv(r) = ĥ(r)2 cos(rv), the pole term is Hv(i/2) + Hv(−i/2), the archimedean term is (1/π)∫₀∞ ĥ2ψ̃cos, and the prime term is the comb. So

Eh(v) = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)2 cos(γρ v)  =  2 ∑γ > 0 ĥ(γ)2 cos(γ v)   under RH.

The sum converges absolutely: the envelope of ĥ(r)2 falls like r−4 — only the envelope, as §3(0) has to correct — and the zeros have density ln(r/2π)/2π. Nothing here is new mathematics — it is the explicit formula — but the arc had never evaluated the right-hand side, and doing so gives a second, independent route to every number in twenty-seven documents.

The zero table

rh11 left an audited table of 1,517 ordinates to T = 2,000 by Euler–Maclaurin. That is not enough: the tail ∑γ>Tĥ(γ)2 is 1.6×10−3 of the diagonal there. The table is extended to T = 20,000 by Riemann–Siegel with three correction terms, giving 22,491 ordinates in all. Ψ(p) = cos(2π(p2−p−1/16))/cos(2πp) is entire — every zero of the denominator is cancelled, since (2k+1)2−5 ≡ 4 (mod 8) — so C0, C1, C2 all come from Cauchy’s integral on a circle of radius ½, which is stable both at p = ¼ where the quotient is 0/0 and at sixth order where finite differences are not.

It is checked twice before use. Against Euler–Maclaurin Z(t) the remainder falls by 1.62 and 1.82 powers of ten per decade of t, against the predicted 7/4 — measured as the worst case in each band, not as an endpoint secant. And it relocates all 1,517 of rh11’s ordinates, with a drift that inherits the same t−7/4: 9.78×10⁻⁵ at γ₁ = 14.13, where Riemann–Siegel is at its worst and where this table takes rh11’s value anyway, and 2.83×10⁻⁸ at the handover at t = 2,000, which is what matters. The count comes to 22,491 against a Riemann–von Mangoldt main term of 22,491.41, so S(20000) = -0.41; the eight zeros the first scan stepped over were found by refining the blocks the count said were short.

And the residue is a shared truncation

The two sides agree to 1.38×10⁻⁷ absolute at Δ′ = 0.48 on a row whose diagonal is 0.707, and to 3.37×10⁻⁵ at the worst of the five supports. What is more interesting is where they agree best. The prime side stops its archimedean integral at r = 4800, which rh27a certified; the omitted piece is (1/π)∫4800∞ĥ2ψ̃cos. That is the same quantity as ∑γ>4800ĥ(γ)2cos, because the smooth zero density is ψ̃/2π. Cut the zero table in the same place and the two truncations cancel. All five curves in figure 1 bottom out at 4800, and past it the residue stops falling and settles on the arch tail — a factor of 1.12 over the last doubling of T.

Which turns a piece of rh17’s bookkeeping into a measurement. L1 lets the arc stop the integral early for free, because ψ̃ > 0 past r₊ = 6.29 makes the discarded tail a positive form: dropping it can only lower the floor. Restoring it from the zeros raises λmin at all five supports and lowers it at none — by 0.0098% at the widest and 1.88% at the narrowest. The arc’s certificates are conservative, and that is by how much.

3 · The measure, and three things that follow

Since Eh(kΔ′) = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)2 cos(k γρ Δ′), the row is exactly the Fourier coefficient sequence of

μ = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)2 δγρΔ′ mod 2π   on the circle,   Tn[j][k] = μ̂(j−k).

The window sets the weights; the mesh sets the positions. That separation is the whole content of §§4–6: two knobs the arc has always turned together can now be turned one at a time, with no new sieve and no compatible lattice.

(0) The window is not a low-pass filter. A digression, but it corrects something three documents have assumed. ĥ(r)2 = Ûc(r)2A(r)2, and only the envelope Ûc2 falls like r−4; A is the coefficient polynomial and is periodic in r with period 2π/δ = 837.8. rh25’s search drove A almost to zero at small r — the pole term 2ĥ(i/2)2 is 5.8×10⁻⁴ against a diagonal of 0.300 — so the atom at γ₁ is 10.8 times lighter than the heaviest of the first 300, at γ = 364.74, and carries 0.10% of the total mass. Any argument in this arc that treated γ₁ = 14.1347 as where the mass sits — §6 was about to be one — is arguing about 0.10% of the measure.

(i) Bochner, with the arrow filled in. rh18 proved lattice ⇔ windows ⇔ Bochner ⇔ RH and could name the measure only as “the one Herglotz gives.” It is this one. The total mass is the diagonal: μ(xgeneral;) = 2∑ĥ(γ)2 = Eh(0), checked at all five supports.

(ii) The row cannot leave its diagonal, and this is a theorem. |Eh(v)| = |∫e−ivrdμ| ≤ μ(xgeneral;) = Eh(0), for every v, at every support, at every mesh. rh28 measured this at 151 nodes and called it the cancellation, noting that the pole term alone climbs by cosh 9 = 4,052 across the same range while the row does not grow at all. Under RH there is nothing to cancel: the row is a Fourier coefficient of a fixed finite measure and has nowhere to go. Measured, the worst case over the five supports is 0.442 of the diagonal, at Δ′ = 0.48; at 0.12 it is 0.155. Every node the arc has ever computed was a test of RH that passed, and §7 works out what a failure would have to look like.

(iii) λmin → 0 is required by RH. μ is purely atomic: countably many points, no absolutely continuous part at all. So the geometric mean exp(∫ln w) of its a.c. density is zero, and by Szegő’s infimum theorem the n-step prediction error — which dominates λmin(Tn) — falls to zero. rh28 read its own depth curve as bad news and wrote that the arc was “in a worse position than it believed an hour ago.” It was not. A decay to zero is what RH predicts; a floor that held uniformly in the depth would have been the alarming observation. What is open is the rate, and only the rate.

4 · (b) The depth curve, past one decade

From the zeros a row of length n costs O(#zeros) per node whatever the depth, so the curve costs a few seconds per support rather than a sieve. What it does not do is extend forever: a truncated measure is a measure with fewer atoms, so its λmin is a lower bound on the true one, and past some n the missing tail dominates. That is measured rather than assumed. At n = 601 the last doubling of T moves λmin by 0.38%; at n = 1201 it moves it by 6.7%, so the curves stop at 601.

Δ′ = 0.48   v = 1.44 to 288   λmin = 4.98×10⁻¹ → 2.21×10⁻⁵   secant 1.89
Δ′ = 0.24   v = 1.2 to 144   λmin = 3.72×10⁻¹ → 3.79×10⁻⁵   secant 1.92
Δ′ = 0.12   v = 1.44 to 72   λmin = 2.81×10⁻¹ → 4.87×10⁻⁴   secant 1.62
Δ′ = 0.06   v = 1.08 to 36   λmin = 1.70×10⁻¹ → 4.92×10⁻³   secant 1.01
Δ′ = 0.03   v = 1.14 to 18   λmin = 8.74×10⁻² → 2.89×10⁻²   secant 0.40

Over rh28’s range the curve reproduces what rh28 saw: at Δ′ = 0.12 the local slope climbs 0.04, 0.41, 0.55, 0.48, 0.51, 0.68, 0.72. rh28 asked whether the steepening tends to a limit. On this evidence it does not settle into anything: past v = 18 the four successive slopes are 2.61, 7.14, 1.22, 3.92, a factor of 5.8 between adjacent steps. λmin of a Toeplitz section is not a smooth function of the section length and there is no reason it should be.

What is clear is the size. Over v ∈ [18, 72] the mean slope at Δ′ = 0.12 is 3.73, against the 0.41 endpoint secant rh28 published for [1, 18]. rh28 called that extrapolation optimistic; it was optimistic by a factor of 9 in the exponent. The end-to-end secants over the extended ranges are 1.89, 1.92, 1.62, 1.01, 0.40 at vmax = 288, 144, 72, 36, 18 — still under the last local slope at every support, so the secant is still not the slope and quoting one for the other is still wrong.

None of this threatens positivity. Under RH every one of these numbers is positive at every n, and by §3(iii) they must also tend to zero. The curve is the rate of an approach that has to happen.

5 · (c) A better test vector — where rh28 was right, and where it wasn’t

rh28’s Fejér ceiling is exactly ∫Kn dμ with Kn the Fejér kernel, which makes its behaviour legible: for a measure whose heavy atoms are eventually resolved it is C/n. Measured over a factor of sixteen in n, n·minθFn sits at 6.6 to within 11%. So the ceiling’s slope tends to 1 while λmin’s own passes 3, and the plane wave stops tracking the eigenvalue at almost exactly the depth rh28 stopped measuring: the gap is 1.12 at v = 18 and 23.4 at v = 72.

rh28 proposed a Hann-tapered plane wave to close the last 7% of the gap it saw at v = 18. For that purpose it fails. At v = 18 the Hann taper is 1.37 times λmin against the rectangle’s 1.12, and the best of six tapers is a wide Gaussian at 1.11, which is nothing. At v = 18 the plane wave is already almost optimal and there is no 7% to win.

But the idea is right in the place rh28 did not look.

n = 151   v = 18   λmin = 8.55×10⁻²  —  rectangle 1.12   Hann 1.37   Hann² 1.61   half-cosine 1.18   Gaussian, 1.11   Gaussian, 1.60   nulls 2.9
n = 301   v = 36   λmin = 2.90×10⁻³  —  rectangle 7.92   Hann 16.95   Hann² 28.94   half-cosine 7.48   Gaussian, 4.02   Gaussian, 28.43   nulls 35.8
n = 601   v = 72   λmin = 4.87×10⁻⁴  —  rectangle 23.39   Hann 5.61   Hann² 12.03   half-cosine 2.99   Gaussian, 4.80   Gaussian, 14.88   nulls 65.0

At v = 72 the rectangle is 23.4 times λmin and a half-cosine taper is 2.99, closing 91% of the gap the plane wave opens. The taper matters exactly where rh28 assumed it would not.

The vector that ought to be ideal is the one that fails. A polynomial that vanishes at the m heaviest atoms should annihilate the mass that matters; at v = 72, with m = 294 nulls, it is 65 times λmin — worse than the plane wave by a factor of three. Forcing the nulls costs a coefficient vector whose norm is all in the corners, and the Rayleigh quotient pays for it. Which locates the real obstruction: the ceiling is not limited by the test vector’s sidelobes, which is what a taper fixes, but by the norm it costs to be small on the atoms. That is a Diophantine statement about {γΔ′ mod 2π} and not an engineering one.

6 · (a) The saturation, third attempt

rh27c found λmin/Eh(0) climbing across its five supports and then flattening, and could not say why. rh27c ruled out the archimedean ceiling. rh28 ruled out the section length and guessed the mesh, observing that the arc had never varied mesh and support separately because it always took mesh = support. In the measure picture that separation is free.

The guess was that the lattice’s Nyquist frequency π/Δ′ crossing γ₁ = 14.1347 was the event, since that happens at Δ′ = 0.22226 and rh27c’s supports straddle it. By §3(0) the guess was doomed before it was tested — the window puts 0.10% of its mass at γ₁, so resolving that atom or not can hardly matter — and it is worth recording that the reason was visible in the measure and invisible without it. Tested anyway, nothing happens there. With the window fixed at the Δ′ = 0.12 optimum and the mesh swept, the ratio is 0.335 just below the crossing, 0.364 at it and 0.415 just above; the step across it is 8.5%, the quietest of the six steps in that region, which run to 50%. If anything happened there it would be the loudest.

Worse for the guess, the mesh moves the quantity the wrong way. Held at a fixed window and a fixed depth, λmin/Eh(0) falls by a factor of 228 as the mesh refines from 0.6 to 0.03 — where rh27c’s curve rises. Swap the window instead, at fixed mesh and depth, and it rises by a factor of 382. Neither knob saturates; each is large and monotone over two decades, and they point in opposite directions.

So the flat stretch is not a property of either knob. Nor is it two independent effects cancelling: predicting the diagonal as (window factor) × (mesh factor) is wrong by 65 at the narrowest support. It is a coupling between the two, and this document does not name it either. Three explanations tested, three dead. What is new is the constraint on the fourth: any account of the flat stretch has to be about the interaction, because neither ingredient does anything of the kind on its own — and rh28’s post-hoc γ₁ correspondence, recorded there as untested, is not it.

7 · What an off-line zero would do

ĥ is entire, so the identity in §2 continues off the real axis. A zero at ½ ± σ ± iγ contributes to Eh(v) the quantity 4 Re[ĥ(γ+iσ)2 cos((γ+iσ)v)], whose size grows like eσv. That is the 4 Re[ĝ(γ+iσ)2] route rh27a named and four documents have carried untried, arrived at from the other side and with the growth rate attached: a quadruple at γ₁ with σ = 0.5 is 3,917 times larger at v = 18 than at v = 0.

So the bound of §3(ii) is a test with teeth in principle. A single off-line quadruple at γ₁ would have pushed |Eh| past Eh(0) somewhere in v ≤ 18 if its σ exceeded 0.3854. It did not; the test passed at all 151 nodes. And by §3(0) γ₁ is not the best place to point it: sweeping the first sixty ordinates, the sharpest test is at γ = 107.17, which catches a quadruple at σ = 0.3258. That is 16% better, and 16% off a useless region is a useless region.

It is also a very weak test, and the weakness is structural. What has to beat the diagonal is eσv, so the excluded σ falls only like 1/V:

V = 18: σ > 0.3854  •  V = 36: σ > 0.1928  •  V = 72: σ > 0.0966  •  V = 144: σ > 0.0486  •  V = 288: σ > 0.0242  •  V = 576: σ > 0.0121

σ = 0.385 at γ ≈ 14 is a region ruled out far more cheaply by counting zeros, and reaching σ = 0.01 by this route would need a certified row to depth 576, which is thirty-two times rh19’s horizon and costs e576 in primes. And the bound above assumes there is one quadruple. Several at different γ can cancel one another at every node measured, and ruling that out needs a zero-density input the arc does not have. That is the obstruction, stated precisely for the first time: not “no bridge from forms to zeros” — the bridge is the identity in §2 — but no control on how many off-line zeros could conspire.

8 · The conjunction, and the bill

Where the conjunction stands. Nine certified points, still measure zero, and this document adds none: everything past §2 assumes RH. What it changes is the picture the certificates sit in. The Toeplitz row is the moment sequence of an explicit atomic measure; the boundedness rh28 measured is a theorem; the decay rh28 found alarming is required; the depth curve is a rate of a required approach rather than a threat. That is worth more than another rung, and it is still not a step towards a proof.

What rh28 got wrong, listed. Its costing of (b) was wrong by a factor of twenty in the gear count and by three in the decades bought, and the work was unnecessary. Its guess for (a) was wrong in the place and in the sign. Its proposal for (c) fails at the depth it proposed it for and succeeds at a depth it did not consider. Its γ₁ correspondence is dead. Its reading of its own depth curve as bad news was the reverse of the truth. What it got right is the thing it was least sure of: the endpoint secant really does understate the decay, by more than it feared.

The bill. 60 named checks in 264 seconds. One new artefact, the zero table, validated against Euler–Maclaurin pointwise and against rh11’s 1,517 audited ordinates individually. No new sieve; rh27b and rh27c’s streams and rh27a’s ψ̃ table reused unchanged; the five prime-side rows re-checked against the λmin their own documents published before anything else ran.

What is left. Three, and for once they are not variations on a rung.

(a) The lower bound. §5 located the obstruction: a ceiling is limited by the norm it costs a polynomial to be small on {γΔ′ mod 2π}. The matching question — how small can ∫|p|2dμ be for a normalised p of degree n — is a Diophantine question about the ordinates modulo 2π/Δ′, and equidistribution of {γα} is known under RH. Whether that gives a usable lower bound on λmin is the first question in this arc that is analysis rather than computation and is not obviously hopeless.

(b) The coupling. §6 leaves the saturation as an interaction between window and mesh with a separable model off by 65. Two knobs and one flat curve is a two-dimensional sweep, and it is now cheap: a grid of (window, mesh) rather than the three lines drawn here would either exhibit a ridge or show there is none.

(c) The conspiracy bound. §7’s test is conditional on a single off-line quadruple, which is not a hypothesis anyone can use. What would make it unconditional is a bound on ∑off-line|ĥ(γ+iσ)|2 over a height range — a zero-density estimate, weighted by the arc’s own window. That is a known kind of object and the arc has never gone looking for one.

9 · Dictionary additions and references

the symbol measure
μ = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)2 δ at γρΔ′ mod 2π. Under RH the arc’s Toeplitz matrix is its moment matrix. Window → weights, mesh → positions.
the matched cut
r = 4800, where the prime side stops its archimedean integral and where the zero table must be cut for the two truncations to cancel. The residue between the two routes is smallest there at all five supports, 1.38×10⁻⁷ at Δ′ = 0.48.
the certificate slack
what rh17’s L1 discards: a positive form, so the floor it produces is conservative. Measured for the first time — restoring it raises λmin by 0.0098% at Δ′ = 0.48 and 1.88% at 0.03, and lowers it nowhere.
Szegő’s infimum
infn λmin(Tn) ≤ the geometric mean of the symbol’s a.c. part, which for a purely atomic measure is zero. Hence RH ⇒ λmin → 0, and the depth curve is a rate, not a warning.
the 1/n ceiling
minθFn = ∫Kndμ, which is C/n once the heavy atoms are resolved: n·min Fn = 6.6 over a factor of sixteen in n. The plane wave’s ceiling and the eigenvalue part company at v ≈ 18.
the conspiracy gap
why §7 is conditional: several off-line quadruples at different γ can cancel one another at every node measured. Closing it needs a zero-density bound, not a longer row.
