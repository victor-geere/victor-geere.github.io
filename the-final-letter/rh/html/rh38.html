1 · Where this stands

The construction has one geometric object and every mesh the arc has used is a sampling choice laid on top of it. Two documents ago that object acquired two constraints: the a-helix has completed, where it passes through a gear, the same number of rotations about the cone as that gear has; and the count must be cumulative in a way no small increment can break. rh37 §2 encoded both and drew a conclusion from them — that rh3 §6’s opposed sign is thereby excluded.

It is not. rh3 §6 now keeps the opposed sign and satisfies both constraints, by a device the arc did not consider: it distinguishes the station from the winding. Equation (8),

(8) ϑn = (n − 1)π − t log n,

says where the a-vertex of gear n sits. It is a position on a clock, defined mod 2π, and the clock — rh3 §2’s twelve digits — has no winding memory. Reading (8) literally as a signed winding is what produces the reversal at x* = t/π, and rh3 now declines to: “A gear turns one way.” What the clock leaves free is the whole-turn part of each step, and fixing that freedom is a gauge choice. Fix it uniformly and the thread never backs off, at any torsion, with the sign left exactly where it was.

So this document does four things. It installs rh3’s lift in the instrument and checks it against rh3’s own printed numbers (§2–3). It states the correction (§4). It proves the whole question is invisible to every eigenvalue the arc has computed (§5). And it records one thing worth keeping: the carry per level is the same number as the one-sided length of the approximate functional equation, whose square root is rh36’s collapse knee (§7).

2 · The lift

rh3 §6’s three new equations. The carry is the smallest whole number of extra turns per level that keeps the rate from ever going negative,

(10) ϑ̃(x) = (x − 1) p − t log x, p = π + cτ, c = ⌈(t − π)/τ⌉+,

its rate is

(11) ϑ̃′(x) = p − t/x,

strictly increasing in x and ≥ 0 everywhere by the choice of c, and the running total is

(12) δn = ϑ̃(n)/τ turns, τ = 2π.

A nut on a bolt: one level deeper advances it by one pitch and turns it by whatever that pitch demands, and it cannot arrive at the next level having decided, halfway, to unscrew.

Everything rh3 prints reproduces. At t = γ1 the carry is 2 whole turns per level and the pitch is exactly 5π, so p/τ = 2.5. The ledger under the first ten gears reads 0, 0.94, 2.53, 4.38, 6.38, 8.47, 10.62, 12.82, 15.06, 17.32 τ — all ten to two decimals, with δ2 = 0.9407 and δ10 = 17.320078 in full. Its increments are 0.94, 1.59, 1.85, 2.00, 2.09, 2.15, 2.20, 2.24, 2.26: rising monotonically towards the pitch and never reaching it, still 0.05555 turns short forty levels down. At the turning ring x* = t/π = 4.499223 the rate is exactly the carry, 2.000000 turns per level.

Three properties make it a lift rather than a different thread. It hits every station (8) fixes: ϑ̃ and ϑ agree mod τ to 7.3×10−12 turns over eight torsions and two hundred levels, which is the roundoff of differencing angles of size 105. Its rate is non-negative over eight thousand depths at each of those torsions, and strictly increasing in depth. And c is the smallest carry that does it — at c − 1 the rate at x = 1 is negative at every torsion tested.

The normalisation the arc wanted is now read off the source rather than imposed on it. δ1 = 0 at every torsion, exactly: gear 1 is depth 0, radius 1 for every σ, rate log 1 = 0, winding 0 — the construction’s one fixed point. And rh3 (20) makes the matched-winding constraint literal. A spine is not a lab meridian but a digit carried by the turning frame, at angle ϑ̃(x) + kπ/6, so spine k meets its own digit on every gear having completed exactly δ turns. The a-helix and the gear it passes through carry one count because the spine is the thread: spine 0 and ϑ̃ agree to the last bit.

3 · Why uniform

Monotone is not enough, and this is the part of rh3’s argument the arc had not reached. rh6 (2) books the minimum forward turn on each segment separately. That is also a lift, and it is also monotone — every segment turn lies in [0, 1) by construction, checked across torsions and sixty levels. But at t = γ1 the five segments then demand 0.94, 0.59, 0.85, 1.00, 0.09 turns, and the collapse of 0.908 between the fourth and the fifth is not geometry. It is the residual left over on a segment that no longer needs a carry.

A bolt has no such feature, so the carry is made uniform — the same whole number at every level — and then there is no residual to leave over. Under (10) every increment is strictly larger than the one before it, at every level checked. That is the sharpening constraint (ii) needed and did not have: it is not enough that the count rise; its rate must not jump. Any lift whose rate jumps is excluded, monotone or not, and the per-segment minimum is the concrete example.

4 · The mirror, and a correction

rh35_helix fixed the sign the other way, writing ϑ(n, t) = (n − 1)π + t log n and reporting that every increment is then positive. That is true, and it is worth being exact about what it is. Since −(n − 1)π ≡ (n − 1)π mod 2π, flipping the torsion’s sign negates the whole angle:

ϑmirror(n, t) ≡ −ϑrh3(n, t) (mod 2π),

measured to 5.7×10−14 turns over three torsions and three hundred levels. The arc has been drawing rh3’s thread reflected through the a–A diameter. Its chain sums (−1)n−1n−σ+it = η(s̄) where rh3’s sums η(s); the two partial sums are conjugate at every level, so their moduli agree to 2×10−14 and the zero set is identical. That is why no computed value in rh31 through rh37 ever moved, and §5 turns the observation into a proof.

The correction. rh34 §2, rh35 §2 and rh37 §2 all say that carry-proof monotonicity excludes rh3 §6’s opposed sign. It does not, and the error is worth naming precisely rather than softening. What has no floor is the bare increment π − t log(1 + 1/n): it crosses zero at t = π/log 2 = 4.5324, reaches −6.6559 radians at the first level at γ1, and is negative at all of the first twenty levels by γ = 331. Every one of those measurements stands. What does not stand is the inference. They are facts about (8) read as a winding, and (8) is not a winding — so they disqualify the reading, not the sign. rh37 took a correct measurement and attached it to the wrong object.

The half-turn floor goes the same way. It is a property of the mirror, not a requirement of the construction. The mirror’s discrete step is ≥ ½ exactly, uniformly in n and t, with equality only as t → 0. rh3’s lift is weaker and still sufficient: its continuous rate is exactly zero at x = 1 whenever t = π + cτ, and its discrete step floor is (½ + c)(1 − log 2), whose global minimum over the torsion is 0.1534 turns at t = π. Both are floors and both are independent of where in the turn the thread happens to sit, which is the property that makes a count carry-proof. Only one of them is half a turn.

5 · What the convention costs

Nothing, and it is worth proving rather than repeating, because the arc has now spent parts of four documents on a choice that cannot affect a single number it has computed.

Build the Gram matrix straight off the a-vertex phases, with no intermediary. The entry for gears i and j is the weighted mean of the cosine of their phase difference over the zeros, and that difference is (i − j)π ∓ γ(log i − log j) — the two conventions differing only in the sign of the second term. Cosine is even, so:

Gij = (−1)i−j E(log i − log j),

the same matrix either way. Measured on rh33’s window at Δ = 0.12 over a sixteen-gear ladder, the two Grams differ by 1.1×10−15 — float noise, not structure — and the identity above holds to 9.1×10−12. The half-turns survive only as the diagonal similarity diag((−1)i), so they do not touch the spectrum either: λmin with them and without them agree to 5.8×10−13, and at four, eight, twelve and sixteen gears the two conventions give the same λmin to 2×10−16. Cholesky inertia bisection and cyclic Jacobi agree with each other to 2.1×10−13, as they have throughout.

So the torsion’s sign is a complex conjugation and the half-turns are a similarity, and neither is visible to an eigenvalue. The whole convention question is a question about drawing. It deserved settling, and it deserves no more than that.

6 · The chain still closes

Checked on the way past, because a construction whose train did not converge would make everything above beside the point. It converges.

The terms are n−σ and go to zero for every σ > 0 — 10−4 at the hundred-millionth gear, with nothing about a zero involved. The alternating partial sums close on 0 at s = ½ + iγ1: |P400| = 0.024988, which is the 0.0250 rh3’s Figure 3 caption prints, still falling at 4000, and |η(½ + iγ1)| = 2.3×10−15 by Borwein at thirty-two terms. The prefactor |1 − 21−s| = 2.3737 is finite and non-zero, so a zero of ζ is a zero of η and the construction sees it.

One readout deserves a note because it reads alarming out of context. With the half-turns off, Figure 2 reports “grows like √N; it does not converge for σ ≤ 1”. That is the un-alternated ζ train, exactly as rh3 §9 describes it, and the claim is correct: the partial sums reach 7.0705 at N = 104 against the predicted √N/|1 − s| = 7.0704, and multiply by 1.99996 when N quadruples. It is what the half-turns buy, stated as a divergence. With them on — the default — the chain closes.

7 · The pitch and the functional equation

One consequence of (10) touches live work. The carry is a function of the torsion alone, and at a zero it is

c(γ) = ⌈γ/2π − ½⌉,

exactly, for every one of rh29’s 22,491 cached ordinates: c − γ/2π never leaves (−½, ½] and averages 0.00017 turns. So the number of whole turns the thread makes per level at a zero is γ/2π — the one-sided length of the approximate functional equation. It is 2, 8, 38 and 226 at γ1, γ10, γ100 and γ1000.

That is the quantity rh36 refuted as the collapse law, its constant moving by 2.54× across four windows. What rh36 confirmed instead was knee = 2.221(γ/2π)0.531, the balanced length. Divide it by √c and it sits at 2.42, 2.36, 2.47, 2.63 as c runs from 2 to 226 — a spread of 1.11 over a hundredfold range, the drift being rh36’s exponent 0.531 against a clean half.

Stated geometrically: the floor collapses at the square root of the number of turns the thread makes per level. Whether that is more than a restatement is not settled here — γ/2π is the natural frequency scale of anything band-limited at height γ, and both quantities inherit it. It is recorded because it is the first time the collapse length has had a reading in the construction’s own coordinates rather than in the lattice’s, and because §9 (a) is going to need one.

8 · Where this leaves the conjunction

Unmoved, and this document does not pretend otherwise. It corrects a claim about a drawing convention, installs a better one, and proves that the whole question was invisible to every eigenvalue the arc has computed. None of that is a step towards RH and none of it was meant to be.

What it does buy is smaller and real. The instrument now speaks the source document’s language rather than a mirror of it, so a future measurement stated in helix coordinates can be checked against rh3 directly. Constraint (ii) has its correct form — a floor on the rate of a uniform lift, not on the increment of a bare angle — which is a condition a future sign or parameter choice can actually be tested against. And §7 gives the collapse length its first reading in the construction’s own coordinates. The certified set stays at nine, renewed once more.

The honest summary is that the arc spent parts of four documents on a question that §5 shows could never have mattered numerically, and got the answer wrong on the way. The value of that is entirely in §3: rate continuity is a real constraint, it is the one that picks the uniform carry out of the monotone lifts, and it came from the source document rather than from here.
