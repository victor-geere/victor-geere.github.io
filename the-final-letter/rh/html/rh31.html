rh31 · the emptiest arc

rh30 ended by ordering a change of direction: stop accumulating certificates, and test its one family-level law to destruction before anyone tries to prove it. This document executes both orders. The law breaks — cleanly, instructively — and what it was a shadow of survives every attempt made here to kill it: λmin is the density of the measure’s emptiest arc, tight to a factor below two across sixteen window families, every outlier, and one window built specifically to fool it.

1 · Where this stands

rh30 §10 left three orders rather than three questions: (a) test the collapse — the claim that λmin/Eh(0) is a function of the single ratio n/Neff — to destruction on a wider family before trying to prove it; (b) find an inequality that uses the positivity of q = |p|² rather than its variation; (c) stop extending the certified set.

All three are executed here. (c) is trivial to execute and is executed: no new certificates. (a) is §3, and the collapse fails off the family it was discovered on. (b) arrives from an unexpected side: the classical moment problem hands over an exact restatement of λmin (§2), the restatement suggests where the floor really comes from, and the quantity that survives all of §§3–5’s attempts to break it is a local density — precisely the kind of object a positivity-aware inequality would bound.

Regressions: the zero-side row still reproduces rh27c’s published λmin at Δ′ = 0.12 to 5.2×10⁻⁴ relative; rh30’s Neff = 95.7 is reproduced; the zero table is rh29’s 4,308 ordinates at the matched cut.

2 · The uniform content

The theorem first, because everything else in this document is downstream of the way it makes you look at λmin.

Theorem (Carathéodory–Toeplitz, restated). λmin(Tn) is the largest c such that the row (Eh(0) − c, Eh(Δ′), …, Eh((n−1)Δ′)) is still the moment sequence of a positive measure. Proof: Tn − cI is Toeplitz with exactly that row; a Toeplitz matrix is PSD iff its row is a truncated moment sequence (Carathéodory–Toeplitz); and the largest c with Tn − cI ⊰ 0 is λmin by definition. ∎

In rh29’s measure picture this says: λmin is the uniform content of μ at resolution n — the largest amount of uniform measure μ can shed, μ ≈ λ·U + ν, and still present the same first n moments with a positive remainder ν. Szegő (rh29 §4) is the n → ∞ limit: an atomic measure has no uniform part, so the content drains to zero. What was missing is what the draining looks like at finite n, and that is constructible.

At the extreme c = λmin the matrix Tn − λI is singular, and the representing measure ν is supported on the circle roots of its kernel vector’s polynomial. Constructed at Δ′ = 0.12: at n = 24 the minimal eigenpolynomial has 23 roots on the circle; they come in mirror pairs because the row is real, and fold to 12 atoms on the half-circle. Solving the moment equations on those atoms:

n = 12   11 circle roots → 6 folded atoms   min weight 6.8×10⁻⁴   mass 0.018830 = Eh(0) − λmin   moment residual 3.6×10⁻¹⁵
n = 24   23 circle roots → 12 folded atoms   min weight 2.0×10⁻³   mass 0.061929 = Eh(0) − λmin   moment residual 6.1×10⁻¹⁶
n = 40   39 circle roots → 20 folded atoms   min weight 2.4×10⁻³   mass 0.112653 = Eh(0) − λmin   moment residual 1.2×10⁻¹⁵

Every weight strictly positive — no pruning, no sign repair, once the mirror fold is made. Moments matched to machine. Mass exactly Eh(0) − λmin. And the geometry is the point:

The witness’s atoms sit on the heavy atoms of μ — median distance 0.018 against a heavy-atom spacing of 0.105 — because ν must reproduce the oscillating moments, and those come from where μ’s mass is. What is left over after ν has claimed the atoms it can resolve is uniform, and that remainder is the certificate. λmin measures what the measure looks like between its resolved atoms. Which is a density statement, and it is why the rest of this document is about arcs.

3 · The destruction

rh30’s collapse was measured on five windows — all five produced by the arc’s own optimiser. The destruction grid adds eleven families no optimiser touched: the bare envelope (A ≡ const), six random coefficient vectors drawn uniformly from the same sphere, and four interpolations between the searched window and fresh random draws. Sixteen families spanning Neff from 7.0 to 467, three meshes, seven depths: 336 certificates-that-could-have-been.

bin [0.03, 0.1)   searched 1.14×   all sixteen 2.21×
bin [0.1, 0.3)   searched 1.93×   all sixteen 4.11×
bin [0.3, 1.0)   searched 2.97×   all sixteen 7.04×

On the searched family alone the law holds exactly as published — spread 1.93× in the bin [0.1, 0.3), matching rh30’s 1.99× on a different grid. Over all sixteen families the same bin spreads 4.11×, and even the early bin [0.03, 0.1) opens from 1.14× to 2.21×. The worst offender is a random window at n/Neff = 0.29: λ/Eh(0) = 0.241, half the searched band’s floor at the same coordinate.

So rh30’s one-coordinate law is a property of the searched windows, not of the measure. rh30 asked for exactly this test before anyone attempted a proof; the answer is that there is nothing there to prove in one variable. Neff is a functional of the weights alone. What it cannot see is where the weights land.

4 · The wrong second coordinate

There is an obvious candidate for the missing position information, and it performs almost perfectly right up until it is refuted. The weighted star discrepancy D*w of the atoms (rh30 §5’s object) is mesh-dependent, position-sensitive, and orders the destruction’s deviations: within the bin [0.1, 0.3), the third of grid points with smallest Neff·D*w has median λ/Eh(0) = 0.92 and the third with largest has 0.50. It also explains why one coordinate sufficed before: the searched windows sit near the discrepancy floor D*w ≥ 1/(2Neff) — their Neff·D*w stays below 2.4 while the random and interpolated windows reach 5.9. On the floor, the second coordinate is a function of the first.

Then the refutation, by construction. Minimise D*w itself over the same coefficient family (coordinate descent from the envelope, at Δ′ = 0.12):

anchored discrepancy D*ₓ   searched 0.01362   D*-minimised 0.00681   (halved)
Neff   searched 95.7   D*-minimised 175.5   (×1.83)
λmin   searched 8.55×10⁻²   D*-minimised 3.02×10⁻³   (÷28)

The discrepancy comes out halved and Neff nearly doubled — better on both candidate coordinates — and λmin comes out 28 times smaller. Anchored discrepancy is not what the certificate sees. It also settles a question §4 raised implicitly: thirteen documents of window searching were not a discrepancy minimiser in disguise, because the actual discrepancy minimiser is a much worse window.

5 · The law that survives

§2 says λmin is what remains between the resolved atoms. The natural quantification is the emptiest-arc density: take the Fejér kernel Kn — a bump of width ~2π/n — and slide it around the circle:

Cn(μ) = minθ ∫ Kn(θ − t) dμ(t).

Cn ≥ λmin always, by one line: the plane-wave vector localised at the emptiest θ is a unit test vector whose Rayleigh quotient is exactly the integral (rh28’s ceiling, rh30 §4’s plane wave — the arc has been computing this object since two documents before it knew what it was). The content is how tight it is, and for whom:

Across every bulk grid point — all sixteen families, the outliers that broke §3’s collapse, and the adversarial window of §4 — the ratio Cn/λmin has median 1.04 and never exceeds 1.81. The worst fit is the envelope window, the one whose heavy atoms are the first hundred consecutive zeros, at 1.81; every other family stays below 1.39. The D*-window of §4 obeys the law at 1.35: its tiny λmin is not an anomaly, it is the law reading a genuinely empty arc that the anchored discrepancy cannot see. Past n ≈ Neff the ceiling detaches — reaching 1.2×10³ on this grid — which is rh30’s regime where the atoms are resolved and no smooth test vector is optimal.

This unifies the last three documents. rh30’s collapse was the shadow of the emptiest-arc law on a family whose emptiest arc happens to be a function of n/Neff — that is what sitting on the discrepancy floor does. The destruction’s outliers are windows whose emptiest arc is emptier than their Neff predicts. The D*-refutation is a window with small anchored discrepancy and a nearly-empty arc. One quantity, three explanations.

And it is the right kind of quantity for rh30 §10 (b). The missing inequality was named there as one that uses q = |p|² ≥ 0 rather than its variation. The measured statement — λmin ≥ Cn(μ)/1.81 across everything tried — is a positivity statement: it says the minimising |p|² concentrates its mass in the emptiest arc and can do only a bounded factor better than the Fejér bump that does the same. Proving that localisation, for atomic measures with equidistributed atoms, is now the sharpest available formulation of the arc’s one analytic question.

6 · The position laws

If λmin prices the emptiest arc, then with the weights flattened it should read the pure geometry of the wrapped ordinates {γΔ′ mod 2π}. Three reference geometries, m = 300 atoms each, mirrored as the measure is:

The crystal first, because it has a one-line law. m equispaced atoms on (0,π), mirrored, are 2m equispaced points on the circle, so E(k) = 0 for every 0 < k < 2m — verified to machine zero — and Tn is the mass times the identity out to n = 2m. The crystal’s law is a step function: λ/mass = 1, then nothing.

Between the crystal and Poisson noise runs the jitter family — crystal plus uniform jitter of ±α spacings — and it is cleanly ordered: at n/(2m) = 0.55 the medians run 0.68, 0.40, 0.16 as α grows from ¼ to 1, with Poisson below 10⁻³. The wrapped ordinates, at all six meshes from 0.05 to 0.24, ride the Poisson band: at n/(2m) = 0.4 every mesh sits inside the twelve-draw band, the largest at 0.69 of the largest draw and a factor of 7.8 below even the full-spacing jitter. One marginal excess survives: at Δ′ = 0.09 the mid-bulk value 0.325 clears all twelve Poisson draws — by 10%. The other five meshes run 0.08 to 0.22, inside the band. It is arithmetic in the mesh, not a property of the ordinates alone.

The sober conclusion: at the arc’s scales the certificates measure equidistribution. The wrapped ordinate steps a fraction of a turn per zero, so neighbours on the circle are zeros far apart on the line, and GUE rigidity never arrives at the circle’s scale. λmin is not a rigidity meter — a temptation rh30 §9 flirted with, now measured away.

7 · Where the conjunction stands

Nine certified points, unchanged by design. What changed is that the arc now knows what its certificates are made of, at three levels of exactness:

exactly
λmin is the uniform content of μ at resolution n (§2, a theorem), and the atomic remainder is constructible, positive, and sits on the heavy atoms.
to a measured factor below two
λmin is the emptiest-arc Fejér density (§5), across sixteen families, three meshes, seven depths, every outlier and one adversarial window. No counterexample found, including by construction aimed at the neighbouring quantity.
and not at all
λmin is not a function of n/Neff (§3 breaks rh30’s law off its native family), not a function of the anchored discrepancy (§4 refutes it by construction), and not a GUE-rigidity meter (§6).

The conjunction-shaped statement this leaves is sharper than the one rh30 left. Then: “prove the collapse.” Now: prove that for a positive atomic measure the minimising nonnegative polynomial localises — that λmin ≥ c·minθ∫Kn dμ for an absolute constant c, in the regime n below the resolved-atom count. The measured c is 1/1.81 on everything tried. A proof would convert every emptiest-arc computation — which is elementary, no eigenvalue needed — into a certified floor, for every window at once. That is a family statement, it quantifies over the same objects Weil’s criterion does, and it is false past the bulk, so its boundary is part of its content.

It should be said plainly that this is still a statement about forms, not about ζ. But it is the first question the arc has produced where the obstruction is a single, clean localisation lemma with a measured constant — not five decades of lost ground (rh30 §5), not a conspiracy (rh29 §7), not a search (rh22–27).

8 · What this document leaves

(a) the localisation lemma
Prove λmin ≥ c·Cn(μ) for n ≤ Neff-ish resolutions, atomic μ, absolute c. The measured c is 1/1.81 ≈ 0.55; the natural tools are the ones rh30 §10 named (Beurling–Selberg, one-sided approximants) now aimed at a localisation rather than a global floor. Fail honestly: find the measure that drives c to zero, which would itself be a structure statement about where certificates die.
(b) the envelope anomaly
The one family at 1.81 rather than ≤ 1.39 is the one whose heavy atoms are consecutive zeros. Consecutive wrapped ordinates carry the only long-range structure §6 detected (the Δ′ = 0.09 excess). Whether the envelope’s worse fit and the 0.09 excess are the same arithmetic phenomenon is a cheap, sharp question.
(c) the certified set stays closed
Standing order from rh30, renewed. The machinery idles until (a) either produces a lemma — at which point every emptiest-arc number becomes a certificate wholesale — or produces the counterexample measure, at which point the arc will finally know what a certificate cannot be made of.
