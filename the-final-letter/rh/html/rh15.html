The wider slices, and the reading as an equation

Ordered: draw slices at wider support, chase the soft tail's scaling in the support, and answer whether the reading of a slice can be turned into a function, written as an equation, or proven. Answered in that order — with one finding that was not expected: the soft tail everyone has been watching, this arc included, is the instrument, and the reading that is free of it costs one scalar inequality per node.

Abstract. rh14 closed on Bochner's reading — RH ⟺ the single kernel E(v) = ∑ρ|B(γ)|²cos(γv) is positive-definite on every interval, the finite slices being certificates manufacturable at will. This document widens them, chases the tail, and asks what the reading is. Wider (§2): the kernel is rebuilt on a junction-free quadrature and pushed to v = 12 — gears to n = e¹² and a zeros table extended to height 5000 by Riemann–Siegel (4520 ordinates, census matching Riemann–von Mangoldt to 0.33) — giving the two theories agreement of 8.7×10⁻⁹ to 6.4×10⁻⁷ of scale, twenty-five times sharper than rh14 and now across the whole range. At the far end the answer is a cancellation: the pole contributes 0.3524, the prime comb 0.3552, and what survives is −2.8×10⁻³ — the pole is 124× the answer, and the gears near e¹² cancel it. The two tails (§3–§4): the bottom eigenvalue of a slice is not the arithmetic margin. The softest mode parks its carrier on the hat transform's own null r* = 2π/Δ — 97–99% of its energy sits within a quarter period of the nulls, carrier ratios 1.023, 0.991, 0.993 — and a parameter-free notch law assembled from the clamped-rod constant κ₁ = 4.730040 and the zero density at the null tracks λmin across four decades (geometric mean ratio 0.59 over 17 slices). One deletion settles it: remove every zero below the first null and λmin moves by ≤ 5.7% while the optimal-window dip D* loses 12–95% — the eigenvalue is instrument, the dip is arithmetic. But the dip does not settle either: it falls by 9–45× per mesh halving, and a ladder carried to Δ = 0.00375 shows no flattening. The equation (§5): the reading of a slice is the Schur/Levinson recursion σk² = σk−1²(1 − ρk²), one scalar inequality |ρk| < 1 per node — and because ρk is built from E(0)…E(kΔ) alone, every slice at every support is a prefix of one sequence per mesh. Those numbers are O(1) and well-conditioned where λmin and D* are not: the margin 1 − max|ρk| is 0.403, 0.623, 0.698 and grows as the mesh refines, moving by ≤ 2.8×10⁻⁶ between independent quadratures; the prediction errors collapse onto one mesh-independent law σ² ≈ 3.9×10⁻³/v². The widest certificate — support 6 at mesh 0.015, all 799 prediction errors positive in 60-digit arithmetic — is a settled fact, not a floating-point opinion. The propositions (§6): what is proven (the recursion ⟺ slice positivity, classical), what is proven modulo a stated numerical bar (each computed certificate, with that bar measured here), and what no finite computation reaches (the conjunction, which is RH).

1 · The three questions

rh14 ended with a clean statement and a loose end. The statement: the whole crossing form is one function E of one variable, and RH is the assertion that E is positive-definite — every Toeplitz slice G[j,k] = E((j−k)Δ) has a nonnegative spectrum, at every mesh Δ and every support a. The loose end: those spectra had a soft tail — bottom eigenvalues of 10⁻⁸, then 10⁻¹⁰, then 10⁻¹¹ — falling fast as the slices grew, and nothing in rh14 said what that fall was made of.

Three questions follow, and this document takes them in order. (i) Draw the slices wider: does anything break at support 6, four times rh13's? (ii) Chase the tail: is there a law for how the margin scales with support and mesh? (iii) Can the reading of a slice — the act of deciding "is this matrix positive?" — be turned into a function, written as an equation, or proven?

The answers, stated in advance so the rest can be checked against them. (i) Nothing breaks; the wide kernel is a delicate cancellation and it survives verification to seven digits. (ii) There are two tails, not one, and they are told apart by a single experiment; one of them is not arithmetic at all. (iii) Yes to all three, in the precise senses of §5 and §6: the reading is the map from E to its Schur coefficients, the equation is a two-line recursion, and the equivalence between that recursion and positivity is a theorem from 1917.

Part I · Wider

2 · Wider: the kernel out to v = 12

A slice of support a needs the kernel out to v = 2a, so support 6 needs v = 12 — and E(v) at v = 12 needs the gear comb out to n = e¹² = 162754 and the archimedean transform computed well enough that the two survive subtraction. Both were tightened for this document. The comb runs over all 18120 prime powers below 200000. The archimedean integral, which rh14 split at r = 30 into a fine and a coarse zone, is now taken on one uniform step to r = 12000: the split was the dominant error, because a composite trapezoid rule pays an Euler–Maclaurin junction term (h₂² − h₁²)f′(30)/12 at the seam, while a single step on an even, strip-analytic integrand is spectrally accurate — its aliasing error is the transform evaluated at 2π/h − v, here e−25.

The other limit was the zeros side. Above its table one must model the ordinates by their smooth density, and that model's residue grows like Δ−2 as the mesh refines — it was what capped rh14 at 1.6×10⁻⁵. So the table was extended: Riemann–Siegel Z, validated first on the top of rh11's table, then scanned from 2000 to 5000, yielding 4520 ordinates whose count matches Riemann–von Mangoldt (θ(T)/π + 1 = 4520.33) — the census closes, so no close pair was skipped. The result is a kernel whose two independent theories agree to

(1) 8.7×10⁻⁹  (Δ = 0.06),   3.71×10⁻⁸  (Δ = 0.03),   6.37×10⁻⁷  (Δ = 0.015)

of scale, over the whole range v ∈ [0, 12] — twenty-five times sharper than rh14 and across twice the reach. Two structural checks come free and are worth naming. First, |E(v)| ≤ E(0) at every one of the 799 nodes: trivial from the zeros side (a sum of cosines with positive weights), a real statement from the primes side, and it holds to the last digit. Second, and more telling:

That cancellation is the honest content of "wider". Nothing about E(12) is soft or asymptotic: it is the statement that the prime powers near 162754, weighted by Λ(n)/√n, sum to within three parts in a thousand of a hyperbolic cosine — and the zeros know the difference to eight digits. Slices of support 6 rest on that.

Part II · Two tails

3 · The first tail: the instrument's own null

Now the slices. Ten of them were cut — supports 1.5, 3, 4.5, 6 at meshes 0.06 and 0.03, plus supports 1.5 and 3 at mesh 0.015 — the largest being 399 nodes, and every one is positive definite. Their bottom eigenvalues run from 1.35×10⁻⁸ down to 3.9×10⁻¹³, falling monotonically along both nesting directions exactly as the variational principle requires. That is rh14's soft tail, four times longer. The question is what it is made of.

Here is the mechanism, and it has nothing to do with the primes. The slice's quadratic form is

(2) xTGx =  ∑γ |B(γ)|²·|P(γ)|²,    P(r) =  ∑j xjeirjΔ

— a sum of squares, each weighted by the mesh profile |B(r)|² = Δ²sinc⁴(rΔ/2). Two facts about that weight collide. The symbol P is 2π/Δ-periodic in r, because the nodes sit on a grid of spacing Δ. And |B|² vanishes to fourth order at exactly r = 2πk/Δ — one null per period, at the same phase in every period. So the cheapest direction available to the form is not some subtle arithmetic conspiracy: it is to park a bump at r ≡ 0 (mod 2π/Δ), where the copy near the origin costs nothing (there are no zeros below γ₁ = 14.13) and every other copy sits in a null. The mode is the instrument's blind spot, and it is built into the sampling, not into ζ.

This is measurable three ways, and all three were run. Where the energy is: for the softest mode of each slice, 96.97%, 99.43% and 98.72% of ∑|B(γ)|²|P(γ)|² comes from ordinates within a quarter period of a null. What the carrier is: the single largest contributing ordinate is 107.169, 103.726 and 207.906 against nulls at 104.72, 104.72 and 209.44 — ratios 1.023, 0.991, 0.993. What it does not know: delete every ordinate below the first null and λmin moves by 5.74%, 0.63%, 3.17%. The bottom of the spectrum has essentially never met the low zeros.

The law follows from the same picture with no fitting. Near the k-th null, |B(2πk/Δ + s)|² = Δ⁶s⁴/(16π⁴k⁴); the symbol restricted to one period is a trigonometric polynomial of degree m ≈ a/Δ, so minimising ∑s⁴|P|² against the zero density at the nulls is the fourth-moment problem on the circle — the Toeplitz matrix of the symbol θ⁴, whose bottom eigenvalue tends to 2πκ₁⁴/n⁴ with κ₁ = 4.730040… the clamped-rod constant. (Computed here directly: the ratio runs 0.607, 0.761, 0.886, 0.941, 0.970, 0.985 as n goes 11 → 399.) Summing the density over all the nulls gives

(3) λmin  ≈  Δ16π⁴ · ζ(4)ln(1/Δ) − ζ′(4)π · λmin(Rn),    Rn[j,k] = π∫−πθ⁴e−i(j−k)θdθ

with no free constant anywhere — the mesh, the clamped rod, ζ(4) and ζ′(4), and nothing else. Against 17 measured slices spanning four decades it lands with geometric mean ratio 0.585 and a spread of 0.14–1.59.

So the tail chased in question (ii) has an answer, and it is a disappointment dressed as a law: λmin ~ Δ⁵ln(1/Δ)/a⁴ — the measured exponent in Δ at fixed support is 5.87 against the law's 5 plus a logarithm — and it describes the hat, not ζ. Any observable read off the bottom of a slice's spectrum is reading the sampling scheme. That is worth knowing before one reads too much into a 10⁻¹³.

4 · The second tail: arithmetic, and unsettled

There is a second margin in the arc's inventory, and it fails the instrument's test in the opposite direction. rh12's optimal-window dip D*(a) — the smallest value the crossing form can be driven to by any window of support a normalised to f̂(0) = 1 — is, on the merged kernel, a one-line computation: the shifted form is Toeplitz too, so D* = 1/(b₀TM−1b₀) is a single Levinson pass rather than rh14's dense inversion. It reproduces rh14's values to 2% at both supports it shares with them, and extends across a 6 × 3 grid.

Run the deletion experiment on it. Removing every ordinate below the first null cost λmin at most 5.7%; it costs D* 95.1%, 12.2% and 17.5% at the same three settings — 17×, 19× and 6× more sensitive. One experiment, two verdicts: the eigenvalue is blind to the low zeros and the dip is largely made of them. The two tails are genuinely different objects, and D* is the arithmetic one.

It is also, at every mesh this document can reach, not converged. D*(a, Δ) falls monotonically in both a and Δ, as a variational minimum over a growing family must — but halving the mesh divides it by 4.2 to 44.8, and the successive ratios do not shrink. A ladder at fixed support a = 1.5, carried down to Δ = 0.00375 (799 nodes), gives 5.85×10⁻⁷ → 5.26×10⁻⁸ → 5.37×10⁻⁹ → 4.03×10⁻¹⁰: ratios 11.1, 9.8, 13.3, with no flattening at all.

Why it does not settle is worth stating carefully, because a plausible guess fails. The continuum infimum is positive: the shifted ordinates have unbounded Beurling density, hence form a sampling set for every exponential type, so the frame bound gives D*∞(a) ≥ Aπ/a > 0. The natural guess for where refinement stops buying room is the point at which the mesh resolves the highest ordinate a window of type a can annihilate — Levinson's density theorem puts that at R(a) = 2πe2a+1, hence Δ ≈ 1/(2e2a+1) = 0.0092 at a = 1.5. The ladder was carried to 0.00375, well past it. The guess is not confirmed: the last step buys as much as the first. Whatever sets the scale of D*∞(1.5), it is not the Nyquist frequency of the grid, and this document does not know what it is. That is the honest state of question (ii)'s second half: the arithmetic tail is real, it is enormously smaller than any margin so far reported, and its limit is out of reach of a mesh this arc can afford.

Which raises the question §5 answers. If the eigenvalue is instrument and the dip is unconverged, is there any reading of a slice that is stable, cheap, and about ζ?

Part III · The reading

5 · The equation: one inequality per node

There is, and it is a hundred and nine years old. A Toeplitz matrix built from E(0), E(Δ), …, E((n−1)Δ) is positive definite if and only if the Schur–Levinson–Durbin recursion runs without stalling:

(4) σ0² = E(0),   ρk = E(kΔ) − ∑i<k ak−1,iE((k−i)Δ)σk−1²,    σk² = σk−1²(1 − ρk²)  >  0  ⟺  |ρk| < 1.

That is the answer to question (iii), in all three of its forms. As a function: the reading is the map E ↦ (ρ₁, ρ₂, ρ₃, …), which by Verblunsky's theorem is a complete invariant — every sequence in the open unit disc is the coefficient sequence of exactly one positive measure, and conversely. As an equation: it is (4), two lines, one scalar inequality per new node. As something proven: the equivalence is Schur's, and it is a theorem, not a numerical hope.

Three properties make it the right reading here, and each was measured rather than assumed.

It does not depend on the support. ρk is built from E(0)…E(kΔ) and nothing else. So there is not a family of certificates indexed by (a, Δ); there is one sequence per mesh, and every slice of every support is a prefix of it. The support ladder of §3, read this way, is a single recursion read to different depths — which is why max|ρ| is literally identical, to sixty digits, across supports 1.5, 3, 4.5 and 6 at a given mesh. "Manufacturable at will" turns out to be an understatement: they were never separate objects.

It is well-conditioned where the eigenvalue is not. The ρk are O(1) numbers formed by ratios of comparable quantities; no 10⁻¹³ ever appears. Between two independent quadratures (uniform step 0.1 to r = 12000, and step 0.08 to r = 15000) the kernel itself moves by 1.6×10⁻⁹ absolute, λmin at the widest fine slice moves by 9%, D* by ~10⁻⁶ — and max|ρ| moves by 2×10⁻⁹, 5×10⁻⁸, 2.8×10⁻⁶. The certificate that costs the least is also the one that survives the arithmetic.

Its margin improves as the instrument does. The measured maxima are max|ρ| = 0.5966 (Δ = 0.06, attained at k = 9), 0.3769 (Δ = 0.03, at k = 54), 0.3018 (Δ = 0.015, at k = 171) — margins 1 − max|ρ| of 0.403, 0.623, 0.698. Refining the mesh pushes the sequence further inside the unit disc, the exact opposite of what λmin and D* do. And the prediction errors, which do decay, decay according to one law that does not mention the mesh at all: σk² ~ c/k² in the index, which is c/v² in the physical horizon v = kΔ, with measured c = 3.91×10⁻³, 4.23×10⁻³, 3.48×10⁻³ across the three meshes — a 22% spread over an 8× change in Δ.

The widest certificate this document offers is stated in that language. Support 6 at mesh 0.015 — 799 nodes, the kernel out to v = 11.97, gears to e¹² — run through (4) in 60-digit arithmetic: all 799 prediction errors positive, max|ρk| = 0.301754, σ798² = 2.14×10⁻⁵. Not "positive to within rounding": positive as a fact about the computed kernel, with the floating-point question removed from the discussion.

One check confirms that the test has teeth rather than merely a pulse. Subtract t copies of the γ₁ atom from the kernel and bisect for the value at which positivity fails: it fails at t* = 1.000245. The recursion flags the boundary at the same place the eigenvalue crosses zero, and that place is, to four digits, "the moment the first zero's own contribution is gone". The slices are not positive with room to spare — they are positive by exactly the margin the zeros supply, which is what a criterion equivalent to RH must look like.

6 · What is proven, what is certified, what is not

The question asked whether the reading can "be proven". It can, in three graded senses that are worth separating precisely, because conflating them is the standard way this subject goes wrong.

P1 — proven, classical

For a fixed mesh Δ and every n, the section [E((j−k)Δ)]j,k<n is positive definite ⟺ σk² > 0 for all k < n ⟺ |ρk| < 1 for all k < n. This is Schur's algorithm and the Levinson–Durbin recursion; the correspondence between such sequences and positive measures on the circle is Verblunsky's theorem. Proof: the recursion computes the Cholesky factorisation of the section, σk² being the successive pivots, so ∏σk² = det — verified numerically here to 1.2×10⁻¹² on a live 60-node slice, alongside the Levinson solver's agreement with Gaussian elimination to 10⁻¹³ and inverse iteration's with the Jacobi eigensolver to 10⁻⁴.

P2 — proven, with the analytic care named

The windows realisable on the grid — f = (hat of width Δ) ⋆ (weights on ΔZ ∩ [−a, a]) — are dense, as Δ → 0 and a → ∞, in the compactly supported test functions of the Weil functional, and the functional is continuous in the relevant norm (its prime sum is bounded by ∑n≤eaΛ(n)n−1/2‖g‖∞, its archimedean term by ‖ĥ‖1). Hence: E is positive-definite on ΔZ for every Δ > 0 ⟺ Weil's quadratic form is nonnegative on every compactly supported window. The step from a dense grid to the line is continuity of E, which is manifest from (1).

P3 — Weil, cited

Weil's positivity criterion: that form is nonnegative for all admissible test functions ⟺ RH. Not proven here; not in dispute.

Chain them and the reading is exactly as strong as advertised: RH ⟺ |ρk(Δ)| < 1 for every k and every Δ, where the ρ are computed from the primes and the Γ-factor by a finite recursion with no zero consulted. That is question (iii)'s answer in one line, and it is a theorem.

What a computed certificate proves is a smaller and more careful statement, and this document measured the smallness rather than waving at it. Each ρk here is a function of a kernel known to finite accuracy. The kernel's own error bar, measured by rebuilding it on an independent quadrature, is 1.6×10⁻⁹ absolute against E(0) = 3.2×10⁻², and its agreement with the entirely independent zeros-side computation is 8.7×10⁻⁹ to 6.4×10⁻⁷ of scale. Propagated to the readings: eigenvalues above 10⁻¹⁰ of scale move by less than 2×10⁻³ relative, those below by up to 9%; the Schur margins move by at most 2.8×10⁻⁶. So the honest form of a certificate is "the recursion at mesh Δ stays inside the unit disc to depth n, for a kernel correct to 10⁻⁹, with margin 0.40–0.70" — a statement that would become unconditional under interval arithmetic on the same quadrature, which is a finite amount of engineering and no new mathematics.

What no finite computation reaches is the conjunction. Every certificate here is a prefix — of one sequence, at one mesh. RH is the assertion that all of them, at all meshes, run forever. The gap is not a gap in rigour or in precision; it is the difference between a prefix and an infinite sequence, and it is the hypothesis itself.

7 · What this settles, and what it costs

Settled. The kernel now reaches v = 12 with both theories agreeing to 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ of scale, on the strength of a table extended to height 5000 and a junction-free quadrature; at the far end it is a 124:1 cancellation between the pole and the gears near e¹², and it survives. Ten slices to support 6 are positive definite, and the widest — 799 nodes at mesh 0.015 — is certified in 60-digit arithmetic. The soft tail is identified: it is the mesh's own null at r = 2π/Δ, holding 97–99% of the softest mode's energy, obeying a parameter-free law built from the clamped-rod constant across four decades, and blind to the low zeros. The optimal-window dip is the arithmetic margin instead, 6–19× more sensitive to those zeros — and it is unconverged at every mesh reachable here, by a mechanism this document could not identify (the natural Nyquist guess is tested and fails). And the reading of a slice is one recursion, support-independent, quadrature-stable to 10⁻⁶, whose margin widens from 0.403 to 0.698 as the mesh refines.

The cost. Two of the three quantities this arc has been quoting as margins — rh14's λmin ladder and, in part, rh12's D* frontier — measure less about ζ than they appeared to. λmin measures the hat. D* measures the arithmetic but has no limit in sight, so its values are a function of the mesh they were computed at and should always be quoted with it. This document's own headline numbers are stated in the one currency that survived the audit: reflection coefficients, their margins, and the horizon law σ²v² ≈ 3.9×10⁻³.

What is now cheap. A certificate used to cost a matrix (rh13: 325 quadratures), then a slice of a curve (rh14), and now costs one more term of one sequence: given E out to kΔ, deciding positivity to depth k is O(k²) with no conditioning to worry about and no eigenvalue to trust. The question the arc has been circling since rh10 has not moved — it is still Weil's, still open — but after fifteen documents it is written in the smallest form this construction can give it: one sequence per mesh, and does it ever leave the disc?
