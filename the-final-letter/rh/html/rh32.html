rh32 · where certificates die

rh31 ordered an attack on its own conjecture — λmin ≥ c·Cn(μ) with an absolute constant — and named the honest failure mode in advance: find the measure that drives c to zero. The measure is found, and it is not exotic. Cut one arc out of any of the arc’s measures and the constant dies exponentially in the arc’s width, at the classical prolate rate; on a crystal the whole mechanism is an exact two-line identity. What survives is sharper than what died: the death curve itself is lawful, the living material never enters its reach, and the one anomaly rh31 left open dissolves under three controlled tests.

1 · Where this stands

rh31 left three successors. (a) The localisation lemma: prove λmin ≥ c·Cn(μ) — the smallest eigenvalue against the Fejér-smeared density of the emptiest arc — with absolute c, in the bulk; rh31 measured c = 1/1.81 across 336 grid points and found no counterexample, and ordered the failure branch pursued as seriously as the proof branch: “find the measure that drives c to zero, which would itself be a structure statement about where certificates die.” (b) The envelope anomaly: the one family fitting at 1.81 rather than ≤ 1.39 is the one whose heavy atoms are consecutive zeros — is that the same arithmetic as the Δ′ = 0.09 excess rh31 §6 found in the wrapped ordinates? (c) No new certificates.

This document executes (a) in its failure branch — deliberately, because the failure branch turned out to be a theorem — answers (b) with three controlled experiments, and keeps (c). The outcome, stated up front: the lemma with absolute c is false. The counterexample is any measure with a void — an arc empty of atoms — wider than about two kernel wavelengths. On a crystal the mechanism is exact: deleting the atoms in an arc subtracts a discrete prolate concentration operator from a multiple of the identity, and the constant dies as the prolate tail, measured slope 3.070 against the classical π. The zeta ordinates with the same arc cut out die on the same curve. And the reason rh31 never saw it: across its entire grid, the living material builds no void wider than K = 1.45 kernel widths — the death zone starts at K ≈ 2 and is unpopulated. The constant 1/1.81 was geography, not law.

2 · The identity: a void is a concentration operator

Take the crystal: 2M equispaced atoms on the circle, unit weights, mirror symmetric. Its certificate is perfect, and the reason is one line. For any polynomial p(z) = Σk<n ckzk with n ≤ 2M, the atom sum of |p|² hits every pair frequency k−l with |k−l| < n ≤ 2M, and a full lattice sum of ei(k−l)θ vanishes unless 2M divides k−l — so only the diagonal survives:

Σatoms |p(θj)|² = 2M·‖c‖²  for every p of degree < n ≤ 2M   (verified on random polynomials to 2.4×10⁻¹⁵),
so the moment matrix is Tn = 2M·I exactly — every off-diagonal moment E(k), 0 < k < 2M, is zero to machine precision (4.3×10⁻¹²).

Now delete the atoms in an arc of width W. The moment matrix of the remaining measure is Tn = 2M·I − A, where A is the moment operator of the deleted atoms alone — a sum, over deleted atoms, of rank-two projectors built from the cosine and sine coefficient vectors ca = (cos kθa)k<n, sa = (sin kθa)k<n. Hence, exactly:

λmin(Tn) = 2M − λmax(A),  λmax(A) computable from the tiny Gram matrix of the deleted atoms’ vectors.
Verified by two independent eigensolvers — Toeplitz inertia bisection on the big matrix against Jacobi on the small Gram — to 9.1×10⁻¹³ at (M = 300, n = 60, K = 2), and to 5.2×10⁻¹¹ at (M = 600, n = 60, K = 8), where the Gram is 160 × 160 and λmin has fallen ten orders below the mass.

A is a familiar object: it is the discrete prolate concentration operator of the void — the operator whose top eigenvector is the degree-n polynomial most concentrated on the deleted arc. Its spectrum has the classical three-part shape (fig. 1, left): a plateau of eigenvalues hugging the mass, a plunge, and an exponential tail. At K = 4 the plateau holds exactly 4 eigenvalues above half the mass — Landau’s count: a void of K kernel wavelengths steals K degrees of freedom — and the top eigenvalue’s deficit is 5.5×10⁻⁵. A polynomial can hide that much of its norm inside the void; what it cannot hide is the rest, and λmin is exactly the part that cannot be hidden.

3 · The collapse: one exponential, four grids

The identity reduces the whole question to one function: how fast does the top concentration eigenvalue approach the mass as the void widens? The classical prolate theory says the deficit 1 − l1 depends on the void only through its time-bandwidth K = nW/2π — the width in units of the kernel wavelength 2π/n — and dies like e−πK. Both statements are measured here directly.

Four crystal grids — M = 300 and 600, n = 60 and 120, sixteen nominal widths each — and the deficit collapses onto one curve in K (fig. 1, right). The two grids that share a lattice ratio share their achieved-width ladder exactly, and their deficits agree to 0.6% through K = 3.1 and within 8% out to K = 8.1 — where the deficit itself has fallen ten decades; the residual drift grows smoothly with K and is the finite-n correction to the continuum prolate limit, not scatter. Two honest quantisation notes are part of the measurement: the crystal can only cut voids at lattice widths, so every point is plotted at its achieved K, and at special nominal widths the deletion boundary lands exactly on an atom pair, shifting the achieved K by one lattice step — those points sit on the fitted line at their true K, which is how the line was fitted.

ln(1 − l1) fitted over all four grids, K ∈ [3, 8.6], 44 points:  slope α = 3.070, within 2.3% of the prolate exponent π = 3.1416;  prefactor 16;  every point within 0.22 in ln.

So the crystal’s certificate under a void is solved to measurement precision: λmin/mass = 1 − l1(K) exactly, and 1 − l1(K) ≈ 16·e−3.070K on the measured range.

4 · The death of the constant

rh31’s conjecture compares λmin with the emptiest-arc ceiling Cn. On the punctured crystal the ceiling is also one integral: centred mid-void, the Fejér translate sees only the two tails that leak past the void edges, and the tail integral gives

Cn ≈ (2/π²)·mass/K = 0.2026·mass/K,  measured to 6.2% for all K ≥ 2 (the residual is the sinc² tail’s own oscillation about its mean).

Divide the identity by the ceiling law and the fate of rh31’s constant is decided:

c(K) = λmin/Cn = (π²K/2)·(1 − l1(K)).
Composed from the two independently measured quantities of §3 and this section, this reproduces the directly measured death curve to 6.2% over five decades, K = 2.1 to 6.1 — and the worst point is the ceiling law’s own oscillation, not the tail’s.

The numbers along the curve (fig. 2): c is still 0.72 at K = 1.5; it is 0.19 at K = 2.1 — a factor 3.8 lost across 0.6 of width, the cliff — and 3.7×10⁻⁶ by K = 6.1. There is no absolute constant; there is a function of the void width, and it dies at the prolate rate.

The crystal proves the mechanism; the arc’s own material confirms it is not a crystal artefact. Take the wrapped ordinates at mesh 0.12, depth n = 61 — equal weights (positions only) or the full searched-0.12 window — and cut the same arc out of the wrapped circle. With no cut, both sit at ratios 0.97 and 0.93, rh31’s regime. With the cut, both death curves track the crystal’s within a factor 1.9 while c falls six decades (fig. 2). At K = 6 the searched window’s own atoms, with one arc removed, give c = 3.5×10⁻⁶: the measure rh31 ordered found is in hand, and it is the arc’s own measure with one void. Certificates die on voids, and on nothing else that thirteen documents of window search, five probes of this one, or 336 grid points have produced.

5 · The safe zone: why rh31 never saw it

If the constant dies on voids, rh31’s measured 1/1.81 across 336 points means its material has no voids. Measure that directly: for each of the sixteen families at each mesh, compute the emptiness profile — for every arc width, the minimum of μ(arc) relative to the uniform share — and define K10 as the widest arc that is 10%-empty or worse, in kernel units at the point’s own depth. Across all 207 bulk points of rh31’s grid:

max K10 = 1.45 — the envelope’s worst point, whose widest thin arc runs 1.45 kernel widths at depth 1.5% of mean density.
Number of bulk points with K10 ≥ 2, where the cliff begins: zero.
Number of bulk points with ratio above 1.4: one — that same envelope point, at 1.81, which sits between the crystal boundary’s 1/c = 1.38 at K = 1.5 and 5.2 at K = 2.1.

That is the resolution of rh31’s constant: the material’s widest emptiness reaches K ≈ 1.45, the death curve at that width allows ratios up to a factor of a few, and the grid maximum 1.81 is exactly such a value. Beyond K = 2 the certificates would collapse outright — and no window built from zeta ordinates on this grid ever gets there, because the wrapped ordinates equidistribute (rh31 §6: they ride the Poisson band at every mesh tried). The certificate floor is an equidistribution statement.

Inside the safe zone, honesty requires the negative result too. Against ln(Cn/λmin) over the 207 bulk points, the width statistic correlates at +0.70, the depth of the emptiest kernel-width arc at −0.71, the smeared floor itself at −0.71, and rh30’s n/Neff at +0.60 — no single coordinate tried reaches 0.72 (fig. 3). The sub-1.4 fluctuations are genuinely multi-factor: the Slepian operator sees the whole mass profile around the thin arc, not two summary numbers. What is clean is the boundary of the zone, not the texture inside it.

6 · The envelope anomaly, resolved

rh31 §8 (b) asked a deliberately cheap, sharp question. The envelope — the window with no polynomial factor, whose heavy atoms are the first hundred consecutive zeros — fits the emptiest-arc law at 1.81 where every other family stays under 1.39; and consecutive wrapped ordinates carry the one long-range structure rh31 §6 detected, the 10% excess over the Poisson band at Δ′ = 0.09. Same arithmetic? Three experiments, all at the envelope’s worst point (mesh 0.12, n = 121, x = n/Neff = 0.92):

Shuffle. Permute the same weight multiset across the same atoms — six draws: median 1.62, and two of the six exceed the original 1.81. The consecutive-ordinate assignment is not required.
Poisson. Same weights on synthetic uniform positions — six draws: median 1.64, one draw at 3.59 — twice the anomaly, from pure noise. 1.81 was never a structural ceiling.
The arithmetic mesh. At Δ′ = 0.09 itself the envelope’s ratio falls to 1.49 (searched-0.12 control at the same mesh and depth: 1.06). The anomaly does not live where the arithmetic excess lives.

Answer: no — different phenomena. What makes the envelope special is its weight profile: a few heavy atoms over a light sea (Neff = 131 across 4,308 ordinates), which is precisely the profile that builds a wide, shallow near-void — the grid’s widest and deepest (§5) — at the bulk edge. Analysis, not arithmetic. The one place arithmetic was on offer, the mesh that over-orders the wrapped ordinates, it made the certificate better, not worse — as equidistribution-priced certificates should.

7 · The conjunction

Nine certified points, unchanged by design — the machinery has now idled through three documents, per the standing order. What this document does to the conjunction-shaped statement is surgery, not growth.

What died. “λmin ≥ c·Cn(μ) with absolute c, in the bulk” is refuted by construction, on the arc’s own material, with the counterexample demanded in advance by rh31 §8 (a). The failure is not a pathology; it is the prolate uncertainty principle doing exactly what it must.

What replaced it. The repaired conjecture is two-parameter: for a mirrored atomic measure whose widest thin arc spans K kernel wavelengths at resolution n,

λmin ≥ Cn·c(K),  c(K) = (π²K/2)(1 − l1(K)) —
exact on the crystal by the identity of §2; obeyed by every constructed void on zeta material within a factor 1.9 (§4); and vacuously safe on rh31’s whole grid, where K never exceeds 1.45 and c(1.45) is still order one. For zeta windows the hypothesis “K stays small” is not an assumption to add — it is what rh31 §6 measured: wrapped ordinates equidistribute, so the material cannot build the void that kills it.

What a proof now needs. Two steps, one of which is done. The concentration step — the void’s operator eats at most 1 − c(K) of the mass — is the classical prolate theory, measured here to 6.2%; on the crystal it is exact. The quadrature step — the atoms dominate a smoothed density on degree-n forms, so that the crystal computation transfers — is exact on the crystal (§2, one line) and is the single remaining analytic gap for real configurations: a one-sided Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund statement for Poisson-band point sets, loss bounded by a function of the gap statistic alone. That is a smaller, sharper target than anything the arc has previously called the missing lemma; and it is a statement about the spacings of the zeros, which is where the arithmetic was always going to have to enter.

Where the conjunction stands, plainly. Still about forms, not about ζ — a proof of the repaired conjecture would convert every emptiest-arc number into a floor for every window at once, which is the family-level statement rh30 asked for, but it bounds no zero and moves no zero-free region. What has changed is that the arc now knows, with a measured law and an exact identity behind it, what a certificate is made of: equidistribution of the wrapped ordinates at every scale down to one kernel width. The programme’s own diagnosis from rh30 — that as a counterexample search it is outclassed — stands; the surviving value is that the objects it certifies are now understood to two-parameter precision.

8 · What this document leaves

(a) the quadrature lemma
The one analytic step left in the repaired conjecture: for mirrored atomic measures whose emptiness profile stays above depth q at all widths beyond K kernel units, prove the atom sum dominates a smoothed density on degree-n forms with loss a function of (q, K) alone. On the crystal the loss is 1, exactly (§2); the concentration step is classical. The objects are Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities with gaps and Nazarov–Turán lower bounds; the honest failure branch is a real-ordinate configuration where domination fails at bounded (q, K) — which the death curve says would itself be a new kind of structure.
(b) the texture inside the safe zone
Below K = 1.5 no single coordinate organises the ratio’s [1, 1.4] fluctuations (best correlation +0.70 of the five tried, §5). Either find the two-parameter law — width × depth with the filler geometry priced — or show by construction that no low-dimensional statistic can: build two measures with identical (K, depth) profiles and measurably different ratios. The second outcome is cheaper and would close the question honestly.
(c) the certified set stays closed
Standing order, renewed a third time. The machinery idles until (a) produces the lemma — at which point every emptiest-arc number on every window becomes a certified floor wholesale, priced by c(K) — or until someone exhibits the failing configuration, at which point the arc will know that even equidistribution is not enough. Both outcomes retire the window search permanently.
