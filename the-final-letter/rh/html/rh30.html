rh30 · how many atoms the window can see

rh29 turned the arc’s Toeplitz matrix into the moment matrix of an atomic measure and left three questions. All three turn out to be about a single number: the mass of that measure divided by its heaviest atom. Two hundred certificates collapse onto one curve in it, both classical routes to a lower bound are governed by it, and it sets — in explicit figures — how high up the critical line this whole programme can ever see. Then §8 redraws the construction on a proper axis, and the redrawing exposes something about the logic of the whole arc that changes the direction: §9.

54 + 42 named checks, 46 + 6 s · five computed figures · 200 points of the (window, mesh, depth) family · the twenty-ninth in the series, after rh29

What is conditional and what is not. The measure picture is rh29’s, and it is conditional on RH: writing Eh(v) = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)² cos(γρv) assumes every ordinate is real. So §§2–5 describe the objects RH predicts, which is the right thing when the question is what the certificates are chasing. §§6 and 7 are different: the conspiracy bound and the reach are unconditional statements about what the arc’s certificates could and could not detect, and they use no hypothesis about where the zeros are — only the counting function. §8 is a construction and an identity, conditional on nothing. §9 is an argument about what the certificates mean, and it is the part to read if you read only one.

1 · Where this stands

rh29 established the identity that everything below rests on. Read the explicit formula from right to left with test function ĥ(r)²cos(rv) and, under RH,

Eh(v) = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)² cos(γρ v) = 2 ∑γ>0 ĥ(γ)² cos(γ v),

so the matrix the arc has been certifying since rh13 — Tn[j][k] = Eh((j−k)Δ′) — is the moment matrix of the purely atomic measure μ = ∑ρ ĥ(γρ)² δ at γρΔ′ mod 2π. The window sets the atoms’ weights; the mesh sets their positions. rh29 verified this against the prime side at every node of all five supports, and the residue at the matched cut r = 4800 was 1.38×10⁻⁷ on a row whose diagonal is 0.707.

It then left three items, and this document takes all three. They looked independent. They are not.

(b) the coupling
rh29 varied the window alone (the ratio climbed by 382) and the mesh alone (it fell by 228), found the diagonal flat, and found a separable model wrong by 65. It asked for a two-dimensional grid.
(a) the lower bound
rh29 located the obstruction to a ceiling as the norm it costs a polynomial to be small on {γΔ′ mod 2π}, called that a Diophantine question, and called it “not obviously hopeless”.
(c) the conspiracy bound
rh29’s off-line test is conditional on there being one off-line quadruple, since several at different heights can cancel at every node measured. What would make it unconditional is a bound on ∑|ĥ(γ+iσ)|² over a height range.

The grid answers (b) and, in answering it, supplies the number that governs (a) as well. The bound (c) needs turns out to be elementary — and the same decay that makes it elementary is what limits the reach of the whole method, which is §7 and is the least comfortable thing here.

Two regressions first. The zero-side row at the matched cut reproduces every λmin rh27b and rh27c published to 5.2×10⁻⁴ relative, and the zero table is rh29’s, cut at 4800, where it has 4,308 ordinates.

2 · The effective count

Here is the number. Define

Neff = ‖μ‖ ∕ (2 maxγ ĥ(γ)²)

— the total mass of the measure divided by its heaviest atom. If all the atoms weighed the same there would be exactly Neff of them; as it is, it is the reciprocal of the largest relative weight, and it says how spread the measure is rather than how many atoms it has.

window Δ′ = 0.48   Neff = 7.0   heaviest atom 14.197% of the mass   participation ratio 15.0
window Δ′ = 0.24   Neff = 17.3   heaviest atom 5.781% of the mass   participation ratio 38.4
window Δ′ = 0.12   Neff = 95.7   heaviest atom 1.045% of the mass   participation ratio 190.4
window Δ′ = 0.06   Neff = 207.0   heaviest atom 0.483% of the mass   participation ratio 454.7
window Δ′ = 0.03   Neff = 467.3   heaviest atom 0.214% of the mass   participation ratio 1104.1

Two things about it. First, it is set by the window alone. The mesh enters μ only through the positions γΔ′ mod 2π, and Neff is a statement about weights, so changing the mesh leaves it exactly unaltered — asserted to 1 part in 10¹² across all five meshes, which is machine zero. Second, it is not the number of atoms carrying appreciable weight: the participation ratio (∑w)²/∑w² is 190.4 where Neff is 95.7, and the two differ by a factor of about two at every support. Neff is the more pessimistic of the two, and it is the one that works.

The figure to keep in mind is that at the arc’s own support Δ′ = 0.12 the measure has 4,308 atoms in the table and Neff = 95.7. Ninety-six. That is the whole of the arc’s sensitivity, and rh29 had already noticed the mechanism without drawing this consequence: the searched window’s coefficient polynomial A(r) is periodic and the optimiser drove it near zero at small r, so the weights are enormously unequal and most of the four thousand atoms count for nothing.

3 · The collapse

rh29 asked for a two-dimensional (window, mesh) grid. Take a three-dimensional one instead: five windows × five meshes × eight depths, 200 certificates, each a λmin the arc could have published. That is affordable because from the zeros a row costs O(#zeros) per node at any depth — rh29’s observation, and the reason none of this needs a new sieve.

Against the depth alone the points scatter over 1.5×10⁷. Against n/Neff they lie on a single curve. Over the four bins covering n/Neff < 0.3 — half the family, spanning two decades of window, two of mesh and two of depth — the spread within a bin is:

n/Neff in [0.003, 0.01)   5 points   λmin/Eh(0) from 9.57×10⁻¹ to 9.99×10⁻¹   spread 1.04×
n/Neff in [0.01, 0.03)   10 points   λmin/Eh(0) from 7.02×10⁻¹ to 9.99×10⁻¹   spread 1.42×
n/Neff in [0.03, 0.1)   30 points   λmin/Eh(0) from 6.16×10⁻¹ to 9.88×10⁻¹   spread 1.60×
n/Neff in [0.1, 0.3)   30 points   λmin/Eh(0) from 4.92×10⁻¹ to 9.78×10⁻¹   spread 1.99×

Never worse than 1.99×. For comparison, binning the same 200 numbers by depth alone spreads them by up to 1.5×10⁷, and by window alone by up to 3.1×10⁷, so this is not a repackaging of either. And it fails past n = Neff: beyond n/Neff = 1 the spread in a bin reaches 5.8×10⁴. The collapse is a statement about the regime where the section is shorter than the measure is wide, and nothing past it.

What that explains

rh27c ran its five supports at n = 18/Δ′ + 1 — fixed depth in v, which is the natural thing to do and turns out to be the trap. Those five sit at:

Δ′ = 0.48   n = 39   Neff = 7.0   n/Neff = 5.54   λmin/Eh(0) = 0.0408
Δ′ = 0.24   n = 76   Neff = 17.3   n/Neff = 4.39   λmin/Eh(0) = 0.1154
Δ′ = 0.12   n = 151   Neff = 95.7   n/Neff = 1.58   λmin/Eh(0) = 0.2846
Δ′ = 0.06   n = 301   Neff = 207.0   n/Neff = 1.45   λmin/Eh(0) = 0.2731
Δ′ = 0.03   n = 601   Neff = 467.3   n/Neff = 1.29   λmin/Eh(0) = 0.2992

The three narrow supports are at n/Neff = 1.58, 1.45, 1.29, a spread of 1.23×, and their λmin/Eh(0) agree to 10%. They were not three supports independently agreeing on a ceiling. They were three readings of very nearly one point on this curve. rh27c called the agreement a ceiling, rh28 called it a saturation and spent a document on it, rh29 called it a coupling and failed to name it. It is an artefact of holding v fixed while Δ′ varies, which holds n/Neff nearly fixed too.

And the two wide supports sit further out, at 5.5 and 4.4, which is why they read lower. There was never a flat stretch with an edge; there is one curve, and rh27c sampled a short arc of it five times.

The separable model deserves its own note, because rh29 reported it as evidence of a coupling. On the full grid at n = 61 it is wrong by up to 7.1×10³ — worse than rh29’s 65 — but the errors are not spread around. Every large one is in the widest window’s row, and that is the only row whose depth exceeds its own Neff (n = 61 against Neff = 7.0, a ratio of 8.7). Over the rest of the grid the model is right to 2.2×. So the surface is not “coupled” in any interesting sense: it is separable wherever the collapse holds, and where the collapse fails it is not a function of (window, mesh) at all, because the third variable — depth — has taken over.

4 · Why no test vector helps

A quick consequence, because it closes two earlier items at once. The plane-wave test vector ak = eikθ/√n gives a*Tna = ∫Kn dμ with Kn the Fejér kernel, so its value can be read straight off the atoms with no matrix at all. Nine grid points:

window 0.48   mesh 0.48   n/Neff = 8.66   plane wave / λmin = 13.50×
window 0.48   mesh 0.12   n/Neff = 8.66   plane wave / λmin = 3.14×
window 0.48   mesh 0.03   n/Neff = 8.66   plane wave / λmin = 3478.82×
window 0.12   mesh 0.48   n/Neff = 0.64   plane wave / λmin = 1.09×
window 0.12   mesh 0.12   n/Neff = 0.64   plane wave / λmin = 1.10×
window 0.12   mesh 0.03   n/Neff = 0.64   plane wave / λmin = 1.03×
window 0.03   mesh 0.48   n/Neff = 0.13   plane wave / λmin = 1.07×
window 0.03   mesh 0.12   n/Neff = 0.13   plane wave / λmin = 1.06×

At every point with n < Neff the plane wave is within 1.10× of the true λmin; past Neff the gap opens to 3.5×10³.

That settles rh28’s (c) and rh29’s (c) together, and explains why rh29’s measurements looked so perverse. rh28 proposed a Hann taper to close “the last 7%”; rh29 measured six tapers and found the taper made things worse at v = 18, then found the null-placing polynomial — the vector that ought to be ideal — 65 times worse still. The reason is that below Neff there is no 7% to close, because the plane wave is already within a tenth of optimal and there is nothing for a cleverer vector to buy. Above Neff the loss is not a sidelobe, so shaping the sidelobes does not touch it. rh28 was optimising in the one regime where the optimum was already achieved.

5 · The lower bound, and the two ways it fails

rh29 read the obstruction as Diophantine: a ceiling is limited by the norm it costs a polynomial to be small on {γΔ′ mod 2π}, and equidistribution of that set is known. It called this the first question in the arc that is analysis rather than computation and not obviously hopeless. It is the first, and this section is why it is hopeless with the tools available.

The anchor

Start with the ideal configuration. If the atoms are n equally spaced points of equal weight, then Gauss quadrature for trigonometric polynomials is exact: ∑j q(θj)·(2π/N) = ∫q for every trig polynomial q of degree < N. So Tn is a multiple of the identity and λmin is the entire mass. With 4,308 atoms and n = 151 that is reproduced to 4×10⁻¹³. Everything the arc has ever measured is a loss against this.

Four controls

The loss splits into weights and positions, and the split is decisive. At Δ′ = 0.12 and n = 151, with λmin as a fraction of the mass:

grid positions, equal weights — the anchor  —  λmin/‖μ‖ = 1.0000
true positions, equal weights  —  λmin/‖μ‖ = 0.7728
true positions, true weights — the arc  —  λmin/‖μ‖ = 0.2846
grid positions, true weights  —  λmin/‖μ‖ = 2.9433×10⁻⁶

Flattening the weights onto the true positions costs a factor of 2.72. Flattening the positions onto an exact grid while keeping each weight attached to its own ordinate costs a factor of 9.7×10⁴ the wrong way — a catastrophe, because it winds the weights monotonically around the circle instead of scattering them. So it is the scattering that holds the floor up, and the scattering is exactly equidistribution of {γΔ′ mod 2π}, which is known unconditionally (Hlawka). rh29’s reading of the obstruction was right. The trouble is turning it into a number.

Route one: Koksma, through the discrepancy

For a trig polynomial q = |p|² of degree n−1 with unit coefficients, Bernstein’s inequality in L¹ gives ‖q′‖1 ≤ 2π(n−1), and Koksma’s inequality against the weighted star discrepancy D*w of the atoms gives

λmin(Tn) ≥ Eh(0) · (1 − 2π(n−1) D*w).

This is the arc’s first lower bound on λmin that is not a Cholesky, and at n = 2 it is within 7% of the truth at the three narrow supports — it is not a weak bound, it is a bound with the wrong shape. It dies at depth v = 1.32, where the certificates reach 18.

And it dies for the reason §2 named: a single atom of relative weight p forces a jump of p in the distribution function, so D*w ≥ 1/(2Neff) always, and the reach of this route is at most 1 + Neff/π whatever the ordinates do. The measured D*w is between 1.20 and 7.93 times that floor, so the bound is not losing to the arithmetic of the zeros. It is losing to the window.

Route two: Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund, through the arcs

Compare the atoms to the uniform measure through their own arcs instead. If the retained atoms cut the circle into arcs ℓj with η = max ℓj, then ∑ℓjq(θj) ≥ 2π(1 − (n−1)η) by the same Bernstein inequality, and

λmin(Tn) ≥ 2π · minj(wj/ℓj) · (1 − (n−1)η).

This has a genuine trade-off in it: keeping more atoms shrinks η and extends the reach, but drags min(w/ℓ) down towards the lightest atom kept. Sweeping the retention threshold, it reaches n = 228 at Δ′ = 0.12, against Gershgorin’s 28 and Koksma’s 12 — three to eight times further than anything the arc has had.

I predicted it would outlive the certified depth at every support. It does not. Its reach is roughly constant in the window — 228 to 319 — while the certified depth is 18/Δ′ + 1 and runs to 601, so it survives at the three widest supports and dies before the certificate at the two narrowest. Where it does survive, its value is between 7.8×10⁻⁶ and 1.7×10⁻⁴ of the truth.

The verdict

Both routes are worst-case over every trig polynomial of degree n−1 with unit coefficients, and both therefore lose linearly in n — Gershgorin through ∑|Eh(kΔ′)|, Koksma through the total variation of |p|², Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund through (n−1)η. Over the same range the truth falls by a factor of three. The mismatch is in the shape, not in any constant, and no sharpening of the constants would close four decades of it.

What is missing is an inequality that uses q ≥ 0 rather than only its variation. rh29 said this question was not obviously hopeless. With the tools actually available it is, and saying so is the useful part: the next attempt should not be another discrepancy estimate.

6 · The conspiracy bound, which closes

rh29 ended by naming its obstruction precisely: not the absence of a bridge from forms to zeros, but no control on how many off-line zeros could conspire. Its exclusion test assumed a single off-line quadruple; several at different heights can cancel at every node measured, and nothing ruled that out.

The control exists and it is elementary. An off-line quadruple at ½ ± σ ± iγ contributes 4 Re[ĥ(γ+iσ)²cos((γ+iσ)v)] to a row entry, which is at most 4|ĥ(γ+iσ)|²cosh(σv). Two facts bound the total of these over every zero above a height T:

the window’s decay is r⁻⁴, on the whole strip
ĥ is entire and its envelope falls like r⁻², so |ĥ|² falls like r⁻⁴. Off the line the growth is cosh(σΔ′) with Δ′ ≤ 0.48, so over the entire strip |σ| ≤ ½ the constant sup γ⁴|ĥ(γ+iσ)|² is within 0.6% of its value on the line. The bound costs essentially nothing for going off the line — all the σ-dependence is in cosh(σv).
the count alone bounds the tail
∑γ>Tγ⁻⁴ needs no zero-density theorem. Writing it as 4∫T∞(N(t)−N(T)) t⁻⁵dt and feeding in Backlund’s explicit bound on N(t)−θ(t)/π−1 at both ends, the smooth parts reproduce exactly the integral of the Riemann–von Mangoldt density against t⁻⁴, and Backlund’s error survives one order down:

∑γ>T γ⁻⁴ ≤ [ln(T/2π)/3 + 1/9] / (2πT³) + (0.56 ln T + 4.07)/T⁴.

At T = 4800 that is 3.36×10⁻¹². It is checked against the table at six heights from 300 to 10 000, and it is not slack — the real ordinates fill between 86% and 99% of it. The Backlund term is not decoration either: the smooth density alone is exceeded by the real table at T = 500, because N(t) carries a +1 that the density does not. That was a bug in the first draft of this section, caught by the check.

Put the two together with Gershgorin — a perturbation ε in every row entry moves λmin by at most (2n−1)ε — and the result is a height above which the entire off-line population is irrelevant:

Δ′ = 0.48   n = 39   λmin = 2.883×10⁻²   immune to everything above T = 4.34×10⁴
Δ′ = 0.24   n = 76   λmin = 4.982×10⁻²   immune to everything above T = 5.02×10⁴
Δ′ = 0.12   n = 151   λmin = 8.546×10⁻²   immune to everything above T = 9.48×10⁴
Δ′ = 0.06   n = 301   λmin = 4.815×10⁻²   immune to everything above T = 2.21×10⁵
Δ′ = 0.03   n = 601   λmin = 2.836×10⁻²   immune to everything above T = 4.81×10⁵

So: above these heights, no configuration of off-line zeros — at any abscissa in the strip, in any arrangement, conspiring or not — can move the certified floor at all. The worst of the five is 4.81×10⁵, and the zeros are known to lie on the line to 3×10¹², six decades further up. rh29’s obstruction is removed. No density estimate is used anywhere in it; Ingham’s N(α,T) would improve the tail from T⁻³ to T⁻³·⁷²⁷ at σ = 0.4 and buy a further 1.3×10³ at T = 20 000, but it is not needed and its constant is not explicit.

What has not closed is the other direction. This bounds what off-line zeros could hide behind; it is not an argument that there are none. rh29’s single-quadruple test excluded σ > 0.326 at γ = 107.17 and nothing here strengthens that.

7 · The reach

The same decay that makes §6 easy is a ceiling on the method, and this is the part I would rather not report.

Turn the Gershgorin estimate around. For a certificate at depth n to be capable of noticing a single quadruple at height γ, that quadruple’s contribution must exceed the floor it would have to eat: 4·ĥ(γ)²·cosh(σV)·(2n−1) > λmin. Since ĥ(γ)² falls like γ⁻⁴, that is a height:

Δ′ = 0.48   n = 39 (v = 18.24)   blind above γ = 3,477
Δ′ = 0.24   n = 76 (v = 18)   blind above γ = 3,901
Δ′ = 0.12   n = 151 (v = 18)   blind above γ = 6,334
Δ′ = 0.06   n = 301 (v = 18)   blind above γ = 11,067
Δ′ = 0.03   n = 601 (v = 18)   blind above γ = 18,580

Above those heights a certificate is blind, and below them the zeros have been on the line since Gram. Worse, at shallow depth the reach is nothing at all: up to n = 20 at Δ′ = 0.12 the threshold exceeds even the heaviest atom, so a certificate to depth v = 2.3 cannot see a single zero of ζ.

How fast does the reach grow? Measured across the depth ladder at Δ′ = 0.12, the exponent over the last three doublings is 1.17, 1.30, 1.11 — call it n1.2. That is a real growth rate, and it is nowhere near enough. Extrapolating it, the section needed to see as high as the zeros are already known to be on the line is of order n = 1.8×10¹⁰, against the 601 the arc has reached and the 601 that already took §3’s largest computation.

I want to be careful about what this does and does not say. It is a statement about this family of windows, whose transform decays like r⁻⁴; a window with slower decay would see higher, at the cost of being less smooth, and the trade is the Paley–Wiener one. It is also a statement about detection through a Gershgorin estimate, which is conservative — rh29’s sharper single-quadruple test reached γ = 107 at σ = 0.33, well inside these heights, by using the actual optimal vector rather than a bound. So the reach could be pushed. It could not plausibly be pushed by eight decades.

8 · The detrended helix

A construction note, and it turns out to carry a consequence. Since rh4 the gears have been drawn at the tail of their step: gear n has radius rn = n−½ and is centred at the partial sum Sn−1, so its marked point Sn lies on its own rim and is also gear n+1’s origin — checked here to 10−14. The origins therefore sit on their predecessors’ perimeters and wander. That is fine for a plan view and fatal for a helix: a helix needs an axis, and there is none. Project the twelve origins onto any direction in the plane and the best of 720 directions puts only 7 of the eleven steps in the same sense.

Detrend them. Put every origin on one axis, gear n at depth z = n−1, one unit apart, the unit being r1 = 1. The marked point on gear n is then the step vector itself, at planar angle

θn = −t log n + (n−1)π,

the torsion plus the half-turn that presents aaaa… as aAaA…, so that rneiθn = (−1)n−1n−s exactly — verified at every level. And then

∑n rneiθn = ∑n (−1)n−1n−s = η(s).

Closure becomes balance. In the offset picture “the helix closes” is a statement about a path returning to 0. Detrended it is ∑ rneiθn = 0: the marked points balance about the axis. Same equation, different kind of object — a condition on a point set carried on coaxial circles rather than on a path. That is the kind of object every document from rh13 onward is about, and it is worth having the picture agree with the algebra. At s = ½ + iγ1 the sum vanishes to 10⁻¹⁴ by accelerated summation; taken the honest slow way, 200 000 gears leave 1.1×10⁻³, the series being only conditionally convergent.

Anti-clockwise at every gear

A marked point is only defined modulo 2π, so how many times the curve winds between gears is a choice, not a fact. Taking each step to be the representative in (0, 2π] gives the unique lift that never turns clockwise; a monotone cubic (Fritsch–Carlson) through it is C1 and, because every slope is a weighted harmonic mean of positive secants, monotone as well. Sampled at 2001 points its derivative is everywhere positive — slowest 0.396 radians per unit of depth, fastest 8.03, so it is a helix in the sense of never reversing and not in the sense of constant pitch. The radius needs no interpolation at all: r = (z+1)−½ is already smooth and already passes through every gear.

What that lift costs is worth stating rather than hiding, because it is exactly rh5’s reversal radius arriving from the discrete side. The true step is π − t log(1+1/n), which is negative while the torsion outruns the half-turn — for the first 4 gears at t = γ1. The first is not a near miss: its raw step is −6.656 radians, more than a whole turn backwards, so the lift pays two revolutions there and one at each of gears 2, 3 and 4. Five turns in all, every one of them spent before gear 5, and the helix is exact from there on. The discrete threshold is n < 1/(eπ/t−1) = 4.018, and rh5’s continuous reversal radius x* = t/π = 4.499 is its large-t limit — the same phenomenon counted two ways.

gear 1 → 2   true step −6.656 rad   lifted step 5.911 rad   reverses
gear 2 → 3   true step −2.590 rad   lifted step 3.694 rad   reverses
gear 3 → 4   true step −0.925 rad   lifted step 5.358 rad   reverses
gear 4 → 5   true step −0.012 rad   lifted step 6.271 rad   reverses
gear 5 → 6   true step +0.565 rad   lifted step 0.565 rad   forward already
gear 6 → 7   true step +0.963 rad   lifted step 0.963 rad   forward already

Past the reversal the pitch settles. The alternation supplies π per gear and the torsion drags it back by t log N, a logarithm against a linear term, so the mean pitch is 0.958 half-turns per gear at 400 gears and 0.999 at 40 000. That limit is what makes the detrended picture a helix rather than a spiral: the turn per unit of depth has a limit, and the limit is the alternation alone.

9 · Where the conjunction stands

The three items first, then the thing they turned up between them, which is larger than any of them.

(b) resolved, and the earlier reading was wrong
There is no coupling and no saturation. There is one curve in n/Neff, and rh27c’s flat stretch was three samples of nearly one point on it. rh28 spent a document on a plateau that was an artefact of holding v fixed; rh29 spent a section on a coupling that lives entirely in one row of the grid.
(a) closed negatively
The Diophantine reading is correct — equidistribution is demonstrably what holds the floor up — but both classical routes from equidistribution to a floor lose linearly in n against a truth that is nearly flat, and land four to five decades low where they survive at all. rh29’s “not obviously hopeless” was too generous. The route needs an inequality that uses q ≥ 0, and I do not have one.
(c) closed positively
The conspiracy bound exists, needs no density theorem, and is comfortable by six decades. This is the one item that came out better than rh29 expected.

And then, from §8’s picture, a question I should have asked twenty documents ago. Detrended, closure is the balance of the marked points, and the weights are ĥ(γ)² — squares. rh29’s identity says the row the arc computes from the primes is the row the zeros give. A row of the form 2∑γĥ(γ)²cos(γv) is a non-negative combination of cosines, so it is positive-definite by Bochner, for every window whatever. Which means the prime-side row inherits that, and positivity is not a thin property to be certified point by point.

It is not a small correction, so here is the measurement rather than the argument. rh29 checked the identity at five searched windows. Checked at 24 coefficient vectors drawn uniformly from the sphere, the prime-side row and the zero-side row still agree to 5.2×10⁻⁶ of the diagonal — and all 24 of those random windows are positive-definite on the prime side, as all 8 tested are on the zero side. Not one indefinite form in the sample.

What that does to the logic. Weil’s criterion is an equivalence: RH holds if and only if the prime-side form is positive for every admissible window. The arc has been verifying instances of the direction that RH implies. A certificate that passes is a consequence of RH and adds nothing to a proof of it; only a certificate that failed would carry information, and it would refute RH rather than establish it. Nine certified points, nine balls around them, or nine thousand — the logical content is the same, and it is not cumulative.

So the programme these documents have been running is, read correctly, a search for a counterexample. As such it should be judged against the other ways of searching for one, and §7 has just supplied the number: at Δ′ = 0.12 this window is blind above γ = 6334, while the zeros have been verified on the line to 3×1012 by direct computation — 8.7 decades higher, and for a small fraction of the effort spent here. The certificates are rigorous and the machinery is sound. They are simply not pointed at the conjunction, and no number of further conjuncts will point them there.

10 · What this document leaves

The direction changes here, and §9 is why. Adding conjuncts does not approach a conjunction. What does is a statement that is already universally quantified over the family — and this arc has exactly one object of that shape, plus two ways of getting another.

(a) prove the collapse
§3 is not a certificate. It says λmin/Eh(0) is a function of the single ratio n/Neff, to within a factor of 1.99×, across two decades of window, two of mesh and two of depth. That is one inequality quantified over a whole family, which is the logical shape a conjunction needs and the shape no certificate has. Proving even a weak version — λmin ≥ c Eh(0) F(n/Neff) with F explicit and positive below 1 — would cover every window in the family at once. The family has 16 free coefficients, symmetrised to 31; the collapse says the objective needs one of them. A reduction from 16 dimensions to one is worth attempting even at low odds, and it is attackable with the machinery already here: the collapse can be tested to destruction on a much larger grid before anybody tries to prove it.
(b) the inequality §5 names
The same target from the analytic side. Every route tried bounds ∫q dμ below using only the total variation of q, loses linearly in n, and dies at a twentieth of the certified depth, while the truth is nearly flat because the minimising q is highly structured — it concentrates in a gap of the atom distribution. A bound that uses q = |p|² ≥ 0, or that localises to the largest gap instead of taking a worst case round the whole circle, would be a uniform statement rather than a point one. Beurling–Selberg majorants are the obvious family and the arc has never tried them.
(c) stop extending the certified set
Stated as a direction because it is one. Further points at further supports, further depths, further windows: §9 shows these cannot bear on RH whether they pass or fail, since passing is implied and failing is out of reach by 8.7 decades. The machinery built for them — the interval kit, the sieve, the parity split, the searched families — is worth keeping, because (a) and (b) will need to be checked numerically before they are proved. It is the accumulation that should stop.

One thing worth saying plainly, since §9 is a hard result about twenty-six documents of work. Nothing computed in them is wrong, and the identity, the conspiracy bound and the collapse are all worth having. What was wrong was the theory of how they added up. They do not add up; they have to be generalised, and §3 is the only place in the arc where a generalisation is in sight.
