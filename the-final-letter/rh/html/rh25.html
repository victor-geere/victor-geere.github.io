1 · The order

rh24 §6 listed three: search the notch-free windows, price the certified comb a smooth window would need, and find the ceiling over all windows of support Δ. They are taken here as (b), (a), (c), because (b) is what makes (a) possible — a notch-free window is not a spline, its Ch is not a cubic, and until the comb can be evaluated over six and a half million gears both cheaply and certifiably there is nothing to search.

One stream, eleven rows. Every (mesh, generator) pair in this document is read off a single pass over the 6,448,870 prime powers below 112,672,958 — the same sieve, gear for gear, that rh22, rh23 and rh24 used, so no comparison here is a comparison between two different sieves. The pass is driven by the certified tables of §2 rather than by an inlined cubic, and the first thing checked is that at c = 0 it reproduces rh24's own comb row for row at both meshes.

Then the regressions, before anything new is built on them: rh24's λmin for the triangle hat at all three section sizes to the last bit, rh24's M = 8 and M = 16 optima reproducing their published values, both bases still reproducing the direct Δ = 0.24 kernel at all seventy-six nodes, and the two families that share a rate agreeing on the same window carried between them to 1.8×10-12 of the pole it cancels against.

That last measurement is worth a sentence, because it is the reason §5 uses interval arithmetic and §3 does not. The recombination Eh(v) = Σd wdEδ(v − dδ) is exact algebra, but its evaluation is not: at M = 16 and v = 18 it adds sixty-one terms of size 570 to produce a number of size 10⁻⁴. The float row is fine for a search, where λmin is 10⁻² and the noise is 10⁻⁹; it is nowhere near fine enough for a certificate, which is why the certified rows are built a second time, in intervals, from scratch.

2 · (b) The certified comb for a smooth window

Everything certified in this arc since rh17 has used a piecewise-polynomial Ch, and rh24 gave the reason: the comb is evaluated once per gear, and a closed-form sinh costs six transcendentals where a cubic costs three multiplications. It also left the estimate untested. Here is the object it applies to.

(1) Uc(x) = sinh(c(δ − |x|))/sinh(cδ) on [−δ, δ],    Vc = Uc ⋆ Uc,    Ch = Σe We Vc(· − eδ).

Uc is the hyperbolic hat — §3 explains why it is the right generator; here it is simply a smooth Ch that the sieve has to evaluate. At c → 0 it is the triangle hat and Vc is the cubic B-spline exactly, which is the anchor for everything below.

The obvious closed form is the wrong one. Integrating Uc ⋆ Uc directly gives, on 0 ≤ x ≤ δ,

(2) Vc(x) = [ (x−δ)cosh cx − sinh(cx)/c + sinh(c(2δ−x))/(2c) + x cosh(c(2δ−x))/2 ] / sinh²(cδ),

and every term in that bracket is O(δ) while the bracket itself is O(c²δ³). The identity is exact, so the answer is right and the evaluation is not: the four terms cancel by a factor (cδ)⁻² — 1111 at θ = 0.03 — and the division by sinh²(cδ) does not give it back. Measured against the same function in the 40-digit interval kit, (2) is wrong by up to 9.0×10-15 where the regrouped form

(3) Vc(x) = [ 2(x−δ)sinh²(cx/2) − T(x) + T(2δ−x)/2 + x sinh²(c(2δ−x)/2) ] / sinh²(cδ),    T(u) = (sinh cu − cu)/c,

in which nothing cancels because every surviving term is already O(c²), is right to 1.0×10-17. The ratio is 865× at the worst θ, and it is worst at small θ — exactly where the family sits closest to the spline family it generalises. This is a trap of the same kind as rh22's two, and it is reported here for the same reason: the wrong version returns a plausible number.

The table, and what it costs. The alternative is to tabulate Vc once and interpolate. Cubic interpolation on a grid aligned to the knot at |x| = δ keeps every panel inside one analytic piece, so the error is h⁴sup|Vc⁗|/24 with an explicit fourth derivative,

(4) Vc⁗ = [ c⁴(x−δ)cosh cx + 3c³sinh cx + xc⁴cosh(cz)/2 − (3/2)c³sinh cz ] / sinh²(cδ),    z = 2δ − x,

every factor of which is monotone on its piece, so the envelope is exact. The nodes are built in the interval kit and the coefficients carry their own widths, and the panel count is chosen automatically: refine until the interpolation error drops below the double-rounding of the stored coefficients, past which a finer table buys nothing. That lands between 512 and 32,768 panels depending on θ, and leaves a worst-case table error of 1.4×10-17 across all 10 generators.

The cost, with all three written the way the sieve's inner loop has them — inlined, no function call, one generator, one shift, 600,000 evaluations:

(5) cubic 0.08 s  ·  certified table 0.16 s (2.09×)  ·  closed form (2) 0.13 s (1.77×)  ·  regrouped form (3), as a call, 0.57 s (7.53×).

Not a decade — under 2.1× either way. Which of the two smooth routes is faster is not stable across runs on a shared machine, and that is the useful finding: the table costs nothing to prefer, so it can be preferred for the two reasons that do not move. It touches no library function at all, and it is right.

What it costs the certificate is the number that matters, and it has to be charged correctly. The table's error is not a single global bound: it is a per-panel quantity, small where Vc is small, and it enters the comb row exactly as the arithmetic rounding does — through a local ℓ¹ norm of the weights against the gear mass. Adding a third term to rh23's binned radius,

(6) ρbinp = δu·Σbins sup|Ch′|·MΔ(p, bin)  +  ε·Σbins sup Qh·MΔ(p, bin)  +  Σbins sup Qtabh·MΔ(p, bin),

with Qtabh(u) = Σd|wd|·err(panel of u − dδ), the radius at n = 76 goes from 2.4023×10-11 to 2.9653×10-11 for the triangle hat — 23% — and by at most 23% over the generators certified here. What is left is not the interpolation, which auto-refinement has driven below the floor, but the double-rounding of the table's own coefficients, which no float table can beat and which the arc's existing ε already charges for the cubic. rh23's 768-bin radius for the hat is reproduced exactly at c = 0 before any of this is used.

3 · (a) The notch-free family, searched

rh24 §4 established the obstruction. For any h in the spline family, ĥ = φ̂δ·A with A(r) = Σdade−irdδ, and φ̂δ vanishes at every r = 2πq/δ whatever the coefficients are. The holes are in the basis. Refining the family pushes the ladder out by a factor M and never removes a rung.

The cure is not to search harder but to change the generator, and there is only one natural way to do it. rh24 named the object: h = g ⋆ ǧ with ĝ zero-free. Applied to the generator rather than the window, with g = ecs on a single cell, it gives (1) — and

(7) Ûc(r) = 2c(cosh cδ − cos rδ) / (sinh cδ · (c² + r²))  ≥  2c(cosh cδ − 1) / (sinh cδ · (c² + r²))  >  0

at every real r, because the numerator is |ecδe−irδ − 1|² and c ≠ 0 keeps the circle off the point 1. At the hat's first notch, δ = Δ/8, Ûc is 4.35×10-5 at θ = 0.24 where φ̂δ is 4.6×10-35 — which is not small, but zero. The whole ladder is gone from the basis, and it is gone by an inequality rather than by a search.

Nothing else changes. That is the point of choosing the generator this way rather than by writing down a smooth window directly. Uc is cardinal — Uc(0) = 1 and Uc(±δ) = 0 — so a window's coefficients are its nodal values, refinement is resampling, and the families nest exactly as rh24's did. Ch = ΣeWeVc(· − eδ) with W the fourth-order autocorrelation of the coefficients, so one sieve pass per (M, c) serves the entire family and rh24's cutting-plane search, its Lanczos best-response and its ceiling machinery all apply verbatim. And rh24's two reference windows are both inside it: the triangle hat is c → 0, and h(t) = sinh(Δ−|t|) is the rate-1 member, reproduced here to 10⁻¹⁵ with Û1A recovering rh24's 2(coshΔ − cos rΔ)/(1+r²) to 10⁻¹².

The trap. ĥ = Ûc·A still vanishes wherever A does, and a free search over a has no reason to keep A positive. It does not: the unconstrained optimum of the exponential family reaches minrA = -9.760. Its transform has real zeros; they simply miss the 1517 cached γ. Screening a window on a list of zeros is not the same as the window being notch-free, and this document reports both numbers for every window in the ladder for that reason.

Fejér–Riesz supplies the constraint. A(r) = |B(e−irδ)|² ≥ 0 precisely when a = β⋆β, so the search variable is β and positivity is structural rather than checked. The cutting plane cannot be used directly in β — the best response is an eigenvector in a-space and a ⋆ a is not linear in β — so the inner maximisation is a direct search on the accumulated bundle, which is a cheap quadratic form, with the outer loop adding the violated cut. A(r) is 2π/δ-periodic, so one period with a Lipschitz gap settles minrA over all real r; the reported margins are certificates, not grid minima.

The answer. At every section size the best provably notch-free window matches or beats the best window of the family rh22–rh24 searched, run through the same two search methods at the same budget:

(8) n = 76 (v = 18):   notch-free 4.6755×10-2  against  spline family 4.4676×10-2  — 1.047×.

rh24 measured the plain sinh window losing a factor 1.95×10⁵ to the searched spline window and read that as the price of removing the notches — "rh21's barrier trade-off made concrete". It was not the price of notch-freeness. It was the price of not searching: the sinh window is a single point of a family with 16 free parameters, and searching that family lands above the best window of the spline family — on the primes side, which is the side the certificates actually spend. The trade rh24 posited is not a trade at all at these support widths.

What (8) had to be careful about. A comparison between two families is only worth as much as the weaker of the two searches, and this one very nearly went wrong twice. Both families are therefore run through both methods at the same budget, and rh24's own published optimum is included in the spline family's corner. It matters: the β-space bundle search beats rh24's cutting plane on the spline family too, by 2.443×, taking it from 1.8289×10-2 to 4.4676×10-2 at v = 18. So the improvement over rh24 is the parameterisation, not the generator — a second correction to that machinery, of exactly the kind rh24 itself made to rh22's power iteration, and reported here for the same reason. What (8) then says is the narrow thing it should say: at the same search, the notch-free constraint costs nothing. And second, the certified notch margins are certificates over every real r, from a Lipschitz grid on one period of A, refined until the gap is a fifth of the minimum — an early ladder rung was reported at −1.7×10⁻³ purely because its grid was too coarse to see a floor that was really there, and the constrained search now keeps B's roots off the unit circle so that the floor is not merely positive but provably so.

The census says the same thing from the other side. Over all 1517 zeros the searched notch-free window starves 0 against the triangle hat's 50 and rh24's searched window's 84, and its typical weight, 8.48×105, beats rh24's searched window's 8.02×103. It is the first window in the arc that is searched, certified, and uniformly positive at once.

4 · (c) The supremum over all windows

rh24 §3's ceilings come from a duality. For a fixed test vector c the form cTThc is linear in Ch and hence quadratic in the coefficients, so its maximum over a family is a generalised eigenvalue; and since maxh minc ≤ minc maxh, the minimum over any set of test vectors is a proven upper bound on m₀ for every window in that family. rh24 observed the ceilings growing along the ladder and asked for the version over all h of support Δ — "a variational problem in ĥ, and the first question in this arc that is genuinely analysis rather than computation".

It is, and the analysis says the question has no answer. Start with the observation that makes it elementary:

(9) at v = 0 the comb is empty, because ln 2 = 0.693 > 2Δ = 0.48 and Ch is supported in [−2Δ, 2Δ].

So Eh(0) — the diagonal entry of every section in the arc — is pole plus arch and contains no prime power at all. It is pure archimedean, and the archimedean term is (1/π)∫Ĉh(r)ψ̃(r)dr with ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π growing like ln(r/2). Put the window's energy at frequency r₀ and the integral has to follow.

Explicitly, for h(t) = √2 cos(r₀t)φΔ(t) at unit energy, Eh(0)/Ch(0) equals ψ̃(r₀) to 0.05% at r₀ = 200, 10³, 5×10³ and 2.5×10⁴ — three decades, three digits. And inside the M-family, where the machinery of §3 computes the maximum exactly, the largest attainable value is ψ̃ at the family's own band edge to 0.28%: 2.809 at M = 8, 5.596 at M = 128, rising by about ln 2 at every doubling and not stopping. Nothing can cancel it. Both of the other terms are bounded uniformly in h at a fixed section size: the comb at node v is at most the gear mass there times sup|Ch| ≤ Ch(0), and the pole is 2ĥ(i/2)²cosh(v/2) ≤ 4Δcosh²(Δ/2)cosh(v/2)·Ch(0) by Cauchy–Schwarz on the support. Only the arch grows. So for any test vector c, choosing r₀ where the almost-periodic T(r) = |Σpcpeirvp|² is near its supremum makes cTThc as large as one likes. Hence

(10) minc maxh cTThc  =  +∞    over h supported in [−Δ, Δ] at unit energy, for every test set.

while m₀ = maxh minc stays finite, because for a given high-frequency h the minimising c is free to sit at a zero of T — and T, a non-negative trigonometric polynomial of degree n−1 per period, has plenty. The duality gap is infinite. There is no ceiling over all windows of support Δ, not because nobody has computed it but because the quantity it would bound is unbounded.

That retires the question and explains the observation that raised it. rh24's ceilings grow along the ladder because each is ψ̃ at its own band edge; the ladder is not converging to a finite all-window ceiling, it is diverging logarithmically, and what §3's ceilings bound is exactly their own family — permanently, not for want of effort. Any ceiling over a larger class has to control the frequency content of h, and the family index M is precisely such a control. The published ceilings remain theorems about the families they were computed for; nothing rh22–rh24 asserted is affected. What is retired is the hope of extending them.

6 · The bill, and what is left

62 named checks in 500 seconds over one 6,448,870-gear stream carrying eleven (mesh, generator) rows. The c = 0 rows reproduce rh24's inline-cubic comb at both meshes; rh24's λmin values are reproduced bit for bit before anything is built on them; every λmin is confirmed by inertia bisection and every positive-definiteness claim by an independent dense Cholesky; the Lanczos eigenpair certifies its own residual at 4.1×10-16 over 1028 calls; Vc is checked against quadrature, against the 40-digit interval kit, and against the cubic B-spline in the limit; and the two corrections of §2 and §3 — the closed form's cancellation and the free search's sign-changing A — each carry their own assertion.

Against the order. All three are answered, and two of them against the expectation rh24 set. rh24 expected the smooth comb to be expensive; it is not. rh24 expected notch-freeness to cost the peaks; it does not. Only (c) came out where it was pointed — as genuinely analysis rather than computation — and the analysis dissolved the question rather than answering it.

What this does not do. The certified cones are the same cones at the same half-supports rh22's audit counted, with one window in them that has no notch. A notch-free window is not a proof of anything about the zeros; §3's statistic is a property of ĥ, not of ζ, and it says only that this window does not happen to vanish near the 1517 heights the arc has cached. Nothing here is conditional on RH — unlike rh21 and rh24, this document never uses the zeros-side kernel — but nothing here is a step towards it either. The measure of what has been verified, against a conjunction over every Δ and every n, is still zero.

What is left. Three, and (c) has changed what they can be.

(a) The support. Every window in this arc since rh14 has had half-support Δ = 0.24, and rh21 fixed that convention with an argument about cost: a wider window pays twice, in the prime comb and in rh18's Klin, both growing like ea. The notch-free family changes the arithmetic of that trade, because Ûc's floor scales like c²δ and the searched window's weight no longer collapses at the notches. Nobody has re-run rh21's comparison with a window that has no holes in it, and it is the one lever the arc has never pulled.

(b) A ceiling that survives. §4 kills the min–max ceiling over the whole space, and it says exactly why: the archimedean logarithm, unopposed at high frequency. Any bound that survives must penalise frequency — a Sobolev norm on h, or a bound on ‖h′‖₂, or the family index itself. The M-family ceiling is the crudest such penalty and it is already a theorem; a ceiling in terms of ‖h′‖₂ would apply to every window at once and would be the first bound in the arc that is not family-bound. §4 gives the mechanism it would have to beat.

(c) The certified comb, generalised. §2 tabulates one generator to double precision and charges the result honestly, and the charge is dominated by the double-rounding of the table's own coefficients — a floor no refinement can cross. A table in the interval kit's arithmetic rather than in doubles would cross it, at a cost this document has not measured, and it would take the radius's rounding term out of the budget entirely. The sieve is 110 seconds; there is room.
