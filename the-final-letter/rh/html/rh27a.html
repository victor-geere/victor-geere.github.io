1 · The order

rh26 §6 listed a bigger cut first and priced it as the expensive one: "a certified arch over eight times as many quadrature nodes, which is the single most expensive thing in §5 and would want a better inner loop before it is attempted." The price was right and the reasoning behind it was not. The expensive part was assumed to be a new error analysis over the longer range. There is none to do.

The ground. No new sieve: every kernel row here comes off the same pass over the 6,448,870 prime powers rh22–rh26 used, through rh25's tabulated generator, with rh26's gear-mass histogram supplying the radius at every node. One window throughout — rh25's searched notch-free optimum at M = 16 — because §4's question is about the lattice, not the window, and changing both at once would answer neither. rh26's λmin on the Δ lattice and rh26's certified floor on Δ/2 are reproduced before anything is built on them.

2 · (a) A bigger cut needs no new error analysis

The certified kernel row is pole + arch − comb, and the arch is the one term that is an integral rather than a sum:

(1) A(v) = (1/π)∫0∞ |ĥ(r)|² ψ̃(r) cos(rv) dr  ≈  (H/π) Σj=0J ″ |ĥ(jH)|² ψ̃(jH) cos(jHv),

with H = 0.05 and the double prime the trapezoid's half-weight at the ends. Two things have to be justified: the rule — trapezoid instead of the integral — and the truncation — stopping at J instead of infinity. rh17 §1 justified both, separately, and the arc has since treated a longer sum as though it reopened the first.

L2 is about the rule, not the range. rh17's second lemma bounds the aliasing error of the infinite trapezoid at step h. The integrand extends analytically in r as far as the nearest pole of ψ(¼ + ir/2), at |Im r| = ½; rh17 works inside that at a = 0.4, where the modulus it needs grows only like e0.4v, and the Poisson aliasing then carries the factor 2/(e2πa/h − 1) =

(2) 2/(e16π − 1) = 2.96×10⁻²²    at h = 0.05.

That statement is about Σj∈ℤ against ∫ℝ. It says nothing about J, and changing J does not touch it.

L1 is about the range, and it is one-sided. ψ̃ crosses zero at r₊ = 6.289836 — a whisker above 2π = 6.283185 — and increases thereafter, so for J ≥ r₊/H every term dropped from the sum is a nonnegative multiple of cos(jHv), and the dropped part of the row is a positive semidefinite Toeplitz form. Truncating lowers λmin and can never raise it. rh26 §3 used this to explain why the cut at 600 manufactured false negatives; the same sentence, read forwards, says that any J at all gives a valid lower bound, and a larger J gives a better one.

So the two together: the quadrature rule is certified once, for every J; the truncation is free, for every J; and a bigger cut is the same theorem with a longer sum. It costs arithmetic and nothing else. That is checked and not asserted — every one of the 96,001 interval nodes of the AT table is verified nonnegative past r₊, which is L1 made concrete on the object the certificate actually uses.

What the longer sum buys, measured. The weight still dropped at v = 0, as the cut moves:

(3) R = 600: 3.12×10⁻³  ·  1200: 3.19×10⁻⁴  ·  2400: 4.64×10⁻⁵  ·  4800: 7.63×10⁻⁶  ·  9600: 1.18×10⁻⁶  ·  19200: 3.18×10⁻⁷

Quadrupling the cut divides it by 67.3 — the R⁻³ the envelope predicts, since |ĥ|² decays like r⁻⁴ and ψ̃ like ln r. Moving from 600 to 4800 divides it by 409. Against that, the aliasing factor of (2) is 15 orders below even the weight dropped at a cut of 19200. There is no cut in this range at which the rule is the binding error, and there never was.

3 · (b) The parity split, spent

rh26 §2 established that Eh is even, hence every section Th is exactly centrosymmetric, hence Cantoni–Butler splits it; measured the dense route getting 3.8× faster; and then issued its certificates on the whole section. Here the split is spent.

The reason it can be is that the change of basis is orthogonal. With J the exchange matrix and

(4) U = (1/√2)[[I, J],[I, −J]]  (bordered by 1 at the centre when n is odd),    U U′ = I,    U Th U′ = diag(B₊, B₋),

any perturbation E of T maps to U E U′, of exactly the same norm. So rh24's binned radius ρ — which bounds ‖E‖ through an ℓ¹ row-sum against the gear mass — applies to each block unchanged, with no factor to re-derive. And λmin(T) = min(λmin(B₊), λmin(B₋)), so a floor for each block is a floor for the section:

(5) λmin(Texact) ≥ min± ( s − ‖B± − sI − R′±R±‖ ) − ρ.

Measured at three sizes the saving is 3.81× — 2(n/2)³ = n³/4, as arithmetic says it should be — and it is what makes the 1201-node section at the finest mesh affordable at all. Two things are worth saying against it. The blocks are Toeplitz-plus-Hankel, not Toeplitz, so the O(n²) Levinson/Durbin recursion the arc uses for its fast λmin does not survive the split and gains nothing; the saving is confined to the dense route, which happens to be the one backend α needs. And the split is a constant factor, not a change of order: it moves the affordable section size by about 1.6×, which was the difference between reaching depth 18 at Δ/16 and not.

4 · (c) The descent, certified at depth 18

Backend α, unchanged, with the sum run to J = 96,000 instead of 12,000 and the Cholesky issued per block:

(6) Δ/4   n = 301, v = 18.00   λmin = 1.2097×10⁻³   ρ = 3.98×10⁻¹⁰   floor 1.1976×10⁻³   [8 s]
Δ/8   n = 601, v = 18.00   λmin = 5.9193×10⁻⁵   ρ = 7.89×10⁻¹⁰   floor 5.8600×10⁻⁵   [457 s]
Δ/16   n = 1201, v = 18.00   λmin = 4.7799×10⁻⁹   ρ = 1.57×10⁻⁹   floor 3.1594×10⁻⁹   [1123 s]

What was not a budget problem. rh26 §5 certified those three meshes at depths 9, 4.5 and 2.25 and said the shortfall was a cost cap: "They are not where positivity stops." That was true, and it left the impression that a larger budget would have gone deeper. It would not have gone this deep. rh26 §4's own ladder, at R₀ = 600, has the section losing positivity at v = 10.80 on Δ/4, 6.66 on Δ/8 and 6.61 on Δ/16. Past those depths there was nothing to certify at that cut, at any price. What moved is the cut, not the budget — and the budget only had to move by the factor §3 supplies.

What the three floors are. 1.1976×10⁻³, 5.8600×10⁻⁵ and 3.1594×10⁻⁹, against radii of 3.98×10⁻¹⁰, 7.89×10⁻¹⁰ and 1.57×10⁻⁹. The margins run 3.0×10⁶, 7.5×10⁴ and 3.0×10⁰ — comfortable at Δ/4 and Δ/8, and thinner at Δ/16, where the floor is 3.1594×10⁻⁹ and the weight still dropped past 4800 is what would decide any attempt to go deeper. That is the honest boundary of this document: Δ/16 at depth 18 is certified, and Δ/16 at depth 36 would need a bigger cut again, by (3) roughly a factor of four in R.

All three sections are confirmed positive definite in exact 60-digit arithmetic by an independent route that shares no code with backend α, and at Δ/4 the 60-digit inertia bisection also reproduces the float λmin that chose the shift. At the two deeper sections that bisection is not run: it is a hundred and forty Durbin passes at O(n²) in Decimal, which at n = 1201 costs more than the certificate it would check. That is a gap in the double-checking, not in the certificate — backend α's floor does not depend on either — and it is stated rather than quietly skipped.

5 · The conjunction, and where this leaves it

The arc's target is a conjunction: Eh positive definite on every lattice, at every depth, for every window — which by rh18 §5 is equivalent to RH. Every document since rh15 has added points to a set that has to become all of them. It is worth saying at each step exactly what the shape of that set now is.

What is new in kind. Before rh26 the certified set was a diagonal: one point per support, at its own mesh. rh26 made it a column of five points at unequal depths. This document levels the column — five meshes at one depth, 18. The depth matters because it is what fixes how much arithmetic the certificate touches: at v = 18 the comb carries every prime power below e18, and the section is a statement about all of them at once.

What is new in reach. A lattice of step δ resolves frequencies up to π/δ, and this is where the level column earns its keep. The Δ lattice reaches π/Δ = 13.1 — which is below γ₁ = 14.1347. On the Δ lattice alone the section cannot separate a single zero: every zero the certificate constrains, it constrains only through aliases. Δ/2 reaches 26.2 and admits 3; Δ/4, 10; Δ/8, 31; Δ/16 reaches 209 and admits 84. So refining is not more of the same certificate. It is the first thing in the arc that makes the certified statement about individual zeros rather than about their folded sum, and the level column is the first time that has been done at full depth.

What is not new. All five points sit at one support, Δ = 0.24. The conjunction is over every support, every mesh and every depth; the certified set is finite; and a finite set has measure zero in that index set no matter how it is arranged. Nothing here changes that, and the level column is not closer to the conjunction than the staircase was in any sense that admits a measure. What it does change is which direction is now cheap. Refining the mesh at fixed support is, after this document, a matter of arithmetic with a known price — R⁻³ in the cut and n³/4 in the factorisation. Varying the support is not; it needs a new sieve pass per support, and it is the one direction the arc has never moved in. That is rh27b.

A direction, named and not attempted. Everything this arc certifies is a statement about a quadratic form. Nothing it has produced is a statement about a zero. There is one bridge between them that is elementary enough to be worth naming. For a real even window g of compact support, ĝ is entire and ĥ = ĝ², so a hypothetical off-line quadruple at ½ ± σ + iγ contributes 4 Re[ĝ(γ + iσ)²] to the Weil functional — negative exactly when |Im ĝ(γ + iσ)| exceeds |Re ĝ(γ + iσ)|, and computable for the arc's actual windows on a (γ, σ) grid. A certified floor bounds what such a contribution could be hidden behind. Whether that can be closed into an explicit zero-free region — a statement of the form "no zero with |β − ½| > σ below height Γ" — depends on bounding the on-line part from above, which nothing here does. It is the only route in sight by which these certificates would say something about ζ rather than about forms, and it is untried.

The honest form of the gap. Suppose every rung of this column were certified to every depth — an infinite amount of arithmetic, but nothing new in kind. What would follow is that Eh is positive definite on δℤ for one window h of support 0.24. rh18 §5's equivalence needs it for every compactly supported window with BV derivative, and the passage from one window to all of them is rh15's density argument, whose constants rh18 computed and whose conclusion needs the family to be dense in the test class. A single support is not dense in supports. So the column, extended to infinity, is not RH; the column extended to infinity and to every support is. That is the shape of the remaining distance, and it is two infinities, not one.

There is a third, smaller, and it should be named too. The column's meshes are Δ/2k — a dyadic family, countable and nowhere near all of ℝ₊. rh18 §5's (L) asks for every mesh. What bridges that is rh15's continuity step, whose constants rh18 §3 computed: EΔ is continuous in the mesh with an explicit O(Δ²) modulus, so positivity on a set of meshes accumulating at every point gives positivity at every mesh. The dyadic ladder does accumulate at 0 and nowhere else, so even that bridge is not free — it would want a second ladder, at ratios other than 2, to fill in. That is arithmetic of the kind §2 made cheap, and nobody has run it.

6 · The bill, and what is left

33 named checks in 1612 seconds, over the same 6,448,870-gear stream rh22–rh26 used. rh26's λmin and rh26's certified floor are reproduced before anything is built on them; r₊ and the aliasing factor are recomputed rather than cited; every node of the certified AT table is verified nonnegative past r₊, which is L1 on the object the certificate uses; the block split is checked to factor both blocks wherever it factors the whole section; and the three certificates are issued from the same backend α rh19 wrote, with two parameters changed and nothing else.

Against the order. rh26 asked for a bigger cut and priced it as the hardest item on its list. It was the easiest, once the two lemmas already in rh17 were read together. The cost was arithmetic, and the arithmetic was paid for by a symmetry rh26 had already found and not used. Neither half was new work; the document is what happens when two existing results are put next to each other.

What this does not do. It does not touch the conjunction — §5 says so at length and gives the shape of the remaining distance. It certifies one window at one support. It says nothing about the zeros that is not already implied by the certificates rh19 through rh26 issued; a deeper section at a finer mesh is a stronger statement of the same kind, not a different kind.

What is left. Three.

(a) The support, which is rh27b. The certified set is a column because every window in the arc since rh14 has had half-support Δ = 0.24. rh21 fixed that by an argument about cost — a wider window pays twice, in the comb and in rh18's Klin — made before notch-free windows existed. A second support turns the column into a region and re-runs that argument at the same time.

(b) The cut, once more. (3) says the price of depth is R⁻³: doubling the certified depth at Δ/16 wants roughly four times the cut, which is four times the AT table and four times the row sums, and nothing else. It is a pure arithmetic ladder now, and the only question is where it stops being worth running.

(c) The 60-digit bisection at scale. Every floor in the arc before this one was checked twice: backend α, plus an inertia bisection in exact arithmetic that reproduced λmin independently. At n = 601 and above that bisection costs more than the certificate, so the two deeper floors here carry the dense 60-digit positive-definiteness check but not the eigenvalue. Running the bisection on the blocks instead of the section would cost a quarter, which is probably enough; it has not been measured.
