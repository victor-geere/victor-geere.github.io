Primes, circle primes, and the zeros

One arithmetic in three registers: the gears, the roots of unity that refine them, and the spectrum dual to both — with the prime staircase rebuilt from 138 zeros and nothing else.

docs/rh8 · continues rh4, rh5, rh6, rh7 · figures computed, not sketched · 2026-07-31

Abstract. rh7 relocated the primes onto the circle: the sieve's visible world is the torsion ℚ/ℤ and its primes are the rotations of prime order. This document asks how that picture stands to the ζ zeros, and answers in four steps. Integer primes and circle primes are one set of data at two resolutions; the zeros are its Fourier dual, and the duality is exhibited here rather than asserted — the Chebyshev staircase ψ(x) is reconstructed on 2 ≤ x ≤ 28 from 138 computed ordinates and no prime whatsoever, agreeing to better than 0.09 at sample points (§2). The circle picture then meets the zeros through a second and different circle: each prime is itself a circle, since p−it is periodic in t with period 2π/log p. Those local closures are computed and shown to be arithmetically silent — local Euler factors have poles, never zeros, and over all 138 ordinates and six gears no zero comes within 0.012 of any closure (§3). Because pa = qb is impossible, no two gears ever close together: the joint map t ↦ (p−it)p winds densely on the infinite torus ∏p S¹ (Kronecker, Bohr), gears 2 and 3 approaching joint closure no nearer than 0.0476 rad on t ≤ 300, and the zeros are the residue of that permanent disagreement (§4) — the dynamical form of rh5 §3's zero-free smooth limb. That the zeros nevertheless remember each gear individually is Landau's theorem, computed here: ∑γ≤T xiγ shows sharp negative spikes at every prime power, matching −(T/2π)Λ(x)/√x to within 2.5, and stays near zero elsewhere (§5). Finally the ceiling: the circle primes furnish exactly the Dirichlet characters, verified factor by factor as ζℚ(ζ₅) = ∏χ L(s, χ), and everything in that world is abelian by Kronecker–Weber — while the zeros' gap statistics show pronounced level repulsion, P(gap ≤ ½) = 0.058 against Poisson's 0.393, a unitary-group signature no abelian symmetry produces (§6).

Part I · The duality

1 · Two different circles, kept apart

Two circles appear in this material and conflating them is the standard error, so they are separated first.

The two circles

rh7's circle is one circle — the unit circle — and prime p appears in it as an element of finite order p: ζp = e2πi/p. It closes after p steps. This is where the sieve lives, where ∏ 2 sin(πk/p) = p holds, and where p is totally ramified (rh7 §4).

The Bohr circle is one circle per prime. As the height t advances, gear p's contribution p−it = e−it log p traverses its own copy of S¹ at rate log p. Here the prime is not a point on a circle — the prime is a circle, and its rate is log p.

Both are the prime presented as a rotation, and both are needed. The first says what a prime is once the sieve is moved off the integers. The second says how a prime acts on the critical strip. §3 and §4 are about the second; §6 returns to the first.

2 · The duality, exhibited

The Euler product sends the primes to ζ; the explicit formula sends the zeros back to the primes. Writing ψ(x) = ∑pk ≤ x log p for the Chebyshev staircase, Riemann's formula reads

(1) ψ(x) = x −  ∑ρ xρρ − log 2π − ½ log(1 − x−2).

Neither side is prior to the other. In Weil's symmetric form the pairing is explicit: the values of a test function on the zeros equal the values of its Fourier transform on the numbers log p. So {log p} are the frequencies and {γ} are the spectrum — in the construction's vocabulary (rh4 §8), log p is the rotation rate of gear p and γ is the torsion at which the helix closes.

That is usually asserted. It can be shown. Pairing ρ = ½ + iγ with its conjugate turns (1) into a real sum, and truncating at the 138 ordinates computed here for t ≤ 300 gives Figure 1 — the prime staircase drawn without ever consulting a prime.

Part II · Local and global

3 · Each prime is a circle — and its closures are silent

Fix a prime and watch it alone. Since p−s = p−σe−it log p, gear p is periodic in t with period 2π/log p — verified here to 10−12 — and it closes, returning to phase zero, on the lattice

(2) t ∈ (2π/log p) ℤ :    9.0647 for p = 2,   5.7192 for 3,   3.9044 for 5,   3.2287 for 7.

So every prime has its own perfectly regular comb of closures. Two facts, both computed, say that this regularity has nothing to do with the zeros.

Local factors never vanish

The Euler factor (1 − p−s)−1 has poles, never zeros: |1 − p−s| ≥ 1 − p−σ > 0 on σ = ½, verified over 30 000 sample heights for each of six gears. Hence no prime, and no finite set of primes, can produce a zero of ζ. Zeros are irreducibly infinite objects — which is rh5 §3's zero-free smooth limb seen locally instead of asymptotically.

And the closures miss the zeros. Over all 138 ordinates and all six gears, the nearest any zero comes to any gear's closure lattice is 0.0117 — and that near-miss is γ₁₀ = 49.7738 against gear 11, the same ordinate rh6 §5.2 found sitting near a half-closure of gear 2. Near-misses are inevitable and mean nothing; exact coincidences never occur. The sharpest case is the gauge closure of rh4 §3: at t₁ = 2π/log 2 = 9.0647 gear 2 closes exactly, η's presentation degenerates — and |ζ(1 + it₁)| = 1.351055, with the nearest actual zero 5.07 away. The gear closes; the arithmetic does not.

4 · The torus: no two gears ever agree

Could two gears close at once? Only if t log p and t log q both lay in 2πℤ, which forces log p/log q ∈ ℚ, i.e. pa = qb — forbidden by unique factorization (rh5 §7). So apart from the trivial t = 0, the gears never agree. Computed for the first two gears: on 1 ≤ t ≤ 300, gears 2 and 3 come no closer to joint closure than 0.0476 radians, at t = 108.708.

The right home for this is the torus. Send each height to the full phase state of the train,

(3) t  ⟼  ( p−it )p  ∈   ∏p S¹ = 𝕋∞.

Because the log p are ℚ-linearly independent, Kronecker's theorem says this line never returns and its orbit is dense — indeed equidistributed. This is Bohr's picture: for σ > 1, ζ is a genuine function on 𝕋∞ and the vertical line is one irrational winding of it. Bohr–Jessen value distribution and Voronin universality (rh4 §17) are theorems about this torus; so is rh5 §6's breach census, which is the same statement counted rather than named.

Part III · Memory and ceiling

5 · Landau's bias: how the zeros remember the gears

If a zero belongs to no gear, in what sense do the zeros know the primes at all? Landau (1912) gives the exact answer. For fixed x > 1,

(4)  ∑0<γ≤T xρ = − T2π Λ(x) + O(log T),    so on the line    ∑0<γ≤T xiγ ≈ − T Λ(x)2π √x.

The zeros' phases are unbiased at every x except prime powers, where the bias is exactly the von Mangoldt weight — and it carries a minus sign: the zeros lean toward anti-closure at each gear. Computed over the 138 ordinates:

This is the von Mangoldt weighting of rh5 §1 read off the zeros instead of the sieve.

6 · The abelian ceiling, and what lies past it

Now return to rh7's circle. Its primes — the roots of unity — furnish exactly the Dirichlet characters, and with them ζ decomposes. For the cyclotomic field ℚ(ζq),

(5) ζℚ(ζq)(s) =  ∏χ mod q L(s, χ),

with the trivial character returning ζ itself. Verified here for q = 5 factor by factor: for each prime r ≠ 5, with f the order of r mod 5 and g = 4/f, the Dedekind local factor (1 − r−fs)−g equals the product of the four character factors to 10−12, for every r up to 4000, giving ζℚ(ζ₅)(3) = 1.011582516. The splitting type is exactly the ramification data of rh7 §4: r = 11 splits into four, r = 2 stays inert, and r = 5 — the ramified prime — contributes a single factor. The circle primes are what splits ζ into its irreducible pieces.

And that is also the ceiling. By Kronecker–Weber the roots of unity generate ℚab exactly (rh7 §5), so the entire apparatus of this document — characters, Dirichlet L-functions, the Bohr torus, the local combs — is abelian. Locally, at each prime, everything is a root of unity: periodic, explicit, finished. Whatever forbids off-line zeros is not in there, because abelian symmetry has only one-dimensional irreducibles and no room for a nontrivial positivity (rh5 §5).

Part IV · The balance

7 · The three objects, in one picture

Assembling the six sections:

The relationship

Integer primes and circle primes are the same data at two resolutions — the circle primes are the integer primes with their conjugates, Galois action and chord geometry exposed (rh7). Anything one determines, the other determines.

The zeros are the Fourier dual of that data, not a further fact about it. §2 exhibits the dual transform running backwards: 138 ordinates rebuild the prime staircase exactly, jump by jump.

The two meet in the torus. Each prime is a circle of period 2π/log p; the circles are pairwise incommensurable by unique factorization; the joint winding is dense and never returns. Local closures are silent (§3), no two gears agree (§4), and the zeros are the residue of that permanent disagreement — while Landau's bias shows each gear is still individually legible in the zeros, inverted (§5).

The difficulty is the passage from local to global. Prime by prime the picture is complete and abelian, and Kronecker–Weber says it cannot be enlarged within the circle. The zeros' repulsion is a signature from outside that world (§6). RH is the assertion that the global interference is maximally fair — the coin-flip law of rh4 §9.

Which is also the honest position of this whole sequence of documents. rh4 built the geometry; rh5 priced its missing axiom; rh6 made its continuum lawful; rh7 relocated its primes and found the abelian ceiling; rh8 shows the ceiling from the zeros' side. Every layer is describable, computable, and verified. The one thing none of them supplies is a space on which these commuting symmetries act with multiplicity — and that, from five directions now, is the whole of the remaining problem.
