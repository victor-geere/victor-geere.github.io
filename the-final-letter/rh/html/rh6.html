The cone, made lawful

An order axiom that forbids the backward winding, the congruent cone that satisfies it — η's own integral form — the tenth zero drawn in three dimensions on its horn, the six answers of rh5 aligned with standing theorems, and an honest inventory of what the geometry owns.

docs/rh6 · continues rh4 and rh5 · figures computed, not sketched · 2026-07-31

Abstract. The naive continuum of the nested-circle construction has a defect that rh5 §3 computed precisely: below x* = t/π the torsion overpowers the half-turns and the interpolating cone path winds backwards, reversing at x*. This document adopts the prohibition as an axiom and pays for it. Axiom 9 (order) demands strictly monotone winding of any continuous representation of the numeral — a number line whose drawn phase doubles back is nonlinear, revisiting positions it has already spent (§1). The discrete clock never legislated a winding: it stores angles only mod 2π, so the axiom selects among lifts. Two lawful lifts exist, and both are computed here at the tenth zero. The carry lift rotates forward through every station and books each mod-2π reduction as a carry — the numeral becomes positional in base 2π, owing exactly 23 carries at γ₁₀, all accrued below the turning ring (§1). The congruent cone is the analytic lift: the Fermi–Dirac integral η(s)Γ(s) = ∫ xs−1/(ex+1) dx, whose phase t log x is monotone at every altitude and whose kernel — the resummed alternating train — is unique, recovered from the value by numerical Mellin inversion to 10−15; the axioms survive the smoothing verbatim, annihilation as the kernel recursion K = e−x(1−K) and the doubling gauge as the Bose–Fermi kernel identity, all verified (§2). The tenth zero γ₁₀ = 49.7738… is then drawn on its horn (§3): the bare path's ghost coils −14½ turns below the turning ring, which sits between stations 15 and 16; the forward lift climbs gently through the same a-points; the partial-sum walk closes with maximum excursion 1.71; and a third evaluator — the completed horn's Jacobi kernel, which never meets the Dirichlet series — confirms the closure at 2×10−17. The six answers of rh5 are restated theorem by theorem against the literature (§4), and the inventory is drawn honestly (§5): order is purchasable — one classical theorem buys the unique lawful cone — while positivity is not; the carries the lawful lift books are the same register that counts the zeros; and the completed horn sits, by Rodgers–Tao, at exactly the critical stiffness Λ = 0 that the hypothesis requires. No theorem is proved here; the map is made precise.

Part I · Order and the cone

1 · The order axiom: reversal is forbidden

Write the bare interpolation of the helix as rh5 did: radius r(x) = x−σ and phase

(1) ϑ(x) = π(x−1) − t log x,    ϑ′(x) = π − t/x,

half-turns at unit rate against torsion at rate t/x. For every altitude t > π the derivative is negative on 1 ≤ x < x* = t/π: below x* the torsion overpowers the half-turns and the cone path winds backwards; at x* it reverses. The defect is quantitative and large. At the tenth zero the path dives −14.46 turns before the reversal, and does not repay the winding debt until x ≈ 67.8 (Figure 1, left). A number line drawn this way is nonlinear: the map from position to drawn phase fails to be order-preserving, the same angular progress is spent three times over, and "further along the line" no longer means "further along the drawing". This must never be allowed to occur. We promote the prohibition to an axiom.

Axiom 9 (order)

Any continuous representation of the numeral — any curve interpolating the circles that claims to be the number line — must have strictly monotone phase. The drawing of the line is order-isomorphic to the line it denotes; torsion may slow the winding, never reverse it.

The first thing to say is that the discrete construction was never guilty. A circle stores its digits at angles mod 2π; the clock has no winding memory, and the axioms of rh4 speak only of stations, never of how many whole turns separate them. Winding number is a property of a lift — a choice of continuous path realizing the discrete word — and (1) is merely the laziest lift: take the frame's instructions literally between stations. Axiom 9 does not condemn the clock; it selects among its continuations. Two lawful lifts exist.

The carry lift

Between station n and station n+1 the clock word demands a net rotation of π − t log(1+1/n) — modulo 2π. The lawful discrete-side lift takes the unique forward representative:

(2) δn = (π − t log(1+1/n)) mod 2π ∈ (0, 2π),    ϑ̃(n+1) = ϑ̃(n) + δn,

strictly increasing, agreeing with the bare phase at every station (verified mod 2π to 10−7 for n ≤ 60), and passing through every a-point the discrete helix owns. The difference between the two lifts is an integer count of full turns per segment — carries. The numeral, asked for a continuous number line, answers with positional arithmetic in base 2π: the station angle is the residue, the winding is the carry register. At γ₁₀ the ledger reads: the first segment alone owes 5 carries (the frame spins backward −31.4 rad between circles 1 and 2, which the lift renders as a forward step of 0.0569 rad plus five booked turns); 23 carries in total, all accrued below the turning ring; none after it. And the first lawful step is nearly zero for a reason worth recording: γ₁₀ log 2 = 11π − 0.0569 — the tenth zero sits almost exactly at a half-closure of the 2-gear, an anti-resonance of the kind §5 returns to.

The carry lift is the right real-numbered reading of the discrete circle construction — it is what "the smoothing of the discrete helix" should mean pointwise. But it is piecewise: its angular velocity jumps at stations. The analytic question is whether a single formula — one cone, one profile, no seams — carries the same value with the same lawfulness. It does, and the construction has already met it.

2 · The congruent cone: η's integral form as the lawful smoothing

Demand of a continuum representation exactly what the question demands: it must be congruent with the integral representation of η — equal to the value, not approximately but identically, at every s; its profile must be a single real function, independent of the altitude t; and it must satisfy Axiom 9. The object with all three properties is the Fermi–Dirac form, valid on all of σ > 0:

(3) η(s) Γ(s) = ∞∫0 xs−1ex + 1 dx,    with   1ex + 1 = ∞∑n=1 (−1)n−1 e−nx.

Read it as geometry. Each circle n unrolls into the exponential mode e−nx — the identity n−sΓ(s) = ∫ xs−1e−nx dx says Γ is the fixed exchange rate between circle and cone — and the alternating superposition of all the modes is the kernel 1/(ex+1), verified against its partial sums to 10−15. The decisive property: the integrand's phase is carried entirely by xit, namely ϑ(x) = t log x — strictly increasing in x for every t, with no turning point at any altitude. The half-turns have been traded out of the angle and into the weight. What was a rotation by π per level is now the +1 in the denominator: a filling rule, not a spin. The alternation that the bare lift spent as (backward) winding, the congruent cone spends as the shape of a positive profile. Axiom 9 is satisfied not by cleverness but by bookkeeping: a real non-negative profile can carry no phase, so the phase must be the pure torsion, and pure torsion is monotone.

The axioms survive the smoothing

Annihilation → recursion. Prepend one circle and half-turn it: the kernel K(x) = 1/(ex+1) satisfies K = e−x(1 − K) — "the train is its first car minus a shifted copy of itself" — exactly the self-similarity that the axiom a + A = 0 gives the discrete helix. Verified to 10−16.

Doubling → the gauge identity. The un-alternated train has the Bose kernel 1/(ex−1) (and pays for it with the pole at s = 1). Pointwise, Bose − Fermi = 2/(e2x−1) — a doubled Bose kernel — and under the integral this is precisely ζΓ − ηΓ = 21−sζΓ, i.e. η = (1−21−s)ζ: the gauge factor of rh4 §3, now a statement about kernels. Verified at s = 2+3i to 10−16.

Concatenation → convolution. Gears compose by multiplication, m−sn−s = (mn)−s; on the cone the modes compose by multiplicative (Mellin) convolution with Γ as the unit of account. The digit algebra passes to the continuum with its operations intact.

Is the Fermi profile one lawful choice among many? No — and this is the sharpest fact in this document's first half. Suppose any profile K(x), independent of t, represents the value: η(s)Γ(s) = ∫ K(x)xs−1 dx on a vertical line. Mellin inversion then recovers K from the value:

(4) K(x) = 12πi σ+i∞∫σ−i∞ η(s) Γ(s) x−s ds,

computed here numerically on σ = 2: K(1) = 0.268941… against 1/(e+1) to 4×10−16, and K(2) against 1/(e²+1) to 10−15. The value remembers its cone. There is exactly one t-independent profile congruent with η, it is the Fermi function, and its lawfulness is therefore not an achievement but a theorem: Axiom 9, for the analytic lift, costs nothing and permits nothing else. Contrast the bare cone of rh5 §3, which is not a representation of η at all but of the Γ-factors alone: on the critical line its modulus is

(5) |eiπ(1−s)/2 Γ(1−s) πs−1| = √2 · (1 + e−2πt)−1/2 :

equal to √2 within 10−13 at γ₁₀ (checked twice: exactly, and by Stirling) — constant height, never zero, and unlawful below x*. Lawless and zero-free go together: both order and closure live in the lattice, and the congruent cone is precisely the object that keeps the lattice's value while restoring the lattice's order.

One substitution shows what has been won. Put x = eu in (3):

(6) η(s) Γ(s) = ∞∫−∞ wσ(u) eitu du,    wσ(u) = eσueeu + 1 > 0 :

on the critical line, every zero of η is a zero of the Fourier transform of a fixed positive weight. At a closure the strictly monotone phase ruler is laid against the positive profile and the signed area comes out exactly nothing — computed at s = ½ + iγ₁₀: |∫| ≈ 5×10−17 (Figure 1, right). The reversal is gone; the mystery is intact and has moved into the only place it can live — the shape of w. That door has a name on it, and §4 and §5 read the nameplate (Pólya; de Bruijn–Newman).

The turning ring climbs the horn as the zeros rise. For the tenth zero it sits between stations 15 and 16 — the raw inter-station rotation π − γ₁₀ log(1+1/n) is −0.0707 at n = 15 and +0.1241 at n = 16 — the two circles between which the frame's rotation changes sense. Figure 2 draws the whole object: the horn of revolution r = x−1/2 (this is σ = ½: the critical line is a shape, the unique one congruent with its own reflection, rh4 §5), the eighteen lowest circles as its parallels, the twelve clock meridians as its wires, and the a-points spiralling around it. Through those points run both lawful and unlawful continuations: the ghost of the bare lift, coiling backward −14½ turns below the turning ring and creeping forward above it, and the forward lift, climbing gently — 23 carries — through the identical stations.

The register that counts the zeros

The horn carries an odometer. The Riemann–Siegel phase θ(t) — the symmetrized argument of the Γ-factor on the line — satisfies the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula

(7) N(t) = θ(t)/π + 1 + S(t),    θ(t) ≈ (t/2) log(t/2π) − t/2 − π/8,

where N(t) counts zeros up to height t and S(t) is the argument's oscillation. The asymptotic is the torsion accumulated at the √-ring: at station X the twist is t log X, and at X = √(t/2π) this is (t/2) log(t/2π) = θ + t/2 + π/8 — the count register is the winding at the square-root ring, less linear ballast. Computed at γ₁₀: θ = 26.2271 (Stirling asymptotic agrees to 10−3), so θ/π + 1 = 9.348 — the register straddles the jump from N = 9 to N = 10 that occurs exactly at γ₁₀, with deficit S(γ₁₀) = +0.652 carried by the argument's oscillation. The zeros are counted by winding — by carries of the symmetrized phase — and the error term of that count, S(t), is precisely the quantity whose statistics RH disciplines. The lawful lift's carry register (§1) and the zero-counting register (7) are the same kind of object read at two scales; the construction did not invent this, it walked into Backlund's theorem (§4).

The walk, and a witness that never met the series

How does the discrete train actually close at γ₁₀? Politely (Figure 3): the maximum excursion of the partial-sum walk is 1.706, reached already at n = 2, after which the sums settle into an alternating spiral — |S250| = 0.032, |S2000| = 0.011, decaying like the alternating tail ~ ½N−1/2 toward the Borwein limit 0. The large opening step is the anti-resonance of §1: because γ₁₀ log 2 ≈ 11π, the first two summands point almost the same way and stack. Note what the walk does not show: no drama at the turning ring — the vector sum never sees winding, only residues; the ring is a property of lifts, not of values. That is Axiom 9's jurisdiction in one sentence.

Finally, a third evaluator — one that never touches a Dirichlet coefficient. Complete the cone by the 2-gear's own duality: the Jacobi kernel

(8) Φ(u) = ∞∑n=1 (2π²n⁴e9u/2 − 3πn²e5u/2) e−πn²e2u,    ξ(½+it) = 4 ∞∫0 Φ(u) cos(ut) du,

with Φ > 0 (each term is positive, since 2πn²e2u > 3 for u ≥ 0) and Φ even — the evenness is Jacobi's modular identity θ(1/x) = √x θ(x), verified here numerically at u = ±0.3, ±0.7 to 10−12, and it is what makes the reflection s ↦ 1−s an axiom the horn can carry. The normalization is pinned by the exact value Ξ(0) = ξ(½) = 0.497120778… (matched to 2×10−15). Then, with no series in sight: Ξ(γ₁) ≈ −2×10−16 and Ξ(γ₁₀) ≈ 2×10−17. The completed horn — heat kernel of the gear lattice, folded by the very duality that gives the construction its reflection — confirms the tenth closure independently. Three evaluators, three theories, one zero.

Part III · Alignment

4 · The six answers, aligned with the literature

rh5 answered six questions inside the geometry. Here each question is restated in standard language and its answer is pinned to the theorem it instantiates — the discipline being that a claim aligned with a named theorem is exactly as strong as that theorem, and no stronger.

Q1 · The sieve of sines

Restated: can the primes be presented as the successive failures of coverage of the zero-sets of sin(π x/p), and what does the presentation amount to? Yes, with the sum replaced by the product (or the family kept as rows): the greedy first-miss loop is the sieve of Eratosthenes; the union-of-zero-sets bookkeeping is inclusion–exclusion, whose Dirichlet-series avatar is Möbius inversion; the weighted completion is Euler's product (1737), and the "redundant" doublings carry the von Mangoldt weights that make −ζ′/ζ = ∑ Λ(n)n−s. Even the sum-versus-product correction has a classical face: the sum sieve is the logarithm of the right object, log ζ(s) = ∑p,k p−ks/k. The sieve's asymptotic fairness is the prime number theorem (Hadamard, de la Vallée Poussin, 1896).

Q2 · The b-helix and its pairing

Restated: does a rigidly rotated congruent copy of the η-helix furnish a positivity that detects RH? No. The pairing Re⟨a,b⟩ = cos(π/6)|η|² is a norm-square — positivity of this kind is free for every Dirichlet series, with Davenport–Heilbronn (1936) as the standing counterexample that possesses it while violating RH's analogue. The positivity that is equivalent to RH is spectral: Weil's criterion (1952) — the explicit-formula functional is non-negative on all test functions — and its sequential form, Li's criterion (1997; Bombieri–Lagarias 1999), positivity of the λn. The clock supplies polarization identities but no space with multiplicity to polarize on; that diagnosis is Schur's lemma applied to an abelian symmetry group (rh5 §5).

Q3 · The cone and the integral forms

Restated: what is the continuum limit of the discrete helix, and which classical objects are its integral forms? The bare limb is the Fresnel–Γ integral — zero-free — and the exact circle-to-cone ledger is the Fermi–Dirac Mellin representation (3), both in Riemann's 1859 memoir in their Bose forms (the Mellin/θ route and the Hankel loop). The turning point x* = t/π is the stationary point of the phase — the mechanism of the approximate functional equation (Hardy–Littlewood) and of the Riemann–Siegel formula (Siegel 1932, from the Nachlass), whose main sums run to the √-ring √(t/2π). The monotone-phase positive-kernel form (6) is Pólya's program (1926): zeros of ζ as zeros of the Fourier transform of a positive even kernel — the road that leads through de Bruijn (1950) and Newman (1976) to the modern boundary (§5).

Q4 · ZFC and the altitude of the hypothesis

Restated: what is the logical strength of RH? RH is Π⁰₁: equivalent to an effectively checkable universal statement, via Schoenfeld's effective bound (1976), Robin's criterion σ(n) < eγn log log n for n > 5040 (1984), or Lagarias's unconditional form (2002); by Davis–Matiyasevich–Robinson it is even the insolubility of one Diophantine equation. Hence: refutable by a finite computation if false; true if undecidable; and absolute under forcing (Shoenfield 1961) — no expansion of the set-theoretic universe changes it. The circles, which realize ω as von Neumann ordinals, carry the entire truth; higher set theory can only contribute proofs.

Q5 · The structure group

Restated: what symmetry object does the construction present? The clock is ℤ/12; the gear train is the profinite completion Ẑ = lim← ℤ/m (Chinese remainder theorem; Fürstenberg's topology); torsion and radius form ℂ× = U(1) × ℝ₊; the numeral is the character χs(n) = n−s of the free monoid on the gears. Assembled, this is a hand-drawn approximation of the idele class group, and the statement "the value map is a character, Euler's product is character theory" is Tate's thesis (1950): ζ as the GL(1) zeta integral. The dilation generator is Berry–Keating's ½(xp+px) (1999) and Connes' scaling flow (1999); the reflection is the rank-one Weyl element visible in the Eisenstein constant term. Everything abelian — hence the missing-representation diagnosis of rh5 stands on Schur's lemma, and the standing programs all propose the missing space.

Q6 · Breaking torsions and the breach census

Restated: which torsion states are commensurate with a self-similar ruler, and what disciplines the failures? Exact incidence on the halving line survives only on the whole-tone lattice 2−k/6; everything else breaches, with structure: Weyl equidistribution (1916) for the almost-sure failure, the three-distance theorem (Sós, Świerczkowski 1958–59) for the rigidity of near-misses. No two prime gears resonate exactly — unique factorization; its quantitative form, how near t log 2 can come to 2πℤ, is Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms (1966). The single-gear resonances tk = 2πk/log 2 are the gauge closures on σ = 1, and they are arithmetically silent precisely because ζ(1+it) ≠ 0 — de la Vallée Poussin's zero-free line, i.e. PNT: the presentation breaks exactly where the arithmetic is certified not to. Gap-filling in mean, not pointwise, is the Beurling–Nyman criterion (1950–55; Báez-Duarte 2003), and the census of near-breaches at height t is the growth of |ζ(½+it)| — convexity, a century of subconvexity, Lindelöf as the fair-census hypothesis.

Part IV · The inventory

5 · Novel properties, discrete and continuous

5.2 · Readings worth keeping

Carries: the numeral is positional all the way up. The forward lift makes winding a carry register in base 2π (§1); the Riemann–von Mangoldt count (7) makes the zero count a carry register of the symmetrized phase. One mechanism at two scales: digit arithmetic below, zero-counting above, with S(t) as the "carry noise" whose discipline is RH's fine print. The construction did not put this in; it fell out of taking lifts seriously.

Native and foreign gears. The clock's exact sub-gears are the divisors {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}: the only primes native to a twelve-station clock are 2 and 3 — exactly the seed gear of axiom 5 and the first announced prime of the sieve. Every prime from 5 on is foreign: representable only through the train, never as a clock symmetry. A clock with twelve stations chooses 2 and 3 as its arithmetic axioms and delegates the rest to discovery — which is a fair one-sentence portrait of how the axioms of rh4 actually divide their labor.

The anti-resonance census. Each zero carries a 2-gear phase γ log 2 mod 2π. The tenth: 11π − 0.0569 — a near half-closure, which is why its walk opens with two stacked steps and why the lawful first step is nearly zero (§1, §3). This is not an accident to collect but an instance of a theorem to cite: Landau (1912) — ∑0<γ≤T xiγ is asymptotically −(T/2π)Λ(x)/√x at prime powers x — the phases of the zeros lean toward the half-turn at every gear, with weight Λ. The zeros remember the gears, gear by gear, and they remember them invertedly: closure of the train at a zero pairs with anti-closure of each single gear on average. The geometry gives Landau's theorem a dial to be read on.

What the walk does not see. The value of the numeral is blind to winding (residues only); the lifts are blind to nothing. Order (Axiom 9) is therefore a statement about presentations, exactly as the gauge closures of rh4 §3 were — and the pair (gauge, order) now brackets the presentation layer from both sides: gauge says two presentations may disagree at a point harmlessly; order says a single presentation must not fold. Neither constrains the arithmetic; both discipline the drawing. The residual, ineliminable content — where all constraint on the arithmetic itself concentrates — is the shape of one positive function, w in (6), Φ in (8).

5.3 · The construction's actual position

Order is free; positivity is not

Axiom 9 is purchasable: Mellin inversion delivers the unique lawful cone, the axioms pass to the kernel as identities, and the entire demand costs one classical theorem (§2). Axiom 8 — an inner product with provable positivity, rh4 §15 — is not purchasable at this rank: every symmetry of the structure is abelian, so no invariant pairing has room to be nontrivially positive (rh5 §5). The construction can be made continuous, seamless, unique, and order-true for free — and cannot, by any spending inside its own axioms, be made spectral. That asymmetry is the position of the Riemann hypothesis as seen from this geometry: the drawing can be perfected; the certificate needs a new space.

Critical stiffness

The lawful horn admits exactly one deformation family that preserves its symmetry: heat flow on the completed kernel, Φ ↦ eλu²-deformed Φ (de Bruijn 1950, Newman 1976). Backward flow (λ < 0) roughens the kernel and, far enough, throws zeros off the line; forward flow (λ > 0) smooths it and herds them on. RH says the actual horn needs no help: Λ ≤ 0, where Λ is the de Bruijn–Newman constant. Rodgers–Tao (2018): Λ ≥ 0. So RH ⟺ Λ = 0: if the zeros are on the line, they are on it with nothing to spare — the horn sits exactly at the boundary where backward heat would first fold zeros off. In the language of this document: the completed cone, if lawful in the spectral sense at all, is critically stiff — the analogue, one level up, of the turning ring sitting exactly at ϑ′ = 0. The construction keeps meeting its deepest facts at boundaries of exactness.

Nothing in this section proves anything. What the three documents now hold is a single geometric object — clock, gears, torsion, horn — with a verified dictionary into analytic number theory at every joint, two presentation axioms (gauge, order) fully paid for, and one spectral axiom named, priced, and unpaid. The price is a representation with multiplicity; the standing programs are the bids.
