1 · The order

rh22 §7 listed three items and put them in the order a successor should take them: the larger family, the certified route at n = 76, and the window at other meshes. They are taken here in a different order, because the second turned out to be the one the other two lean on — every floor quoted below is limited by the radius of the row it is computed from, and §2 makes that radius smaller for every window at once.

Everything runs on one sieve. The comb at horizon v = 18.48 needs every prime power below ev+2δ = 112,672,958, and one streaming pass over those 6,448,870 gears supplies all three meshes this document uses — δ = 0.015, δ = 0.03 and Δ = 0.24 — so no comparison below is between two different sieves. Before anything new is computed, rh22's own numbers are rebuilt from it: the hat's λmin at all three section sizes to the last bit, the searched window's at n = 76, both bases reproducing the direct Δ = 0.24 kernel at all seventy-six nodes, and both at the hat's own energy 2Δ/3.

2 · (ii) The radius, and what governs it

A certified floor is s − ‖residual‖ − ρround − ρD, and at v = 18 the term that decides everything is ρround, the proven radius of the prime comb. rh22 computed it by recombination. The composite kernel is Eh(pΔ) = Σd wdEδ(8p−d), so it bounded the error of that sum term by term:

(1) ρrecp  =  Σd |wd| · [ (2/3)·δu + δ·7.8×10⁻¹⁶ ] · Mδ(8p−d),

with Mδ(k) the gear mass within 2δ of the node kδ, and 2/3 = sup|Cδ′| — rh19's Lipschitz constant for the cubic B-spline. rh22 measured that this costs 4.6× more than rh19's own direct rows at Δ = 0.24, and §7 proposed building the composite rows directly to recover it. The direct bound is

(2) ρdirp  =  [ sup|Ch′|·δu + sup|Ch|·1.17×10⁻¹⁵ ] · MΔ(p).

The inference was wrong. (1) and (2) are governed by different norms — Σ|wd| in one, sup|Ch′| in the other — and which is smaller depends on the window. For the triangle hat sup|Ch′| is exactly 2/3, rh19's own constant, and (2) beats (1) by 4.64×, which is the number rh22 measured. For the searched window Ch oscillates and sup|Ch′| = 11.95, 18× the hat's, so (2) is 2.29× worse. rh22 read a property of the hat as a property of the route.

The bound that beats both. It needs no new computation, only a better accounting. In the recombined sum the twenty-nine terms are evaluated at ud = ln m − pΔ + dδ, and the error in each comes from the same error in ln m — δu does not depend on d. So the first-order error of the whole sum is δu·Σd wdCδ′(ud) = δu·Ch′(v), v = ln m − pΔ. The per-term rounding does not share a sign pattern and keeps a local ℓ¹ norm, Qh(v) = Σd|wdCδ(v−dδ)|. Splitting the support [0, 2Δ] into twenty-four bins and asking the sieve where the mass actually falls:

(3) ρbinp  =  δu · Σbins supbin|Ch′| · MΔ(p, bin)  +  1.17×10⁻¹⁵ · Σbins supbinQh · MΔ(p, bin).

The result is 9.9× smaller than rh22's for the hat and 1.93× for the searched window — better than either of rh22's bounds, for both windows, at every section size. The mechanism is visible in the figure: sup|Ch′| = 11.95 is attained in a narrow band near the spline's steepest point, and that band carries a small fraction of the gear mass. A supremum over a support is the wrong constant when the measure is not uniform.

What it buys. rh22 could not certify the triangle hat at n = 76: its λmin there is 1.558×10-10 and the recombined radius was 2.539×10-10, a margin of 0.61× — below one, so the Cholesky had nothing to spend. With (3) the radius is 2.568×10-11 and the margin is 6.1×. The section is certified, floor 1.285×10-10. That is rh22's own loose end, closed by arithmetic that was available all along.

3 · (i) The larger family

rh22 searched h = Σkakφδ(·−kδ) with δ = Δ/8 and |k| ≤ 7 — eight free coefficients after symmetry — and asked what δ = 0.015 would give. It said seventeen. It is sixteen: with Δ = Mδ the support stays exactly [−Δ, Δ] only for |k| ≤ M−1, so M = 16 gives thirty-one coefficients and sixteen free ones. A small correction, and it matters only because the whole question is how much the count is worth.

The families are nested, exactly. A window of the M = 8 family is piecewise linear on the δ = 0.03 grid; sampling it on the δ = 0.015 grid and interpolating linearly returns the same function, because linear interpolation of a piecewise-linear function on a refined grid is exact. Refining rh22's optimum this way reproduces its λmin to 5.9×10-12 at every section size — the residue is the inertia bisection's own floor. So the two searches run over nested sets and the comparison is a real one.

The search gains 2.2× at v = 4.08, 7.7× at v = 12 and 7.4× at v = 18. What the number means is fixed by the ceiling of rh22 §4 — for any test vector c, maxhminc ≤ mincmaxh, so the minimum over the cutting planes the search itself generates is a proven upper bound on m₀ for every window in the family. In the sixteen-coefficient family that ceiling sits 1.80–3.17× above the optimum, against 2.33–2.69× in rh22's. Comparable — and that is the answer to item (i). rh22's search was near its own family's best, exactly as its ceiling claimed; what limited it was the family, and doubling the family buys another 2.2–7.7×. Nothing here says where the supremum over all windows of support Δ sits: each ceiling bounds only its own family, and the sequence of families is not bounded by any of them.

The searched windows are shown above the plot. Both oscillate; the sixteen-coefficient one oscillates faster, which is what makes §2's binned bound matter more the further this line is pushed — a finer family has a steeper Ch′ and would be punished hardest by rh22 §7's direct bound.

4 · (iii) The window at other meshes

rh20 scanned six thousand mesh ratios θ and found that 1020 of them gain almost nothing over a single mesh, and that every such failure is caused by one of exactly four zeros that Δ = 0.24 already starves — γ = 52.970, 79.337, 103.726, 105.447, each within 0.05 of a notch of both meshes. It called the mechanism arithmetic and finite rather than Diophantine, and closed the route: no finite family of meshes carries (W).

Those four zeros are reproduced here exactly. And they are a property of the hat's transform. The hat's weight at γ is |BΔ(γ)|² = (Δ sinc²(γΔ/2))², whose zeros sit at γ = 2πq/Δ; a zero that lands near one is starved by construction. A general window in the family has |ĥ(γ)|² = |φ̂δ(γ)|²A(γ)², A(γ) = Σkakcos(kγδ), and its notches are wherever the search put the roots of A — not at 2πq/Δ.

The searched window's four worst zeros are a different set — not one of rh20's four is among them — and they are 1.3×105× higher. They are also flat: the hat's four are strung over five orders of magnitude, a lottery on how near a notch each fell, while the searched window's four sit inside a factor 4.0 of each other. Nothing is starved.

The second mesh becomes nearly redundant. Rerunning rh20's scan: the hat's floor over the first forty zeros is 8.087×10-10 at one mesh and 1.361×10-5 at its best pair, a gain of 16834×. The searched window's floor at one mesh is 1.043×10-4 — 7.66× above what the hat achieves with two — and its best pair reaches 9.085×10-4, a gain of only 8.7×. A second mesh was worth four orders of magnitude to the hat because it was patching four holes. There are no holes to patch.

rh20's constant does not survive; its conclusion does. rh20's ceiling was c₀ = 2.1002465 with |BΔ(γ)|² ≤ c₀/γ² for every Δ, and the impossibility of (W) for any finite family of meshes followed from it. The searched window exceeds c₀/γ² by 41× — c₀ was a statement about the triangle hat's transform, and this document has changed the transform. What replaces it is rh21's barrier, w ≤ πC(0)/T, which was proved for every compactly supported window and which the searched window still obeys. rh20's route stays closed; the proof of its closure now runs through rh21 rather than through c₀. This is exactly the kind of drift rh22 §2's audit was built to catch, and it is recorded here in the same terms.

The (W) side, extended. rh20 certified six union node sets — the interleaved nodes of two incommensurable meshes — as positive definite in exact arithmetic. All six are rebuilt here with the searched window, and all six stay positive definite, with minimum pivots up by 1153–3274×. The hardest of them, θ = 16001/24000, has nodes as close as 3×10⁻⁵ apart and rh20's pivot there was 2.758×10-9; it is now 3.180×10-6.

5 · The certificates

Everything above is a float measurement until it goes through rh19's backend α. The rows are built the way rh22 §7 asked and §2 explains: the archimedean integral and pole are computed directly at the Δ = 0.24 nodes in certified interval arithmetic, which needs one new object — an interval table for A(r)² = (Σkakcos(krδ))² at the 12001 quadrature nodes — and costs seventy-six rows instead of the six hundred and seventeen the δ-basis route needs. The comb midpoint is the exact recombination, its radius is (3), and the trapezoid aliasing radius is inherited from rh22 and scaled by Σ|wd|. Lemma L1 — that truncating the archimedean integral at R₀ drops a positive-semidefinite piece — holds for any window, since the dropped form is (1/π)∫|ĥ|²ψ̃|ΣcpeiprΔ|².

Nine certificates: three windows at three section sizes, none missing.

Two regressions hold the lane honest. The hat's certified floor at n = 18 and n = 51 reproduces rh19's own to within 3% — through a third independent route, since rh19 computed Δ = 0.24 rows directly with the hat's own kernel, rh22 recombined certified δ = 0.03 rows, and this document computes a direct interval arch with an A(r)² table and a binned comb radius. The interval arch is narrow: width below 9.0×10-22 at every window, so the radius is entirely the comb's, as it has been since rh17. The deepest certified floor in the arc is now 2.5778×10-1, against rh22's 1.3894×10-1.

6 · The bill, and what is left

56 named checks in 248 seconds over one stream of 6,448,870 prime powers. rh22's λmin values are reproduced bit for bit before anything is built on them; both bases reproduce the direct Δ = 0.24 kernel at all seventy-six nodes; the hat's sup|Ch′| comes out at exactly 2/3, rh19's own constant, which is the check that §2's machinery and rh19's agree where they must; every λmin is bisection on the inertia confirmed in exact 60-digit arithmetic, and every positive-definiteness claim by an independent dense Cholesky; rh20's four starved zeros, its θ-scan and its six union pivots are all rebuilt from its stored constants.

Against the order. All three items are answered. Two of them were mis-stated in rh22 and are corrected rather than merely completed: the direct route is a property of the hat, and the larger family has sixteen coefficients. The third is answered in the strongest available form — the mechanism rh20 identified is dissolved, not worked around.

What this does not do. It does not move the audit of rh22 §2 by one inch. The certified cones are the same cones, of the same total dimension, at the same half-supports; nine floors are larger and one section that was uncertified is now certified, and the measure of what has been verified against a conjunction over every Δ and every n is still zero. rh20's route stays closed. Nothing here touches the sieve, which rh21 showed is the end of the method at v ≈ 27–30.

What is left. Three again, and they are sharper than the three that came in.

(a) Where does the sequence of families converge? Each family's ceiling bounds only that family. M = 8 gives 3.986×10-3, M = 16 gives 2.949×10-2 at v = 18, and the ratio of successive gains is the only evidence about the limit. M = 32 costs a δ-row twice as long and a search in thirty-two dimensions; two more rungs would say whether the sequence is converging to a finite supremum over all windows of support Δ or growing without bound, and that is a question about the construction rather than about a computation.

(b) The binned radius has further to go. §2 bins by |u| only. The gear mass and the spline's slope are both known functions of u, and the bound uses a supremum within each bin; twenty-four bins already gave 9.9×, and the limit of the refinement is the exact mass-weighted integral of |Ch′|. What that limit is worth is a one-line calculation this document did not do, and it sets the real ceiling on how deep the certified horizon can be pushed at fixed arithmetic.

(c) The starved-zero mechanism, generalised. §4 shows the four zeros were the hat's. It does not show that a searched window has no starved zeros — only that these four are not starved and that its worst forty-zero weight is flat. The zeros above γ = 123 are untested, and the search never saw them: it optimises λmin on the primes side and the flatness at the zeros is a by-product. Whether that by-product persists to γ = 10³ is checkable with rh21's zeros-side kernel and costs no sieve at all.
