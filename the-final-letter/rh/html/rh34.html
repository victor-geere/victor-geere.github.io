rh34 · no mod 2π

The construction the whole arc rests on has no mod 2π in it. Gear n sits at cumulative angle (n−1)π + t·log n, on a depth axis that keeps increasing: after 2π comes 3π, then 4π, and the thread never returns to an angle it has left. The arc’s Toeplitz row throws all of that away — not at rh29, where the wrapped measure was drawn, but at rh17, where the row was first sampled on a lattice. This document establishes that, builds the object that does not fold, and reports what it is worth. The honest answer is: it kills rh33’s counterexample and it does not produce a floor.

1 · Where this stands

rh33 left three successors, all of them about the signed prime comb. This document does not execute them. It executes an objection, raised against the whole line rh29–rh33, that turns out to be correct and to be older than the documents it was raised against: there is no mod 2π.

In the construction (rh3 §§5–6) the cumulative angle of gear n is ϑn = (n−1)π + t·log n — the half-turns that are there by construction, plus the torsion, one dial driving every gear at its own rate log n. Both bookkeeping axes are cumulative and one-way, and the levels sit at increasing depth, so 2π is not 0; it is the next floor up. §2 verifies that and retires a “reversal” that rh3 §6 and rh5 §3 both report, which is an artifact of writing the torsion with the sign opposed to the half-turns.

What the arc does with that structure is discard it. Its Toeplitz row is E(v) sampled at v = kΔ, and a lattice of v makes the trial polynomial periodic in γ with period 2π/Δ, so the ordinates fold. That is where mod 2π entered — at rh17, with the first Toeplitz row, not at rh29 with the measure — and §2 shows the two are numerically the same object to 3.3×10⁻¹⁴ of the mass, because E(v) = ∑ w cos(vγ) is even and keeps nothing but the folded angles.

So §3 builds the object that does not fold: sample E at the gear rates instead. The result is a Gram matrix whose trial function is a Dirichlet polynomial, and which carries no mesh at all. §§4–6 measure it. It is worth stating the verdict up front, because it cuts both ways: the unwrapped object is immune to rh33’s counterexample and is worse conditioned than the lattice, and it has no floor either.

2 · The lift, and what the row cannot see

Count both rotations the same way and every increment is positive. In n: ϑn+1 − ϑn = π + t·log(1+1/n), whose least value over n < 4000 and t from γ1 out to 4800 is 3.1451 — just above π, approaching it only in the limit. In t: dϑn/dt = log n, least at n = 2. The thread climbs and does not come back.

One correction to the corpus while we are here. rh3 §6 computes a phase velocity π − t/x vanishing at x* = t/π and reads off a reversal — “the first four or five circles turn one way, everything deeper turns the other” — and rh5 §3 repeats it. Both reproduce exactly: x* = 4.4992 at t = γ1, with the level increment turning positive at n = 5. But it is a consequence of writing the torsion as −t·log n against half-turns of +π, and the relative sign is a convention, not a fact about the construction. With the signs aligned there is no reversal to explain.

Now the part that matters. E(v) is even. The row the arc has bounded since rh17 is E(kΔ) = ∑γ ĥ(γ)² cos(kΔγ), and a cosine cannot tell which way anything turned. Evaluated on the unwrapped ordinates it agrees with rh33’s wrapped row to 3.3×10⁻¹⁴ of the mass. Nothing but the folded angle multiset survives.

And the fold is not a technicality. At Δ = 0.12 the 4,308 ordinates below 4800 wind 92 times round the circle, so 47 of them land on each turn on top of one another; at Δ = 0.24, 23.5; at 0.06, 94. The winding count is discarded wholesale.

3 · The unwrapped Gram

The gear rates log n are not a lattice. Sample E there and nothing folds:

G[i][j] = E(log i − log j), and ∑i,j cicj G[i][j] = ∑γ ĥ(γ)² ∣∑i ci iiγ∣2

— so G is positive definite by construction, and the trial function is a Dirichlet polynomial. Both facts check out: λmin(G) > 0, and two independent eigensolvers — Cholesky inertia bisection and cyclic Jacobi — agree on it to 1.2×10⁻¹². At N = 24, λmin(G)/mass = 0.2939.

One structural remark, which is the reason this object is worth building at all. The lattice needs E at n points kΔ, so it has two parameters, (Δ, n). G needs E at the log(i/j), so it has one, N. The arc’s entire (Δ, n) grid — every mesh, every ladder, rh31’s 336 points — is a property of the sampling, not of the measure. The same E serves both.

4 · The Dirichlet floor

So: does a Dirichlet polynomial of length N have to be large somewhere on the zeros? λmin(G)/mass is exactly that question, and the answer is that it decays, for every window tried.

For the searched-0.12 window the floor runs 0.5685 at N = 8 to 0.0034 at N = 96. Only the Δ = 0.06 window is even a power law: forced through one it gives N−0.63 with every point within 0.17 in ln. The 0.12 window is not — forced through a power its exponent reads −1.76 and the worst point is 1.52 out in ln, because the decay accelerates. And the gentler window collapses too, just later: 0.040 at N = 128 and 8.6×10⁻³ at N = 160.

There is no uniform Dirichlet floor here. Changing the sampling did not buy one, and it is worth being plain that this was the outcome hoped for when the section was written.

5 · What the fold cost

It bought something else. rh33 killed the quadrature lemma by modulating the weights at frequency n−1 in the wrapped angle: on the crystal that gives ρ = 1 − ε/2 exactly, driving the constant from 1 to 0.550 at ε = 0.9. The modulation could be placed at any frequency, because the fold makes every frequency below n available.

Unwrapped, it has nowhere to stand. Apply the same ε = 0.9 modulation to the measure itself — W → W(1 + ε cos(v0γ)) — and the worst it achieves is a factor 0.707, at v0 = log 2. The other gear rates cost between 8% and 29%. The four non-gear frequencies tried cost as little as 4.3%.

The mechanism is not subtle: the only frequencies that couple to G are its own sampling points, and those are the gears. A modulation at a frequency that is not some log m has nothing to resonate with. rh33’s counterexample was not a fact about atomic measures; it was a fact about aliasing, and it needed the fold to exist.

6 · What the fold buys

The trade is not a floor for a floor, and the lattice wins the half that can be measured. At matched dimension the lattice holds 0.9586 at n = 8 where G holds 0.5685, and 0.7938 at n = 24 where G holds 0.2939; the ratio falls to 0.33 across that range.

The reason is bunching. The gear rates crowd together at the top — the smallest gap in a length-32 Dirichlet polynomial is log(32/31) = 0.0317, about a quarter of the lattice’s Δ = 0.12 — so G has near-duplicate rows that a Toeplitz matrix on an equispaced lattice never has. Equispacing is the best-conditioned sampling of a given reach, which is why the arc chose it, and it pays for that conditioning with the fold.

So the two objects divide cleanly. The lattice is the better conditioned one; the multiplicative sampling is the better structured one — it cannot be fooled by an alias, because it has none.

7 · Where this leaves the conjunction

The objection is sustained, and it is about the arc rather than about rh29. The fold belongs to the lattice, it dates from rh17, and E’s evenness means the certified row never encoded the winding at all. Five documents of geometry on the wrapped circle — rh29’s measure, rh30’s Neff, rh31’s emptiest arc, rh32’s void law, rh33’s refutation — were all conducted on an object that had already thrown the construction’s defining structure away. That is worth knowing and it is not comfortable.

What it does not do is move the conjunction. The unwrapped object has no floor either: the Dirichlet floor decays for every window tried and collapses by N = 160 on the gentlest of them. The one real gain is negative and clarifying — rh33’s counterexample is an aliasing artifact, so the quadrature lemma it killed is not dead in the unwrapped setting, merely unproven there, and any future attempt should be made on G rather than on Tn.

rh33’s verdict therefore stands with one amendment. The arc remains a certified-computation programme whose certified set grows only by paying for it. But the thing to compute a floor for may be the wrong matrix, and this document has built the other one.

8 · What this document leaves

(a) the conditioning of G, priced
§6 blames bunching for the multiplicative sampling’s worse floor, on one number (the smallest gap) and one comparison. Price it properly: is λmin(G) governed by the minimum log-gap, by the counting function of {log i − log j}, or by neither? A thinned gear set — only the primes, or only the squarefree, or a geometric subset with controlled gaps — would separate the hypotheses, and the squarefree case is the one with arithmetic content.
(b) the quadrature lemma, reopened on the right matrix
rh32 §8 (a) asked for a domination bound with gap hypotheses and rh33 refuted it — with a construction that §5 now shows requires an alias. The lemma is therefore open again for G, where no alias exists. The honest failure branch is unchanged in shape: find a weight profile, band-limited or not, that drives λmin(G) down while the gear gaps stay bounded. §5 tried eleven frequencies and the worst cost 29%; a real search has not been run.
(c) the certified set stays closed
Standing order, renewed a fifth time. Nothing in this document certifies anything, and its main result is that five previous documents measured the wrong matrix carefully.
