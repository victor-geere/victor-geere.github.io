The certificates made unconditional

Ordered: execute the successor's first move — rh16 priced it as "a finite amount of engineering and no new mathematics," the only item on its list certain to succeed. Executed: an outward-rounded interval arithmetic, two structural lemmas in place of the two error terms that would have sunk the budget, and a non-iterative Cholesky certificate. The three Schur certificates of rh15 are now theorems about the exact kernel. The bill is itemized at the end, because the price was almost as quoted but not quite: run blindly, the intervalization fails by five orders of magnitude, and the recursion itself refuses to carry intervals past a few dozen steps.

Abstract. rh15 measured three certificates — the Toeplitz sections E(kΔ), k < n, of the prime-built kernel at (Δ, n) = (0.06, 199), (0.03, 399), (0.015, 799) are positive definite, margins 0.403, 0.623, 0.698 — in 60-digit floating point, with error bars that were themselves measurements. This document re-derives them as theorems. The decomposition (§2): the exact kernel splits, on each grid, as Ecert + (discrete tail) − D, where the tail beyond a cut R₀ is a nonnegative combination of cosine kernels — positive semidefinite by inspection, because ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼+ir/2) − ln π ≥ 1.1558 for every r ≥ 20 (lemma L1, three elementary lines) — and |D(v)| ≤ 2M₁(v)/(e16π−1) ≤ 5×10⁻²⁰ per entry at trapezoid step h = 0.05 (lemma L2, one strip bound with every constant written out). So the tail is dropped without being bounded, and the aliasing radius ρD ≤ 4.2×10⁻¹⁸ sits five orders below the smallest eigenvalue it must not disturb. The cut itself carries a finding: truncated at 3.8 notches the finest section loses positive definiteness — its softest modes feed on the whole ladder of B's nulls, not the first — and recovers only from 5.7 notches; the cut was placed at 7.6. The instrument (§3): intervals over Decimal with directed rounding, enclosures for π, ln, sin, cos, cosh and Re ψ at complex arguments, each containment-tested, and the whole pipeline re-deriving rh15's stored rows to the last bit before a single new claim is made. The theorem (§4–5): a verified-Cholesky certificate — one 40-digit factorization, one exact-product residual, radius row-sums — gives certified floors λmin ≥ 5.45×10⁻¹¹, 4.17×10⁻¹³, 1.43×10⁻¹³ for the three exact sections, brackets closing around the pilot values, and therefore: every one of the 1394 Schur parameters of the three exact kernel sections lies strictly inside (−1, 1) — the rh15 equation, unconditional on everything except IEEE-754 and libmpdec behaving as specified. The wall (§4): the recursion itself, run forward on intervals, certifies only k ≤ 19, 33, 62 before dependency growth (measured ~0.35 digits per step; worst case from rh15's own ρ-sequence, 1051) devours any precision — 120 digits buy zero steps over 40. That is why the theorem enters by a non-iterative door, and it is the honest answer to "just run it on intervals": you cannot. §6 audits rh16's price quote against the bill.

1 · The order

rh16 §6 left three moves for a successor and priced the first: "Make the certificates unconditional. Everything here is a floating-point statement with a measured error bar… Redo the same quadrature in interval arithmetic and every computed certificate becomes a theorem about the kernel — a finite amount of engineering and no new mathematics. This is first because it is the only item on the list that is certain to succeed." This document executes that move and audits the quote.

What "unconditional" means here, precisely. The kernel is a definite mathematical object — E(v) = 2Bi²cosh(v/2) + (1/π)∫₀^∞ B(r)²ψ̃(r)cos(rv)dr − Σ Λ(n)n−1/2[C(log n − v) + C(log n + v)] — and rh15's certificates were statements about a floating-point approximation of it, with error bars obtained by recomputing on a second quadrature and watching what moved. A theorem about the kernel must instead carry every error — truncation, discretization, transcendental evaluation, rounding — as a proved bound or eliminate it structurally. Nothing else changes: same kernel, same sections, same recursion. The claims at the end are conditional only on two named hypotheses about machine arithmetic (§5), which is the standard residue of any computer-assisted proof.

Part I · The mathematics of the error budget

2 · What stands between a float and a theorem

Write the archimedean integral as the even half of a line integral and discretize by the infinite trapezoid sum at step h = 0.05,

(1) (1/π)∫₀^∞ B²ψ̃ cos(rv) dr  =  Sh[0,R₀](v)  +  Sh(R₀,∞)(v)  −  D(v),

where Sh is the trapezoid sum (half-weight at 0), the middle term is its tail past a cut R₀, and D is the aliasing error of the full sum against the true integral. Four error sources stand between rh15's floats and a theorem: the tail, the aliasing, the transcendental values (ψ̃, cos, log p), and rounding. The last two are routine — intervals at 40 digits (§3). The first two would sink the budget if bounded naively, and the whole design reduces to two lemmas that make them cheap.

L1 — the tail is never bounded, because it helps. The honest worst-case bound on the tail past R₀ is ~10⁻⁸ per entry at the finest mesh, against a smallest eigenvalue of 10⁻¹³: hopeless by five orders. But the tail does not need a bound. Each dropped term is cjcos(rjv) with cj = h·B(rj)²ψ̃(rj)/π, and a cosine kernel cos(r(u−w)) is positive semidefinite; a nonnegative combination of them is too. So the dropped tail is PSD on every grid the moment every cj ≥ 0, i.e. the moment ψ̃ ≥ 0 past the cut — and then Eexact = Ecert + (PSD) − D means certifying Ecert certifies Eexact. The sign fact is three elementary lines: for r ≥ 20, |z| = |¼+ir/2| ≥ 10, and Stirling with its corrections summed crudely gives Re ψ(z) ≥ ln|z| − C₀ with C₀ ≤ 0.0021, so

(2) ψ̃(r) ≥ ln(r/2π) − C₀ ≥ ln(10/π) − C₀ ≥ 1.1558    for every r ≥ 20,

evaluated as intervals, not floats. The density is negative only below r = 6.3 — far below any cut. Where the cut may sit is a different and more delicate question, and the answer was a surprise (Figure 1, right): positivity of the truncated section demands far more tail than the notch analysis of rh15 suggested. All three meshes are still indefinite with the cut at 2.4 notches; the coarser two recover at 3.8, the finest only past 5.7 — and when it fails, it fails at k = 785 of 798, in the last two percent of the recursion. The certificate's softest modes are fed by the entire ladder of B's nulls, and the ledger only balances when several rungs are kept.

L2 — the aliasing bound, with its constants shown. For a function analytic in the strip |Im r| < ½ (the integrand's poles are those of ψ at ¼ + ir/2) the infinite trapezoid at step h errs by at most 2M₁/(e2πτ/h − 1), where M₁ bounds the line integrals of |f| along |Im r| = τ (Trefethen–Weideman). Take τ = 0.4, h = 0.05: the denominator is e16π − 1 ≈ 6.8×10²¹. The numerator chain is elementary: |sinc(u+iw)|² ≤ cosh²(w) — from sin²u ≤ u², sinh²w ≤ w²cosh²w and the mediant inequality — gives ∫|B(x+ib)|²dx ≤ (16/3)Δcosh⁴(τΔ/2); two recurrence steps and the same Stirling corrections give |g(x+ib)| ≤ ln(3+|x|/2) + Cψ with Cψ ≤ 22.37 on the whole strip; and |cos((x+ib)v)| ≤ eτv. Folded together (the script evaluates every constant as an interval),

(3) |D(v)| ≤ 2KΔe0.4v/(e16π − 1),    ρD = Σ-row bound ≤ 4.2×10⁻¹⁸   (Δ = 0.015),

which is five orders below λmin. At rh15's step h = 0.1 the same bound is 10¹¹ times weaker — e8π in the denominator — and the theorem would fail; halving the step is what buys it. This is the one place the certified object differs from rh15's: same kernel, same cuts' side, finer lattice.

Part II · The machine

3 · The instrument, and what the floats got wrong

The arithmetic is an interval class over Python's Decimal: libmpdec rounds +, −, ×, ÷ and √ correctly under a directed-rounding context, so [lo, hi] endpoints computed in paired floor/ceiling contexts enclose rigorously. On top of it, enclosures for π (Machin, alternating-tail bound), ln (dyadic scaling and the atanh series), sin and cos (range reduction against the π enclosure — arguments are exact decimals by construction, so reduction loses nothing — then Taylor with an explicit remainder), cosh, sinh, exp, and Re ψ(x+iy): the recurrence ψ(z) = ψ(z+m) − Σ1/(z+j) pushed to |z| ≥ 40, then six Stirling terms with remainder ≤ 86/|z|14 — a Boyd-type bound taken with a factor-four cushion. Only real parts are ever needed, so the complex kit is powers and reciprocals; no arctangent exists anywhere in the pipeline.

Each enclosure is containment-tested against the float library on spot grids, and the ψ kit passes three γ-free identities as intervals: ψ(½) − ψ(¼) = ln 2 + π/2, ψ(2) − ψ(1) = 1, and the duplication identity Re ψ(¼+it) + Re ψ(¾+it) − 2Re ψ(½+2it) + 2 ln 2 = 0 at four heights up to t = 400 — every enclosure straddles 0 at width below 10⁻¹⁹. The ψ̃ table (64,001 points to r = 3200) has width ≤ 6.5×10⁻²¹; the finished row enclosures have width ≤ 2.2×10⁻²² (3.2×10⁻²³ at the finest mesh) and radius row-sums ρΣ ≤ 2.8×10⁻²⁰ — the full budget is drawn in Figure 2. Before any of it is believed, the pipeline re-derives rh15: the h = 0.1 rows rebuild bit for bit (difference 0.0), the h = 0.05 cert rows agree with their h = 0.1 counterparts within the asserted 3×10⁻⁹ gate (the Euler–Maclaurin ripple of the cut's endpoint, exactly as expected), the pilot floats sit inside the enclosures, and the 60-digit margins of the cert sections match rh15's stored ones to 1.3×10⁻⁴ — 0.4034, 0.6230, 0.6981 against 0.4034, 0.6231, 0.6982.

The floats deserve one honest paragraph, because three of them lied during construction, and each lie shaped the design. The plain double-precision recursion at n = 799 reports a positivity failure that is not there — rh15 already knew this and ran 60 digits; it is why every verdict here runs through the 60-digit recursion or the interval layer. Inverse iteration in doubles, asked for λmin of the miscut finest section, returned +1.4×10⁻¹³ for a matrix that is not positive definite — it converged to the eigenvalue its starting vector could see and missed the negative one; the 60-digit recursion caught the failure at k = 785. And a shifted variant, iterating on T + cI with c ~ E(0), has convergence ratio 1 − 10⁻¹² at these gaps — it reports its warm start, dressed as an answer; an early version of the cut experiment used it and placed the finest mesh's recovery point at 2.4 notches when the truth is 5.7. In this document floats survive in exactly one load-bearing role: choosing the shift s that the certificate then proves things about. A wrong s costs tightness, never truth.

4 · The two backends

α — the theorem, by a non-iterative door. Take the enclosure's midpoint row Mc (exact decimals) and radius row-sum ρΣ. Factor Mc − sI ≈ R̂ᵀR̂ once, at 40 digits, with s a dyadic fraction of the pilot's eigenvalue; form the residual F = Mc − sI − R̂ᵀR̂ with exact products — 40-digit entries multiply exactly at 90 digits — so ‖F‖∞ is a rigorous number with a blanket rounding slop. Since R̂ᵀR̂ ⪰ 0 always, every symmetric matrix within the radii of Mc has

(4) λmin ≥ s − ‖F‖∞ − ρΣ,    and for the exact section,   λmin ≥ s − ‖F‖∞ − ρΣ − ρD.

No iteration, so no dependency growth: the certificate's quality is set by how well s was guessed and how small the three subtractions are. Figure 2 places all of them on one axis; Figure 4 is the resulting card. The certified floors reach 99% of the pilot values, and the brackets close: an interval Rayleigh quotient with the pilot's eigenvector caps each λmin from above at 1.45×10⁻¹³ (finest), so the certificate is tight, not merely true.

β — the recursion itself, and the wall. The natural move — run Levinson–Durbin forward on the interval rows, get certified enclosures for every ρk — works, briefly. At 40 digits the finest mesh certifies k ≤ 62 of 798. At 120 digits: k ≤ 62 — the same wall, to the step. Eighty additional digits buy nothing, because the wall is not rounding: it is the dependency problem — each interval operation treats correlated enclosures as independent, and the recursion compounds the overestimate at a measured ~0.35 digits per step (1.28 and 0.71 at the coarser meshes, which fall at k = 19 and 33). A control run on exact, zero-width inputs climbs at the same slope and gains nine steps (k ≤ 71) — the price of pushing the width floor from 10⁻²² down to the 40-digit rounding floor at 10⁻⁴⁰. The worst case computable from rh15's own sequence — Π(1+|ρk|)/(1−|ρk|) — is 10²⁴, 10³⁵, 10⁵¹ for the three meshes; no precision ladder survives that. What β delivers is still real: over the tight prefixes (radius < 10⁻³, k ≤ 18, 31, 60) it certifies |ρk| < 1 with margins 0.4034, 0.6338, 0.7808 — and for the coarsest mesh that prefix covers k = 9, where its largest reflection coefficient lives, so the headline margin 0.403 is certified coefficient-by-coefficient. The finer meshes' maxima sit at k = 54 and 171, beyond their walls — which is why their prefix margins run higher than their headline 0.623 and 0.698 — and belong to α: positive definiteness of the whole section is equivalent to every |ρk| < 1, so the theorem covers all 1394 coefficients without the recursion ever certifying most of them individually.

Part III · What is now proved, and what it cost

5 · The theorems

Let E be the kernel above with its exact constants, Δ ∈ {0.06, 0.03, 0.015}, n = 199, 399, 799, and let MΔ = [E((i−j)Δ)] be the n×n exact sections. Let Ecert be the explicitly finite object: pole + Sh[0,R₀] − comb at h = 0.05, R₀ = 600, 1200, 3200.

T1 (certified sections). Every symmetric Toeplitz matrix whose first row lies within the computed enclosures of Ecert is positive definite, with λmin ∈ [5.45×10⁻¹¹, 5.50×10⁻¹¹], [4.17×10⁻¹³, 4.22×10⁻¹³], [1.43×10⁻¹³, 1.45×10⁻¹³] for the three meshes.

T2 (exact sections). MΔ = (matrix in T1's enclosure) + (PSD tail matrix, L1) − (matrix of ∞-norm ≤ ρD, L2). Hence each exact section is positive definite with λmin ≥ 5.45×10⁻¹¹, 4.17×10⁻¹³, 1.43×10⁻¹³.

T3 (the equation). Positive definiteness of a Toeplitz section is equivalent to all its reflection coefficients lying in (−1, 1) with all prediction errors positive. Therefore every one of the 198 + 398 + 798 = 1394 Schur parameters of the three exact sections of E lies strictly inside the open unit disc — rh15's reading, σk² = σk−1²(1 − ρk²) with |ρk| < 1 at every step, as a theorem about the kernel.

Hypotheses, in full. (i) IEEE-754 double arithmetic behaves as specified (used in the residual's magnitude bookkeeping and the pilots); (ii) libmpdec's Decimal arithmetic rounds correctly in directed-rounding contexts. Plus two cited classical facts with explicit constants: the strip aliasing bound and the Stirling remainder. There is no other input: no zero table, no float quadrature, no library special function stands anywhere under the theorems.

What is not proved. RH — three finite sections are certified, not all sections of all meshes; rh15's proposition P2 (the continuity/density step from finite certificates to the full Bochner statement) is untouched and remains the open link. The certificates are also statements about this test family — the cubic-hat autocorrelation windows of rh14 — not about an arbitrary one. And T1/T2 certify positivity with an explicit floor, not the exact value of λmin: the brackets in Figure 4 are ~1.0–1.3% wide.

6 · The bill, audited

rh16's quote: finite engineering, no new mathematics, certain to succeed. The audit:

Succeeded. Runtime 21 minutes (1276 s) for the whole pipeline — pilots, lemmas, rows, both backends — under 54 named checks; precisions 40 (rows, α), 60 (recursion verdicts), 90 (exact residual), 120 (β's ladder). Nothing beyond a laptop-class budget, exactly as priced.

"No new mathematics" — almost. Both load-bearing lemmas are classical: PSD-ness of nonnegative cosine sums is Bochner's easy direction, and the trapezoid strip bound is nineteenth-century analysis with a modern citation. But the selection of them was the work. The naive execution of rh16's sentence — intervalize the existing quadrature as it stands — fails outright: the honest tail bound is five orders too large, and rh15's step h = 0.1 leaves the aliasing bound 10¹¹ too weak. The move that saves the tail is refusing to bound it (L1); the move that saves the aliasing is halving the step. Neither is deep; both were mandatory; neither was in the quote.

The genuine surprise. The recursion — the arc's protagonist since rh15 — cannot itself be run rigorously to depth: forward interval propagation dies at k ≈ 62 of 798 at any usable precision, with the worst case at 10⁵¹. The certificates had to come from a non-iterative argument (α), with the recursion reduced to an equivalence (T3) rather than an execution. The finest mesh added a second lesson: cut the archimedean tail at 3.8 notches and positivity is lost — the certificate's own softest modes live on the entire null ladder. Both were found because verification failed loudly, which is the argument for building the rigor rather than asserting it.

For the successor, rh16's remaining two moves stand — ρk as an arithmetic object, and the two unexplained constants (σ²v² ≈ 3.9×10⁻³ and D*'s missing mesh limit) — and this document adds one: push β's wall with an a-posteriori method (verify each ρk from a high-precision candidate by a Krawczyk-type contraction, rather than propagating forward), which would turn the margin figures themselves into per-coefficient theorems at all k.
