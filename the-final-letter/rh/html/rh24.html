2 · (b) The radius, to its limit — and the horizon

rh23's bound was

(1) ρbinp  =  δu · Σbins supbin|Ch′| · MΔ(p, bin)  +  ε · Σbins supbinQh · MΔ(p, bin),

with 24 bins across [0, 2Δ], MΔ(p, bin) the gear mass the sieve put in that bin at node p, and Qh the local ℓ¹ norm that multiplies the per-term rounding. Refining the bins can only shrink it — a supremum over a smaller set is smaller — and in the limit it becomes the exact mass-weighted integral of |Ch′| against the prime-power measure. The question rh23 left was how much of that limit remains.

Not much. Going from 24 bins to 768 adds 1.069× for the hat and 1.480× for the searched window, and the curve is visibly flat by 384. Against rh22's recombined bound the totals are 10.57× and 2.85×, so rh23 already had 94% of what the method can give for the hat and 68% for the searched window. There is nothing more here: the answer to "how much further" is a few per cent.

What it buys is a horizon. rh19's deepest certified section was Δ = 0.24 at n = 76, v = 18.00, and what stopped it there was the radius, not the sieve — rh19's own comb reached v = 19.92. Recomputing the ladder over that longer sieve with (1) at 768 bins, the triangle hat's margin is 6.48× at n = 76, 2.08× at n = 80, and drops below 1 at n = 83. So

(2) the deepest section the triangle hat can certify moves from rh19's n = 76 (v = 18.00) to n = 82 (v = 19.44).

Modest in v, and the first time the arc's certified horizon has moved since rh19. For the searched windows the picture is different in kind. Their margins never fall below 1.5×107 at any n the sieve reaches, and their floors barely decay with the horizon at all: from v = 18.00 to v = 19.92 the M = 8 optimum loses a factor 1.11 and the M = 16 optimum 1.78, against the hat's 3.25. rh21 closed by saying the arithmetic was never the wall and the sieve is, at v ≈ 27–30. For a searched window that is now literally true: the radius has stopped being the binding constraint at any horizon this sieve can reach, and what remains is the price of the primes.

3 · (a) Where the families converge

The family is h = Σ|k| ≤ M−1 ak φδ(· − kδ) with δ = Δ/M, giving M free coefficients after symmetry. rh22 ran M = 8, rh23 ran M = 16 and showed the first is exactly a subspace of the second. The same refinement nests every rung: M = 8 ⊂ 16 ⊂ 32 ⊂ 64, each search warm-started at the exact refinement of the last, so the ladder measures the family and not the starting point.

A correction first. rh22 and rh23 computed the family's best response to a test vector as the top generalised eigenpair of (Mc, G), by a power iteration on the whitened form shifted by σ = max row sum + 1. That shift makes the ratio of successive eigenvalues (σ+λ₂)/(σ+λ₁) approach 1 as the dimension grows. Measured against Lanczos with full reorthogonalisation over eight test vectors: at 8 coefficients the two agree to 5.6×10-16; beyond that the value drifts by up to 2.2×10-3, which is harmless because rh22 and rh23 quoted factors of two and three. The vector is what the search follows, and at 64 coefficients it is off by sin(angle) = 6.1×10-1 — enough that the cutting-plane direction is wrong and the line search finds nothing at any step size. That is what stalled the first attempt at this section.

With Lanczos the ladder runs. It also, uncomfortably, improves rh22 and rh23's own families: re-searching M = 8 and M = 16 beats their published optima by up to 1.35×. Their searches were local. Nothing they asserted is wrong — every published value still sits under its published ceiling, and the ceiling is a theorem while the search is a heuristic — but "near the family's best" was a claim about the ceiling, not about the search, and this document is the reason to say so plainly.

The answer. At v = 18 the ladder runs 5.378×10-3 → 2.741×10-2 → 2.252×10-1 → 2.511×10-1, and the successive gains are 5.10×, 8.22×, 1.11×. At the other two section sizes the pattern is the same: five to eight for the first doubling, under 1.30 for the last. The sequence is converging, not growing. Doubling the coefficient count again would, on this evidence, buy well under a factor of two, and the cost is a δ-row twice as long and a search in 128 dimensions.

What that does not establish is a bound. Each family's ceiling bounds only that family; the ceilings themselves grow along the ladder (8.980×10-3 at M = 8, 8.456×10-1 at M = 64 at v = 18), so nothing here bounds the supremum over all windows of support Δ. A decaying sequence of gains is evidence of convergence and not a proof of it, and the document says so on the card.

4 · (c) The notch ladder

rh23 §4 found the searched window's weight flat over the first forty zeros — its four worst inside a factor 4 of each other where the hat's spread over five orders — and asked whether that persists to γ = 10³. The arc has 1517 zeros cached to height 2000 and the question costs no sieve, only rh21's zeros-side kernel.

It does not persist. On the scale-free statistic γ⁴|ĥ(γ)|²/Ch(0) — scale-free because every compactly supported window's transform decays like r−2 — the M = 8 optimum's worst zero over all 1517 is 2.25×10-12, against the triangle hat's 1.04×10-9. It is not merely no better; it is 462× worse. And the two are starved at the same γ = 628.326.

That coincidence is the explanation. For any window in the M-family,

(3) |ĥ(γ)|²  =  |φ̂δ(γ)|² · A(γ)²,    A(γ) = Σk ak cos(kγδ),    φ̂δ(γ) = 4 sin²(γδ/2)/(γ²δ),

and φ̂δ vanishes at γ = 2πq/δ = 2πqM/Δ whatever the coefficients are. The M = 8 family's basis notches sit at γ = 209.44q — a sub-ladder of the hat's own 2πq/Δ = 26.18q, M times sparser — and A contributes its own zeros on top, wherever the search happened to leave them. Refining the family pushes the basis ladder out by M and never removes it, which is why the M = 8 optimum and the hat are starved at the very same γ: it is a basis notch of one and an ordinary notch of the other. The holes are put there by the basis, not by the search, and no amount of searching inside the family can close them.

Outside the family it is elementary. Take h(t) = sinh(Δ − |t|) on [−Δ, Δ]. Its transform is

(4) ĥ(r)  =  2(cosh Δ − cos rΔ)/(1 + r²)  ≥  2(cosh Δ − 1)/(1 + r²)  =  0.0578770/(1 + r²)  >  0    for every real r,

verified against quadrature to 5.3×10-10. The bound is attained exactly at r = 2πq/Δ, where the triangle hat has its own notches — so the window that is worst for the hat is where this one is merely at its floor. It is admissible for the same reason every window is: the Weil test function is Ch = h⋆ȟ with Ĉh = |ĥ|² ≥ 0, and h need only be square-integrable with support in [−Δ, Δ]. Over all 1517 zeros not one falls below 10⁻² on the scale-free statistic, where the hat has 50 and the M = 8 optimum 37; its typical weight, 1.723×102, still beats the hat's 1.059×102.

The price. Scored on λmin through rh21's zeros-side kernel — validated against the primes side here to 1.2% before use, and conditional on RH as that instrument must be — the sinh window gives 1.939×10-8 at v = 18 against the hat's 1.577×10-10 and the M = 8 optimum's 3.787×10-3. It beats the triangle hat by 123× and loses to the searched window by 2.0×105×. Uniformly positive and five orders below: removing the notches costs the peaks, which is rh21's barrier trade-off made concrete. Its uniform weight on [0, 2000] is 2.25×10-14 against rh21's barrier πC(0)/T = 1.57×10-3 there — notch-free and large are very different goals, and no window in this document is both.

6 · The bill, and what is left

59 named checks in 1182 seconds over two streams, 6,448,870 gears and 37,422,901. rh22's λmin values are reproduced bit for bit before anything is built on them; all four bases reproduce the direct Δ = 0.24 kernel; every λmin is inertia bisection confirmed in exact 60-digit arithmetic and every positive-definiteness claim by an independent dense Cholesky; the Lanczos eigenpair certifies its own residual at 2.2×10-7; the sinh transform is verified against quadrature; and the two corrections of §3 — the power iteration's error and the locality of the published optima — each carry their own assertion.

Against the order. All three are answered, and one of them — (c) — is answered against the expectation rh23 set. rh23 hoped the flatness would persist; it does not, and finding out why produced the only structural statement in this document: the notch ladder belongs to the basis. That is worth more than the flatness would have been, because it says where to look next.

What this does not do. The certified cones are the same cones at the same half-supports as rh22's audit counted them, and one section that was out of reach is now in it. A converging sequence of gains is not a proof that the supremum over all windows of support Δ is finite. The measure of what has been verified against a conjunction over every Δ and every n is still zero. And §4's last column assumes RH, as rh21's instrument must.

What is left. Three, and they are the sharpest the arc has had.

(a) Search the notch-free windows. §4 gives one window with no real zeros in its transform and §3 gives a search that works. They have never been combined, because the family §3 searches is exactly the family §4 shows cannot be notch-free. The right object is h = g⋆ǧ with ĝ zero-free — the sinh window is the case g(t) = et on [−Δ/2, Δ/2] — and that is a family with its own parameters and its own ceiling machinery, inside which λmin and the uniform weight can be traded explicitly rather than accidentally.

(b) The certified comb for a smooth window. Everything certified in this arc since rh17 has used a piecewise-polynomial Ch, because the comb is evaluated once per gear and a closed-form sinh costs six transcendentals where a cubic costs three multiplications. A notch-free window needs a certified comb it does not yet have. Tabulating Ch with a rigorous interpolation error is the obvious route and nobody has priced it.

(c) The supremum over all windows. §3's ceilings grow along the ladder because each bounds only its own family. A ceiling over all h of support Δ would follow from the same duality if the inner maximisation could be done over the whole space rather than a spline basis — which is a variational problem in ĥ with the constraint that h is supported in [−Δ, Δ], and is the first question in this arc that is genuinely analysis rather than computation.
