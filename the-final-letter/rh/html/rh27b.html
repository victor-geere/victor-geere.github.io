The second support

Every window this arc has certified since rh14 has had half-support Δ = 0.24, and rh21 fixed that number by an argument about cost made before a window with no holes in it existed. rh27a §5 named the consequence: the certified set is a column, and a column extended to infinity is not RH, because a single support is not dense in supports. This document moves the other axis. (a) Twelve searches — three supports, four shapes, M = 16 throughout — all provably notch-free, and the support turns out to move λmin 8.7× more than the shape does. (b) rh21's ledger, re-run: both of its prices are real, and there is a third it could not have known, running the other way. Scale-free, the verdict reverses — λmin/Ch(0) is 10.3× larger at 0.12 than at 0.48, so the narrow window wins. (c) Three certified floors at three supports at depth 18, which turns the column into a cross. And the cross comes with the obstruction attached: the window family is exactly scale-free, the kernel is not, and the failure is a clean power law, λmin/Ch(0) ∝ Δ′−1.7111. If that exponent were zero, one support would give all of them.

1 · The order

rh27a §6 listed the support first and said why: "The certified set is a column because every window in the arc since rh14 has had half-support Δ = 0.24. rh21 fixed that by an argument about cost — a wider window pays twice, in the comb and in rh18's Klin — made before notch-free windows existed. A second support turns the column into a region and re-runs that argument at the same time." Both halves of that are done here, and the second half is the one that produced a surprise.

The ground. A new sieve pass, because the comb rows depend on the support: 12,721,497 prime powers below 231,479,067, which is 2.05× further than rh25's stream had to reach, precisely because a support of 0.48 needs e2Δ′ more of the primes. Twelve rows — three supports × four shapes — from one pass. rh26's gear-mass histogram is rebuilt at width w = 2·0.12/768 so that every node of every support falls on an exact multiple of it; each support's radius bins are then a whole number of histogram cells wide, and the certified radius at all three comes from bin arithmetic on a single pass. rh27a's certified ψ̃ table to r = 4800 is reused unchanged. Before anything is built on it, the new stream is checked to reproduce rh25's comb row for row at Δ = 0.24 at both ends of the θ ladder, and rh27a's certified floor on Δ/4 is re-read from its constants.

What is held fixed. M = 16 at every support, so the window has the same thirty-one coefficients everywhere and the only thing that changes is the width. The generator mesh is δ = Δ′/16, so the shape parameter θ = cδ is the same ladder at every support and the four θ values name the same four shapes at three scales. The lattice step is Δ′ itself and the depth is 18 throughout. That is deliberate: rh27a varied the mesh at one support, this varies the support at one mesh, and the two documents cross at (0.24, Δ).

2 · (a) The search at three supports

rh25 built a generator family with a shape parameter — Uc(x) = sinh(c(δ − |x|))/sinh(cδ) on |x| ≤ δ, whose transform Ûc(r) = 2c(cosh cδ − cos rδ)/(sinh cδ · (c² + r²)) is strictly positive at every real r, so the window's transform has no holes for the archimedean tail to hide in. The search is over the coefficients, in the β parametrisation that makes non-negativity of the trigonometric factor structural, with a bundle of cutting planes standing in for the eigenvalue during the inner iterations and the true λmin evaluated only at the accepted points.

Twelve of them: θ ∈ {0, 0.06, 0.24, 0.96} at each of Δ′ ∈ {0.12, 0.24, 0.48}. Every one is checked notch-free after the fact, by minimising the trigonometric factor over a whole period rather than at the search's own sample points; the worst margin is 5.99×10⁻⁷, at Δ′ = 0.48 with the triangle generator, and it is positive. At Δ = 0.24 the best of the four agrees with rh25's own optimum to within 7% — 6.6% above it, the two being stochastic restarts of the same procedure over the same θ ladder — which is the regression that licenses reading the other two supports as comparable.

The first thing the figure says. The curves are flat and the gaps between them are not. Taken scale-free — divided by Ch(0), for the reason §3 gives — the four shapes at a fixed support move λmin by at most 1.24×, while the three supports at a fixed shape move it 10.7×, a lever 8.7× longer. The arc has spent rh24 and rh25 on the shape — the notch ladder, the hyperbolic generator, the β search — and got real improvements out of it, but on this scale the shape is a fine adjustment and the support is the coarse one. That is worth knowing before any further shape engineering is attempted.

The second thing. The optimal shape is not the same at every support. At Δ′ = 0.12 it is θ = 0.06; at 0.24 it is θ = 0.96, and at 0.48 the same, θ = 0.96. The two are not far apart in value — the curves are flat — but the fact that the argmax moves at all is the first sign that the family is not simply being rescaled, and §3 makes that precise.

3 · (b) rh21's ledger, re-run — and the term it is missing

rh21 §4 set Δ = 0.24 and gave two reasons not to widen it. Both are real and both are re-measured here.

Price one: the comb. The kernel row at depth v needs every prime power below ev + 2Δ′, because h = g ⋆ g has half-support 2Δ′ and reaches that much further than the lattice does. From 0.12 to 0.48 that is a factor e0.72 = 2.05 in the number of prime powers the sieve must stream, and it is why this document's pass is 2.05× longer than rh25's.

Price two: Klin. rh18 §3's linear-continuity constant, which controls the passage from a lattice to the line, grows with the support in the same exponential way. That one is quoted from rh18 rather than re-measured; it is not what this document tests.

The third term, which runs the other way. At fixed M, the window's generator mesh is δ = Δ′/16 — so a wider support means wider generators, and the window is smoother, not rougher. Its transform therefore decays faster, and less of the archimedean integrand is left beyond the cut. Measured at v = 0, the weight still dropped past R₁ = 4800 is 2.76×10⁻⁵ at Δ′ = 0.12, 7.67×10⁻⁶ at 0.24 and 2.46×10⁻⁶ at 0.48 — raw, the wide window drops 11.2× less; taken scale-free, for the reason the next paragraph gives, 39× less.

The mechanism is in the generator and can be read off it. Far from the origin Ûc(r) = 2c(cosh cδ − cos rδ)/(sinh cδ · (c² + r²)) is 2c(cosh θ − cos rδ)/(sinh θ · r²) to leading order, whose mean prefactor 2θ cosh θ/(δ sinh θ) is proportional to 1/δ. So doubling the support halves the transform out there, and at the shared θ = 0.96 the measurement returns exactly that factor of two. The integrand itself falls like r−4 — doubling the band divides its scale-free mean by 16 at every support, checked — and over three decades past the cut those band means fall monotonically with the support. One qualification the figure makes visible and the number hides: the three transforms reach comparable peaks out at the cut. It is only in the mean, which is what the dropped weight integrates, that they separate. rh21 could not have priced any of this, because at the time the window was a fixed triangle and M was not a free parameter. The trade is not one-sided.

Comparing eigenvalues across supports, honestly. λmin is homogeneous in the window's normalisation, so its raw value at two different supports is not a comparison of anything. The scale to divide by is Ch(0), the kernel's own diagonal — and it can be taken out exactly, because the window family is scale-free: Ch(0)/Δ′ depends on θ alone, and the same shape at three widths gives the same number to eleven figures. At θ = 0, where the generator is the triangle, that number is 2/3 exactly. So λmin/Ch(0) is the quantity to compare, and it is a genuine comparison.

The verdict, and it goes the other way. λmin/Ch(0) is 1.069 at Δ′ = 0.12, 0.360 at 0.24 and 0.104 at 0.48 — a factor 10.3 in favour of the narrow window. rh21's conclusion was that Δ = 0.24 was a reasonable stopping point on the way up in support; nothing in rh21 argued that going down was bad, and it turns out that going down is where the eigenvalue is. On the arc's own figure of merit the convention has been on the wrong side of the optimum tested here, in the direction nobody looked.

A prediction, and it was refuted. The reason one might still want a wide window is not λ but resolution: |ĥ|² is what multiplies the archimedean density, and the narrower it is in r the more the certificate is a statement about individual frequencies. At fixed shape that is exactly a scaling law — the family is scale-free, so |ĥ|²'s half-width is proportional to 1/Δ′, and the measurement confirms it to nine figures: 17.4017, 8.7008, 4.3504, ratio 0.250000. The prediction was that the searched optima would inherit it, and they do not. Their half-widths run 14.22 at Δ′ = 0.12, 6.55 at 0.24 and 31.64 at 0.48 — the last is the broadest of the three, where the law says it should be the narrowest by a factor of four. The searched optima are not scale-similar shapes; the search at 0.48 buys its eigenvalue with a flatter main lobe, and the resolution argument for a wide support does not survive contact with the search either. Both of rh21's directions of preference change sign once the window is searched rather than fixed.

And a third reason the narrow support is the better one. A lattice of step δ separates frequencies only up to π/δ, and here the lattice step is the support. This is a different argument from §3's — it is about the lattice rather than the window — but it points the same way, and between them the case for the arc's Δ = 0.24 convention is now: it is not optimal for the eigenvalue, it is not optimal for resolution, and its own lattice cannot see the first zero.

What is new in kind. Every certified point in the arc before rh26 lay on the diagonal — one support, at its own mesh. rh26 and rh27a built a column off it, five meshes at Δ = 0.24. This document extends the diagonal itself, and the union is a cross of 7 points spanning two supports in one direction and five meshes in the other. It is the first time the certified set has had extent in both, and the first time anything in the arc has been certified at a support other than 0.24.

5 · The conjunction, and the obstruction named

The arc's target is a conjunction: Eh positive definite on every lattice, at every depth, for every window — which by rh18 §5 is equivalent to RH. It is worth saying at each step exactly where that stands, and this document is the first that can say something sharper than "a finite set is measure zero".

What the cross is, as a set. 7 points in a three-dimensional index — support, mesh, depth — of which two of the three supports are new. It is still finite and still measure zero, and no arrangement of finitely many points is otherwise. Extended to infinity in the mesh direction alone it would give positivity on δℤ for one window of support 0.24, which rh27a already said is not RH. Extended to infinity in both, it would.

The obstruction, and it can be measured. Here is the question this document was built to answer and did. Is the support axis a real second infinity, or is it a gauge direction — could one certified support be pushed to all of them by rescaling? The window family says gauge: it is exactly scale-free — Ch(0)/Δ′ a function of θ alone to eleven figures, and, at fixed shape, |ĥ|²'s half-width exactly proportional to 1/Δ′. But the kernel is not scale-free, because it has two scales of its own that do not move with Δ′ — the archimedean density ψ̃, whose crossing at r₊ = 6.289836 is a fixed frequency, and the prime lattice ln p, whose spacing is fixed on the v axis. So a certificate at one support cannot be rescaled into a certificate at another, and the failure is quantitative:

(1) λmin/Ch(0)  ∝  Δ′−1.7111    at fixed shape, the two dyadic exponents being 1.7225 and 1.6998.

Measured on the triangle row, where the shape is genuinely held fixed and the only thing changing is the width: 0.9612, 0.2913, 0.0897. The two exponents agree to 1.3%, so it is a power law and not a drift over the range tested. If that exponent were 0, one certified support would give every support. It is −1.7111, and that number is the exact sense in which the support axis is a second infinity. This is the first quantitative statement the arc has about why the conjunction needs two limits rather than one, as against the qualitative observation that it does.

What it costs, and what it does not. The exponent is negative in Δ′, which means narrowing the support makes the form more positive, scale-free. That is a favourable direction: the hard supports are the wide ones, and a proof strategy that worked from the narrow end and pushed outward would be pushing uphill in exactly the way the exponent describes. It also means the arc's own convention — Δ = 0.24 — sits in the middle of the tested range with easier ground below it and harder above, and nothing about the choice is optimal. What the exponent does not give is a uniform bound: −1.7111 is a measured slope over one dyadic range at one depth with one M, not a theorem, and an extrapolation of it to Δ′ → ∞ is exactly the kind of step this arc has refused elsewhere.

A direction, named and still not attempted. rh27a recorded it and this document did not get to it, so it is repeated rather than quietly dropped. Everything the arc certifies is a statement about a quadratic form; nothing it has produced is a statement about a zero. For a real even window g of compact support, ĝ is entire and ĥ = ĝ², so a hypothetical off-line quadruple at ½ ± σ + iγ contributes 4 Re[ĝ(γ + iσ)²] to the Weil functional — negative exactly when |Im ĝ(γ + iσ)| exceeds |Re ĝ(γ + iσ)|, and directly computable for the arc's actual windows on a (γ, σ) grid. A certified floor bounds what such a contribution could hide behind. Closing that into an explicit zero-free region needs an upper bound on the on-line part, which the arc does not have. It remains the only route in sight by which these certificates would speak about ζ rather than about forms.

The honest form of the gap, restated. rh27a put it as two infinities plus a dyadic third. That is unchanged in kind. What has changed is that the second of the two is no longer a bare unknown: it has a measured slope, and the slope says the direction the arc has never gone is the easy one. Three supports is no more dense in (0, ∞) than one was, and this document does not claim otherwise.

6 · The bill, and what is left

41 named checks in 659 seconds, over a fresh 12,721,497-gear stream to 231,479,067. The new stream is checked against rh25's comb row for row at the shared support before anything is built on it; rh27a's certified floor is re-read rather than re-derived; every one of the twelve searched windows is verified notch-free over a whole period; the histogram pass is checked to have seen the same gears the comb stream did; the three certificates come from the same backend α with rh27a's parameters unchanged; and each is confirmed twice, by exact 60-digit positive-definiteness and by 60-digit inertia bisection reproducing the eigenvalue.

Against the order. rh27a asked for a second support and expected the answer to be that rh21 was right. It was not. rh21's two prices are real, a third runs the other way, and on the scale-free figure of merit the narrow window beats the wide one by an order of magnitude. The prediction that the searched optima would inherit the family's exact scaling of |ĥ|² was also wrong, and in the interesting direction — the widest support returns the broadest main lobe, not the narrowest.

What this does not do. It does not close the conjunction, and §5 says at length what remains. Three supports is a finite set. The exponent in (1) is measured over one dyadic range, at one depth, with one M, and is not a theorem about anything. The certificates here are shallow in nodes — 151, 76 and 39 — because depth 18 on a lattice of step Δ′ is a short section; they are not comparable in arithmetic to rh27a's 1201-node certificate and should not be read as though they were.

What is left. Three, and the first is now the obvious one.

(a) The cross filled in. The set is a cross because the two documents varied one axis each. Filling the rectangle — Δ′ = 0.12 at meshes Δ′/2 … Δ′/16, and the same at 0.48 — is arithmetic of exactly the kind rh27a made cheap, and it would be the first two-dimensional region the arc has certified. At 0.12 it is affordable: 151 nodes at the coarse mesh becomes 2401 at Δ′/16, which the half-turn split brings inside rh27a's budget.

(b) The exponent, at more than three points and more than one depth. (1) is two dyadic ratios. Whether −1.7111 is a genuine exponent or the local slope of something with curvature is decidable by running four more supports, and it matters: if the true behaviour flattens as Δ′ grows, the wide end is not as hard as the two points suggest.

(c) A ceiling that survives, which is rh27c. rh25 §4 killed the min–max ceiling over all windows of a fixed support and named the mechanism a survivor must beat — the archimedean logarithm, unopposed at high frequency. §3 here adds a reason to want one: the scale-free comparison is only as good as the family it is taken over, and a bound in terms of ‖h′‖₂ or a Sobolev norm would be the arc's first that is not family-bound, and the first that could say something about supports it has not searched.
