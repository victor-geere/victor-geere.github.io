3 · (b) A ceiling with the mesh in it

rh27c's ceiling ends in λmin(Tn) ≤ Eh(0), which is true for the elementary reason that the smallest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix is at most any diagonal entry. It is also the worst test vector available: a coordinate vector, which sees none of the lattice. Any other unit vector does better, and one of them is free.

(1) uj = eijθ/√n  ⟹  u*Tnu = Σ|k|<n (1 − |k|/n) Eh(kΔ′) eikθ = Fn(θ),

the Fejér mean of the row, because the number of (i, j) with i − j = k in an n × n block is n − |k|. So

(2) λmin(Tn)  ≤  minθ∈[0,π] Fn(θ),

computable from exactly the row the arc already builds, at the cost of one scan over θ. That is the bound rh27c asked for, and the whole of its content is that the section is Toeplitz.

How much it buys. The diagonal exceeds λmin by 24.5, 8.7, 3.5, 3.7, 3.3 at the five supports; the Fejér minimum by 2.75, 1.53, 1.12, 1.14, 1.13. So (2) closes 93% of the gap at the worst support and 95% at the best — one line of linear algebra against an inequality that took rh27c three.

Where it is not tight, and why. At Δ′ = 0.48 the ratio is 2.75, which is not close. That section has 39 nodes: a plane wave truncated to thirty-nine terms is a poor approximation to anything, and the Fejér weights (1 − |k|/n) throw away a third of the row's mass. The bound improves with the section and is inside 1.14× from n = 151 on. Stated rather than averaged away, because the arc's convention is that a bound is described where it fails as well as where it works.

What it is not. It is still an upper bound, and §6 of rh27c applies verbatim: upper bounds say how much positivity is available, never that any is present. It does not certify anything and it does not enter any floor.

4 · (c) What saturates — a prediction, refuted

rh27c measured λmin/Eh(0) at five supports — 0.041, 0.115, 0.285, 0.273, 0.299 — and called it out as a climb that stops: "what limits the narrow end is not the archimedean ceiling but something about the section's own spectrum, and this document does not know what."

The prediction. rh27c varied the support and the mesh together, taking the mesh equal to the support at depth 18. So its n was 18/Δ′ + 1 and grew as the support narrowed: 39, 76, 151, 301, 601. Two variables moved at once, and the obvious reading is that the flattening is a section-length effect — a plane wave, or any smooth test vector, becomes a better approximation to the true minimiser as the section lengthens, so the ratio should rise and settle for reasons that have nothing to do with the support. §3's Fejér ratio does exactly this across supports: 2.75, 1.53, 1.12, 1.14, 1.13.

The test. Take the single support Δ′ = 0.03 and slice its own row to the same five lengths, 39, 76, 151, 301 and 601. If the effect is section length, the curve should reappear.

n = 39   v = 1.14   λmin = 8.6831×10⁻²   λmin/min Fn = 0.997
n = 76   v = 2.25   λmin = 8.3874×10⁻²   λmin/min Fn = 0.992
n = 151   v = 4.50   λmin = 7.2728×10⁻²   λmin/min Fn = 0.951
n = 301   v = 9.00   λmin = 5.0013×10⁻²   λmin/min Fn = 0.958
n = 601   v = 18.00   λmin = 2.8347×10⁻²   λmin/min Fn = 0.886

It does not. The ratio is flat and near 1 where the cross-support version climbed from 0.363, and it moves the other way — the Fejér ceiling is tightest on the shallowest slice and loosens as the section deepens, where across supports at fixed depth it tightens as the mesh refines. The prediction was wrong, and it was wrong in the informative direction: the two variables are not interchangeable, and a short section on a fine mesh is a completely different object from a short section on a coarse one.

Where that leaves the question. rh27c ruled out the archimedean ceiling; this document rules out the section length. Two of the three obvious answers are gone and the third — that it is a property of the mesh itself — is not tested here. The honest statement is that §3 gives a much better ceiling and §4 does not explain the saturation, and those are separate results.

One correspondence, flagged. Three quantities in this document flatten at the same place: the diagonal ratio, the Fejér ratio, and §6's depth secant, all of which move sharply from Δ′ = 0.48 to 0.12 and barely at all from 0.12 to 0.03. Δ′ = 0.12 is also the first support whose lattice reaches past the first zero — π/Δ′ = 26.18 against γ₁ = 14.1347, where 0.24 reaches only 13.09. Three quantities and one threshold is more than one, but the observation was made after the fact, three flat points are not evidence, and no mechanism is proposed. It is recorded so that a later document can test it rather than rediscover it.

5 · The cancellation, and why there is no symbol

§3's Fejér mean is the truncation of a Fourier series, and the natural question is what it truncates. For an ordinary Toeplitz operator the answer is the symbol f(θ) = Σk∈ℤEh(kΔ′)eikθ, and then λmin(Tn) ≥ ess inf f for every n — which would collapse the whole depth direction into one number. It does not apply here, and the reason is worth measuring.

The kernel row is pole + arch − comb. The pole carries 2ĥ(i/2)²cosh(v/2) and rises by cosh 9 = 4,052 from v = 0 to v = 18. The comb carries the gear mass Σ|ln m − v| ≤ 2Δ′Λ(m)m−½, which by the prime number theorem also rises like ev/2, and it does: at v = 18 the two agree to 0.8%. The archimedean term has decayed to 3.5×10⁻⁸ by then and is irrelevant. What is left is the row, and the row does not grow: its largest entry over the whole eighteen is the diagonal itself, and at v = 18 it is 7.6×10⁻³ of the pole term that made it.

That is the explicit formula's own balance, seen directly rather than inferred. It is also the answer to the symbol question. Individually the two series diverge; the row is their difference and is bounded but not obviously summable, and the comb's lumpiness — comb/pole is 5.30 at v = 3 and 0.77 at v = 6, one prime power at a time — is what a Fourier series would have to absorb. So there is no L¹ symbol to take an infimum of, and the Szegő route to the depth infinity is closed. The arc works at finite depth because that is the only place the objects exist.

6 · The depth, and it is not a power law

Every document in this arc has treated depth as the direction one simply goes further into. rh19 fixed the horizon at v = 18; rh26 measured where positivity was lost with a short cut; rh27a restored it. None of them measured how λmin falls with depth, which is odd, because it is the cheapest measurement in the arc — the row is already built, and every prefix of it is a section. It is done here, and it does not say what one would have guessed.

End to end the decay is a factor 3.4 at Δ′ = 0.12 over a factor 18.8 in depth, which as a single exponent is v−0.414; across the five supports the endpoint secants are 0.972, 0.703, 0.414, 0.440, 0.394. Taken at face value that would be reassuring — a power below 1 never reaches zero — and it is the first quantitative statement the arc has about the one direction the conjunction requires infinitely much of.

It is not a power law, and the direction of the error matters. The curves in Figure 4 are not straight. At Δ′ = 0.12 the local slope over the six segments runs 0.04, 0.45, 0.49, 0.52, 0.73, 0.77 — nearly flat where the section is shallow and steepening throughout, by a factor of 18 end to end. The same happens at every support. So the endpoint secant is not an exponent, it is an average dominated by the shallow segments where almost nothing is happening, and the decay past v = 18 is steeper than the secant, not equal to it. An extrapolation built on 0.414 is therefore optimistic, and the extrapolated costs — 2.0 to reach v = 100, 5.3 to reach 1000 — are lower bounds on what it would actually cost, not estimates.

What that leaves. Something honest and unwelcome: the arc now has its depth curve, and the curve is worse than a power law. Whether the slope steepens to a finite limit — in which case λmin stays positive — or grows without bound — in which case it does not — is not decided by six points over one decade, and this document does not decide it. What can be said is that the previous silent assumption, that going deeper is a matter of arithmetic, is not supported by the only measurement of it. rh27b fitted an exponent over one dyadic range, called it the obstruction, and rh27c refuted it one document later; this section declines to make the same mistake and reports the curvature instead of the fit.

7 · The conjunction, and the bill

Where the conjunction stands. Nine certified points, still measure zero. Nothing here is a step towards RH: (a) adds two points to a finite set, (b) is an upper bound and upper bounds never certify, and (c) refutes an explanation without supplying one. What §6 adds is the depth curve, which is the first thing the arc has that speaks to an infinity rather than to another rung — and it is not the shape one would want. The decay steepens; the endpoint secant understates it; and whether the steepening stops is not decided here. That is a worse position than the arc believed it was in an hour ago, and it is the position.

What §5 rules out. One route that looked open is now closed. If the row had an L¹ symbol, positivity at every depth for a fixed window and mesh would reduce to ess inf f ≥ 0, a single number, and the depth infinity would be a computation rather than an infinity. It does not: the pole and the comb each diverge and only their difference is bounded. Ruling out a route is worth stating; it is why the finite-depth machinery exists.

A direction, named and still not attempted. Carried now for four documents rather than dropped. For a real even window g of compact support, ĝ is entire and ĥ = ĝ², so a hypothetical off-line quadruple at ½ ± σ + iγ contributes 4 Re[ĝ(γ + iσ)²] to the Weil functional — negative exactly when |Im ĝ(γ + iσ)| exceeds |Re ĝ(γ + iσ)|, and directly computable for the arc's actual windows on a (γ, σ) grid. A certified floor bounds what such a contribution could hide behind. It is still the only route in sight by which these certificates would speak about ζ rather than about forms, and it is still untried.

The bill. 43 named checks in 840 seconds. No new sieve; rh27c's stream and histogram and rh27a's ψ̃ table reused unchanged; all five rows checked against the λmin their own documents published; the two certificates issued by rh27a's backend with its parameters untouched and each confirmed twice; the Fejér bound checked to hold at every support; the pole–arch–comb decomposition checked to reconstruct the row it came from to machine precision.

What is left. Three.

(a) The saturation, third attempt. Two explanations are dead. The one not tested is that it is the mesh — that the Fejér ceiling's tightness, and the depth exponent, are functions of δ alone. That is one more slice of the same rows: fix the depth and vary the mesh at a fixed support, which the arc has never done because it has always taken mesh = support or support = 0.24.

(b) The depth curve, past one decade. §6 measures v ∈ [1, 18] and finds the decay steepening. Whether the local slope tends to a limit or grows without bound is the whole question, and six points cannot answer it. The certified horizon is 18 because rh19 put it there and the comb costs ev; but the float λmin costs nothing beyond the sieve, and a stream to e24 would extend the curve by a third of a decade at about eleven times rh27c's gear count. That is the cheapest thing in the arc that bears directly on the conjunction, and it should be next.

(c) A better test vector. §3's plane wave is the crudest thing that is not a coordinate vector, and it already closes 93% of the gap. A tapered plane wave — the same eijθ against a Hann or prolate envelope — replaces the Fejér weights with the taper's own autocorrelation and would close most of the rest, at the cost of one more parameter to minimise over. It is worth doing only if a ceiling is worth having, which §7's first paragraph doubts.
