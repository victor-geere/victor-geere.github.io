The window, searched

rh21 left one open item and it was the only one on the arc's list: the triangle hat has been the construction's window since rh14 and has never been varied. It is varied here, inside a family that needs no new analytic machinery, and the result is large — the certified floor rises 2.70×106× at v = 12 and the typical weight the low zeros receive rises 1691×, closing rh21's universal barrier from 3797× to 2.25×. Before any of that, and at the reader's request, §2 audits the whole arc against the Riemann hypothesis itself: 17 certified sections, total cone dimension 2760, and a plain statement of the distance still to run. §3 is a correction: the first draft of this document read the search as failing, and both halves of that reading were instrument error.

1 · The order

rh21 §6 closed with one item and framed it as the largest lever in the construction: "Find the h, supported in [−Δ, Δ] at unit energy, that maximises the typical weight over the low zeros — or better, that maximises the certified floor m₀ directly, which is the quantity the certificates actually spend. A Slepian gains 62× without trying; the barrier says up to 158× more is available." It attached two constraints, both of which are respected below: score on a typical weight and never a minimum, because the minimum swings by two orders under refinement; and search shapes at fixed support, never supports, because a wider window pays twice.

A second item was added by hand rather than by rh21: an audit of where the arc actually stands relative to the Riemann hypothesis. Eighteen documents have accumulated and none of them has ever totalled the account. §2 does that first, before the new work, so that whatever §§4–6 win is measured against a stated baseline rather than against an impression.

The two turn out to belong together. The audit's honest summary of class A is a dimension count — how much of Weil positivity has been verified unconditionally, and inside how wide a window. The search's result is that the same computational budget buys a floor four to seven orders larger. Neither changes the measure-zero verdict; both change the constant in front of it.

2 · The audit — where the arc stands

This section asserts nothing new. Every figure in it is re-read from the constants file the document that produced it wrote, re-asserted in compute_rh22.py, and classified. That is deliberate: an audit written from memory drifts, and this arc has already had to correct itself twice.

Class A · unconditional. 11 certified positive-definite lattice sections with explicit floors — rh17's three (Δ = 0.06, 0.03, 0.015 at n = 199, 399, 799) and rh19's eight at the coarse meshes, the deepest being Δ = 0.24 at n = 76, v = 18 — plus rh20's six certified union node sets, 17 in all. rh18's equivalence, with every continuity constant computed: the lattice statement (L), the window statement (W), and Bochner positivity (Bo) are the same statement, and by Weil's criterion (cited, not reproved) that statement is RH. rh18 and rh19's escape balls of non-lattice windows, best radius 5.74×10-9. rh20's weight ceiling c₀ = 2.1002465, with a certified enclosure. rh21's barrier w ≤ πC(0)/T, which is one line and holds for every compactly supported window. The named hypotheses under all of it: Weil's criterion for the RH face only, Rosser–Schoenfeld, IEEE-754, libmpdec, and libm's logarithm within one ulp — the last spot-verified against 20007 exact evaluations in rh19.

Class B · conditional on RH. The instruments, not the results. rh21 §2's zeros-side kernel and everything the zeros list carries: rh18's zeros witness, rh20's zero positions, rh21's entire decay ladder. These are flagged rather than folded in, because a construction validated against the zeros cannot then be evidence about them.

Class C · measured, not proved. The decay law v⁻⁵·⁷⁴¹ at R² = 0.9934; rh15's σ²v² ≈ 3.92×10-3; rh19's horizon ceiling; rh20's Diophantine dip structure; rh21's Slepian gain and this document's larger one. Real numbers, honestly fitted, and none of them a theorem.

Class D · routes closed. rh18: the certificate does not descend to sub-lattices. rh20: no finite family of meshes carries (W), and the starvation that blocks it is a property of the hat rather than of the lattice — which is precisely the observation this document acts on. rh21: the arithmetic was never the wall; the sieve is, at v ≈ 27–30.

Class E · the distance. (L) is RH — not a proxy for it, not an approach to it, the same statement. What has been verified is finitely many points of a conjunction that ranges over every mesh Δ and every section size n, so the measure-theoretic answer is zero and nothing in this arc changes that. The honest positive statement is a dimension count: Weil positivity has been verified, unconditionally and with explicit floors, on explicit finite-dimensional cones of total dimension 2760, inside windows of half-width up to 9.24. Against a hypothesis that needs every window at every mesh, that is a fixed finite number against an infinite conjunction. The arc's own ledger, for what it is worth as a measure of effort rather than of progress: 875 named checks across 15 computations, re-counted here from the stored files.

3 · Two traps, and the instrument

The first draft of §5 read the optimised window as failing — positive at n = 51, negative at n = 76 — and concluded that the search had overfitted one section size. Both halves of that reading were wrong, and both were instrument error rather than arithmetic error, so both are reported here at the same prominence as the result.

Trap 1 · the comb outran the sieve. The kernel is E(v) = pole + arch − comb, and at v = 18 the pole term 2Bi²cosh(v/2) reaches 468. What cancels it is the prime comb, and the comb at v needs every prime power below ev+2δ. A sieve to 2×10⁵ supplies the δ = 0.03 comb only out to v = ln(2×10⁵) − 2δ = 12.15; past that the comb is simply empty and the row is uncancelled pole. Nothing announces this. Below the horizon the short sieve and the long one agree to 10−14, so the row looks entirely normal; the damage is confined to the last few entries, where it is largest. rh19 hit exactly this and fixed it; the fix did not survive into a new script. The section it produces at n = 51 fails its Cholesky at row 49 — precisely where v = 11.76 crosses the sieve's reach — and its true smallest eigenvalue is -1.8681.

Trap 2 · the Levinson lie. That would have been caught immediately if λmin had been computed honestly. rh19–rh21 computed it by inverse iteration through the Levinson–Durbin recursion, which is fast and exact-enough on a positive-definite Toeplitz row. On an indefinite one it divides by βk = detk/detk−1, which has changed sign, and what comes out has no relation to the spectrum. Here it comes out positive, at 7.114×10-9 — for a matrix whose smallest eigenvalue is -1.8681. And it is worse than a wrong number: 7.114×10-9 is within 43% of the correct section's 4.990×10-9, so it survives every order-of-magnitude sanity check a reader would apply. It is not the first time in this arc that a float lane has been caught lying about an indefinite section. It is the first time the lie was plausible.

The instrument. Every λmin in this document is computed by bisection on the inertia. By Sylvester's law the number of non-positive Durbin pivots of T − σI is the number of eigenvalues at or below σ, whatever their signs; bisecting on that count has no breakdown mode. It costs O(n²) per test and about seventy tests, which is cheaper than the inverse iteration it replaces. Each reported value is then re-derived in exact 60-digit decimal arithmetic — the two agree to 10−8 relative at every section size, including the one whose λmin is 1.558×10-10 — and positive-definiteness is confirmed independently by a dense exact Cholesky that shares no code with the Durbin recursion. The bottom eigenvector, which the search needs, is found by inverse iteration on T − σI with σ just below λmin, which makes the shifted matrix positive definite so that a plain Cholesky applies and nothing is ever divided by an indefinite pivot.

Two regressions close the section. rh19's certified floor at n = 18 is 2.2962×10-6 and the exact λmin recomputed here is 2.3190×10-6; at n = 51 the pair is 4.9371×10-9 and 4.9897×10-9. Both floors sit below their eigenvalue, as a certified lower bound must, and within 2% of it — which is the arc's strongest statement so far that backend α's error accounting is not merely valid but tight.

4 · The family, and a ceiling over it

The whole design rests on one observation. Write Δ = 0.24 as 8δ with δ = 0.03, and let the window be a piecewise-linear function on the δ-grid:

(1) h  =  Σ|k| ≤ 7 ak φδ(· − kδ),    ak = a−k,    φδ = the triangle hat of half-width δ.

Then h is supported in [−Δ, Δ] automatically, Ch = h⋆ȟ in [−2Δ, 2Δ], and — writing wd = Σj−k=d ajak for the autocorrelation of the coefficients —

(2) Eh(v)  =  Σ|d| ≤ 14 wd · Eδ(v − dδ).

The new kernel is an exact linear form in w applied to the old kernel on the δ-lattice. It holds piece by piece — pole, archimedean integral and prime comb separately — because w is symmetric, so every sine term cancels and each piece factors through Σdwdcos(rdδ) = A(r)², A(r) = Σkakcos(krδ). Three things follow, and all three are what make this document cheap. The triangle hat is in the family, at ak = 1 − |k|/8, so the search can only improve on it. δ = 0.03 is a mesh rh17 already certified, so Eh's rows are exact combinations of certified interval rows and rh19's backend α applies unchanged. And rh18's identity T1a is a Fubini argument valid for any h, so the whole (L)⟺(W)⟺(Bo) chain carries over with no re-derivation at all.

Identity (2) is the one thing that must not be taken on trust, so it is checked against an independent computation. rh19's stored stream sieved 7.24×10⁸ prime powers at Δ = 0.24; this document sieves 6,448,870 at δ = 0.03. The two share no arithmetic beyond the interval kit. Reconstructing the hat's Δ = 0.24 kernel from the δ rows and comparing at all seventy-six nodes gives a worst relative difference of 4.91×10-10. The reconstructed energy is Ch(0) = 2Δ/3 = 0.16 exactly, which is the constraint the search holds fixed.

A ceiling for every window at once. The search below is a maximin, and a maximin always admits the same bound. For a fixed unit vector c, the quantity cᵀThc is linear in Ch and hence a quadratic form aᵀMca in the coefficients; its maximum over the family at fixed energy aᵀGa = Ch(0) is the top generalised eigenvalue of (Mc, G), computed by a shifted power iteration on the whitened form. Since maxh minc ≤ minc maxh,

(3) m₀(h)  ≤  minc ∈ 𝒞  λtop(Mc, G)·Ch(0)    for every h in the family and any finite set 𝒞.

This is the analogue of rh20's c₀, but for the quantity the certificates actually spend rather than for the weight at a zero. It costs nothing extra: every worst-case vector the search meets is a legitimate member of 𝒞, so the ceiling tightens automatically as the search runs. Because the power iteration runs on an indefinite form it is shifted before iterating — otherwise it converges to whichever eigenvalue is largest in modulus, which can be the most negative one, and the first draft of this document reported a negative ceiling for exactly that reason.

5 · The search

Maximise λmin(Th) over the eight free coefficients at fixed energy. The objective is a minimum of smooth functions, so it is not differentiable where the bottom eigenvalue is degenerate and plain ascent stalls; the cutting-plane form does not care. Given the current a, take the worst vector c as the bottom eigenvector; given that c, take the family's best response by (3); line-search between the two. Started from the triangle hat, at each of the three section sizes rh19 certified.

The result is larger than rh21 expected from a Slepian, and it is not a change of scale. At n = 51 (v = 12, matching rh19's comparable certificate) λmin rises from 4.9897×10-9 to 1.3424×10-2, a factor 2.7×106×. At n = 76 (v = 18, rh19's deepest coarse section) it rises from 1.5578×10-10 to 3.9862×10-3, a factor 2.6×107×. At n = 18 (v = 4.08, rh19's escape-optimal corner) the factor is 5.8×104×. Over the same moves Eh(0) changes by at most 28×, so the scale-free ratio λmin/Eh(0) still improves by 9.7×104× at n = 51 and 9.7×105× at n = 76: the conditioning itself gets better, not just the units.

Against the ceiling. Each optimum stops 2.3–2.7× below the bound (3) computed from the cutting planes the search itself generated. That number does two jobs. It says the search is close to the best this eight-parameter family allows — so there is no large unexploited gain hiding inside the same design space. And it says the triangle hat was not accidentally near-optimal: at v = 18 the hat sat 2.6×107× below the family's own reach, which is the strongest form of rh21's suspicion that the window had never been looked at.

Against rh21's Slepian. Sampling rh21's band-concentrated window onto the same eight coefficients puts it in the family too, and it behaves exactly as rh21 predicted — a real improvement on the hat at every section size (1.7×104× at n = 51) and nowhere near the optimum. The Slepian is the extremal for concentration of ĥ in a band; λmin(Th) is a different functional, and optimising the right one is worth another 1.6×102× over optimising a proxy for it.

It is not tuned to one n. The window found at n = 51 is used unchanged at the other two sizes: 1.4034×10-1 at n = 18 and 3.5047×10-3 at n = 76, which is 6.1×104× and 2.2×107× times the hat there. All three sections are positive definite in exact arithmetic. This is the check that failed in the first draft, for the reasons §3 gives.

What the window looks like. It oscillates, and its integral nearly vanishes: Bi = ĥ(i/2) falls from the hat's +0.2403 to -0.0113. That is not incidental — Bi is what multiplies the pole term in both the kernel and rh18's Klin, so a window that integrates to almost nothing suppresses the very term the prime comb has to fight. Nothing in the search knew that; it emerged from maximising λmin alone.

6 · The certificate, and what the gain buys

A float eigenvalue is not a theorem, and §3 is the reason to say so twice. The optimised window therefore goes through rh19's backend α unchanged: a certified Cholesky at 40 digits with a shift s, a residual norm at 90 digits, and a floor s − ‖residual‖ − ρround − ρD. What is new is only where the rows come from. Certified interval rows for Eδ at δ = 0.03 are built out to v = 18.48 — 617 rows of rigorous archimedean integral and pole, each entry of width below 5.69×10-13, minus a comb interval whose radius is rh19's proven certified-float bound applied to the gear mass this document's own stream recorded. Identity (2) then carries them to the composite window, with the entry radius and the aliasing radius both scaling by Σd|wd|. The trapezoid aliasing radius at this mesh and this horizon is 5.53×10-17. Lemma L1 — that truncating the archimedean integral at R₀ drops a positive-semidefinite piece, because ψ̃ > 0 beyond r ≈ 2π — holds for any window, since the dropped form is (1/π)∫|ĥ|²ψ̃|ΣcpeiprΔ|², so it carries over with no re-derivation.

(4) m₀  =  1.3894×10-1 at v = 4.08,    1.3290×10-2 at v = 12,    3.4696×10-3 at v = 18    — certified, for a window the arc has never used.

Against the hat on the same route: 6.05×104× at n = 18 and 2.70×106× at n = 51. At n = 76 the comparison cannot be made, and the reason is worth stating rather than hiding. Recombining twenty-nine certified δ-rows into each Δ-row costs radius — 4.6× more than rh19's direct Δ = 0.24 rows, 2.54×10-10 against its 5.47×10-11 — and the hat's own λmin there is only 1.558×10-10, a margin of 0.61× against that radius. So this route certifies nothing for the hat at v = 18. rh19 certified that same section directly at Δ = 0.24, where no recombination is needed; this is a limit of the δ-basis route, not a retraction of rh19. The optimised window on the identical route has a margin of 1.5×107×. That is the clearest single statement of what the search bought: the same machinery, the same radius, and one window certifies where the other cannot.

The weight at the zeros. Scored on rh21's own statistic — the geometric mean of |ĥ(γ)|² over the first forty zeros at unit energy — the hat gives 6.729×10-6, rh21's Slepian in this family 1.615×10-4, and the optimum 1.138×10-2: a factor 1691 over the hat, against the 62× rh21 measured for a continuous Slepian. rh21's barrier w ≤ πC(0)/T = 2.5552×10-2 still holds, as it must for every compactly supported window, but the room under it has almost gone: the hat sat 3797× under, rh21's Slepian 158×, the optimum 2.25×. The geometry behind that number is visible in the figure — an exactly even spread of |ĥ|² across the low zeros would give a mean weight of 2.887×10-2, and the optimum's mean is 2.946×10-2, or 1.02 of even. It is spending its energy almost uniformly over the range where the zeros are, which is precisely what saturating the barrier means. Two things are checked to make sure this is not a normalisation artifact: Parseval, ∫0∞|ĥ|² = πCh(0), holds numerically for all three windows; and no single weight exceeds 2Δ, which Cauchy–Schwarz forces on the support. The minimum over the forty zeros also improves by more than four orders, and is reported without being leaned on — rh21 showed the minimum is a lottery on where the nulls fall.

The escape radius. rh18's T3 turns a certified floor into a ball of genuinely non-lattice windows around the certified configuration, of radius r* = m₀/Klin. Klin was derived for the triangle hat, so every Ch-derived norm inside it is inflated here by Θ = max(1, ‖w‖₁/‖what‖₁) — the worst-case amplification, charged against every term at once and never allowed to work in the new window's favour. It comes out at Θ = 1, so the improvement is carried entirely by m₀ and by Bi. The hat's own r* recomputed this way is 5.7424×10-9 against rh19's published 5.74×10-9. The optimum reaches

(5) r*  =  3.7749×10-4    at Δ = 0.24, n = 18, a = 2.28    — 6.57×104× rh19's best.

The corner is the same one rh19 found: coarsest mesh, smallest support, because Klin grows like ea while the floor decays. What has changed is only the numerator, and it has changed by four orders.

7 · The bill, and what is left

65 named checks in 21 seconds over a stream of 6,448,870 prime powers, plus 875 re-read from rh7–rh21 in §2. The identity (2) is verified against rh19's independent sieve at every node before anything is built on it; every λmin is confirmed in exact 60-digit arithmetic and every positive-definiteness claim by an independent dense Cholesky; rh19's two certified floors are reproduced through a completely different route; rh21's hat statistic and barrier are reproduced from its stored constants; and the two traps of §3 each carry their own assertion, including the assertion that the wrong answer is plausible.

Against the order. rh21's single open item is answered rather than corrected, which is the first time in three documents. The window was the largest lever, it is worth four to seven orders on the certified floor and 1691× on the weight at the zeros, and the barrier rh21 identified is now nearly saturated — 2.25× of room where there were 3797×. The audit that was added alongside it produced no correction to any published claim, which is worth saying because the previous two documents both did.

What this does not do. It does not move the verdict of §2 by one inch. The certified cones are the same cones, of the same dimension 2760, at the same half-supports; only the floors on them are larger. A 6.57×104× escape radius is still 3.7749×10-4 — a ball around the lattice, not a neighbourhood of the line. And the search's own optimality is bounded, not established: the ceiling says it is within 2.3–2.7× of the family's best, and nothing at all bounds what a larger family would reach.

What is left. Three items, in the order a successor should take them.

(i) The larger family. δ = 0.015 gives seventeen free coefficients instead of eight, at the cost of a δ-row twice as long and a certified lane twice as slow. The ceiling machinery of §4 extends unchanged and would say immediately whether the extra freedom is worth anything — the present ceiling bounds only the eight-parameter family, and nothing here suggests where the true supremum over all windows of support Δ sits.

(ii) The certified route at n = 76. The δ-basis costs a factor 4.6 in radius through the recombination, which is what put the hat's deepest section out of reach in §6. Building certified rows for the composite window directly at Δ = 0.24, rather than recombining δ rows, would recover it — the machinery is rh19's, and the only new object needed is a certified interval evaluation of A(r)² inside the archimedean integrand.

(iii) The window at other meshes. Everything here is at Δ = 0.24. rh20 proved that no finite family of meshes carries (W) with the triangle hat, and identified four Δ-starved zeros as the mechanism. Those four zeros are a property of the hat's transform, and §6 has just shown the transform is negotiable. Whether the optimised window at two incommensurable meshes clears them is a direct question with rh20's machinery already written for it.
