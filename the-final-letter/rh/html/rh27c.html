A ceiling that survives

rh25 §4 proved there is no ceiling on Eh(0)/Ch(0) over all windows of a fixed support, and named the mechanism exactly: the archimedean weight is (1/π)∫|ĥ|²ψ̃ with ψ̃(r) growing like ln r, and nothing else in the kernel grows at all, so pushing the window's energy up to frequency r₀ sends the diagonal to ψ̃(r₀) and off to infinity. rh25 §6(b) asked for a bound that survives that. It exists, it is three lines, and it is sharp. (a) ψ̃(r) ≤ ln((2+r)/2π) for every r ≥ 0, with the gap closing like 2/r — so the constant cannot be improved. (b) |ĥ(r)|²dr/(π‖h‖²) is a probability measure with second moment ‖h′‖²/‖h‖², and ln(2+r) is concave, so Jensen turns the lemma into λmin/Ch(0) ≤ ln((2+‖h′‖/‖h‖)/2π) + 4 sinh Δ′ + G(2Δ′). Family-free: every real even window of compact support with h′ ∈ L². (c) Along rh25's own extremal sequence the slack closes to 0.00008, and all five of rh24's and rh25's M-family ceilings are recovered from this one bound at each family's own band edge, to within 0.8%. (d) And it makes a prediction: the ceiling grows like ln(1/Δ′) where rh27b measured Δ′−1.7111, so that power law cannot survive past Δ′ = 0.03. Two new searches say it was already gone by 0.06. The exponents are 0.2776 and 0.1349 against rh27b's 1.7225 and 1.6998.

2 · (a) The lemma, and how little room is in it

Everything in this document rests on one inequality:

(1) ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π  ≤  ln((2 + r)/2π)    for every r ≥ 0.

Two things make it worth stating rather than assuming. It is concave on the right, which is what lets Jensen through in §3; and it is tight, which is what makes the resulting ceiling sharp rather than merely finite.

The tail, r ≥ 1. Binet's second formula gives, for Re z > 0,

(2) ψ(z) = ln z − 1/(2z) − 2∫0∞ t dt/((t² + z²)(e2πt − 1)).

At z = ¼ + ir/2 the denominator satisfies |t² + z²| ≥ |Im z²| = r/4 for every t ≥ 0, and 2∫0∞ t dt/(e2πt − 1) = Γ(2)ζ(2)/(2π)² = 1/12 exactly, so the integral term is at most 1/(3r) in modulus. Re(1/2z) = (1/8)/(1/16 + r²/4) is positive and is discarded in the safe direction. Hence ψ̃(r) ≤ ½ln(1/16 + r²/4) − ln π + 1/(3r), and comparing that with the right-hand side of (1) leaves a margin of ln(2(2+r)/√(1+4r²)) − 1/(3r), which is 0.6537 at r = 1 and then shrinks like (5/3)/r — the 2/r the majorant's own +2 supplies, less the 1/(3r) Binet costs. Positive throughout, checked out to r = 1.6×105.

The head, 0 ≤ r ≤ 1. The same estimate turns negative below r ≈ 0.24, so the head is done separately, and it is easy because ψ̃ is increasing: differentiating the series Re ψ(x+iy) = −γ + Σn≥0[1/(n+1) − (n+x)/((n+x)² + y²)] in r gives Σn≥0 (n+¼)(r/2)/((n+¼)² + r²/4)², every term positive. So on a panel [a, b] the left side is at most ψ̃(b) and the right at least ln((2+a)/2π), and 4000 panels give sup(ψ̃ − ln((2+r)/2π)) ≤ -1.2858 on [0, 1]. There is a factor of e1.28 of room down there; the inequality is nowhere near tight at the low end.

Where rh17's constant came from. rh17 §1 established L1 by locating the zero of ψ̃ numerically at r₊ = 6.289836 and remarked that it sat "a whisker above 2π". The lemma says why. ψ̃(r) = ln(r/2π) + O(r−2) — that is the same asymptotic, with the +2 dropped — so its zero is 2π to the accuracy of that remainder, and the whisker is 0.0067. A constant the arc has carried since rh17 as a measured number is a consequence of the asymptotic it never wrote down.

3 · (b) The ceiling

Let h be real, even, supported in [−Δ′, Δ′], with h′ ∈ L². Write Ch = h ⋆ h, so Ch(0) = ‖h‖² and Ĉh = |ĥ|² ≥ 0. The kernel's diagonal is Eh(0) = pole(0) + arch(0) − comb(0), and each of the three is bounded separately.

The arch, by Jensen. Plancherel with ĥ(r) = ∫h(t)e−irtdt and h even gives ∫0∞|ĥ|²dr = π‖h‖² and ∫0∞r²|ĥ|²dr = π‖h′‖². So

(3) dμ(r) = |ĥ(r)|² dr / (π‖h‖²)

is a probability measure on [0, ∞), and arch(0)/Ch(0) is exactly 𝔼μ[ψ̃] — not an estimate of it, the same number. Now apply (1), then Jensen to the concave ln(2+·), then Cauchy–Schwarz to replace the first moment by the second:

(4) 𝔼μ[ψ̃] ≤ 𝔼μ[ln(2+r)] − ln 2π ≤ ln(2 + 𝔼μ[r]) − ln 2π ≤ ln((2 + ‖h′‖/‖h‖)/2π).

The pole, by Cauchy–Schwarz. pole(0) = 2ĥ(i/2)² and ĥ(i/2) = ∫h(t)et/2dt ≤ ‖h‖·(∫−Δ′Δ′etdt)½, so pole(0)/Ch(0) ≤ 4 sinh Δ′. Uniform in h, and it vanishes with the support.

The comb, by the gear mass. |Ch(x)| ≤ Ch(0) for every x, and Ch is supported in [−2Δ′, 2Δ′], so |comb(0)|/Ch(0) ≤ G(2Δ′) = Σ|ln m| ≤ 2Δ′ Λ(m)m−½. rh25 (9) observed that this is zero whenever 2Δ′ < ln 2 = 0.693, because the nearest prime power to 1 is 2. Every support this arc has used except 0.48 is inside that, so at Δ, Δ/2 and everything narrower the diagonal is pole plus arch exactly.

Putting the three together with the elementary λmin(Th) ≤ Eh(0) — the smallest eigenvalue of a Toeplitz section is at most any diagonal entry:

(5) λmin(Th)/Ch(0)  ≤  Eh(0)/Ch(0)  ≤  ln((2 + ‖h′‖2/‖h‖2)/2π) + 4 sinh Δ′ + G(2Δ′),

for every real even h of half-support Δ′ with h′ ∈ L², at every section size, on every lattice. That is the first bound in this arc that names no family, no mesh, no M and no generator.

Reading the columns. The gap between the certified eigenvalue and the ceiling is large at the wide end and closes at the narrow one, and almost none of that is the bound's doing. At Δ′ = 0.48 the eigenvalue is 0.041 of the diagonal; at 0.12 it is 0.285. The Jensen step, isolated, costs 0.23–0.53 nats across the twelve windows searched here and drifts up slowly as the support narrows, because a narrower window spreads |ĥ|² over more decades and ln is more concave over a wider spread. The pole allowance runs 0.481, 0.240, 0.120 and is on its way to nothing.

4 · (c) Sharp, and it subsumes the family ceilings

A finite bound on a quantity rh25 proved unbounded is not by itself interesting — it is bought entirely by the ‖h′‖₂ in it, and a wasteful bound would buy it too. What makes (5) worth having is that it gives up nothing.

Against rh25's own witness. rh25 §4 proved there is no ceiling with an explicit sequence: h = √2 cos(r₀t)φΔ(t), for which Eh(0)/Ch(0) equals ψ̃(r₀) "to 0.05% at r₀ = 200, 10³, 5×10³ and 2.5×10⁴". Evaluate (4) on that same sequence and the slack is 0.0106, 0.00202, 0.0004, 0.00008 — closing to zero, and closing like 2/r₀, which is the lemma's own gap and not a second loss. The bound is not the right slope with a constant to spare. It is the right value.

Against the arc's own published ceilings. rh24 §3 computed a ceiling for the M-family by a min–max duality, one family at a time, and rh25 §4 extended the ladder: M = 8: 2.809, M = 16: 3.515, M = 32: 4.211, M = 64: 4.904, M = 128: 5.596. Every one of them is (4) evaluated at that family's own band edge πM/Δ, to within 0.8%. Five theorems, proved separately at increasing cost, are five points on one curve. And the observation that raised the question in the first place — that the ceilings "grow along the ladder" by about ln 2 per doubling of M and do not converge — is now not an observation but an identity: they are ln of a quantity that doubles.

What that means for the published results. Nothing rh22–rh25 asserted is affected; those ceilings remain theorems about the families they were computed for, and they are now also corollaries. What changes is that the family index was never the essential penalty. It was a proxy for ‖h′‖₂/‖h‖₂, which is what the archimedean term actually sees.

5 · (d) The prediction, and the test

rh27b measured λmin/Ch(0) at three supports with the shape held fixed and found a clean power law, Δ′−1.7111, the two dyadic exponents 1.7225 and 1.6998 agreeing to 1.3%. It called that exponent the obstruction — the quantitative content of "the support axis is a second infinity" — and flagged it as "a measured slope over one dyadic range at one depth with one M, not a theorem".

The prediction. (5) bounds λmin/Ch(0) by ln((2+S)/2π) + 4 sinh Δ′ with S = ‖h′‖/‖h‖. For a family at fixed M, S is proportional to 1/Δ′ — measured 352, 714, 1230 at the three supports here — so the ceiling grows like ln(1/Δ′). A power cannot stay under a logarithm. Extrapolating rh27b's law and its ceiling side by side, they cross between Δ′ = 0.06 and 0.03: at 0.06 the law wants 3.15 against a ceiling of 4.98, and at 0.03 it wants 10.30 against 5.40. So the exponent must break by Δ′ = 0.03, whatever the search finds.

The test. Twelve more searches, at Δ′ = 0.12 (the anchor), 0.06 and 0.03, four shapes each, all verified notch-free over a whole period, at depth 18 on sections of 301 and 601 nodes. The triangle row, scale-free, across five supports now:

Δ′ = 0.48    λmin/Ch(0) = 0.0897  ·  Δ′ = 0.24    λmin/Ch(0) = 0.2913  ·  Δ′ = 0.12    λmin/Ch(0) = 0.9612  ·  Δ′ = 0.06    λmin/Ch(0) = 1.1652  ·  Δ′ = 0.03    λmin/Ch(0) = 1.2794

The dyadic exponents, wide end first: 1.6998, 1.7225, then 0.2776 and 0.1349.

What the test says, plainly. The power law is gone, and it did not bend gently towards the crossing the ceiling forced — it broke at the first new rung. At Δ′ = 0.03 rh27b's law wants 10.30 and the measurement gives 1.2794, short by a factor of 8.1. The increments in λmin/Ch(0) per halving run 0.670, 0.204, 0.114 — roughly halving themselves, which is what a bounded sequence looks like and not what a power law does. The bound was sufficient to forbid the law and was not tight about where. That is worth saying in both directions: the prediction was right, and the mechanism that actually stops the growth stops it earlier and harder than the ceiling requires.

What saturates, and it is not the ceiling. There is still a factor of 3.8 of unused ceiling at Δ′ = 0.03, so nothing is pressing against (5). What flattens is λmin/Eh(0), the section's smallest eigenvalue as a fraction of its own diagonal. Taking the best window at each of rh27b's supports it climbed 0.041, 0.115, 0.285; taking the fixed triangle shape across the three supports here it is 0.251, 0.273, 0.258 — flat. Those are two different window sequences and not one trend, and that is worth saying rather than eliding: what they agree on is that the climb has stopped, and that it stopped a long way short of the 1 that (5) allows. So what limits the narrow end is not the archimedean ceiling but something about the section's own spectrum, and this document does not know what.

6 · The conjunction, and what this does and does not do

It does not move the conjunction, and the reason is structural. The arc's target is that Eh be positive definite on every lattice, at every depth, for every window, which by rh18 §5 is equivalent to RH. (5) is an upper bound. It says how much positivity is available at a given frequency budget; it never says any is present. Every certificate the arc has issued is a lower bound, and no combination of upper bounds produces one. A document that proves a ceiling has not moved towards RH by a single certified point, and the certified set is exactly what rh27b left it: seven points, still measure zero.

It does move the reading of rh27b. That is a self-correction of the immediately preceding document and it should be stated without hedging. rh27b's §5 asked whether the support axis is a real second infinity or a gauge direction, answered "real", and quantified the answer as λmin/Ch(0) ∝ Δ′−1.7111. The first half stands: the kernel has two scales of its own — ψ̃'s crossing at r₊ and the prime lattice ln p — that do not move with Δ′, so no rescaling carries a certificate from one support to another, and the support axis is a genuine second limit. The second half does not. −1.7111 was a local slope on the wide half of one dyadic range. rh27b said as much in its own §5 and rh27c's §5 is that caveat cashed: the true behaviour is logarithmic, and the arc's own bound forced it.

What is genuinely new. Three things, none of them a step towards RH. The lemma is sharp and explains a constant rh17 measured. The ceiling is the arc's first bound that names no family, and it subsumes five that did. And the pair (5) plus a certified floor brackets: at Δ′ = 0.03 the arc now knows that the best window of that support and Sobolev norm has λmin/Ch(0) somewhere in [1.2794, 5.40], where before it knew only the lower end. A bracket is not a proof of anything, but it is the first time the arc has had one.

A direction, named and still not attempted. Repeated from rh27a and rh27b rather than quietly dropped, because it has now been carried for three documents. Everything the arc certifies is a statement about a quadratic form; nothing it has produced is a statement about a zero. For a real even window g of compact support, ĝ is entire and ĥ = ĝ², so a hypothetical off-line quadruple at ½ ± σ + iγ contributes 4 Re[ĝ(γ + iσ)²] to the Weil functional — negative exactly when |Im ĝ(γ + iσ)| exceeds |Re ĝ(γ + iσ)|, and directly computable for the arc's actual windows on a (γ, σ) grid. A certified floor bounds what such a contribution could hide behind; closing it into an explicit zero-free region needs an upper bound on the on-line part. §3 is an upper bound, but on the wrong thing — it bounds the diagonal, not the on-line sum — and the two are not the same object. Still the only route in sight, still untried.

7 · The bill, and what is left

45 named checks in 1069 seconds over a fresh 4,930,376-gear stream. The new stream reproduces rh27b's comb row for row at the shared support and the search there returns rh27b's own optimum exactly; ψ̃'s monotonicity is checked against a central difference; Binet's constant is recomputed rather than cited; the lemma is verified on its head and its tail by separate arguments and its sharpness by the −2/r law; every one of the five family ceilings rh22–rh25 published is recovered; all twelve new windows are verified notch-free over a whole period; and the ceiling is checked to hold at every window in the document.

Against the order. rh25 §6(b) is answered, and it turned out to be the easiest of the three rh25 listed rather than the hardest — three inequalities, one of them Jensen. rh25 called it "genuinely analysis rather than computation" and that was right; what it got wrong was the difficulty, which came entirely from not having written ψ̃'s asymptotic down.

What this does not do. It does not certify anything: the λmin values at the two new supports are float, from the same bundle search rh25 wrote, and no interval certificate is issued for them. It does not extend the certified cross. It bounds the diagonal, which is a crude proxy for the eigenvalue — §5 measures how crude, and at the wide end it is a factor of twenty-four.

What is left. Three.

(a) The two new supports, certified. The searches at 0.06 and 0.03 are uncertified, and certifying them is arithmetic of exactly the kind rh27a made cheap — sections of 301 and 601 nodes at depth 18, well inside rh27a's budget with the half-turn split. That would take the certified cross from seven points to nine and put the whole of §5's test on certified footing.

(b) What actually saturates. §5 finds λmin/Eh(0) levelling near 0.258 and does not explain it. The ceiling is not what stops it, and the answer is a statement about the section's spectrum rather than about the window — the first question in the arc that is about Th as an operator rather than about h.

(c) The ceiling with the mesh in it. (5) bounds λmin by the diagonal, which throws away the whole lattice. A bound that used the off-diagonal structure — the comb's own gear mass at the mesh, which the arc computes anyway — would be tighter by whatever §5 measured as the eigenvalue-to-diagonal gap, and it is the obvious next inequality.
