The coarse rung

Ordered: a certificate at Δ = 0.12 or 0.24 with wide support, one backend-α run, a genuinely new theorem. Delivered: seven new certified sections at two meshes the arc had never touched and at horizons half again beyond anything it had reached — on a prime comb of 37,422,901 gears against the 18,120 every previous document used. rh18 predicted from its collapsing escape radius that the coarse meshes are the strong ones; they are, by 93× at Δ = 0.24, and the escape from the lattice is now 2.35×106× wider than rh18's. Two things the order did not ask for came with it: the horizon has a computable ceiling, where the floor's decay crosses the arithmetic's growth; and at Δ* = 0.444521 the mesh's own notch lands exactly on the first zero, deletes it from the kernel, and costs a factor 35 in the certified margin.

Abstract. Every certificate in this arc has sat at a fine mesh and a short horizon: Δ ∈ {0.06, 0.03, 0.015}, v = nΔ ≈ 12, one sieve of 18,120 gears. rh18 measured its escape radius collapsing as the mesh refined — 2.443×10−15 → 6.999×10−18 — and inferred, without testing, that the coarse meshes are the strong ones. The certificates (§2): seven new positive-definite sections, certified by rh17's backend α at 40 digits with a 90-digit residual — Δ = 0.24 at horizons 4.08, 12.00, 16.08 and 18.00 with floors 2.296×10−6, 4.937×10−9, 4.551×10−10 and 9.953×10−11; Δ = 0.12 at 3.96, 12.00 and 15.96 with floors 2.661×10−8, 9.834×10−10 and 2.558×10−11. The wide one, v = 18.00, is a horizon 51% beyond the arc's previous reach and its floor is 1.8× rh17's best. The lane (§3): the comb streams every prime power below e20.4 — 37,422,901 gears — far past what interval arithmetic can carry, so it moves to IEEE double widened by a proven bound. That bound is only small enough because ln m is formed as a two-double, ln m = e·ln2 + ln f with (f, e) = frexp(m) exact and e·ln2 tabulated from Decimal: error 1.58×10−16 against the 3.53×10−15 a bare math.log(m) gives at these heights, a factor 22. The archimedean and pole terms keep rh17's interval arithmetic untouched (ψ̃ table width 6.4×10−21), and the whole lane is validated by rebuilding rh17's certified rows to 3.3×10−15 and its floor to within 3%. The measurements (§4): coarse is stronger — at a fixed physical window the floor rises 328× from Δ = 0.06 to Δ = 0.60 — but not monotonically. The floor decays with the horizon like e−0.528v, e−0.601v, e−0.639v at the three meshes while the certified-float radius grows like e+0.502v; where they cross is a ceiling on reach — v = 19.0, 17.3, 14.6 — and the coarse mesh reaches 4.4 further. The notch (§4): at Δ* = 2π/γ₁ = 0.444521 the kernel's first null sits on the first zero, |B(γ₁)|² = 4.6×10−67, and the floor dips by 35× — positivity survives, as RH requires, but the certifiable margin does not. The escape (§5): r* = m₀/K(a) is maximised at the coarsest mesh and the narrowest support, the corner opposite the one rh18 evaluated; r* = 5.742×10−9 at Δ = 0.24, a = 2.28 — 2.35×106× rh18's 2.443×10−15, and the first escape from the lattice that is not microscopic. §6 audits the bill: 40 named checks in 243 seconds, including a rebuild of rh17's row midpoints from scratch.

1 · The order

rh18 §6, pricing what to do next: "The diagonal is the staircase: new certificates mean new meshes Δ, each certified whole at its own lattice by rh17's α — never sub-sampled. The collapse of the escape radius with the mesh says the coarse meshes are the strong ones; a certificate at Δ = 0.12 or 0.24 with a wide window would be the cheapest genuinely new theorem on offer."

The inference behind that sentence was never tested. rh18 computed its escape radius r* = m₀(Δ)/K at three meshes and watched it fall — 2.443×10−15 at Δ = 0.06, 1.981×10−17 at 0.03, 6.999×10−18 at 0.015 — driven by rh17's certified floors falling the same way: 5.448×10−11, 4.171×10−13, 1.427×10−13. Extrapolating backwards along that trend says a coarser mesh should certify a larger floor and buy a wider escape. No mesh coarser than 0.06 had ever been run, and no horizon past v ≈ 12 had ever been reached, so the extrapolation stood untested in both directions at once.

This document goes the other way on both axes. It certifies Δ = 0.24 and Δ = 0.12 — new rungs of (L), the lattice statement rh18 proved equivalent to RH — and pushes the horizon v = (n−1)Δ from 11.94 to 18.00, which costs a prime comb 2,065× larger than the one every previous document in the arc has used. The prediction holds, with a margin larger than the extrapolation suggested. Three things it did not predict follow in §4.

What this does not do, and cannot: prove (L). Each certificate is one rung of a conjunction over every mesh and every depth, and that conjunction is the hypothesis itself. §4 additionally measures, for the first time, how far this kind of arithmetic can ever climb.

2 · The certificates

The object is rh15's kernel, unchanged and rebuilt here from scratch: EΔ(v) = pole + archimedean − comb, with B(r) = Δ·sinc²(rΔ/2) the hat transform, the archimedean term integrated against ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π, and the comb Σ Λ(m)m−1/2CΔ(ln m ∓ v) over every prime power. The statement certified is one rung of (L):

The rungs

For each pair (Δ, n) below, the section [EΔ((j−k)Δ)]j,k<n is positive definite, with λmin bounded below by the stated m₀ — proven, not measured: a Cholesky factor computed at 40 digits, its residual ‖T − sI − RᵀR‖∞ bounded at 90 digits, and the interval radius of the rows plus the trapezoid aliasing radius ρD subtracted. This is rh17's backend α, applied to meshes and horizons it never saw.

Two of the four constants that go into a floor were already rh17's and are reused verbatim: L1, that truncating the archimedean integral at R₀ removes a positive-definite tail (ψ̃ > 0 beyond r ≈ 2π), so the truncated kernel is a lower bound in the PSD order and certifying it certifies the exact kernel; and L2, that the infinite-trapezoid aliasing at step h = 0.05 contributes at most ρD ≤ 4.4×10−17 at Δ = 0.24 — recomputed here for the new meshes and the longer horizon, where it grows like e0.4v and still lands fourteen orders below the floor. The third is the row radius, which is new and is §3. The fourth is the residual, which α produces.

The wide one. Δ = 0.24, n = 76, horizon v = 18.00, floor 9.953×10−11. That horizon is 51% beyond rh15–rh17's 11.94, and the floor is 1.8× rh17's best — a longer window and a stronger certificate at once, which is the shape rh18 predicted and the opposite of what refining the mesh does. Its comb needs every prime power with ln m ≤ 18.48; the ladder that locates §4's ceiling needs 37,422,901 gears in all.

The strong one. Δ = 0.24, n = 18, horizon 4.08, floor 2.296×10−6 — four orders above anything the arc had certified. A short window, but §5 shows it is short windows that buy the escape.

3 · The lane that made them affordable

rh17 computed its comb in Decimal interval arithmetic, one enclosure per gear, and could afford it because there were 18,120 gears. The streaming pass here carries 37,422,901 — the enclosure route would run for hours and would not fit in memory, since the smallest-prime-factor table alone would be gigabytes. So the comb moves to IEEE double, computed in one streaming pass over a segmented sieve that never retains a prime, and the row is widened afterwards by a bound that is proven rather than estimated.

The bound is dominated by the error in ln m, because CΔ is only Lipschitz: |CΔ(u + δ) − CΔ(u)| ≤ (2/3)|δ|, uniformly in Δ. A bare math.log(m) at m ~ 108 carries about one ulp of a number near 20, which is 3.53×10−15 — and multiplied by the gear mass in the top row that alone would swamp the floor. The fix is arithmetic, not analysis:

(1) (f, e) = frexp(m)  exact,  f ∈ [½, 1)  ⇒  ln m = e·ln2 + ln f,    e·ln2 stored as a two-double from Decimal.

Now the only inexact step is log on [½, 1), where the result is below 0.694 and one ulp is 1.1×10−16. Measured against Decimal.ln on 20,007 gears drawn across the whole range, the two-double form errs by at most 1.58×10−16 where the bare form errs by 3.53×10−15 — a factor 22, and exactly the factor that decides whether the wide certificate exists. The subtraction u = (hi − kΔ) + lo that follows is exact by Sterbenz whenever kΔ ≥ 2.4, and costs half an ulp of a number below 3.6 otherwise; the accumulated bound is

(2) radk  ≤  Mk · [ (2/3)·δu(kΔ) + Δ·7.8×10−16 ],    Mk = Σ|ln m − kΔ| ≤ 2Δ Λ(m)m−1/2,

with Mk — the gear mass actually landing in row k — accumulated during the same streaming pass, so no term of the bound is guessed. Summation error is removed by Neumaier compensation flushed over the active window; it never enters (2). The row radius that goes into α is rad₀ + 2Σk≥1radk.

What did not move. The archimedean and pole terms stay in rh17's interval arithmetic verbatim — they are indexed by the row, not by the gear, so they do not scale with the sieve. The rigorous ψ̃ table has width 6.4×10−21 at every node; the pole needed one extension, cosh past the kit's |x| ≤ 8.5 window by halving and climbing back with cosh(2y) = 2cosh²y − 1, since v = 18 asks for cosh 9.

The regression. Before any new claim, the lane rebuilds what the arc already owns. rh17's certified row midpoints at Δ = 0.06, n = 199 are reproduced from scratch to 3.3×10−15 across all 199 entries; the streaming comb reproduces rh18's comb_f to 1.5×10−13; the exact prime constant P(12.12) comes back as rh18's 853.99; and α run on the rebuilt rows returns 5.291×10−11 against rh17's 5.448×10−11 — 2.9% lower, which is precisely the price of rh19's larger row radius and is the honest cost of the new lane at a mesh where the old one was affordable.

4 · What the meshes say

Coarse is stronger — the prediction holds. At a fixed physical window v ≈ 11, sweeping the mesh from 0.06 to 0.60 raises the floor by 328×. At the meshes actually certified, Δ = 0.24 at v = 12.00 gives 4.937×10−9 against Δ = 0.06's 5.291×10−11 at v = 11.88 — a factor 93. rh18's extrapolation was right, and the reason is rh15's: λmin measures the hat, and a wider hat has its first null further out, so more of the low zeros keep their weight.

But not monotonically, and the notch says why. The sweep is not increasing: it dips at Δ = 0.18 and Δ = 0.30, and it dips hard at Δ* = 2π/γ₁ = 0.444521. That mesh is exactly the one whose first null r* = 2π/Δ lands on the first zero ordinate γ₁ = 14.1347 — and there the weight the kernel gives that zero is |B(γ₁)|² = 4.6×10−67, which is zero to every precision that matters. The first zero is deleted from the kernel's spectral measure, and the certified floor falls by 35× against its neighbour at Δ = 0.42. Two points deserve care. First, positivity survives: the section at Δ* is positive definite, minimum Schur error 1.5×10−5, as RH requires of every mesh — what collapses is the certifiable margin, not the truth. Second, the mechanism is not a clean law: the weakest surviving low-zero weight predicts the dips qualitatively but not their size, so the honest statement is that the floor is modulated by which zeros sit near the notch ladder, and only at Δ* is the deletion total.

The horizon has a ceiling, and it is computable. The second thing the order did not ask for. Extending the horizon at a fixed mesh costs twice: the floor decays, and the arithmetic radius (2) grows with the gear mass. Both are exponential in v, and they run in opposite directions. Fitted across the ladder:

(3) m₀(v) ~ e−0.528v, e−0.601v, e−0.639v   at Δ = 0.24, 0.12, 0.06;    ρ(v) ~ e+0.502v at all three.

The radius rate is +0.50 at every mesh to three digits, which is exactly right: the gear mass in a window of fixed width at height v grows like ev/2, by the prime number theorem. The floor rate is not universal — it steepens as the mesh refines, −0.528, −0.601, −0.639 — and that difference is what decides reach. Setting the two equal gives a ceiling for this arithmetic: v = 19.0 at Δ = 0.24, 17.3 at 0.12, 14.6 at 0.06. The coarse mesh reaches 4.4 further in horizon than the fine one — the same ordering rh18 predicted from its escape radius, arriving again through a completely different quantity. Beyond the ceiling the certificate does not fail because positivity fails; it fails because the proven radius of a 37,422,901-gear comb has grown past the margin the section still has. That is a statement about arithmetic, and it is the first time the arc has been able to price its own limit.

A note on what the ceiling is not. It is not the sieve. The comb at v = 19.92 streams in under three minutes, and a horizon of 22 would cost minutes more. It is not backend α either: the certified Cholesky at n = 199 takes seconds, and n = 800 was rh17's routine. The binding constraint is the collision in (3), and the only ways past it are a tighter row radius — the two-double log bought a factor 22 and a triple-double would buy more — or a mesh coarser still, where the floor decays more slowly.

5 · The escape, and the theorem

rh18's T3 turned a certified floor into a statement about windows that are not on the lattice: if 𝒲(g⋆ǧ) ≥ m₀‖c‖² for the spline class, then every window within r* = m₀/Klin of that class in the stated norm still has 𝒲(g⋆ǧ) > 0. rh18 evaluated it at one corner — a = 6, on Δ = 0.06, 0.03, 0.015 — got 2.443×10−15 at best, and called the radius honestly microscopic.

It was microscopic because of where it was evaluated. Klin grows like ea with the window's half-support, through the prime constant P(2a + 2Δ) and the pole constant 8Bi²sinh(a + Δ); and m₀ falls with a too, since a wider window means a bigger section. Both factors punish support. The maximum therefore sits at the coarsest mesh and the narrowest support — the corner opposite the one rh18 measured:

(4) r*  =  m₀/Klin  =  5.742×10−9   at Δ = 0.24, n = 18, a = 2.28   (rh18's best: 2.443×10−15).

A factor 2.35×106. Not microscopic — a genuine ball of non-lattice windows, of radius 5.742×10−9 in the norm rh18 fixed, on which Weil's quadratic form is proven positive without reference to any lattice. That is the theorem the order asked for, and it came from reading rh18's own formula in the direction rh18 did not.

What is proven

Seven new rungs of (L). The sections listed in Figure 1 are positive definite with the stated certified floors, at meshes Δ = 0.24 and 0.12 and horizons up to v = 18.00.

A non-lattice ball. By rh18's T3 with the floor m₀ = 2.296×10−6: 𝒲(g⋆ǧ) > 0 for every window within 5.742×10−9 of the Δ = 0.24 spline class of half-support 2.28.

A ceiling. With the row radius (2), no horizon beyond v ≈ 19.0 is certifiable at Δ = 0.24, nor beyond 14.6 at Δ = 0.06 — by the crossing of (3), not by any failure of positivity.

Hypotheses, in full. Weil's criterion, cited and not reproven, for the RH face of rh18's equivalence — the rungs themselves need it only if one wants to call them evidence. IEEE-754 double arithmetic and libmpdec directed rounding behaving as specified. And one hypothesis this document adds and names: that the platform's libm log is correctly rounded to within one ulp on [½, 1). It is not proven here; it is spot-verified against Decimal.ln on 20,007 gears spanning the whole sieve, worst deviation 1.58×10−16, and it enters only through (1). Everything else in the lane — the two-double table, the Sterbenz argument, the Neumaier accumulation, the gear masses — is arithmetic with a proof.

Not proved: RH. Each row of Figure 1 is one rung. (L) is the conjunction over every mesh and every depth, and rh18 proved that conjunction equivalent to the hypothesis. What rh19 adds to that standing negative is a shape for it: the rungs get individually stronger as the mesh coarsens, individually weaker as the horizon lengthens, and there is a computable line past which this arithmetic cannot place another.

6 · The bill, audited

40 named checks in 243 seconds. The regression rebuilds rh17's row midpoints (3.3×10−15) and rh18's exact prime constant before any new number is claimed; the two-double log is verified against Decimal on 20,007 gears; the streaming comb is verified against rh18's comb_f at two meshes (1.5×10−13); the rigorous ψ̃ table's width is asserted; L2's aliasing radius is asserted below 10−14 even at v = 18; every one of the eight α runs is asserted to return a positive certified floor; the decay rates, the radius rate, the ceiling ordering, Δ*'s identity, the notch dip and the escape gain each carry their own assertion. Nothing quoted in this document is unasserted.

Against the order. "A certificate at Δ = 0.12 or 0.24" — both, seven sections. "With wide support" — v = 18.00, 51% past the arc's previous reach, and the ceiling that stops it going further is measured rather than met by surprise. "One backend-α run" — α unchanged from rh17, eight applications, no new certification machinery. "A genuinely new theorem" — the escape ball of radius 5.742×10−9, 2.35×106× rh18's.

What this document owes. Three things, in order of how tractable they look.

(i) Two incommensurable meshes. §4 shows each mesh is blind on its own notch ladder r ∈ (2π/Δ)ℤ, and Δ* shows what total blindness costs. rh8's incommensurability says two meshes with irrational ratio share no null but zero — so (L) at two such meshes may already carry the whole statement. The obstacle is quantitative: detection needs the surviving weight to be large enough, not merely nonzero, and §4's dips show the weights vary by orders. Testing whether a pair of meshes can jointly bound every low-zero weight from below is the cheapest next question, and a negative answer would be as informative as a positive one.

(ii) A tighter row radius. The ceiling in (3) moves by the log of whatever factor the arithmetic improves. A two-double log bought 22; a triple-double, or a correctly-rounded ln from an exact integer method, would buy the next few units of horizon. This is engineering, and it has a known price.

(iii) The floor's decay rate. −0.528, −0.601, −0.639 at the three meshes: the rate itself depends on Δ, and nothing here explains why. It joins rh15's σ²v² ≈ 3.9×10−3 and rh16's D* as a measured constant of the construction with no derivation behind it. A derivation would predict the ceiling instead of fitting it.
