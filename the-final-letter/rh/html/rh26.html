The half-turn, and a wall that was not there

Asked whether the gears would do better undivided, or divided alike. 1 · The order

The question was whether the gears would be better undivided — smooth circles rather than rh4's twelve arcs — or divided all alike, one mesh across the whole cone. Checking the arc's own record rather than answering from memory turned up two things worth a document. The subdivision is not decoration and not information thrown away: it is the sample lattice, and rh18 closed refining it. And a common division across gears is not an engineering gap but unique factorisation itself, which §4 quantifies rather than restates. What sits between them — the one symmetry the construction has that the arc has never used — is the π rotation, and that is §2.

The ground. Nothing here streams a new sieve. Every kernel row in this document is read off the same pass over the 6,448,870 prime powers below 112,672,958 that rh22, rh23, rh24 and rh25 used, through rh25's tabulated generators, and the first thing checked is that rh25's λmin and rh25's certified floors come back unchanged before anything new is built on them. Five windows are carried throughout: the triangle hat, rh25's searched spline optima at M = 8 and M = 16, and rh25's searched notch-free optima at the same two meshes. Carrying the searched spline as well as the searched notch-free window is what makes §3's diagnosis possible — without it, "notch-free" and "searched" cannot be told apart.

One new pass, and it is not a sieve. The certified radius needs to know where the gear mass sits relative to each node, and rh23's routine bins it for one fixed node spacing. A sub-lattice needs the same object at Δ/2, Δ/4, Δ/8 and Δ/16, and re-streaming six and a half million gears per lattice would be silly. Every node of every lattice used here is an exact multiple of w = 2Δ/768, so one histogram of the gear mass in ln m at width w serves them all — the bins of the node at v = qw are H[q+i] and H[q−i−1], which is bin arithmetic and not a re-binning. It reproduces rh24's streamed 768-bin mass at every Δ node to 10⁻¹².

2 · (a) The π rotation splits the section

Turn the construction half a turn. The kernel row is a function of the separation alone and Eh(−v) = Eh(v), so the section

(1) Th[p][q] = Eh((p − q)h),    p, q = 0 … n−1

is symmetric and persymmetric: with J the exchange matrix — the half-turn, p ↦ n−1−p — one has JThJ = Th. That is measured, not assumed: over all 45 sections in this document the largest entry of JTJ − T is zero, exactly, in floating point. A centrosymmetric matrix commutes with J, so it is block-diagonal in J's eigenbasis, and the two blocks are the +1 and −1 eigenspaces: vectors symmetric about the centre and vectors antisymmetric about it. Cantoni–Butler makes this explicit. Writing P for the top-left quarter and Q for the top-right quarter reversed, the odd block is P − Q and the even block is P + Q, bordered when n is odd by √2 times the centre column. Then

(2) λmin(Th) = min( λmin(P + Q), λmin(P − Q) ),

reproduced here to 5×10⁻⁹ relative at every section size and every window. The two halves are the cosine and sine channels: a symmetric u has a real cosine transform on the lattice and an antisymmetric one a pure sine transform, so (2) is the section's own version of the reflection v ↦ −v that the functional equation puts on the critical line.

Which half binds. This is the part worth having. For the triangle hat the even block binds at all nine section sizes tested, from n = 9 to n = 76, and never narrowly: the odd block is between 2.65× and 34.9× above it. For every one of the four searched windows the two blocks are within 26%, they agree to 0.14% at the deepest section, and which one binds alternates with n — the sign pattern over the nine sizes is O O O E E O E O O for the searched M = 16 spline and O E O E E O O O O for the notch-free one. Two consequences, one practical and one not.

The practical one is negative and should be said plainly: neither channel can be certified on its own. A certificate that establishes positivity of the cosine block at every n proves nothing, because at some n the sine block is the smaller. The split is a factor, not a reduction.

The other is that the window search — which is told only to maximise λmin, and knows nothing about J — drives the two channels together. That is what a minimax optimum looks like when a symmetry splits the space: raising the smaller block is free until it meets the larger one, and after that any further gain must lift both. The hat, which was never optimised, sits where an unoptimised window sits: one channel 2.65× to 34.9× slacker than the other. The searched windows sit at the balance point. It is a structural signature of optimality, and it fell out of a symmetry the search does not know exists — which also means it is a check on the search, and one the search cannot have gamed.

What it costs. Honestly: the blocks are Toeplitz-plus-Hankel, not Toeplitz, so the O(n²) Levinson/Durbin recursion that the arc's fast λmin uses does not apply to them at all. What the split helps is the dense route — the O(n³) Cholesky that §5 uses as its independent confirmation, and the dense eigenproblem. Two matrices of half the order cost 2(n/2)³ = n³/4, and measured at n = 76 the whole section factors in 3.8 ms against 1.0 ms as two blocks, 3.8×. That is the gain and there is no other.

3 · (b) The wall was the cut

rh18 §4, on refining the lattice: "The cut kernel certified at mesh Δ is indefinite on every sub-lattice — at δ = Δ/2 the 60-digit recursion fails at k = 370 while float inverse iteration reports +1.4×10⁻¹²." It gave a cause: "the sub-lattice at δ resolves frequencies to π/δ, and in particular — already at δ = Δ/2 — the mesh's own notch r* = 2π/Δ, where B(r*) = 0 and the kernel's spectral density genuinely vanishes."

rh25 §3 built windows with no notch. Ûc(r) ≥ 2c(cosh cδ − 1)/(sinh cδ (c² + r²)) > 0 at every real r, the coefficients constrained by Fejér–Riesz so that A = |B|² has no real zero either, and the resulting windows lose nothing on the Δ lattice. If rh18's diagnosis were the whole story they should walk through the sub-lattice. They do not. At equal depth v = 18, with the archimedean integral cut at R₀ = 600 exactly as every certificate in the arc has cut it:

(3) triangle hat   Δ/2 4.104×10⁻¹¹  ·  Δ/4 8.739×10⁻¹²  ·  Δ/8 -3.673×10⁻⁷
notch-free M = 8   Δ/2 2.863×10⁻⁴  ·  Δ/4 -3.439×10⁻⁴  ·  Δ/8 -7.708×10⁻⁴
notch-free M = 16   Δ/2 1.096×10⁻³  ·  Δ/4 -1.865×10⁻³  ·  Δ/8 -5.717×10⁻³

The notch-free windows, which are eight orders better than the hat on the Δ lattice, fail one refinement earlier than it. The prediction that brought this document here is refuted, and refuted in the direction that says the notch was not what was in the way.

The cut is one-sided. What is in the way is in rh17, stated once and never used since. rh17 §1, lemma L1: "ψ̃(r) = Re ψ(¼ + ir/2) − ln π > 1.10 for r ≥ 20, so the dropped tail beyond R₀ is a POSITIVE combination of cosine kernels — positive semidefinite — and never needs a bound at all." Recomputed here, ψ̃(r) crosses zero at

(4) r₊ = 6.289836   (2π = 6.283185),    ψ̃(20) = 1.1578,    ψ̃(600) = 4.5591,

and is increasing on all of [r₊, 4800]. So for any cut R ≥ r₊ the discarded piece is

(5) DR(v) = (1/π)∫R∞ |ĥ(r)|² ψ̃(r) cos(rv) dr,   so
Σp,qupuqDR((p−q)h) = (1/π)∫R∞ |ĥ(r)|² ψ̃(r) |Σpupeirph|² dr  ≥  0

for every u and every lattice. The computed kernel is the exact kernel minus a positive semidefinite form. It follows that a cut section which is positive definite certifies the exact section with no tail budget at all — and that a cut section which is indefinite certifies nothing whatever. Every negative eigenvalue in (3) is, a priori, a candidate artefact.

Put it back. Computing (5) on [600, 4800] and adding it to the row is arithmetic, not a bound. Every section in (3) returns:

(6) hat Δ/8 -3.673×10⁻⁷ → 3.169×10⁻¹²  ·  nf M = 8 Δ/4 -3.439×10⁻⁴ → 5.484×10⁻⁵  ·  nf M = 16 Δ/4 -1.865×10⁻³ → 1.210×10⁻³  ·  nf M = 16 Δ/8 -5.717×10⁻³ → 5.919×10⁻⁵

and so does every other one, at every window and every sub-lattice down to Δ/16; the worst residual over all twenty-two is -6.6×10⁻¹⁴ of that window's own Δ-lattice floor, which is where the float bisection's resolution is. At Δ/2 and Δ/4 the restoration is not marginal — each window keeps at least 0.9% of its Δ-lattice floor where the cut had taken it negative. From Δ/8 down it turns marginal and window-dependent, and honestly so: the hat still keeps 2.0% of its own floor at Δ/8, while the searched M = 8 windows have already fallen to the arithmetic's own floor there, and at Δ/16 what is still discarded past 4800 is what decides. Only a larger cut can say, and by (5) a larger cut can only help.

The accounting closes. rh18 had two numbers on its witness direction: the truncated form u′Mu = -4.18×10⁻⁵ and the zeros side +1.072×10⁻², with the gap "sitting inside the dropped-tail budget". The third number is now computed rather than bounded. On the minimising direction of each failing section,

(7) u′Mu  +  (dropped tail on u)  =  2Σγ|ĥ(γ)|²|Σpupeiγph|²   to 6.6%, 3.9% and 8.2%,

the residue being the zeros above height 2000 and the weight still dropped past 4800. There is nothing else in the discrepancy. rh18's witness was not evidence about the kernel; it was evidence about where the integral had been cut.

What actually costs the searched windows. Not the notch. The weight discarded at R₀ = 600, relative to Ch(0), is 1.54×10⁻⁶ for the triangle hat, 2.517×10⁻³ for the searched spline at M = 8 and 2.509×10⁻³ for the searched notch-free window at the same M — the same to within a per cent — and 1.938×10⁻² against 2.247×10⁻² at M = 16, within 17%. Notch-freeness is not what thickens the tail; building the window on a sub-mesh is. A window assembled from M shifted generators has structure at scale δ = Δ/M and therefore carries energy out to r ∼ 2π/δ, which is exactly the region a cut at 600 throws away. The better the window is at the mathematics, the more the cut costs it — and that, not the notch, is why rh25's best windows were the first to fail.

4 · (c) How far the certificate descends

rh18 concluded: "sub-lattice positivity of the exact kernel is exactly as strong as the zeros' reality, with no truncation shortcut, and the diagonal (Δ, mesh-Δ) family rh15–rh17 certified is not a convenience but the only computable staircase." The first clause is right and is not touched here. The second is wrong, and §3 says why: the wall was a property of the window rh18 tested, not of the lattice.

The first rung is free. At R₀ = 600, with no new machinery whatever, every one of the five windows reaches depth 18 on the Δ/2 sub-lattice. What differs is by how much. The searched M = 16 window carries λmin = 1.096×10⁻³ there; the triangle hat carries 4.104×10⁻¹¹. Run backend α on the hat at exactly that section and its own certified radius comes out 5.76×10⁻¹¹ — so the hat's eigenvalue is a factor 1.40 below the smallest positive number its own certificate can resolve, and backend α has nothing to return. That margin is not large, and it should not be dressed up as one: a radius 1.40× tighter would flip it. What is large is the other side of the comparison. At the same section the searched window's floor clears its radius by a factor 5.4×10⁶. The hat is at the edge of what the method can see; the searched window is nowhere near it. That, and not the lattice, is what rh18 ran into.

How far below. With the cut at 600 the reach shortens as the mesh refines — Δ/4 at depth 10.80 for the M = 16 optimum, Δ/8 at 6.66, Δ/16 at 6.61 — and with the tail restored to 4800 every window runs out of streamed comb instead of running out of positivity: at Δ/4 and Δ/8 all five stop at the sieve's own limit near v = 18, and the notch-free M = 16 window reaches n = 1203 at Δ/16, depth 18.03. The certified region is no longer a staircase along the diagonal; it is a column, five meshes deep at one support. Widening it to a second support needs another sieve pass and is not done here.

And the division that cannot be shared. This is the half of the original question that no refinement touches. The gears' clock times are k log p, and rh4 §7 already had the reason: log n = Σ vp(n) log p, so unique factorisation is the statement that {log p} is linearly independent over ℚ. A mesh δ commensurable with two gears would give log p/log q ∈ ℚ, i.e. pa = qb. So exactly one family is exactly commensurable — one prime and all its powers, on the lattice δ = log p — and no two primes ever are. What a mesh can do is a convergent of log 3/log 2 = 1.584962…, whose continued fraction is [1; 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, …]: the near misses are 2/1, 3/2, 8/5, 19/12, 65/41. The fourth is the twelve-tone scale, missing by the Pythagorean comma 3¹²/2¹⁹ = 1.013643; the fifth still misses by 1.0115 on 2⁶⁵ against 3⁴¹. (rh4 also divides by twelve, at multiples of π/6. That is a division of the phase, not of log 2, and the shared numeral is a coincidence — worth naming so that it is not mistaken for a derivation.)

What the near misses are worth, measured rather than argued: across all five meshes of the ladder and all 6,448,870 gears, the closest any gear comes to a node is 2.09×10⁻⁹, at m = 65,659,969, and every one of the five meshes has some gear sitting essentially exactly at its half-step — the worst position there is. A mesh can be commensurable with one prime and its powers, and with nothing else; the census says the rest of the gear train is spread against it as badly as it can be. That is not a defect of the meshes tried. It is unique factorisation, and no refinement of §3 touches it.

5 · The certificates, off the diagonal

Backend α unchanged from rh19 and rh25 — a verified Cholesky at 40 digits, an exact-product residual at 90, plus the radius row-sums — with two things made parameters. The certified arch and pole are evaluated at node spacing s·δ rather than at Δ, and the comb row is recombined at stride s. The radius is charged as rh24 charges it, over 768 bins, against the gear mass at each node read off §1's histogram. Four floors on sub-lattices, where the arc had none. The deepest in v is 1.0852×10⁻³ at n = 151 on Δ/2 — the same depth 18 the Δ-lattice certificates reach, at half the mesh. Every λmin is confirmed by inertia bisection in exact 60-digit arithmetic rather than by the float bisection that found it, and every positive-definiteness claim by an independent dense Cholesky sharing no code with the bisection.

The depths on the finer rungs are short — 9, 4.5 and 2.25 — and it is worth being exact about why. They are not where positivity stops; §4's ladder puts the M = 16 window's reach at 10.80, 6.66 and 6.61 on those meshes at R₀ = 600, and past 18 with the tail restored. They are where the section size was capped so that the 40-digit Cholesky and the 90-digit residual stay inside this document's budget, which is O(n³) in each. The certificates are chosen to fit the bill, not the other way round, and that is a cost statement and not a mathematical one.

6 · The bill, and what is left

56 named checks in 257 seconds, over the same 6,448,870-gear stream rh22–rh25 used plus one histogram pass that reproduces rh24's binned mass to 10⁻¹². rh25's λmin and rh25's certified floor are reproduced before anything is built on them; the centrosymmetry is measured rather than assumed; the block split is checked against the whole section at every size and, at one section, in exact 60-digit arithmetic; both block minimisers are checked to be genuine Rayleigh minimisers of the whole section; the crossing of ψ̃(r) and its monotonicity are recomputed rather than cited; and the refuted prediction carries its own assertion, so that a future change which quietly made it true would fail the suite.

Against the question. Undivided gears are not available — the smooth object is Weil positivity itself, which is the hypothesis. What was available was the one symmetry the divided construction has and the arc had never used, and it does split the problem exactly in half, at a real if modest gain in the dense route, with a structural bonus: the searched windows sit at the point where the two halves are equally critical, and the unsearched one does not. A single division across all gears is not available either, and §4 says why in one line rather than three paragraphs. But the question sent this document at rh18's closure, and that closure does not survive contact.

The correction, stated plainly. This document set out expecting notch-freeness to be the thing that let the certificate refine. It is not, and at fixed R₀ the searched windows are worse — notch-free or not, since the searched spline behaves the same — because they are built on a finer sub-mesh, and a finer sub-mesh puts more weight past the cut. What lets the certificate refine is that the cut only ever removes positive weight — rh17 knew this and rh18 did not use it — together with a window whose sub-lattice λmin is large enough to be seen at all. rh25's windows supply the second, by seven orders at Δ/2 and eight at Δ/4. Two effects in opposite directions, and the one named in the plan was the smaller and had the wrong sign.

What this does not do. It does not touch the conjunction. Four sub-lattice floors at one support, at depths 18, 9, 4.5 and 2.25, are four more points than the arc had, and four points is still measure zero against every Δ and every δ and every n. The one-sidedness of the cut is a lemma about the archimedean integral, not about ζ; it makes a class of negative results inadmissible, which is worth having, and it makes no positive result easier than it was. Nothing here is conditional on RH — the zeros-side sum in (7) is used as a measurement of the discrepancy, not as an input to any certificate.

What is left. Three.

(a) The parity, used rather than observed. §2 finds the searched optima sitting exactly where the two channels balance. That is a necessary condition at an optimum, and it is computable: a search run on the balance condition — maximise the smaller block subject to the two being equal — has half the dimension and a stopping criterion the current search lacks. Whether it finds the same windows faster, or different ones, is untested.

(b) A bigger cut, once. §3 restores [600, 4800] in floats. Doing it in the interval kit would turn Δ/4, Δ/8 and Δ/16 at depth 18 from float experiments into certificates, and by (5) the piece still dropped beyond the new cut costs nothing at all — it can be discarded rather than bounded. The cost is a certified arch over eight times as many quadrature nodes, which is the single most expensive thing in §5 and would want a better inner loop before it is attempted.

(c) The second support. The certified region is a column at Δ = 0.24. rh19 and rh22 certified Δ = 0.12 and Δ = 0.06 on the diagonal, with the spline family and a much smaller λmin. Running §5's parameterised backend at those supports with a searched notch-free window would make the certified set two-dimensional for the first time — and would be the first honest test of whether rh22's escape radius turns a column of points into a region with interior.

7 · Dictionary additions and references

centrosymmetric
JAJ = A with J the exchange (half-turn) matrix. Every symmetric Toeplitz matrix is; the arc's sections are, exactly, because Eh is even.
Cantoni–Butler split
the block-diagonalisation (2) of a centrosymmetric matrix into its even and odd parts under J. The blocks are Toeplitz-plus-Hankel, so the Levinson/Durbin route does not survive the split; the dense route gains 4×.
even / odd channel
the +1 and −1 eigenspaces of J, equivalently the cosine and sine transforms on the lattice. The section's realisation of the reflection v ↦ −v.
the one-sided cut (rh17 L1)
ψ̃ > 0 beyond r₊ = 6.289836, so truncating the archimedean integral at any R ≥ r₊ subtracts a positive semidefinite form. Cut-PD ⟹ exact-PD with no budget; cut-indefinite ⟹ nothing.
DR
the dropped tail (5), computed here on [600, 4800] rather than bounded. Its Toeplitz form is the missing third number in rh18's witness identity.
the mass histogram
Σ Λ(m)m−1/2 binned in ln m at width w = 2Δ/768. Every node of every lattice in the ladder is an exact multiple of w, so one pass gives the radius bins at every mesh.
the column
the certified set as it now stands: one support Δ = 0.24, five meshes Δ … Δ/16, depths 18, 18, 9, 4.5, 2.25. Not a staircase and not yet a region.
