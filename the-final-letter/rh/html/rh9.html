The case that was proved

Run the same construction over 𝔽q[t] instead of ℤ and every axiom survives, the critical line becomes a literal circle, and the hypothesis becomes a theorem — because the infinite torus of rh8 collapses to a single circle.

docs/rh9 · continues rh4–rh8 · figures computed, not sketched · 2026-07-31

Abstract. Five documents have converged on one diagnosis: the missing axiom needs a representation with multiplicity, and every symmetry the construction presents is abelian (rh5 §5, rh7 §5, rh8 §6). There is exactly one setting where that missing structure exists and the hypothesis is a theorem — the function field case — and rh8 supplies the reason it works. Replace ℤ by 𝔽q[t]: the gears become monic irreducible polynomials, and a gear of degree d has norm qd, hence period 2π/(d log q) — every one of which divides the single period 2π/log q. Where over ℚ no two gears ever close together, over 𝔽q they all close together, verified here; the infinite-dimensional torus becomes one circle, ζ becomes a function of T = q−s alone, and is therefore rational — finitely many zeros instead of infinitely many (§2). Everything follows. The critical line σ = ½ becomes the circle |T| = q−1/2, an actual circle in an actual plane (§3). Genus 0 has no zeros at all; genus g has exactly 2g. Computed here: for y² = x³+x+1 over all 780 good primes below 6000, both zeros lie on the circle to machine precision and Hasse's bound holds with maximum ratio 0.9917; for a genus-2 curve, all four zeros lie on it at every prime tested, checked by an exact integer criterion as well as numerically (§4–5). The explicit formula becomes exact and finite — qn + 1 − Nn = ∑αin, a sum of 2g terms rather than infinitely many — and RH becomes the provable inequality |·| ≤ 2g qn/2, measured here to reach 0.997 of its bound (§6). Even the statistics are settled: the Frobenius angles follow the Sato–Tate law, which is a theorem, where rh8 could only honestly report level repulsion (§7). Finally §8 says why none of it transports: the proof runs on a surface, and Spec ℤ has no second factor.

Part I · The collapse

1 · The same construction, over a different ring

Everything rh4 built rests on a ring with a size function, a supply of primes, and unique factorization. ℤ is not the only such ring. Take 𝔽q[t], the polynomials in one variable over a finite field, with

(1) |f| = qdeg f,    primes = monic irreducible polynomials,    T = q−s.

The dictionary is exact, not analogical. Unique factorization holds. The counting function of the gears is not asymptotic but closed: the number of monic irreducibles of degree n is

(2) πq(n) = 1n  ∑d | n μ(d) qn/d  =  qnn + O(qn/2/n),

verified here by brute-force enumeration for q = 2 up to degree 8, q = 3 to degree 5 and q = 5 to degree 4. That error term is the point: the prime number theorem in this setting comes with a square-root error, exactly and provably — the very statement that RH asserts over ℤ and nobody can prove. Computed for q = 2, the deviation |π₂(n) − 2n/n| never exceeds 2·2n/2/n for any n ≤ 12.

And the train sums exactly. Every monic polynomial of degree n contributes Tn, and there are qn of them, so

(3)  ∏P (1 − Tdeg P)−1  =   ∑f monic Tdeg f  =  ∞∑n=0 qnTn  =  11 − qT,

the Euler product of rh5 §1 with both sides now finite computations — verified coefficient by coefficient to T10 for q = 2, 3, 5. Note what (3) already says: the zeta function of the affine line is 1/(1 − qT), a rational function with no zeros whatsoever.

2 · The collapse: why this case is tractable

Now the structural fact this whole document turns on, and it is a direct consequence of rh8 §4. There, each prime was a circle: p−it has period 2π/log p, and because pa = qb is impossible, no two gears ever close together — gears 2 and 3 come no nearer than 0.0476 radians on t ≤ 300. The joint orbit winds densely on an infinite-dimensional torus and never returns.

Over 𝔽q the arithmetic is different in exactly one respect, and it changes everything. A gear of degree d has norm qd, so its period is

(4) 2π / log(qd) = (1/d) · 2π/log q :

every gear's period is an exact divisor of the single period 2π/log q — verified for q = 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 and degrees up to 8. All the logarithms are commensurable, because there is only one of them.

The consequence

Because every Euler factor is (1 − Tdeg P)−1 with T = q−s, the whole zeta function is a function of T alone — verified: T is periodic in t with period 2π/log q to 10−13. A function of one complex variable, built from a point count that grows like qn, and it turns out to be rational: Z(T) = L(T)/((1−T)(1−qT)) with L a polynomial of degree 2g.

So the zeros are finite in number — exactly 2g of them — where over ℚ they are infinite. That is the whole difference in tractability, and it traces back to a single fact: the number of independent logarithms. ℚ has infinitely many; 𝔽q[t] has one.

Part II · The circle, and what sits on it

3 · The critical line is a circle

Under T = q−s the vertical line σ = c maps to the circle |T| = q−c. So the critical strip becomes an annulus, and

(5) σ = ½  ⟺  |T| = q−1/2.

This is the sentence the whole sequence of documents has been circling. In rh4 the circles were a construction imposed on the numeral; here the critical line is a circle, in the ordinary plane, with an ordinary radius. The Riemann hypothesis for a curve over 𝔽q reads: the 2g zeros of a degree-2g polynomial all have the same modulus. That is a statement one can imagine proving — and Weil did.

4 · Genus 0 and genus 1: no zeros, then two

Genus 0. For the projective line, #ℙ¹(𝔽qn) = qn + 1 exactly, and Z(T) = 1/((1−T)(1−qT)): numerator 1, degree 0, no zeros at all. The rational function field is the analogue of a zeta with an empty critical strip — and it is the exact counterpart of rh5 §3's finding that the smooth cone limb is zero-free. Zeros require genus; genus requires a curve that is not a line.

Genus 1. Take E: y² = x³ + x + 1, and reduce it modulo each prime. Counting points gives ap = p + 1 − #E(𝔽p), and

(6) L(T) = 1 − apT + pT²,    zeros on |T| = p−1/2  ⟺  ap² ≤ 4p.

The condition is Hasse's theorem (1933), and it is an integer inequality. Computed over all 780 good primes below 6000: it holds every time, with maximum ratio |ap|/(2√p) = 0.991668 — close to sharp, never violated. Both zeros lie on the circle to 10−13 in every case.

The two zeros also carry the whole tower. Since Nn = pn + 1 − (αn + ᾱn), the point counts over every extension are determined by α alone. Checked the hard way — by constructing 𝔽p² explicitly and counting all its points by brute force, for p = 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 — the prediction from the two zeros matches the count exactly, every time.

5 · Genus 2, and RH as two integer inequalities

Take C: y² = x⁵ + x + 1, of genus 2. Counting points over 𝔽p and 𝔽p² determines the whole L-polynomial, because the functional equation fixes the rest:

(7) L(T) = 1 − e₁T + e₂T² − q e₁T³ + q²T⁴,    e₁ = q+1−N₁,   e₂ = (e₁² − (q²+1−N₂))/2.

Now the pretty part. Substituting u = x + q/x in the reciprocal polynomial factors it into two real quadratics x² − bjx + q, whose coefficients are the roots of u² − e₁u + (e₂ − 2q) = 0. And |α| = √q for all four roots exactly when both bj are real with |bj| ≤ 2√q. So:

RH for this curve, in integers

e₁² − 4(e₂ − 2q) ≥ 0  and  bj² ≤ 4q. Two inequalities in integers derived from two point counts. Verified at p = 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29, 31 — exactly, in integer arithmetic, and independently by finding the four roots numerically and confirming they share the modulus q−1/2 to 10−7.

Compare what the same statement costs over ℚ. There it is an assertion about infinitely many zeros of a transcendental function, equivalent (rh5 §4) to a Π⁰₁ sentence no one can decide. Here it is a finite check on a quartic. The difference is not that the mathematics is easier; it is that the object is finite-dimensional, which is precisely the multiplicity rh5 §5 said was missing.

Part III · What the finiteness buys

6 · The explicit formula, exact and finite

rh8 §2 rebuilt the prime staircase from 138 zeros and called the agreement good. Here the corresponding statement has no error term at all:

(8) Nn = qn + 1 − 2g∑i=1 αin ,

a sum over finitely many zeros, exact for every n. Set beside ψ(x) = x − ∑ρxρ/ρ − … this is the same formula with the infinite sum made finite: main term, oscillating correction from the zeros, done. And since |αi| = √q, the correction is bounded by 2g qn/2 — RH as a provable inequality about counting points.

7 · The statistics are settled too

rh8 §6 ended with an honest failure: the gap statistics of the ζ zeros show level repulsion, but at heights ≤ 300 they do not match GUE and one cannot claim they do. In the function field setting the corresponding question is not open either. Writing ap = 2√p cos θp, the Frobenius angle θp says where on the critical circle the two zeros sit, and the angles equidistribute with respect to the Sato–Tate measure (2/π)sin²θ — a theorem for non-CM elliptic curves.

8 · Why it does not transport

So the construction works perfectly over 𝔽q[t], and the hypothesis is a theorem there. Why is that not a proof over ℤ? Two answers, at different depths.

The shallow answer is §2: rationality. Over 𝔽q the zeta function is a ratio of polynomials because there is one logarithm; over ℚ there are infinitely many independent logarithms, the function is genuinely transcendental, and there are infinitely many zeros. Every finiteness argument used above evaporates.

The deep answer is what Weil's proof actually uses. It runs on the surface C × C: the Frobenius acts on the 2g-dimensional cohomology H¹(C), correspondences on the surface give a genuine intersection form, and the Hodge index theorem makes that form definite on the relevant subspace. Positivity of the intersection pairing, plus the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality it supports, forces |α| = √q. That is the mechanism, and it is exactly the object rh4 §15 and rh5 §5 named as missing: a finite-dimensional space carrying a positive form on which the symmetry acts with multiplicity.

The obstruction, stated precisely

Over 𝔽q a curve C comes with a base field to be a curve over, so C × 𝔽q C is a surface and intersection theory applies. Spec ℤ has no such base. There is no field beneath ℤ, so "Spec ℤ × Spec ℤ" is not a scheme, there is no arithmetic surface to intersect on, and the 2g-dimensional H¹ has no counterpart. Everything else in Weil's argument has an arithmetic shadow; this one thing does not exist.

That is why the standing programs are shaped the way they are. Deninger's search for a dynamical system whose cohomology would play the role of H¹; the field-with-one-element, which would supply the missing base; Connes' adele class space, which builds a space with multiplicity out of Ẑ × ℝ₊ — rh5 §5's exact assembly; Arakelov geometry, which compactifies Spec ℤ to make it more surface-like. Each is an attempt to manufacture the one ingredient that 𝔽q hands over for free.

So this document is not a step toward a proof, and should not be read as one. It is the control experiment. It shows that the geometry of rh4–rh8 is not defective — run it on a ring where the missing object exists and it delivers the theorem, cleanly, with every axiom intact and every claim computable. What is missing over ℤ is not an axiom, an identity, or a better presentation. It is a base.
