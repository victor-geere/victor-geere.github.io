1 · Where this stands

rh30 §9 proved that the arc’s certified points cannot bear on RH whether they pass or fail, and §10 set three directions in their place: (a) prove the collapse λmin/Eh(0) = F(n/Neff), the only statement the arc has that is universally quantified over a family; (b) the inequality §5 names, from the analytic side, for which “Beurling–Selberg majorants are the obvious family and the arc has never tried them”; (c) stop extending the certified set.

rh31 through rh36 kept (c) faithfully and touched neither (a) nor (b). Six documents went instead on a quadrature lemma that rh36 finally showed was false rather than merely unproven. This document takes (b), which means building machinery the arc does not have and then testing the two specific ideas rh30 attached to it. Both fail. Saying so precisely, with the reason, is the useful part — which is also how rh30 §5 ended.

2 · The constraints

Two constraints on the construction go into the instrument first, because everything downstream is stated in their coordinates.

(i) Matched winding. Where the a-helix passes through a gear it has completed the same number of rotations about the cone as that gear has itself. One cumulative count, never reduced mod 2π.
(ii) Carry-proof monotonicity. That count’s increment carries a uniform floor — half a turn per level, at any torsion — so no small positive increase, and no residual swinging the whole way round through a carry, can interrupt it.

Constraint (i) fixes the normalisation the sign convention left implicit. Writing W(n, t) = θ(n, t)/2π, the count is the helix’s winding at depth n − 1 and gear n’s own rotation count, and W(1, t) = 0 at every torsion. So gear 1 never turns: depth 0, radius 1 for every σ, rate log 1 = 0, winding 0 — the origin of all four coordinates and the construction’s one fixed point. rh36 §6 noticed gear 1’s stationarity as an accident of a frequency list; it is an axiom. At zero torsion W(n, 0) = (n − 1)/2 exactly, so consecutive a’s sit antipodally.

Constraint (ii) is the one with teeth. The increment is ½ + (t/2π) log(1 + 1/n) discretely and (π + t/n)/2π on the continuous extension, and both are ≥ ½ for every n and every t ≥ 0, with equality only as t → 0. Because that floor does not depend on where in the turn the helix happens to be, a carry is pure bookkeeping: between gears 3 and 4 at t = 331 the residual falls by 0.345 while the count climbs by 15.66.

And it excludes a convention rather than merely declining it. rh3 §6 and rh5 §3 write the torsion as −t log n against half-turns of +π, whose increment π − t log(1 + 1/n) has no floor. At t = 1 its least value is still positive, 2.448; it crosses zero at t = π/log 2 = 4.53, below the first zero of zeta; by γ₁ it is −6.656 and by γ = 331 it is −226.3. The “reversal at x* = t/π” those documents report is the signature of the missing floor. A corollary used throughout: nothing defined mod 2π may be made to carry the monotonicity, so the fold and every residual statement are disqualified as carriers by construction.

3 · The machinery

Beurling’s function B is the extremal majorant of the sign function: entire of exponential type 2π, B(x) ≥ sgn(x) everywhere, meeting sgn at every nonzero integer, with ∫(B − sgn) = 1 and no majorant of that type doing better.

Two things had to be got right that recollection got wrong, and both were caught by measuring rather than citing. B is B alone. Writing B = H + K also majorises — K is the Fejér kernel and nonnegative — but with excess 2, handing away a factor of two before the argument starts; the integral said 1.993 and that is how it was found. And the poles must be removed analytically: B’s defining bracket has a double pole at every non-positive integer that the sin² factor cancels, and doing that cancellation in floating point costs ten digits near each one. Reflection gives a pole-free form for z > −1, and the functional equation

B(z) + B(−z) = 2 K(z)

carries it to the rest of the line. It holds here to 5.6×10−17. With that, B ≥ sgn is verified to −1.7×10−13 over 400,000 offset samples, and exactly to zero out to |x| = 4000 where the sampled maximum is attained at the integers themselves.

From B, Selberg’s majorant and minorant bracket any interval’s indicator at any type 2πδ, verified pointwise to 3.2×10−15 at four intervals and four types. Their L¹ deficiency is exactly 1/δ — independent of the interval’s length, and falling in the type rather than growing in it. That last clause is precisely what rh30 §5’s three routes lack: Gershgorin, Koksma and Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund all lose linearly in the degree. It is why (b) looked worth taking.

4 · Regression

Before anything new is compared to rh30, rh30 is rebuilt from the zeros. Gershgorin’s bound is positive at n = 24 (0.1365) and negative at n = 32 (−0.5142), bracketing the reach of 28 that rh30 §5 reports. And rh30 §3’s published point, λmin/Eh(0) = 0.2846 at n = 151, sits between the 0.3265 measured here at n = 128 and the 0.1539 at n = 192.

5 · The gap idea

rh30 §10 (b)’s first suggestion: a bound “that localises to the largest gap instead of taking a worst case round the whole circle.” Its sharp form is an uncertainty principle rather than a variation bound — a nonnegative trig polynomial of degree n − 1 can put nearly all its mass in one arc of length about 1/n, and in no less. So the adversary gets one hiding place, and the quantity is the avoidable mass: the weight that survives the best window of width 1/n.

It fails, and it fails by saturating. At the 0.12 window the avoidable mass is 0.9455 at n = 24 and 0.9847 by n = 512, while the truth over the same range falls to 0.0024. The ratio of truth to avoidable mass runs from 2.05 at n = 2 to 0.0024 at n = 512 — three decades, moving the wrong way — and past n = 8 the avoidable mass never drops below 0.858 at any depth.

The reason is countable. There are 8,616 atoms on the circle, so a window of width 1/n contains almost none of the mass at any depth worth having. The minimising polynomial is not hiding in a gap. It is small at many atoms at once, which is a different thing and not a localisation at all.

6 · The q ≥ 0 idea

The second suggestion, and the one rh30 wanted most: “an inequality that uses q = |p|² ≥ 0 rather than only its variation.” There is a clean one, and it is Fejér’s own extremal problem. A nonnegative trig polynomial of degree n − 1 with mean 1 has sup q ≤ n, attained by the Fejér kernel; hence ‖q‖₂² ≤ n, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives

λmin/E(0) ≥ 1 − √(n−1)  ( 2 ∑0<k<n (E(kΔ)/E(0))² )1/2

— √n where Gershgorin has n, using nonnegativity and nothing else. It is exactly the shape rh30 asked for.

It is worse. At the 0.12 window Gershgorin survives to n = 24 and this dies at 16; at 0.03, where the arc has the most room, Gershgorin reaches 96 and this reaches 64. At no window of the five does it beat Gershgorin. The √n is real, but it is bought by trading max|E| for the ℓ² norm of the whole row, and the row does not decay fast enough to pay for the trade.

7 · Where the decades go

Four routes now, all with the same shape of failure, which is a reason to stop looking for a fifth and ask what they share. They share one step. Each controls

∑|k|<n q̂(k) E(kΔ)  by  |q̂(k)| ≤ 1,

the worst case, one sign flip per lag. The true minimiser does not look like that.

Take the exact minimiser at n = 96 on the 0.12 window and read off its autocorrelation. q̂(0) = 1 by normalisation, and the mean of |q̂(k)| over the 95 nonzero lags is 0.220, not 1. The worst case charges 2∑|E(kΔ)|/E(0) = 6.27 against a budget of 1, which is why Gershgorin dies at 24; the true minimiser is charged 1.34, a factor 4.7 less, because its autocorrelation decays where the worst case does not.

So the loss is not in any constant, and no sharpening of one closes it — rh30 §5 said as much and this is the mechanism. It is not in the discrepancy either, and not in where q concentrates. It is in assuming a polynomial can be adversarial at every lag at once, when a nonnegative one cannot. The missing inequality rh30 named is a joint bound on the autocorrelation of a nonnegative polynomial across lags. Beurling–Selberg does not supply one. Neither does Fejér.

8 · Where this leaves the conjunction

Nowhere new. What has changed is the status of rh30 §10 (b): it is now tried rather than open. The majorants are built and verified, both suggestions attached to them are refuted by measurement, and the four failures turn out to be one failure rather than four.

That leaves rh30 §10 (a) — prove the collapse — as the only one of the three directions not closed, and still the only statement the arc has with the logical shape a conjunction needs. It has now been open for seven documents and untouched for all seven, which is worth saying plainly rather than leaving to be inferred.

The honest summary of §§5–7 is that this document converts a vague “the constants are wrong” into a specific missing lemma. That is smaller than a bound and larger than nothing: a fifth attempt of the same kind can now be predicted to fail before it is run, and the thing that would not fail has a name.

The measured mean of 0.220 at n = 96 is one data point on whatever the truth is; the profile as a function of n and of the window is measurable with the machinery already here, and would say whether the joint constraint has a clean form worth trying to prove.
