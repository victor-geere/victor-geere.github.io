The instruments

The four operational criteria the arc kept naming — the pairing, the carousel, the thermometer, the ruler — each built and read as an actual instrument, with both of its sides computed wherever it has two.

Abstract. Six documents built a geometry and mapped it into analytic number theory; along the way four equivalent reformulations of the hypothesis surfaced and were left standing as names. This document builds each one and takes a reading. Part I · Two spectral instruments

1 · The pairing: Axiom 8 as an instrument

rh4 §15 named the missing axiom: a pairing with provable positivity. Weil's criterion says exactly which pairing. For an even test function h with Fourier partner g, the Riemann–Weil explicit formula reads

(1)  ∑ρ h(γρ) = h(i/2) + h(−i/2) + 12π ∞∫−∞ h(r)[Re ψ(¼+ir/2) − ln π] dr − 2  ∑n≥2 Λ(n)√n g(log n),

and RH is equivalent to the right-hand side being ≥ 0 whenever h = |f̂|² — positivity of the arithmetic pairing on all test functions. Both sides are implemented here. For Gaussian h centred at t₀ = 20, 35, and γ₁ itself, the two sides agree to 3×10−6 — the formula is not quoted but running.

Then the instrument: take a narrow Gaussian window (width 0.7), slide its centre t₀ across the strip, and evaluate W(t₀) from the arithmetic side only — primes, prime powers, and the Γ-factor; no zero is consulted. If RH holds, W is a sum of squares-in-waiting and must stay nonnegative; where it is large, a zero is near.

What the instrument cannot do is as important as what it shows. Each reading is one test function; the criterion quantifies over all of them, and the reason no finite battery suffices is rh8 §6's: positivity of this functional for every h = |f̂|² is a statement about a quadratic form on an infinite-dimensional space, and the present symmetries give that space no basis to be diagonalised in. The dips to 2×10−5 also show the criterion is sharp: between zeros the pairing has essentially nothing to spare. Positivity, if true, is true with no margin — the same knife-edge that §3 will measure as Λ = 0.

2 · The carousel: Li's criterion on the rim

The arc has drawn circles since rh4; the second instrument turns out to be one. Apply the Möbius map

(2) ρ ⟼ zρ = 1 − 1/ρ ,    |zρ| = |ρ−1|/|ρ| = 1  ⟺  Re ρ = ½ :

the critical line maps to the unit circle, exactly. The hypothesis becomes: the zeros, so mapped, all sit on the rim. Li's criterion is this statement made numerical: with λn = ∑ρ[1 − (1−1/ρ)n], RH ⟺ λn ≥ 0 for all n (Li 1997; Bombieri–Lagarias 1999). On the rim, each conjugate pair contributes 2(1 − cos nθρ) ≥ 0 — term-by-term positivity, for free — while a single pair off the rim has one image inside and one outside, and the outside one's powers grow exponentially and eventually drag λn to −∞. The criterion is a carousel: spin the zeros n times; if anyone leaves the rim, the books stop balancing.

Two remarks the computation makes concrete. First, the two λ-computations are independent in the strongest sense: the contour never sees a zero, the zero-sum never sees ζ — their agreement is the explicit formula again, in coefficient form. Second, the geometric reading explains why Li positivity is, like §1, sharp but shallow: on the rim the terms are nonnegative pointwise, which is rh5 §2's "free positivity" — the criterion's real content is not that the terms are positive but that the arithmetic side (the contour) keeps matching a sum that would be poisoned by a single point off the rim.

Part II · Two metric instruments

3 · The thermometer: the flow, run cold

rh6 §5.3 stated the reading; here the flow is actually run. Deform the completed horn's Jacobi kernel Φ (rh6 eq. 8) by heat:

(3) Hλ(t) = 4 ∞∫0 Φ(u) eλu² cos(ut) du,    H₀ = Ξ.

Forward heat (λ > 0) smooths the kernel and herds the zeros toward regularity; backward heat (λ < 0) roughens it, and far enough back, real zeros collide in pairs and leave the line. The de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ is the threshold: all zeros real exactly for λ ≥ Λ. RH ⟺ Λ ≤ 0; Rodgers–Tao proved Λ ≥ 0; so RH ⟺ Λ = 0.

At λ = 0 the computed flow reproduces the first eight ordinates to 2×10−3. Run cold, the zeros drift pairwise together, and the first collision is found by bisection: γ₄ and γ₅ merge at λ* = −0.9885, at height t = 33.10. For λ < λ* that pair is complex — so this computation, by itself, proves Λ ≥ −0.9885. (The historical lower bounds improved exactly this experiment: closer pairs higher up — Lehmer pairs — collide at λ much nearer 0, and Rodgers–Tao closed the gap to 0 entirely.) Run warm, the spacings regularise: the normalised spacing spread of the first eight zeros drops from 0.360 at λ = 0 to 0.310 at λ = ½.

4 · The ruler: writing 1 with fractional parts

rh5 §6 ended at Beurling–Nyman: exact incidence is unreachable, so the geometry must approximate in mean, and RH is precisely the statement that the approximation can be completed. The instrument: in L²(0,1), measure the distance from the constant function 1 to the span of the fractional-part dilates,

(4) dN = distL² (1, span{ρ(1/nx) : n ≤ N}),    RH ⟺ dN → 0

(Nyman 1950, Beurling 1955; the restriction to n ≤ N is Báez-Duarte's). Every inner product needed is computed exactly, by piecewise integration between consecutive breakpoints of the fractional parts, with the classical check ∫₀¹ρ(1/x)dx = 1 − γE reproduced to 10−6. The Gram system then gives

(5) d₁ = 0.5606,   d₂ = 0.4160,   d₃ = 0.2745,   … ,   d₂₀ = 0.1269,

strictly decreasing, and tracking the conjectured law dN² ~ (2+γE−ln 4π)/ln N (Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias; Burnol): measured d₂₀² ln 20 = 0.0482 against the limit 0.0462. The convergence is logarithmic, so no numerical run approaches 0 — the point of the instrument is different: the distance is the gap-filling axiom of the geometry, made into one number, and the constant governing its decay is the same 0.0462 = 2λ₁ that §2 measured on the carousel.

Part III · The balance

5 · One constant, three dials

Three of the four instruments turn out to display the same number:

(6) λ₁ = 1 + γE2 − ½ ln 4π  =   ∑γ>0 1¼+γ²  =  0.0230957 … ,

verified here in both directions: the arithmetic value from the contour integral to 10−10, and the zero-sum from the 138 ordinates plus an integrated tail to 3×10−6. It is the first Li coefficient (§2); its double 2λ₁ = 2+γE−ln 4π is the conjectured Beurling–Nyman decay constant (§4); and it is the total weight ∑1/|ρ|² that sets the overall scale of the pairing's spectral side (§1). One number: the total inverse-square mass of the spectrum, visible on three dials at once. The arc met it before without naming it — it is what the carry-register noise of rh6 §3 integrates to at the bottom of the tower.

Reading the four dials together closes the arc the way it deserves to close — without a flourish. Every instrument is consistent with the hypothesis, with margins exactly as computed, and each is sharp in its own way: the pairing has nothing to spare between zeros, the thermometer sits (by Rodgers–Tao) exactly at its threshold, the ruler converges at the slowest rate a convergent process can have. RH, if true, is true with no slack anywhere — every instrument says so independently.

And none of them can be pushed to a proof by more computation, each for a reason the arc has already given. The pairing quantifies over an infinite-dimensional cone of test functions with no spectral basis in sight (rh5 §5); the carousel's positivity must hold for all n, and the tail of the spectrum always outruns any finite check (rh8 §4's torus never closes); the thermometer needs the collision boundary at zero, not near it; the ruler converges logarithmically by theorem. The missing ingredient is the one rh9 named: a base — a finite-dimensional positive geometry for these instruments to be the shadow of. Over 𝔽q(t) every dial locks: the pairing is the Hodge index form, the carousel has 2g points that provably sit on the rim, the thermometer's threshold is attained, the ruler's distance is zero. Over ℚ the instruments all work, all agree, and all wait.
