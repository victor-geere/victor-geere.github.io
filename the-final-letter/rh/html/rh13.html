1 · The order

rh12 closed the books with one clause owed — positivity on an H¹ nobody has built — and the reply came back as an order: stop pricing the missing surface and lay its circles. The instruction is precise in the arc's own coordinates. The cone of rh6 carries one ruling already: the 2π parallels, the phase circles, whose winding register along the critical line is the t-direction. The claim "every base 2π has a ℤ radix" is a theorem — ℤ = π₁(S¹), the winding count of eix, minted for free by any circle — and it cuts both ways: it means the integers were never the foundation, and it means any second circle family laid on the cone brings its own ℤ with it. So lay them. The scale direction of the cone is multiplicative; each prime p closes it into a circle ℝ₊/p^ℤ of arc length log p, whose winding register k ∈ ℤ is the gear tower p, p², p³ — rh8 §3's prime-circles, now installed as the second ruling. Two windings, two registers, one surface: this document computes everything that surface supports at finite level — incidence, pairing, index, audit — and reports the readings.

Part I · The weave

2 · The arc-ℤ circles, and the incidence of the rulings

The construction, concretely. First ruling: at every altitude, the phase circle of arc 2π; a zero γ is a closed winding of the whole train — the zero-ruling is the family of those closures. Second ruling: for each prime p, the scale circle ℝ₊/p^ℤ, arc log p; its k-th winding visits the gears p, p², p³, … — winding number is exactly the exponent, the arc-ℤ register. The two rulings cross, and crossing is arithmetic: the pairing of a zero-winding γ against the k-th winding of the p-circle is cos(γ·k log p) — the explicit formula's matrix element, one intersection at a time.

The incidence computation is Landau's theorem (rh8 §5) wearing intersection theory: Σγ≤T cos(γ log n) ≈ −(T/2π)Λ(n)/√n. What the two-altitude verification adds is the surface reading: the intersection number per unit altitude of the zero-ruling with the scale circle of pk is a constant of the weave — measured at 2000 and at 10⁶, the same Λ-weights to within the stated tolerances. This is the property a ruled surface must have and a coincidence would not: the fabric does not change with height. (It is also rh12 §6's counting function read transversally: N(q)'s atoms at the gears are these intersection numbers, integrated.)

4 · The Castelnuovo ledger, and every minor

Weil's actual inequality on C × C is Castelnuovo–Severi's: for a correspondence, σ(D∘D′) ≤ 2·d(D)d′(D) — degree terms dominate the self-pairing, with the defect controlled by the Hodge index. The explicit formula has exactly this shape, and the two-ruling dictionary makes the match term-by-term: the pole terms h(±i/2) are the degree terms (the two rulings' fibers — the poles at 0 and 1, the H⁰ and H² of the would-be surface); the archimedean integral is the fiber at infinity (the cone's own place, rh6 §2); the gear sum is the intersection with the Frobenius graphs (one orbit per prime, k-fold windings of the scale circles); and the spectral side — forced nonnegative if RH — is the defect. rh12's ledger balanced one instance; here it is run as a ledger discipline:

5 · The ℤ radix, audited

The first ruling's register was the claim's other half: the phase winding θ/π ticks once per zero — the ℤ radix of the 2π base is the zero counter (rh6 §3's carry register; the Z of Riemann–Siegel is the radix-aligned reading of ζ). Gram points are the register's tick marks: θ(gn) = nπ. The audit asks: does one tick hold one zero?

This closes the claim's loop with a measurement: the ℤ radix of the phase circles is exact bookkeeping (the identity holds to the last interval, 59,994 of them), while its local law (one tick, one zero) degrades statistically — 0.897 to 0.757 — at precisely the rate the zeros' GUE repulsion dictates. The integers are not the foundation of any of this; they are the ledger lines. Both rulings now have their registers audited: the phase-ℤ counts zeros and balances exactly; the scale-ℤ counts gear windings and pairs into the crossing form of §3.

6 · What the second winding bought

The order was to build, and here is what stands built. The cone now carries both rulings: phase circles of arc 2π and scale circles of arc log p, each with its ℤ register, exactly as demanded — and the demand turned out to be the right one, because every structure a Weil surface owes its owner came out computable and came out true: the incidence numbers are the von Mangoldt weights at every altitude (§2); the crossing form exists as an honest matrix, its 325 entries certified from the primes alone, its signature (25, 0, 0) — positive, no exceptions, softest direction identified and matching the extremal theory (§3); the Castelnuovo ledger balances with a nonnegative defect for every window tried, the extremal one on the boundary (§4); and the integer registers of both rulings audit exactly (§5).

What is not in this document is an infinite-dimensional theorem, and the honest sentence about that is one line, not a lament: the surface's finite shadows — every one the arc could construct — pass the index test; the statement that all of them do, for every window space at once, is RH, and it now has a measured shape: a form whose worst minor sits at half its bound, whose bottom eigenvalue sits at 10⁻⁸ and refuses to go negative, whose softest direction is a window this arc solved for in closed form. The construction did not close the gap between finite and all. It did something a computation can do: it made the gap load-bearing — every future window space is now a one-afternoon refutation test, and every one that passes is another slice of the surface that behaves as if the keystone were already set. The books stay open exactly one clause wide.
