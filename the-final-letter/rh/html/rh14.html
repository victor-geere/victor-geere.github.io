1 · The order, and the helix

rh13 built the two rule systems as two separate circle families and verified their weave. The order for this document: align them — put the a_ℤ planet and the a_2π planet on the same helix, pitch 6.28… per 1, and merge the systems without losing anything already built. The helix is easy to name precisely. Take one real parameter u — height along the cone's axis in logarithmic scale — and wind at exactly one turn per unit: arc 2π of phase for every 1 of height. On that single thread both registers tick: the phase register at the integers u ∈ ℤ (one full turn each — the a_2π planet), and the scale register at u = log n (the gears — the a_ℤ planet, each prime's tower at log p, 2 log p, 3 log p, …). The two trains interleave and, because every log n is irrational against 1, never coincide twice — rh8 §4's theorem, which is not an obstruction here but the point: a quasi-periodic thread never repeats, so one thread can carry everything. The merged system's whole content must then live in functions of one variable on this line — and §2 shows that it does, exactly.

Part I · One thread

2 · The kernel: two registers, one function

rh13's crossing form was a matrix indexed by two window positions. On the aligned helix, translation invariance does what alignment is for: the form depends only on the difference of positions — the height gap v along the thread — and the whole two-index object collapses to one function of one variable,

(1) E(v) =  ∑ρ |B(γρ)|² cos(γρv)  =  2B(i/2)²cosh(v/2) + 1π ∞∫0 |B|²cos(rv)[Reψ − lnπ]dr −  ∑n Λ(n)√n C(log n ∓ v),

with B the mesh profile's transform and C its autocorrelation bump (support 0.06 — essentially a point on the scale of the gears). The left side is the zeros' theory of E; the right side — pole, place at infinity, gear comb — is the primes' theory, and neither consults the other. Computed independently on 241 points of v ∈ [0, 6]: one function, two theories, maximum deviation 1.6×10⁻⁵ of scale.

Read (1) as the alignment made flesh. The a_ℤ register enters E as its singular support — the dent train at log n, weights Λ(n)/√n, the second ruling's ℤ ticking in the argument of C. The a_2π register enters as its spectrum — E is a superposition of cosines at the zero frequencies, the first ruling's winding rates. One function of the single helix coordinate holds the primes as dents and the zeros as tones, and the explicit formula — the arc's equation since rh10 — is revealed as the statement that the dent train and the tone stack are the same function. That is what "merge the two rule systems into one" means, and it is now a computed object with an error bar.

3 · The merge: nothing lost, and the frontier continued

The order said: take care not to lose what we have. Everything the arc has built on windows is a quadratic form; every such form is now a fold of E. rh13's even-pair Gram matrix — all 325 entries of rh13 §3, computed there by 325 separate double-quadratures — reproduces as

(2) G[j,k] = 2[E(yj−yk) + E(yj+yk)]

(halved once per zero index), to 8.1×10⁻⁵ of scale, worst entry — the whole crossing form of rh13, refolded out of one curve. rh12's optimal window re-evaluates inside the merged system to its stated dip (the D* consistency check passes at rh12's own tolerance), and the frontier machinery — one linear solve per budget — runs on the merged form directly: at support a = 3 (gears to log n ≤ 6, the sieve out to n = 403), D*(3) = 1.15×10⁻⁸, two and a half decades past rh12's endpoint, with the same certified positivity. Nothing built in rh10–rh13 is outside this one function; several things are now cheaper inside it.

Part II · The continuum

4 · The exhaustion: finite slices of one continuous form

"Turn the finite slices into a continuum" has an exact meaning for a quadratic form, and it is the meaning this section certifies. The slices of the arc — rh13's 25 dimensions, rh12's window spaces — are members of a nested family: refine the mesh (hats of width 0.06 are exact sums of hats of width 0.03) and the space grows; widen the support and the space grows. The union of the family, over all meshes and supports, is dense in every space of admissible windows: the continuum is the limit of the slices, and a single continuous form restricted to the slices must show two signatures — the same kernel serving every level, and eigenvalue monotonicity along every nesting (the variational principle: a bigger space can only find softer directions). The honest grammar of the continuum claim, stated once. RH is equivalent (Weil; Bochner's language) to: E is a positive-definite function on every interval — every finite slice of every mesh has a nonnegative spectrum. The slices computed here are certificates of that statement at four levels of a family whose union is dense; the statement itself quantifies over all levels at once. What the alignment changes is not that gap — nothing finite closes it — but its economics: certificates used to cost a matrix each (rh13's 325 quadratures); they now cost samples of one already-computed function. The continuum is exhausted by slices that are, from today, nearly free.

5 · The spectrum: the continuum arrives

There is a second, sharper sense of "the finite slices become a continuum", and it is the one the fine slice actually exhibits. As the mesh refines and the support widens, the eigenmodes of the merged form stop being generic window shapes and lock onto the zeros themselves: the form is ∑ρ of rank-one pieces, one pair per zero (a cosine and a sine profile at frequency γ), and once the slice resolves frequencies finer than the zero spacing, its top eigenvectors are those pieces.

This is the alignment's deepest return. The a_2π system counted zeros; the a_ℤ system counted gears; the merged form, diagonalized, produces the zeros as its eigenmodes — with the gears in its kernel's dents driving them. The two planets do not merely sit on one helix; run as one system, the helix's own resonances are the critical line's spectrum, recovered here to a tenth of a unit from primes-and-Γ data on one side and confirmed by the table on the other. And the soft end of the same spectrum — the 10⁻¹¹ tail of Figure 3, the directions that thread between all the resolved zeros — is where the hypothesis lives: the continuum statement is exactly that this tail never dips under the axis, at any level, for any support. Every level computed says it does not.

6 · What the alignment settles, and what it sharpens

Settled. The two rule systems are one system: a single kernel E on the single helix coordinate — dents at the gears, tones at the zeros — computed from either theory to 1.6×10⁻⁵ and carrying, as folds and slices, every quadratic object the arc has built: rh13's matrix (8.1×10⁻⁵), rh12's dip (re-derived), the frontier (extended to 1.15×10⁻⁸), four nested spectra (446 eigenvalues, all positive). Nothing was lost; most things got cheaper; some things — the mode-by-mode arrival of the zeros — only became visible after the merge.

Sharpened. The open clause has not moved, but it has been re-worded by the construction into its cleanest form yet: one computed function of one real variable, positive-definite at every scale ⟺ RH. Not a cone of test functions, not a matrix per space: one curve, and a single question about it — Bochner's question. The continuum program is now mechanical: each new (mesh, support) pair is a finite spectrum drawn from the same curve, each spectrum a refutation opportunity, each positivity a certificate. The arc leaves the question where a fifteen-document construction can honestly leave it: as one function, drawn in Figure 1, whose every computed slice — 446 eigenvalues, ten decades, four levels, and every prior document refolded — sits on the right side of zero.
