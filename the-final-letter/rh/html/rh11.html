Three digs at the base

rh9 ended on an obstruction: the proof that works lives on a surface, and Spec ℤ has no visible second factor. This document takes the three threads rh10 left pointing at that hole — the cone of test functions, the Lehmer-pair hunt, and the field with one element — and digs each one as far as computation honestly reaches.

Abstract. rh9 §8 located the missing ingredient by name: Weil proved RH for curves on the surface C × C , using a base field beneath the curve, and Spec ℤ sits over no visible base — no second factor, no H¹, no intersection form. Three computations now price that absence from three sides. The cone (§2): rh10 §1's positivity scan is generalised from one Gaussian window to four families — Gaussian, Fejér (f = rect), B-spline (f = tent), Hann (f = cosine arch) — the last three with compactly supported g, so each consults only finitely many gears (the Fejér window at FWHM 1.74 needs exactly 13 of them). The explicit formula is re-verified for all twenty family–width pairs, and the dip-versus-resolution trade-off is measured: at matched resolution the Fejér floor sits ≈ 1.3×10³ above the Gaussian floor — slow tails in h are what lifts the dips — with the 1/r⁴ pair between. Injecting a hypothetical off-line quadruple gives each family a detection threshold: B-spline and Hann see a pair at σ = 0.60 (β* = 0.10), Fejér at 0.65, while the Gaussian never swings negative through β = 0.45 — it reads the pair as more mass, a positive bump indistinguishable from honest zeros. The hunt (§3): the zero table is extended to 1517 ordinates below T = 2000 (audited window-by-window against Riemann–von Mangoldt), and the backward heat flow is run as zero dynamics, validated against rh10's directly-integrated collision to 0.1%. The table's tightest pair, γ₁₄₉₆–γ₁₄₉₇ at gap 0.0975, collides at λ* = −0.00119 — three decades past rh10's −0.989 — and all measured collision times obey τ* = gap²/8 to 1%, which is why the march toward the Rodgers–Tao wall at 0 is precisely a hunt for ever-tighter pairs. The base (§4–5): over 𝔽₁, counting is the q → 1 limit, and every limit lands where Tits said — [3]q → 3, the q-binomial → the subset count, #GL₃/(q−1)³q³ → 3!, so GLn(𝔽₁) = Sn: rh5 §5's clock world is the linear algebra of the missing base. Soulé's ζ of P¹ and P² over 𝔽₁, computed as actual limits, come out 1/s(s−1) and 1/s(s−1)(s−2) — the completed ζ's factor from pure counting. And the determinant dream is run at computation's level: ξ rebuilt from its 1517 zeros plus a density tail matches direct ξ to 1.1×10⁻⁸ across σ ∈ [−1.5, 3]; Borger's Frobenius lifts on ℤ are the identity, their congruence exactly Fermat's np ≡ n (mod p); the Bost–Connes gas has partition function ζ(β) and internal energy ∑Λ(n)n−β = −ζ′/ζ, verified to 3×10⁻¹¹ — the von Mangoldt weights are the energy of the gear gas. What no dig produces is an H¹ with an intersection form (§6). The obstruction stands, now measured from three sides.

1 · The question under the floor

rh9 §8 ended the arc's only proved chapter with a verdict. Over 𝔽q(t) the hypothesis is a theorem because the curve has a base to stand on: Weil's argument runs on the surface C × C, where Frobenius has a graph, the graph has a self-intersection, and the Hodge index theorem makes a quadratic form negative-definite exactly where RH needs it. Spec ℤ, asked the same question, offers no second factor: nothing to cross it with, no H¹ to carry an intersection form, no base field under the arithmetic. The dial that would read proved has no mechanism behind it.

rh10's scoreboard closed with every instrument reading consistent, margin as computed — and left three threads hanging, each pointing at the same hole. The positivity scan used one window; the criterion quantifies over a whole cone of them, and which windows sharpen the reading is a question about what the pairing can and cannot see. The thermometer's collision at λ* = −0.9885 was the first rung of a ladder that the literature has climbed eleven decades further; what governs the climb is the census of near-degenerate zero pairs — Lehmer's phenomenon. And rh9's own closing pages named a candidate for the base itself: the field with one element, the 𝔽₁ of Tits' remark, under which Spec ℤ would become a curve and ζ a characteristic polynomial. This document digs all three. None of the digs moves the obstruction; each one measures it — that distinction is held to throughout.

Part I · The cone

2 · Dig one — the cone: which windows tighten the dips

The Weil criterion (rh4 §15's Axiom 8, built as an instrument in rh10 §1) is a positivity statement about a cone: for every test function h = |f̂|², the explicit-formula functional must be nonnegative. rh10 took one Gaussian window and found dips of ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ between zeros. The question left standing: is that floor a property of the zeros, or of the window? Four families answer it. Each is h = |f̂|² with g = f⋆f̃ its Fourier partner on the gear side:

The compactly supported g is the point: a bandlimited window consults finitely many gears. The prime side of the explicit formula truncates exactly at log n ≤ 2a — the Fejér window at FWHM 1.74 asks thirteen gears (2 through 23) and nothing else about arithmetic. For every one of the twenty family–width pairs the Riemann–Weil identity was re-verified with both sides truncated at the same height T = 2000, where their tails cancel against each other because (2π)⁻¹[Re ψ(¼+ir/2) − ln π] is the smooth zero density: agreement between 5×10⁻⁶ (Gaussian) and 5×10⁻⁴ (Fejér, whose 1/r² tails converge slowest), relative to h(0). Then each window was slid across t₀ ∈ [12, 46] using the arithmetic side alone, and the smallest reading between zeros — the dip — was recorded.

Three findings, in order of weight. First: the dips are the window's, not the zeros'. Slow tails in h leak mass from every far zero into the gap under inspection; the leakage floor scales with the tail exponent, and the measured ordering — 1/r² worst by three decades, 1/r⁴ intermediate, super-exponential best — is exactly that. "Which families tighten the dips" has a clean answer: fastest h-decay at fixed resolution, with the Gaussian as the ideal and the B-spline/Hann pair the best finite-gear approximations to it, reaching within ≈ 30× of the Gaussian floor on a budget of a few dozen gears. The classical end of this game is the Beurling–Selberg extremal problem — majorize an indicator with a bandlimited function, minimally — and Vaaler's survey is the map of it; our four families are working instances of the trade-off it formalises.

Second: what the cone detects is sign, and only bandlimited windows read it. An off-line zero enters the functional through h evaluated off the real axis — h(γ − t₀ ± iβ) — where h = |f̂|² no longer applies and the value can go negative; that sign structure is the entire content of the criterion. The injected quadruple at σ = ½+β becomes visible to the compact families at β* = 0.10–0.15 somewhere in their width range (which width sees it depends on where the negative lobe, spaced ~1/a, lands relative to the true zeros — the criterion needs the whole cone, not one window). The Gaussian never sees it: its envelope e−(δ²−β²)/2a² crushes the sign oscillation cos(δβ/a²) before it turns; the pair registers only as a positive bump of +4.2 at the injection height — four honest zeros' worth of mass, sign unread. A blind instrument with the deepest dips: depth of floor and power of discrimination are different virtues, and the cone needs both.

Third: the sharpness has a price schedule. The Gaussian's 2.6×10⁻⁵ floor at FWHM 1.65 needs every gear in the sieve — its g never cuts off; the finite-gear families pay for their budgets with lifted floors on the schedule Figure 1 tabulates. Between zeros the pairing has essentially nothing to spare (rh10's knife-edge, rh8 §6's no-basis-to-diagonalise), and now the schedule says how nothing: no finite battery of windows, however chosen, certifies positivity — but the battery's failure modes are quantifiable, family by family.

Part II · The hunt

3 · Dig two — the hunt: pushing λ* toward the wall

rh10 §3 cooled the flow until γ₄ and γ₅ fused: λ* = −0.9885, hence Λ ≥ −0.9885 from that data. The de Bruijn–Newman constant sits in a corridor closed at one end — Λ ≥ 0 (Rodgers–Tao 2018) — with RH ⟺ Λ = 0: the hypothesis is the statement that the corridor has zero width. Improving the computed side means finding zeros that fuse faster, and the governing phenomenon has a name: a Lehmer pair, two zeros so close that an almost-vanishing gap between them is the flow's whole story (Lehmer 1956; formalised, and turned into bounds, by Csordas–Smith–Varga 1994).

First the table. The Euler–Maclaurin Z-machinery of rh10 was pushed to T = 2000: 1517 ordinates, scanned at step 0.05, audited in every 100-window against the Riemann–von Mangoldt count θ(T)/π + 1 (total expected 1516.99, found 1517), first ten matching published values to 10⁻⁷. Then the census: every consecutive gap, normalised by the local mean spacing 2π/ln(t/2π). GUE repulsion makes tight pairs rare — the probability density vanishes like g² at small gap — and the table's three tightest are the hunt's quarry.

Direct integration of Ξ dies past t ≈ 90 (the e−πt/4 decay), so the flow is run in its other standard form — as dynamics of the zeros themselves. Under backward heat flow the zeros repel pairwise:

(1) dxkdτ = −2  ∑j≠k 1xk − xj ,   τ = −λ,

summed over the table with mirror images at −γ and a density-integral tail beyond it (the picture behind Csordas–Smith–Varga's analysis and Polymath 15's computations). Implementation: an active window of 40 zeros around the target pair integrated by RK4 with adaptive steps, the frozen background field tabulated once and interpolated (error ≲ 2×10⁻⁴ against pair forces of order 1/gap). The model was validated against rh10's direct integral on the same pair it measured: the ODE collides γ₄, γ₅ at λ* = −0.98899, against −0.9885 computed from Ξ itself — agreement to 0.1%, comfortably inside the experiment's stated 8% acceptance. For a tight pair the dynamics reduces to a one-line law: keeping only the mutual term, dg/dτ = −4/g gives τ* = g₀²/8 — the collision time is an eighth of the squared gap.

The three tightest pairs in range collide at λ* = −0.00119, −0.00330, −0.00486 — the best is three decades past rh10's reading, and every collision lands on the g²/8 law to 1%. That law is the whole economics of the hunt: the computed bound is set by the tightest pair you have found, quadratically. Lehmer's pair near t = 7005 (gap ≈ 0.0005 of mean spacing) gave CSV 1994 their −4.379×10⁻⁶; nine further decades of the ladder — Odlyzko's −2.7×10⁻⁹, Saouter–Gourdon–Demichel's −1.145×10⁻¹¹ — were bought with ever-rarer pairs at ever-greater height, each rung a Lehmer pair harvested from a larger table. (Our λ* values are measurements of the validated dynamical model; the ladder's entries are rigorous bounds by construction, which is why they are cited beside our points rather than reproduced.)

Two readings. The optimistic one: the corridor is now [−0.00119, 0] as measured here, and nothing but table size stands between this method and more decades — the small-gap tail of GUE guarantees tighter pairs exist forever. The sober one: that is exactly why computation cannot finish the job. The bound improves quadratically in the rarest gap found, the rarest gap improves only logarithmically in table size, and the wall itself was not reached by climbing — Rodgers–Tao proved Λ ≥ 0 by a global argument about the flow, not by exhibiting pairs. The remaining half, Λ ≤ 0, is RH. The thermometer's needle can be pushed toward the wall from below at documented cost; touching the wall was a theorem, and crossing to it from the other side would be the theorem.

Part III · The base

4 · Dig three — counting over 𝔽₁

The base rh9 asked for has a candidate with a sixty-year pedigree and a paradoxical name: the field with one element, 𝔽₁ — Tits' 1957 observation that the formulas of linear algebra over 𝔽q keep making sense at q = 1, where they stop describing vector spaces and start describing sets. There is no field with one element; 𝔽₁ is the name of the limit q → 1, and the limit computes. Everything below is an actual limit evaluated at q = 1+ε with Richardson extrapolation, checked against its exact integer target.

The q-integer [n]q = 1+q+⋯+qn−1 counts the points of Pn−1(𝔽q); at q → 1 it is n: projective space over 𝔽₁ is a finite set of points ([3]q → 3, verified to 10⁻¹²). The q-binomial [4 2]q counts planes in 𝔽q⁴; its limit is 6 = the number of 2-element subsets of a 4-element set: Grassmannians over 𝔽₁ are subset lattices. And the group count #GLn(𝔽q) = qn(n−1)/2(q−1)n[n]q! (verified as an exact integer identity at q = 2, 3), normalised by its torus-and-unipotent factor, limits to [n]₁! = n! —

(2) GLn(𝔽₁) = Sn :   #GL₃/(q−1)³q³ → 3! = 6,  #GL₄/(q−1)⁴q⁶ → 4! = 24.

The symmetric group — rh5 §5's clock world, the permutation substrate the arc has used since its first factorisation — is the general linear group of the missing base. That identification is not decoration; it is the first hard evidence that "geometry over 𝔽₁" is the right name for structures the arc already possesses.

Then the decisive computation, due in concept to Soulé (2004): a variety X counted over every 𝔽q has a zeta function Z(X/𝔽q, q−s), and its 𝔽₁-zeta is the normalised limit ζX/𝔽₁(s) = limq→1(q−1)N(1) Z(X/𝔽q, q−s). Computed:

(3) ζP¹/𝔽₁(s) = 1s(s−1) ,    ζP²/𝔽₁(s) = 1s(s−1)(s−2) .

The factor s(s−1) — the polynomial prefactor that completes ζ to ξ, the two poles the arc has carried since rh4 — emerges from pure counting over the one-element field. The completed ζ's shape is what a curve over 𝔽₁ would answer if asked how many points it has. That is the strongest sense in which the base question is well-posed: the language produces, from first principles, the exact objects the analytic theory already needed.

5 · Spec ℤ over 𝔽₁: the determinant dream, computed

If Spec ℤ were honestly a curve over 𝔽₁, three things would follow, in rising order of strength: ζ would be a determinant over its spectrum; Spec ℤ would carry a Frobenius; and the numeral train would be the state space of a system whose symmetries are the arc's. Each has a computable shadow, and all three shadows check out. What does not follow — the fourth thing, the one that proves RH — is the subject of §6.

The determinant. Deninger's dream reads ξ(s) as a regularised determinant det(s − Θ) over a hypothetical cohomology. At computation's level the statement is the Hadamard product: ξ is determined by its zeros,

(4) ξ(s) = ξ(0)  ∏γ>0 [ 1 + s(s−1)¼+γ² ] ,

and with the new table the product is testable at scale: 1517 zero factors, the tail beyond T = 2000 supplied by the density integral (the rh10 constant, log-tail s(s−1)·∑γ>T1/(¼+γ²) with ∑γ>T ≈ 5.38×10⁻⁴). Against direct ξ — Euler–Maclaurin ζ times the Γ-factor — the product agrees to 1.1×10⁻⁸ across the whole interval σ ∈ [−1.5, 3], through both trivial-zero-free flanks and the central point ξ(½) = 0.49712…, and the factor at s = ½+iγ₁ vanishes identically to 10⁻¹². The spectrum is the function, eight digits deep. What is missing is only everything: an operator Θ whose eigenvalues those zeros are, acting on a space with a positive form — the determinant exists, the cohomology it would be the determinant of does not.

The Frobenius. Borger's proposal makes "descent to 𝔽₁" precise: an 𝔽₁-structure on a ring is a Λ-ring structure — a coherent family of commuting lifts of all the Frobenii x ↦ xp. On ℤ itself the lifts are forced to be the identity map, and the condition that the identity is a p-Frobenius lift is the congruence ψp(n) ≡ np (mod p) — that is, Fermat's little theorem is the statement that Spec ℤ descends to 𝔽₁. Verified on the grid: np ≡ n (mod p) for the first ten primes and n ≤ 200, two thousand congruences, no exception. The reading is quietly remarkable: arithmetic already carries all its Frobenii — the structure Weil's proof leans on exists over ℚ — but they act invisibly, with no cohomology to leave fingerprints on, no graph in a surface to self-intersect.

The gas. Bost–Connes (1995) built a quantum statistical system whose partition function is ζ: states indexed by the numerals, energies log n, so Tr e−βH = ∑n−β = ζ(β). Its symmetry group is Ẑ× — rh7 §5's profinite dial, the arc's own symmetry — and its internal energy is

(5) U(β) = −ζ′ζ(β) =  ∑n≥2 Λ(n) n−β :

the von Mangoldt weights — the gear weights the arc has summed since rh5 — are the energy of the gear gas. Verified both ways at β = 2 and 2½ (prime side summed to 10⁶ with PNT tail): agreement 3×10⁻¹¹. The pole at β = 1 is the system's phase transition — the same divergence that is the infinitude of primes — and below it the Gibbs state melts into the symmetry-broken phase whose structure is class field theory. The numeral train is not like a statistical system; it is one, and its thermodynamics is the prime-counting the arc started from.

The pattern across the three digs is the same and is worth stating once, plainly. In every direction the computable shadow of the missing proof exists and behaves exactly as the proof would require: the pairing stays positive as far as any window can read, the flow's corridor narrows to within 10⁻³ of the wall by pure table-work, and the 𝔽₁ grammar generates the completed ζ's shape, the Frobenii, and the right symmetry group on demand. What never materialises is the same object each time, seen from three angles: a finite-dimensional positive geometry — a space for the cone's quadratic form to be diagonal in, a mechanism forcing the corridor shut, an H¹ for Frobenius to act on with a Hodge index. rh9 §8 called it the base. The digs did not find it; they established, with numbers, that everything above it is already in place — the arch is built and self-consistent to eight digits, and it stands on a keystone that is not there.
