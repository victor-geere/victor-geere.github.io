rh36 · the vector

rh35 left two questions and a guess attached to each, and both guesses were wrong in the same instructive direction: the answers are not statistics of the gear set, they are statements about one vector. The threshold rh35 measured is not a scale but a level crossing. The vector that carries it — the one four documents have been bounding below without naming — is the zeta partial sum on the critical line, overlap 0.995 with n−1/2 and a fitted exponent that walks to 0.500. That names the object, explains the collapse, and says why rh32’s programme could not have worked.

1 · Where this stands

Five documents have now been spent on one matrix. rh32 proposed a quadrature lemma — that no admissible weight profile can drive the loss constant below some fixed fraction — and proved it exactly on the mirrored crystal. rh33 refuted it on the arc’s folded lattice. rh34 showed that refutation was an aliasing artifact of the fold and reopened the lemma on the unwrapped Gram. rh35 ran the search rh34 had not and refuted it there too. That is a complete line of attack, closed from the inside, and rh35 said so.

What rh35 could not say was why, in either half. It measured that displacing the gear rates by 0.006 in log doubles the floor, and looked for a length scale to explain a threshold no gap statistic could see. It measured that the whole attacking budget goes to log 2, and offered four readings of why without a way to choose between them. Both are answered here, and neither answer is a statistic. They are both facts about a single eigenvector.

2 · The instrument

rh35 §4 modulated the moment function by averaging two shifted copies of it, which is correct and expensive: every new frequency needs the whole zero table swept again for a fresh key set. The same object is reached by re-weighting the measure,

wγ → wγ(1 + a cos(v₀γ)),   nonnegative for |a| ≤ 1 by inspection,

because E is the same linear functional of the weight either way: ½(E(k+v₀) + E(k−v₀)) = 2∑γ wγ cos(kγ) cos(v₀γ). The cosines depend only on the key set, which is fixed by the gear set, so they are computed once and a re-weighting is one pass. The two forms agree to 1.9×10−12 of the mass, which is the roundoff of summing four thousand cosines two different ways. That is what makes a sweep affordable, and a sweep is what both of rh35’s questions turn out to need.

Two regressions fix the instrument against rh35 before it is used: the floor on the first twenty-four gear rates is 0.293898, and putting the whole budget on log 2 drives it to 0.101006 — the number six restarts of coordinate descent found there, reproduced in one line.

The vocabulary is rh34 §3’s and rh35’s as corrected. A gear is a level on the unit ladder, at depth n − 1. A gear rate is its log n. G is built on the rates.

3 · The threshold is a crossing

Slide the twenty-four gear rates towards equispaced by a fraction θ and watch not the floor but the bottom two levels.

The floor doubles from 0.2939 to 0.5893, as rh35 reported. But nothing deforms into anything. Hold the θ = 0 minimiser fixed and push it through the slide and its own Rayleigh quotient climbs monotonically from 0.2939 to 0.9888: the vector that carried the floor is not made a little dearer, it is destroyed. The floor that replaces it is a different vector — the two minimisers overlap 0.088, near enough orthogonal — and the height it lands at was already sitting in the matrix at θ = 0 as the second eigenvalue, 0.5727 against the 0.5893 it arrives at, 2.9%.

So there is no length scale to find. There is a level that climbs, a level that does not, and an avoided crossing between them at θ = 0.007 where they pass within 0.0426. The “threshold” is where the climbing level overtakes the stationary one, and its height is read off the unperturbed spectrum.

A prediction of mine, refuted. Before measuring the crossing I expected the threshold to be the reciprocal of the measure’s reach — a displacement is invisible until δ·γ ≈ 1 — which predicts that δ×γ is constant across windows. Over five windows γ runs from 91 to 1099, a factor 12.1, and the product runs 0.459 to 2.229, a factor 4.9. It is not the bandwidth. It is the crossing, and a crossing is a property of the matrix rather than of the measure.

4 · The vector

Which raises the only question that matters: what is the vector? It is worth saying first what a vector means here. G’s quadratic form is ∑γ ĥ(γ)² |∑n cn niγ|², so a unit vector c is the coefficient list of a Dirichlet polynomial and its Rayleigh quotient is that polynomial’s weighted mean square over the zeros. The minimiser is whichever Dirichlet polynomial comes closest to vanishing on them.

Every coefficient has the same sign, the mass sits at the shallow end of the ladder — gears 1 to 4 carry 0.517 of it where an even spread would give 0.167 — and the overlap with n−1/2 is 0.99495. Fitting the exponent instead of assuming it gives 0.455 at twenty-four gears and 0.500 at sixty-four; the overlap never leaves 0.9921 anywhere from four gears to eighty. The truncated zeta scores 0.29899 against a floor of 0.29390 — the exact minimiser beats it by 1.7% and never by more than 6.4% at any length tried.

The vector is ∑n≤N n−1/2+iγ, the zeta partial sum on the critical line. Every floor rh32 through rh35 measured is its weighted mean square over the zeros, divided by the harmonic number HN.

5 · Why the collapse is forced

HN is not an arbitrary normaliser: ∑1/n is exactly the mean square a random-phase Dirichlet polynomial with those coefficients would have. So the floor is a suppression factor — how far below chance the truncated zeta sits when it is read at the zeros of zeta. At twenty-four gears it is 3.34 times below; at sixty-four, 23.1; at eighty, 90.0.

Put that way the collapse rh34 measured and could not explain stops being mysterious and starts being obligatory. A partial sum shortened far below the length at which it can resolve a zero knows nothing about that zero, and sits near chance. Lengthen it past that point and it begins to see what it is standing on.

The length is measurable and it moves. Over four windows whose mean γ runs 91, 159, 331, 661 — a factor 7.3 — the half-floor point runs 9.15, 12.38, 18.28, 26.26. Fitting knee = C·(γ/2π)p gives p = 0.531, C = 2.221, with a residual of 0.3% in log. That is √(γ/2π), the balanced approximate functional equation’s length. The one-sided γ/2π is refuted outright: its constant would have to move by a factor 2.54 across the same four windows, where the square root holds to 6.3%.

Which settles the programme. A floor uniform in N would assert that the zeta partial sums cannot be small at the zeros. They are small at the zeros, from length ≈2.49√(γ/2π) onward, for the plainest reason available. rh32’s lemma was not merely unproven and then refuted twice; it was asking a bounded quantity to stay away from a value it is converging to.

6 · Why log 2

rh35 §7 (b) offered four readings of why the attacking budget goes entirely to log 2: it is the smallest prime, the largest rate gap, the shallowest level, and the first nonzero rate. One experiment kills all four. Take gear 2 out of the ladder. Then log 3 is the smallest prime, the largest gap, the shallowest level and the first nonzero rate, and log 2 is not a rate at all — and log 2 still wins, factor 0.465 against log 3’s 0.618. Start the ladder at gear 3, so there is no gear 1 to measure a first nonzero rate from, and log 2 wins by more: 0.317.

A fifth reading, and this one was mine: that the damage is just the moment function, whose spikes rh33 matched to the prime powers. It is not. E(log 7)/mass is −0.1937, half again the size of E(log 2)/mass at −0.1294, and log 7 does barely half the damage — 0.2528 against 0.1010. log 5, 11 and 13 all have larger moments than log 2 and all do less. Across the fifteen frequencies the moment carries no information about the damage whatever: correlation +0.06.

What survives is geometric. Shift the whole rate ladder by v₀ and some of it lands back on itself; on the integers the fraction that does is 1/2 at log 2, 1/3 at log 3, 1/m at log m. That fraction orders the damage at −0.883, on the same fifteen frequencies where the moment scores +0.06 and rh35’s rate-distance scores −0.03. It is an ordering rather than a formula — 25 of the 105 pairs invert, all of them in the tail where the fraction ties at one or two gears out of twenty-four — but the top four, log 2, log 3, log 4, log 6, come out in self-map order exactly.

The decisive test removes the arithmetic entirely. Build the ladder geometrically, ratio e0.12. Its own step maps twenty-three of twenty-four rates back onto it and takes the floor to a factor 0.010, where log 2’s best against the integers was 0.344. And log 2, which maps nothing back, does nothing here: factor 0.880. The integers’ winner and the geometric ladder’s winner have traded places. The control that fixes it is ratio 21/6, where log 2 is six steps of the ladder and maps eighteen of twenty-four back: log 2 recovers to 0.271 while log 3, still mapping nothing, stays at 0.816.

And the primes are the opposite extreme. No shift maps them onto themselves at all, since 2p is never prime, which is why they are simultaneously the best-conditioned of the five gear sets — floor 0.790 — and the least attackable, a best single-frequency factor of 0.596 against the geometric ladder’s 0.010.

7 · What this corrects in rh35

rh35 §5 read its two halves as pointing at opposite ends of one thread: the conditioning set where the rates crowd, at the deep end, and the leverage mounted where they are sparse, at the shallow end. The first half of that is wrong, and it was wrong for a reason worth naming. It rested on the minimum-gap statistic — the closest pair of rates lives at the deep end — which rh35 §3 had already refuted two sections earlier, by explicit counterexample, as a predictor of the floor. The section used a statistic the document had just discarded.

The vector settles it. The floor is carried at the shallow end: centroid depth 6.26 against 11.5 for an even spread, participation ratio 10.0 of a possible 24, half the mass on the first four gears. The deep end is where the floor goes after the slide has destroyed the shallow vector — centroid 18.92 at θ = 0.03, with 0.803 of the mass on the deepest four gears. rh35 read the damaged state as the natural one.

So both halves point the same way after all, and the corrected reading is tighter than the one it replaces: the conditioning is carried at the shallow end because that is where a truncated zeta puts its weight, and the leverage is mounted there because that is where a ladder’s self-similarity is densest. Doubling maps half the integers back; only one in twenty-four survives a shift by log 13.

8 · Where this leaves the conjunction

Nowhere new, and it is worth being exact about what has and has not changed. No bound here is uniform in anything, nothing is certified, and the nine certified points of rh30 §10 (c) are still nine.

What is different in kind is that this is the first document in the line that explains rather than measures. rh32 through rh35 measured a floor, argued about what set it, and closed a lemma from the inside without ever naming the object they were bounding. It has a name: the zeta partial sum on the critical line, read at the zeros. Once it is named, the floor’s collapse is not a datum to be fitted but a consequence, and its length is the functional equation’s.

That is a negative result about the arc’s own method, and it is better to hold it clearly than to keep it vague. Any programme that tries to bound λmin of this Gram matrix away from zero, uniformly in the ladder length, is asking for something false. The lever will have to be somewhere else. The frame — the helix, the gears, the rates — is undamaged, and rather more useful now that it is known what lives on it; what is dead is one particular thing to try to prove about it.

9 · What this document leaves

(a) The suppression factor, not the floor.
HN makes the floor a ratio to chance, and the ratio is what carries the arithmetic: 3.34 at N = 24, 90.0 at N = 80. That is a statement about how much cancellation the zeros force in a zeta partial sum. It is not a bound and it is not uniform, but it is the quantity that was actually being measured for five documents, and it is worth a document of its own — how the suppression grows with N, whether the growth rate is uniform in the window, and what it looks like for Dirichlet polynomials that are not n−1/2.
(b) Self-similarity as the design variable.
§6 says the attackable ladders are the self-similar ones and the primes are the extreme case with no self-map at all. That is a statement about which gear sets are well-conditioned, and it is testable further: ladders interpolating between geometric and prime should interpolate in both the floor and the best attack. If the floor is monotone in a self-similarity index, that index is the statistic rh35 §3 went looking for and did not find.
(c) The exponent.
The fitted collapse exponent is 0.531 over four windows, not 0.500, at a residual of 0.3% — tight enough that the 6% excess is probably real rather than noise. Four windows is a thin fit. More windows, and a wider span in γ, would say whether the excess is a genuine correction or an artifact of measuring the knee by half-height.
(d) The certified set stays closed.
Nine, unchanged, renewed once more.
