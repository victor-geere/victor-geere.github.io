# Theorem (Rodgers–Tao, 2018; upper bound Polymath15, 2019)

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh4.html`](../../html/rh4.html) — §11 "The canonical deformation: Λ = 0 as exact criticality", theorem box at lines 561–564

## Statement

> Λ ≥ 0. Combined with de Bruijn and with the current upper bound: 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.22, and RH ⟺ Λ = 0.

## Hypotheses and notation

- Ξ(<i>z</i>) = ∫ Φ(<i>u</i>) cos(<i>zu</i>) d<i>u</i> is the completed, symmetrized helix written as a Fourier transform.
- The canonical heat-flow deformation: <i>H</i><sub>τ</sub>(<i>z</i>) = ∫<sub>0</sub><sup>∞</sup> <i>e</i><sup>τ<i>u</i>²</sup> Φ(<i>u</i>) cos(<i>zu</i>) d<i>u</i>, with <i>H</i><sub>0</sub> = Ξ.
- Λ is the de Bruijn–Newman constant: de Bruijn (1950) proved that for τ ≥ ½ every closure is real, so there is a finite constant Λ with "the closures are all on the line iff τ ≥ Λ". RH is exactly Λ ≤ 0. Newman (1976) proved Λ &gt; −∞ and conjectured Λ ≥ 0.
- RH = Riemann Hypothesis.

## Proof / evidence

CITED: the source attributes Λ ≥ 0 to Rodgers–Tao (2018) and the upper bound to Polymath15 (2019), and gives no proof. It sketches the mechanism:

> if Λ were negative, the backward-flowed zeros would have to be too regularly spaced — more crystalline than the true zeros are permitted to be.

and names the Lehmer phenomenon as what forces it: near <i>t</i> ≈ 7005.06 two closures approach within 0.04 of each other, "<i>Z</i> grazing the axis so closely that RH survives by a hair's breadth".

## Dependencies and related results

- de Bruijn (1950): τ ≥ ½ ⟹ every closure is real.
- Newman (1976): Λ &gt; −∞, and the conjecture Λ ≥ 0.
- Hurwitz's theorem, invoked for the continuous motion of closures under the deformation ("zeros of analytic limits cannot appear or vanish silently").
- rh4 §11 reads the result in construction language: "If RH is true, the zeta gear train sits exactly at the critical parameter of its own canonical deformation family — not inside the rigid region but on its boundary."
