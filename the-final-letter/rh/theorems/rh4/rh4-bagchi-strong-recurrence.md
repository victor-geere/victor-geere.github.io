# Theorem (Bagchi)

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh4.html`](../../html/rh4.html) — §17 "Universality: the helix that can imitate anything", theorem box at lines 661–664

## Statement

> RH ⟺ ζ is <i>strongly recurrent</i> in the right half-strip: ζ can approximate <b>itself</b> — for every ε and every compact <i>K</i> ⊂ {½ &lt; σ &lt; 1}, the set of shifts τ with sup<sub><i>K</i></sub> |ζ(<i>s</i>+<i>i</i>τ) − ζ(<i>s</i>)| &lt; ε has positive lower density.

## Hypotheses and notation

- ζ is the Riemann zeta function; σ = Re <i>s</i>; τ is the real vertical shift.
- <i>K</i> ranges over compact subsets of the right half-strip {½ &lt; σ &lt; 1}; ε &gt; 0.
- "Positive lower density" is the density of the set of shifts τ, as stated.
- RH = Riemann Hypothesis.

## Proof / evidence

CITED: the source attributes the theorem to Bagchi (1981) ("Bagchi (1981) made the contact exact") and gives no proof. It also cites the result the theorem is built beside:

> Voronin (1975): for any compact set in the strip ½ &lt; σ &lt; 1 (with connected complement) and any continuous non-vanishing target function analytic in its interior, vertical shifts of ζ approximate the target uniformly to any precision — and do so with positive lower density in the shift.

The source explains the role of "non-vanishing" in Voronin's hypothesis via Rouché: an approximand with a zero would force ζ to have nearby zeros in the open strip.

## Dependencies and related results

- Voronin (1975) universality theorem (stated in §17, not boxed).
- Rouché's theorem, for the zero-avoidance reading.
- rh4 §18 lists "strong recurrence" among the exact restatements of RH in the dictionary.
- The source's construction reading: "RH is the statement that the gear train is a legal target of its own mimicry — self-similarity of the tail, elevated from aesthetic to criterion."

## Notes

- CITED: attributed to Bagchi (1981); no proof is given.
- The equivalence is quoted exactly: recurrence of ζ to itself, with positive lower density of the shifts.
