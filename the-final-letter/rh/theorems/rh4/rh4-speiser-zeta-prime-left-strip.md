# Theorem (Speiser)

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh4.html`](../../html/rh4.html) — §12 "Forced evolution, made precise", theorem box at lines 574–577

## Statement

> RH ⟺ ζ′(<i>s</i>) ≠ 0 in the open left half-strip 0 &lt; σ &lt; ½.

## Hypotheses and notation

- ζ is the Riemann zeta function, ζ′ its derivative, and σ = Re <i>s</i>.
- The open left half-strip is 0 &lt; σ &lt; ½ — left of the critical line, inside the critical strip.
- RH = Riemann Hypothesis.

## Proof / evidence

CITED: the source attributes the theorem to A. Speiser (1935) ("the honest fixed-train statement is Speiser's") and gives no proof. It supplies the geometric reading:

> the flattened helix family has <i>no stationary point of its shape</i> anywhere left of the line. A critical point of ζ in the left half-strip would be a place where the helix pauses — where an infinitesimal change of <i>s</i> produces no first-order change in the value — and Speiser says such a pause is <i>equivalent</i> to a failed RH.

The source adds, parenthetically:

> (On the line, the pairing is exact and proved conditionally: between consecutive closures <i>Z</i>′ vanishes once, zeros of ζ′ interlace, and the flow never stalls to the left.)

## Dependencies and related results

- rh4 §12 first states the fixed-train isolation fact: η is analytic and not identically zero, so its zeros are isolated points of the plane, by the identity theorem, before any axiom is invoked.
- The section contrasts Speiser's fixed-train statement with the deformation results of §11 (de Bruijn–Newman, Λ = 0).

## Notes

- CITED: attributed to Speiser (1935); no proof is given in the source.
- The statement is an equivalence and is quoted exactly, including the open half-strip.
