# The notch law cannot tell which process it is looking at

**Kind:** Claim
**Status:** VERIFIED (numerical; evidence absent; the source is prose only, no committed evaluator)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh16.html`](../../html/rh16.html) — §4 "Which readings were ever about ζ"

## Statement

> rh15 argued that the bottom eigenvalue of a slice measures the sampling grid — the hat transform's null at r = 2π/Δ — and not the arithmetic. The argument was internal: an energy decomposition and a parameter-free law. It can be settled from outside. Build point processes with the same mean density (1/2π)log(r/2π) as the zeros and no arithmetic whatever — Poisson (exponential gaps), GUE-like (Wigner-surmise gaps), and perfectly rigid (equal gaps), all generated by unfolding through θ(r)/π with a fixed seed — and run the identical readings on each.

and the outcome:

> measured λ<sub>min</sub> divided by rh15's parameter-free notch law … The scatter across all four is 11.1×; ζ alone scattered 11.6× across rh15's own ten slices. The law fits a Poisson process exactly as well as it fits the Riemann zeros — it does not know which process it is looking at, because it was never looking at the process.

## Hypotheses and notation

- Surrogates have the same mean density as the zeros, (1/2π)log(r/2π), and contain no arithmetic: Poisson (exponential gaps), GUE-like (Wigner-surmise gaps), perfectly rigid (equal gaps), generated by unfolding through θ(r)/π with a fixed seed.
- The law being tested is rh15's parameter-free notch law, λ<sub>min</sub> ≈ (Δ/16π⁴)·(ζ(4)ln(1/Δ) − ζ′(4))/π · λ<sub>min</sub>(R<sub>n</sub>), with the clamped-rod constant κ₁ = 4.730040…
- Scatter is measured as the spread of the ratio (measured λ<sub>min</sub>)/(law) across the runs.

## Proof / evidence

The source reports the comparison: the law's scatter across the four processes is 11.1×, against 11.6× for ζ alone across rh15's own ten slices. The source's reading: "That closes rh15's argument from the other side. The eigenvalue was the instrument; the Schur margin is the measurement."
