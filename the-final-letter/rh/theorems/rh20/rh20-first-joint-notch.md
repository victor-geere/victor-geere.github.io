# The first joint notch of two rational meshes

**Kind:** Lemma
**Status:** PROVED (paper argument, elementary)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh20.html`](../../html/rh20.html) — §2 "Incommensurability, for meshes that are all rational", equation (1)

## Statement

> Every mesh in this arc has been an exact decimal, so every ratio θ = Δ₂/Δ₁ is rational, and two rational meshes always share notches: writing θ = p/q in lowest terms, the ladders N(Δ₁) = (2π/Δ₁)ℤ and N(Δ₂) = (2π/Δ₂)ℤ coincide first at

> (1)  r<sub>joint</sub> = 2πq/Δ₁,

> and nowhere below it.

## Hypotheses and notation

- The notch ladder of a mesh Δ is N(Δ) = (2π/Δ)ℤ, the zero set of B<sub>Δ</sub>.
- θ = Δ₂/Δ₁ = p/q in lowest terms, with Δ₁, Δ₂ exact decimals (hence rational), so θ is rational.
- r<sub>joint</sub> is the height of the first exact joint notch of the two ladders.
- The source's computable criterion: "whether r<sub>joint</sub> lies above the horizon — above the R₀ at which the archimedean integral is cut, and above the heights of the zeros that carry weight".

## Proof / evidence

The source states the identity and the "and nowhere below it" claim without a separate proof; illustrations given: at θ = 1/2 the first joint notch is at 52.4 and at θ = 2/3 it is 78.5, both inside R₀ = 600; Δ₂ = 0.1483 against Δ₁ = 0.24 (θ = 1483/2400) puts it at 6.28×10⁴, "and each further decimal multiplies that by ten. Rationality is not the obstacle; small denominators are." The source's commentary: "This is the first thing the question needed and did not have: a version of 'incommensurable' that a certificate can check."
