# The Davenport–Heilbronn counterexample to functional-equation-alone confinement

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature)
**Area:** workspace
**Source:** [`qa.md`](../../research/various/qa.md) — Q8, lines 248–254; listed as "Rigorous, classical" in the Part I "Notes on rigor", line 281

## Statement

> Ask the question concretely: take ζ(s), keep its functional equation (the symmetry ξ(s) = ξ(1 − s)) but *break* its Euler product, and see whether the zeros stay on the line. They do **not**. The Davenport–Heilbronn function satisfies a functional equation of essentially the same Riemann type, has infinitely many zeros in the strip — and has zeros **off** the critical line. So the critical-line confinement is not a generic consequence of the functional equation alone.

## Hypotheses and notation

- ξ(s) = ξ(1 − s) is the symmetry of the completed zeta function; the critical line is Re s = 1/2; the critical strip is 0 < Re s < 1.
- "Break the Euler product" means: retain a Riemann-type functional equation but drop the multiplicative (Euler product) structure of ζ.
- The Davenport–Heilbronn function is the classical Dirichlet-series counterexample built from a non-principal character, with a Riemann-type functional equation and no Euler product.
