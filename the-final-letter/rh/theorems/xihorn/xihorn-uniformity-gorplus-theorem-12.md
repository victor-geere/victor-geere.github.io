# [GOR⁺] Theorem 1.2 — RH_m(T) and d ≤ T² give hyperbolicity for n ≥ m

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature — Griffin, Ono, Rolen, Thorner, Tripp, Wagner, Adv. Math. **397** (2022) 108186)
**Area:** xi-horn

## Statement

> **[GOR⁺] Theorem 1.2.** *If RH_m(T) is true and d ≤ T², then J_{d,n}(X) is hyperbolic for all n ≥ m.*

## Hypotheses and notation

- RH_m(T) is the hypothesis of the statement, as named by the source; the source does not expand it beyond the use "If RH_m(T) is true and d ≤ T², then…".
- J_{d,n}(X) = Σ_{j=0}^{d} C(d,j)·γ(n+j)·X^j ([O'S] (1.3)); γ is the papers' γ(n) = the directory's a(n).
- Hyperbolic = all zeros real; d ≤ T² is the exchange rate between verified-zero height and Jensen degree.
- The statement appears in the file's map as the row "d ≤ 9×10²⁴ | all n ≥ 0 | [GOR⁺] Cor. 1.3 | RH verified to height T (Platt–Trudgian), d ≤ T²".

## Proof / evidence

The source quotes it without proof and states its structural role: "Theorem 1.2 is the only mechanism in either paper that converts knowledge *about zeros* into coverage *in d*, and its exchange rate is d ≤ T²: the corollary's 9×10²⁴ is Platt–Trudgian's T = 3 000 175 332 800, squared. Covering all d at that rate needs T = ∞ — i.e. the input is RH itself. That is the precise sense in which C1 is the whole difficulty and not a technical gap."

The source also records the paper's own remark on the converse: "[GOR⁺] Remark (3) says what lives there: a converse to their Theorem 1.2 is 'highly unlikely … unless one has hyperbolicity for all n ≥ m and all d ≥ 1', and Jensen polynomials 'are ultimately quite inefficient at detecting zeros that violate RH_n'."
