# Chasse 2013 and [GOR⁺] Corollary 1.3 — what is already known of hyperbolicity

**Kind:** Corollary
**Status:** CITED (literature — Chasse 2013 via Borcea–Brändén and Obreschkoff; [GOR⁺] Corollary 1.3 using Platt–Trudgian)
**Area:** xi-horn
**Source:** [`xi-horn/H_track.md`](../../xi-horn/H_track.md) — §"What the H track adds up to", item 4

## Statement

> **None of it is a route to RH**, and this is not a hedge. The criteria are
> sufficient conditions on finitely many (d, n); Chasse 2013 has d ≤ 2 × 10¹⁷
> for all n and [GOR⁺] Corollary 1.3 reaches 9 × 10²⁴. What the H track
> contributes is a measured account of *how expensive each criterion is to
> check*, which is a different question and the only one this directory can
> answer.

The fuller form of the same two results, as the source gives it elsewhere in the
workspace (TODO.md, entry E8):

> Chasse proved J_{d,n} hyperbolic for **all n ≥ 0 and d ≤ 2 × 10¹⁷** ([Cha13],
> via Borcea–Brändén and Obreschkoff); [GOR⁺] Corollary 1.3 extends that to
> **d ≤ 9 × 10²⁴** using Platt–Trudgian's verification of RH₀ to 3 × 10¹².

## Hypotheses and notation

- J_{d,n}(X) := Σ_{j=0}^{d} C(d,j)·γ(n+j)·X^j is the Jensen polynomial of ξ; hyperbolic = all zeros real.
- Chasse's range: all n ≥ 0 and d ≤ 2 × 10¹⁷; [GOR⁺] Corollary 1.3: d ≤ 9 × 10²⁴.
- The H track's own objects sit far inside these ranges — B1 at d ≤ 6, n ≤ 30; H3's onsets at d ≤ 1021.

## Proof / evidence

The source cites both results without proof. Its use of them is as the calibration of what the H track does *not* claim: "nothing here extends what is known about hyperbolicity".
