# [GOR⁺] Lemma 2.3 — the renormalised Jensen family has Hermite limit H_d(X/2)

**Kind:** Lemma
**Status:** CITED (literature — Griffin, Ono, Rolen, Thorner, Tripp, Wagner, Adv. Math. **397** (2022) 108186; statement recorded in the source's words, not quoted verbatim)
**Area:** xi-horn
**Source:** [`xi-horn/uniformity-in-the-jensen-degree-d.md`](../../xi-horn/uniformity-in-the-jensen-degree-d.md) — §3 "Where each exponent is manufactured"

## Statement

> Lemma 2.3 gives the limit exactly — the renormalised J̃_{d,n} equals H_d(X/2) up to a defect of degree ≤ d−3 — and Theorem 2.4 bounds the defect's coefficients A_{d,k}(n).

## Hypotheses and notation

- J̃_{d,n} is the renormalised Jensen polynomial: the source's context is "J_{d,n} is not a Hermite combination; it has to be renormalised into one."
- H_d is the Hermite polynomial; the limit is H_d(X/2), up to a defect of degree ≤ d−3.
- A_{d,k}(n) are the defect's coefficients, bounded by [GOR⁺] Theorem 2.4 (named in the source; its statement is not given there).

## Proof / evidence

The source states the lemma's content as [GOR⁺]'s, without reproducing the proof. Its role in the e^d accounting: "Lemma 2.3 gives the limit exactly … and Theorem 2.4 bounds the defect's coefficients A_{d,k}(n). Feeding those bounds into the Turán condition (their (2.9)) costs (2+16C²)^{d/3}: **the exponential is the price of the renormalisation error**, the distance between J and its Hermite limit at finite n, compounding across d coefficients."
