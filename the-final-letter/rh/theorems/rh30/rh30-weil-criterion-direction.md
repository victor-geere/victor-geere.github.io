# What that does to the logic: Weil's criterion

**Kind:** Theorem
**Status:** CITED (literature, Weil's criterion)
**Area:** rh-html
**Source:** [`rh30.html`](../../html/rh30.html) — §9, boxed statement at lines 1673–1682

## Statement

> Weil's criterion is an equivalence: RH holds if and only if the prime-side form is positive for every admissible window. The arc has been verifying instances of the direction that RH *implies*. A certificate that passes is a consequence of RH and adds nothing to a proof of it; only a certificate that *failed* would carry information, and it would refute RH rather than establish it. Nine certified points, nine balls around them, or nine thousand — the logical content is the same, and it is not cumulative.

## Hypotheses and notation

- "the prime-side form" is the quadratic form of the row the arc computes from the primes; "admissible window" is as in Weil's criterion.
- The equivalence is classical; the source quotes it as the rung on which §9's reading of the programme stands.

## Proof / evidence

The source states Weil's criterion without proof (it is classical) and draws the logical consequence. The consequence is supported by the measurement in [rh30-prime-side-row-positive-definite](rh30-prime-side-row-positive-definite.md): all tested instances are positive-definite, so no certificate has failed.
