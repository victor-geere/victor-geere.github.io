# (iii) THE 1/2 is not a convergence threshold

**Kind:** Claim
**Status:** CITED (literature, classical abscissa facts) — the interpretive conclusion is the source's own
**Area:** workspace
**Source:** [`round2_agentB.md`](../../research/various/round2_agentB.md) — §(iii) "THE 1/2", lines 39–43

## Statement

> Not forced by convergence. Σ n^{-s} converges for σ>1; conditional convergence of Σ n^{-it}
> is σ>0. The abscissa of ζ is 1, which is what convergence sees. σ=1/2 is the functional-equation
> axis and the abscissa of the Euler product's mirror — a symmetry, not a convergence threshold.
> Also Σ|μ(n)|n^{-s} = ζ(s)/ζ(2s) has abscissa 1 and its own "critical" line at 1/2.

## Hypotheses and notation

- σ = Re s and t = Im s; convergence of Σ n^{−s} is absolute for σ > 1 and conditional for the
  imaginary-axis series Σ n^{−it} as stated.
- "the abscissa of ζ is 1" refers to the abscissa of convergence of the Dirichlet series Σ n^{−s}, not to a zero of ζ.
- μ is the Möbius function.

## Proof / evidence

The source states the classical convergence/abscissa facts and the identity
Σ|μ(n)|n^{−s} = ζ(s)/ζ(2s) and draws the conclusion that σ = 1/2 is a symmetry axis rather than a
convergence threshold. No proof is given in the document.
