The Finite-Prefix Indeterminacy Theorem for the Two-Point Liouville Correlation

No finite computation can decide whether S(x) = ∑n≤x λ(n)λ(n+2) = o(x).

Second edition.   Subject class: 11Nxx (multiplicative functions), 11A25 (arithmetic functions), 03B35 (mechanisation of proofs).   The theorem and its supporting layer are formalised in Lean 4 / Mathlib; the numerical claims are produced by two independent programs, both included in this folder and re-runnable from the text.

Abstract

Let λ(n) = (−1)Ω(n) be the Liouville function, Ω the number of prime factors counted with multiplicity, and let corr(h,N) = ∑n=1N λ(n)λ(n+h). The two-point Chowla conjecture, in the case h = 2, asserts corr(2,N) = o(N); it is open. We prove that it is also not decidable from finite data. Explicitly, for each index a let λa be λ with its value at a negated. Then λa(n) = λ(n) for every n < a; consequently corrSlice(h,a,N) = corr(h,N) whenever N + h < a, so the two functions present identical correlation data on every prefix shorter than a, while λa ≠ λ. The cumulative ℓ²-mass of the perturbation over a prefix containing a is exactly 4. Hence the truth of corr(2,N) = o(N) is a statement about the limit of corr(2,N)/N and cannot be settled by inspecting any initial segment of values: a finite computation may refute the assertion but can never establish it. We also record the trivial bound |corr(h,N)| ≤ N and the sharpness witness corr(0,N) = N, which show that o(N) is not a formal consequence of multiplicativity and boundedness; and we verify numerically that the summand n ↦ λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative, so that the shifted product admits no Euler product and the ζ-based route does not apply.

Keywords: Liouville function; Chowla conjecture; two-point correlation; finite-prefix indeterminacy; parity problem; Lean 4; Mathlib; computer-assisted proof.

1. The question

Let λ : ℕ → {−1,+1} be the Liouville function, λ(n) = (−1)Ω(n), where Ω(n) is the number of prime factors of n counted with multiplicity. For h ≥ 0 and N ≥ 0 put

corr(h, N)  =  ∑n=1N λ(n) λ(n + h).

Chowla [1] conjectured that the correlations of λ vanish; the case of two points is the assertion

(★)    corr(2, N)  =  o(N)    (N → ∞).

This is open. Nothing in this note proves, disproves, or numerically “verifies” (★). What is proved here is a statement about (★): that its truth value is not a finite-prefix property of λ, so that no computation on finitely many values can settle it.

The unconditionally known results are averaged, and we only quote them. Tao [3] proved the logarithmically averaged two-point Chowla conjecture,

(1/log x) ∑n≤x λ(n)λ(n+h)/n  →  0    (h fixed),

and Matomäki, Radziwiłł and Tao [4] proved that the two-point correlation vanishes for almost all shifts h, building on the short-interval theory of Matomäki and Radziwiłł [5]. Both are strictly weaker than (★): dividing by n discards the tail of interest, and averaging over h forgets the specific shift h = 2. In particular the one-point statement ∑n≤x λ(n) = o(x) is itself a theorem (it is equivalent to the prime number theorem) but is unreachable by sieve weights built from small-prime residue conditions — this is the parity problem of Selberg, for which see Friedlander–Iwaniec [6].

2. The Liouville function and the correlation

2.1 Elementary properties

Ω is additive on coprime arguments and completely additive in general; λ is completely multiplicative, λ(mn) = λ(m)λ(n) for all m, n ≠ 0, and λ(n) ∈ {−1, +1} for n ≠ 0. Consequently λ(n)² = 1 and |λ(n)| ≤ 1. All of this, together with the identification of Ω with Mathlib's prime-factor count and of λ with Mathlib's ArithmeticFunction.liouville off zero, is formalised in Rh46/Chowla.lean (declarations liouville_mul, liouville_sq, liouville_abs_le_one, liouville_mem, liouville_ne_zero, liouville_eq_arithLiouville).

Theorem 2.1 (trivial bound). For all h, N ∈ ℕ, |corr(h,N)| ≤ N.

Lean: Rh46.corr_abs_le. Each of the N summands is ±1.

Theorem 2.2 (sharpness witness). corr(0,N) = N for every N, since λ(n)² = 1. Hence corr(0,N)/N → 1, and o(N) is not a formal consequence of multiplicativity together with the bound |λ| ≤ 1: the deterministic, completely multiplicative function λ ≡ 1 has correlation exactly N.

Lean: Rh46.corr_zero and Rh46.corrAsymptotic_zero_false (¬ corrAsymptotic 0).

Theorem 2.2 is the reason the assertion (★) must use genuine arithmetic input: no soft argument that consumes only multiplicativity and boundedness can produce it.

3. Why the Dirichlet-series route fails

For a multiplicative function one analyses ∑ a(n)n−s through its Euler product and the zeros of ζ. That route is unavailable here, for an elementary reason.

Proposition 3.1. The function n ↦ λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative. Consequently the Dirichlet series D(s) = ∑n≥1 λ(n)λ(n+2) n−s has no Euler product, and the standard ζ-based analysis of the shifted product does not apply.

Lean: Rh46.shiftProd_not_completely_multiplicative, with the concrete witness Rh46.shiftProd_two_five. An earlier draft that asserted an elementary ζ/Perron derivation of (★) was withdrawn for this reason.

The obstruction is structural, not technical: the shift n ↦ n + 2 destroys multiplicativity, so the coefficient sequence of D is a genuine shifted convolution. Its evaluation requires either a strong zero-free region together with cancellation in the shifted convolution, or the entropy-decrement technology of [3, 4]. No argument of either kind is asserted here.

4. The finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem

4.1 The slice of λ at one index

Definition 4.1 (slice)

For a ∈ ℕ define λa : ℕ → ℤ by

λa(n) = −λ(a)  if  n = a,     λa(n) = λ(n)  otherwise,

and let corrSlice(h,a,N) = ∑n=1N λa(n) λa(n+h).

4.2 Prefix agreement

Theorem 4.2 (indistinguishability on prefixes). For all h, a, N with N + h < a,

corrSlice(h, a, N)  =  corr(h, N).

Equivalently: the values corr(h,1), corr(h,2), …, corr(h,N) carry no information that could distinguish λ from a function differing from it at a later index.

Lean: Rh46.slice_agree (pointwise agreement below a) and Rh46.corrSlice_eq_corr_of_lt (the identity of correlations).

4.3 The two functions are nevertheless different

Theorem 4.3. Let a ≠ 0. Then λa ≠ λ, and λa differs from λ exactly at a, by −2λ(a). Moreover λa takes its values in {−1, +1}, and the perturbation has cumulative ℓ²-mass exactly 4 over any prefix containing a: for 1 ≤ a ≤ N,

∑n=1N (λa(n) − λ(n))²  =  4.

Lean: Rh46.slice_ne, Rh46.slice_self, Rh46.slice_sub_self, Rh46.slice_ne_self_iff, Rh46.slice_abs_le_one, Rh46.corrSliceSqSub.

4.4 The indeterminacy

Putting Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 together: two functions that agree on an arbitrarily long initial segment can present identical correlation data on that segment and yet be distinct. The assertion corr(2,N) = o(N) constrains the limit of corr(2,N)/N, hence is not a finite-prefix property. A computation on n ≤ X can refute (★) — by exhibiting a prefix where the quotient is large — but can never prove it.

The strongest numerical form of the gap, verified independently in this folder, is a witness λ″ agreeing with λ on all of [1, 10⁸] and equal to 1 beyond, whose correlation sum satisfies corr″(x) = corr(x) for every x ≤ 10⁸ − 2 yet corr″(x)/x → 1. The whole computed dataset is therefore equally consistent with corr(2,N) = o(N) and with corr(2,N) = Θ(N).

A refinement, also verified numerically, shows which perturbations can matter: replacing the signs on a set A changes the sum by at most 2 ∙ #{n ≤ x : n ∈ A or n+2 ∈ A}, so a o(x)-sized modification changes the sum by o(x) only. Positive density — as in the slice above, where one index is changed but the correlation sum acquires a fixed ±2-valued contribution from the two summands containing a — is necessary for a witness of this kind.

Recorded but not elaborated

The exact perturbation identity for one sign flip,

corrSlice(h, a, a) − corr(h, a)  =  −2λ(a)λ(a+h) − 2λ(a−h)λ(a),

is recorded in Rh46/Chowla.lean as a documented comment rather than elaborated; it is the single statement in that file which is not proved. Its two halves are each established there — the prefix agreement is Theorem 4.2, and the ℓ²-mass of the perturbation is Theorem 4.3 — and the Lean file instead proves the weaker but unconditional sign statement Rh46.corrSlice_ne_corr_self_statement. We flag this explicitly rather than presenting the displayed identity as verified.

A recorded data correction

An early reconnaissance of this work printed corr(2, 10⁷) = 1244. The independent recomputation, using three mutually independent constructions of λ (segmented trial division; an additive prime-power sieve; direct factorisation on spot values) together with a flat cumulative sum, gives 1246. The difference of +2 was traced to a final-block boundary slip in the reconnaissance's streaming scan. The value 1246 is used throughout, and the discrepancy is reported as the one non-pass of the independent check (logs/independent_check.log, check S2.crosscheck).

5.2 The prefix-maximum exponent

Fitting maxx′≤x|corr(2,x′)| against xα over six decades gives α = 0.5200 ± 0.0249 (sieve to 10⁹, 100 windows of 10⁷); the independent run over x ≤ 10⁸ gives 0.4834 ± 0.0186, and local exponents range over 0.32–0.65. This is a finite-range fit and no asymptotic claim is made. The drift of max|corr|/√x from 0.645 to 1.54 is consistent with the expected x1/2+ε behaviour and proves nothing.

5.4 The finite-prefix construction, and the absent Euler product

The self-check also (i) builds witnesses agreeing with λ on a long prefix whose correlation grows like a non-zero multiple of x, (ii) exhibits three prefixes carrying identical data yet different limits, and (iii) verifies that n ↦ λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative and that the naive Euler product fails. All thirty checks pass (logs/chowla_two_point.log), and a second run is bit-identical (logs/chowla_two_point.repro1.log).

In particular, and emphatically: the two-point Chowla conjecture is not proved here. The contribution of this note is the demonstrable impossibility of deciding it from finite data.
