The qrime programme: what was actually achieved

A twin-prime analogue over 𝔽q[x], attacked until the problem split into one finite-field inequality, one character-sum identity, and one machine-checked impossibility theorem. This document reports each of those pieces at its true status — proved, Lean-certified, numerically verified, or still open — and explains what the whole structure is worth.

This is not a proof of the twin prime conjecture — not over ℚ, and not over 𝔽q[x] either. It is a harvest of the programme's map: what is proved, what is computed, what is guessed, and where the single remaining gap sits. Speculative items are relabelled probes throughout; the sources call them “wild guesses”, and the change of word is deliberate.

Abstract

The programme studies the twin-prime problem in the polynomial model: over a finite field 𝔽q, “primes” become monic irreducibles (qrimes) and “twin primes” become pairs (f, f+2) of irreducibles. In that model the twin count is a theorem (Pollack; Bary-Soroker–Stix), so the interest shifts: which statistics can each tool see?

Three results stand: (i) a separation theorem — the zeta function determines exactly the linear (one-point) statistics, and no function of it determines the twin count, proved with an explicit witness over 𝔽7 and formalised in Lean; (ii) a finite-field cap — for every quartic over 𝔽q, the reducible set has size at least (q+1)/2, proved unconditionally in the degenerate cases and reduced in the generic case to three standard theorems, with the algebraic links machine-checked; (iii) a parity theorem — every bounded-support divisor sum is periodic while the twin indicator is not, so no Selberg-type sieve weight equals the twin indicator, proved and Lean-checked. On the quantitative side, exact closed forms are obtained for the twin-qrime count at degrees 3 and 4 (including A = (3q²+2q+3)/16 and m = (a²+1)/2 for q = a²+b²), verified at 28 primes and identified with the quartic Jacobi sum J(χ₄,χ₄). The entire remaining arithmetic is one twisted-Parseval identity: verified exactly at 80 primes with its hypothesis sharpened to q ≡ 1 (mod 8), and still unproved. The programme's own verdict is retained here: the tools it built map a barrier precisely; they do not cross it.

1The research

The twin prime conjecture asks whether n and n+2 are both prime for infinitely many n. The programme takes the standard move of transplanting the question into 𝔽q[x], where “prime” means “monic irreducible”. The transplant has a cost and a benefit: the count becomes provable (so the experiment can run), while the method that proves it — two-dimensional equidistribution, i.e. Chebotarev for the pair (f, f+2) — is exactly what is missing over ℤ.

1.1 The dictionary

The transposition that defines the programme.
ℤ𝔽q[x]

p, p+2f, f+2 — twin qrimes
ζ(s) = Σ n−s1/(1 − q1−s)
log ζ coefficientqn = Σd|n d·I(d)

Two counting functions carry everything. I(n) is the number of qrimes of degree n; T(n) — written T(d;q,h) when the shift and degree need emphasis — is the number of monic degree-n polynomials f such that both f and f+h are irreducible. I is linear data (a sum of one-point indicators); T is bilinear (a sum of products of two one-point indicators at a related pair). That distinction is the whole programme.

1.2 The three targets

After the early rounds the programme fixed three targets, chosen so that a proof of all three would give a complete, auditable account of the barrier. The synthesis at round 106 states them as follows (round106-final-synthesis-v2.md).

Target A — the d = 4 cap

|(−Im g) ∪ Q| ≥ (q+1)/2 for every quartic g over 𝔽_q .

Equivalently: for every quartic g and every constant b, at most (q−1)/2 values of b make g+b reducible. This is a finite, self-contained statement about value sets of quartics — the part of the programme that stands on its own.

Target B — closed forms

Exact formulas for T(d;q,h) as a function of the shift class and q, and for the constants inside them. This is the quantitative heart: it says precisely how many twin qrimes there are, up to one integer.

Target C — the parity barrier

A theorem that the twin indicator lies outside the divisor-sum (Selberg-sieve) class, so that no sieve of that type can prove the twin prime conjecture. This is the “negative” target, and the programme treats it as the honest one.

1.3 Reading the labels

The single most useful thing a reader can carry into this material is the status ladder. The programme uses several different standards of evidence, and a claim's value depends entirely on which rung it is on.

Status labels used in this document and in the sources.
LabelMeansWhat it does not mean
Lean A Lean 4 / Mathlib theorem exists; the statement is machine-checked from the axioms. Nothing about the informal reading of the statement, which still has to be checked by eye.
Proved A complete human argument exists, with no gaps the author is aware of. It is not certified, and “modulo X” claims are not included here.
Verified Checked exactly by computer on a finite set of cases (often primes q). It is not a proof; a pattern at 28 primes is strong evidence, nothing more.
Open The statement is precisely formulated and unproved. —
Probe A speculative mechanism offered to explain a pattern; the sources' own name for the same register is replaced here (§4). It is not evidence of proof, and several probes in this programme have already been refuted.
Refuted Contradicted by an explicit computation or counterexample. —
Rule of thumb used throughout this harvest. A claim is reported at the weakest rung that honestly supports it. In particular: “proved modulo Ritt + Poincaré + the S₄ decomposition” is filed as conditional, not as proved; “verified at five primes” is filed as verified, not as a theorem — and now at 80 primes, still not a theorem; and a probe is never counted as a finding.

2Findings, with proofs

Seven findings follow. Each states the result, gives the argument where the argument is short enough to be reproduced, and records the evidence and the status. Proofs of the machine-checked algebraic steps are given in the programme's own notation; the Lean identifiers are cited so the certificate can be located.

Finding 1The zeta function cannot see the twin count

ProvedLean Over 𝔽q[x], the zeta function determines exactly the degree counts of a set of qrimes, and no function of those counts determines the twin count. This is the programme's cleanest structural result, and it is a statement about information, not about primes.

Lemma 1.1 (Euler–Möbius)

For a set S of monic polynomials with Z(n) = #{f ∈ S : deg f = n} finite, define ζS(T) = ∏d≥1(1−Td)−Z(d). Then

log ζ_S(T) = Σ_n (1/n) ( Σ_{d|n} d·Z(d) ) T^n ,

so ζS and Z(S) determine each other. For the true world S = Irrq, ζS = 1/(1−qT), giving qn = Σd|n d·I(d) and, by Möbius inversion, I(n) = (1/n) Σd|n μ(d) qn/d.

Proof

Expand the logarithm of the Euler product: log ∏d(1−Td)−Z(d) = Σd Z(d)·(−log(1−Td)) = Σd Z(d) Σk≥1 Tdk/k. Collect the coefficient of Tn: the pairs (d,k) with dk = n contribute Σd|n Z(d)/ (n/d) = (1/n) Σd|n d·Z(d). This is a bijection between (Z(d))d≥1 and the logarithmic derivative of ζS, hence between Z(S) and ζS; the Möbius form follows by inverting qn = Σd|n d·I(d). ∎

A curve's Frobenius eigenvalues likewise determine the point counts #C(𝔽qn) = Σd|n d·ZC(d), so the zeros also see only linear data. The decisive case is ℙ¹: its zeta is 1/((1−T)(1−qT)), whose numerator is 1 — there are no zeros at all, yet the twin count is positive (over 𝔽3 at n = 3 there is 1 such pair; over 𝔽7 at n = 2 there are 7). A function with no zeros cannot be encoding a positive count that varies.

Theorem 1.2 (separation)

Quantify over admissible worlds: sets S ⊆ Irrq with a degree function. Then there is no Φ such that B(S) = Φ(Z(S)) for all admissible worlds, where B(S) = #{f ∈ S : f+2 ∈ S}.

Proof (explicit witness over 𝔽7, degree 2)

All eight degree-2 monic polynomials over 𝔽7 are irreducible. Take

S = { x²+1 , x²+3x+1 , x²+4x+1 , x²+3x+6 } B(S) = 1 T = { x²+1 , x²+3x+1 , x²+4x+1 , x²+2 } B(T) = 0

Both are sets of qrimes with the same degree profile Z = (0,4,0,…), hence the same zeta function. The twin counts differ: in S the only element whose shift lands back in the set is x²+3x+6 (its shift is x²+3x+1), while in T the shift of x²+2 is x²+4, which is reducible because 4 = 2² is a square. Hence B(S) ≠ B(T) while Z(S) = Z(T), so no Φ exists. ∎

Lean: Separation.no_zeta_observable, Separation.separation_F7, Separation.witness_irreducible. Numerics: checks/round3.py §[C] tabulates the twin count on fibres of Z.

Two warnings attach to this theorem, and the programme is explicit about both. First, it concerns the indicator, not the count: it does not say twins are infinite, only that this channel of information is insufficient. Second, the theorem is about Φ depending on the zeta alone; a proof may use the pair structure directly — which is exactly what the function-field theory does, via Chebotarev for (f, f+2).

What the zeta is missing is 2-dimensional equidistribution. Over 𝔽q[x] it is available (the Frobenius gives a Galois action on the splitting field of f·(f+2)); over ℤ there is no Frobenius and no Chebotarev, and the two-point equidistribution is precisely the twin prime conjecture. This is the structural reason a function-field proof exists and a ℤ proof does not follow from it.

Finding 2The d = 4 cap: a proved inequality about quartics

ProvedLean (algebraic links and finite base) For every quartic g(x) = x⁴+a₃x³+a₂x²+a₁x+b over an odd 𝔽q and every b, the reducible set R(g) satisfies

|R(g)| ≥ (q+1)/2 , equivalently I(a) = q − |Im(a) ∪ Q(a)| ≤ (q−1)/2 .

Here I(a) counts the b making ga+b reducible, Im(a) is the value set of ga = x⁴+a₃x³+a₂x²+a₁x, and Q(a) is the set of b for which ga+b has a quadratic factor. The identity I = q − |Im ∪ Q| is the observation that a quartic is reducible exactly when it has a linear or a quadratic factor.

The proof is a four-step reduction. Steps (1)–(3) and the finite base are machine-checked; step (4) invokes three standard theorems.

(1) Translation to the depressed quartic

The substitution x ↦ x − a₃/4 is the algebra automorphism algEquivAevalXAddC (−a₃/4) of 𝔽q[x], hence preserves irreducibility and permutes the family {g+b}b. So it suffices to treat g = x⁴+a₂x²+a₁x. Lean: Shift.irreducible_shift.

(2) The reducible set is a union

Factor a reducible quartic as (x²+px+q)(x²−px+s) — always possible for a depressed quartic whose quadratic factors have no cubic terms. Matching coefficients gives

s + q − p² = a₂ , p(s − q) = a₁ , qs = b .
Proof of the union decomposition

If p ≠ 0, the second relation gives s = q + a₁/p; adding 2q = a₂ + p² − a₁/p gives q = (a₂+p²−a₁/p)/2, s = (a₂+p²+a₁/p)/2, and therefore b = qs = ((a₂+p²)² − (a₁/p)²)/4. If p = 0, the second relation forces a₁ = 0 and gives b = q(a₂−q). Finally the linear-factor case gives b = −g(x). Hence R = (−Im g) ∪ Q with Q = Qp≠0 ∪ (a₁=0 ? {q(a₂−q)} : ∅). ∎

Verified for all quartics at q = 11,13,29,37. Lean: Factorization.quadratic_factor, Factorization.even_factor.

(3) The composition case a₁ = 0: the cap holds exactly

Here g = x⁴+a₂x² = h(x²). Put q0 = x²+a₂. Then for every x,

−g(x) = −x²(x²+a₂) = q₀·(a₂ − q₀) ∈ Q₀ ,

so −Im g ⊆ Q and R = Q. Lean: Composition.neg_image_mem_Q. The size of Q splits into two cases.

  • a₂ = 0: Q = −squares ∪ (fourth powers)/4. If q ≡ 1 (mod 4) then −1 is a square, so Q = squares and |Q| = (q+1)/2; if q ≡ 3 (mod 4) the fourth powers coincide with the squares and Q = 𝔽q. Lean: NegSquare.neg_sq_mem_sq.
  • a₂ ≠ 0:
    |Q| = |Q₀| + |Q₁ \ Q₀| = (q+1)/2 + ⌊(q−1)/4⌋ = q − ⌊(q+2)/4⌋ .
    The first term is the quadratic image, of size (q+1)/2. The new part is an orbit count: the involution σ(u) = −u−2a₂ preserves the value ((a₂+u)/2)², and its character argument −(2a₂+σ(u)) = u never has value −1 when u is a square, so the count of remaining square classes is (N−Σ)/2 − ½·1χ(−2a₂)=1 = ⌊(q−1)/4⌋, with the character sum Σ = −(χ(−1)+χ(−2a₂))/2. Lean: QSize.size_identity, QOrbit.sigma_y, QOrbit.sigma_arg.

(4) The generic case a₁ ≠ 0

This is where the three cited theorems enter. The quartic is not a composition (else a₁ = 0), so by Ritt, in the form of Guralnick–Saxl, its monodromy is primitive. The primitive transitive subgroups of S₄ are A₄ and S₄, with fibre-size ratios 3/4 and 5/8; hence |Im|/q → ≥ 5/8. In the S₄ case the indicator 1k≥1 is expanded in the class functions of S₄:

1_{k≥1} = (5/8)·1 + (1/8)·sign + (3/8)·std − (1/8)·(std⊗sign) − (1/4)·χ₂ ,

(here χ₂ is the two-dimensional irreducible of S₄, not the quadratic character of 𝔽q). Since Σyχstd = Σy(N(y)−1) = q−q = 0, the three non-trivial class sums give

|Im g| = (5/8)q + (1/8)( S_sign − S_{std⊗sign} ) + O(1) .

Both sums are traces of abelian varieties. S_sign = Σyχ₂(Δ(y)) with Δ the cubic discriminant, which equals −aE for the elliptic curve E : t² = Δ(y). The pullback identity Δ(g(x)) = g′(x)²·S(x) with

S(x) = −16x⁶ − 32a₂x⁴ − 40a₁x³ − 20a₂²x² − 36a₁a₂x − 27a₁² − 4a₂³

makes Sstd⊗sign = SC − S_sign with SC = Σxχ₂(S(x)). The genus-2 curve C : s² = S(x) maps to E by (x,s) ↦ (g(x), g′(x)s), so by Poincaré reducibility Jac(C) ~ E × E′ and Sstd⊗sign = ±1 − aE′. The Weil bound |aE| ≤ 2√q then gives

|S_sign| ≤ 2√q , |S_{std⊗sign}| ≤ 2√q + 1 , |Im g| ≥ (5/8)q − (1/2)√q − O(1) .

Finally (5/8)q − (1/2)√q − 2 ≥ (q+1)/2 exactly when q − 4√q − 20 ≥ 0, i.e. for q ≥ 49. The range q < 49 is discharged finitely: q = 5,…,23 by the Lean decide theorems SmallCap.cap_*, and q = 29,31,37,41,43,47 by Python checks.

Status. Steps (1)–(3), the finite base, and the orbit step are Lean-certified. Step (4) is a correct-in-outline argument that depends on Ritt/Guralnick–Saxl, Poincaré reducibility, the Weil bound, and the S₄ class-function decomposition. None of those four is formalised, and the synthesis itself lists their formalisation as the remaining work for Target A. So the honest reading is: the cap is proved modulo three standard theorems plus one unformalised character-theoretic decomposition. It is the strongest result in the programme, and it is not yet a certificate.

Finding 3Closed forms for the twin-qrime count

ProvedVerified The twin count has an exact structural description at d = 3, 4: it is constant on d-th power classes, its amplitudes are a d-point discrete Fourier transform of integer class counts, and two of its constants have closed forms.

(a) Scaling and power classes

Proved The substitution f(x) = ad·g(x/a) is a bijection on monic degree-d polynomials with f + adh = ad(g(x/a) + h), and it preserves irreducibility. Hence

T(d; q, a^d h) = T(d; q, h) and T is constant on d-th power classes.

So the shift variable lives in 𝔽q×/(𝔽q×)d, a cyclic group of order d when d ∣ q−1, and T is a function on ℤ/d.

(b) The amplitudes are a DFT

Proved Writing Tk = T(d;q,uk) for a choice of class representatives uk, and Ac = Σk Tk ζdck for the d-point DFT,

d·T_k = Σ_{c=0}^{d−1} A_c ζ_d^{−ck} , c₄ = (1/4)[(A₀−A₂) − i(A₁−A₃)] .

The class counts Tk are integers, so each Ac lies in ℤ[ζd]; at d = 4 the assembled amplitude satisfies the parity integrality c₄/(2q) ∈ ℤ[i], which is proved. Lean: TwinEven.

(c) Divisibility by q

ProvedLean For d < q, q ∣ T(d;q,h).

Proof

Let σa : f(X) ↦ f(X+a) for a ∈ 𝔽q. This preserves monicity, degree, irreducibility, and the pair condition (f+h)(X+a) = f(X+a)+h, so it acts on the set counted by T. The action is free when d < q: a nonzero translation-invariant polynomial has degree at least q, the minimal example being Xq−X. A finite set carrying a free action of a group of order q has cardinality divisible by q. ∎

The freeness hypothesis is sharp: at d = q = 5, T(5;5,h) = 98 for every h ≠ 0, and 5 ∤ 98, because X5−X is translation-invariant. Verified for d = 2,…,6 and every odd prime q = 5,…,31 with d < q; no failures. Lean: the abstract engine is FreeOrbit.card_dvd_card_of_free_action (orbit–stabiliser plus the class formula).

Sourcing note. The round-91 note paraphrases the action as t·(f,g) = (f+t, g+t), i.e. translation of the value. That operation does not preserve irreducibility (e.g. x⁴+b is reducible for about half the b). The preserved operation, and the one the round-23 argument actually uses, is translation of the variable, f(X) ↦ f(X+a). The Lean file proves the abstract orbit-counting lemma, which is correct and reusable; the application step is the round-23 argument.

(d) The even symmetry

ProvedLean T(d;q,h) = T(d;q,−h).

Proof

For d ≥ 1 the map f ↦ f+h is a bijection of the monic degree-d polynomials (adding a constant preserves monicity and degree). It carries the property “f and f+h both irreducible” to “g = f+h and g−h = f both irreducible”, which is exactly the defining property of T(d;q,−h). Hence the two counts agree. ∎

Lean: TwinEven (including add_right_injective').

(e) The even-quartic irreducibility set, exactly

Proved This is the cleanest complete description in the programme.

x⁴ + a₂x² + b irreducible ⟺ χ₂(b) = −1 and χ₂(a₂²−4b) = −1 .
Proof

Write f = h(x²) with h(y) = y²+a₂y+b. (i) h is irreducible iff its discriminant is a non-square, χ₂(a₂²−4b) = −1. (ii) If h is irreducible with root r ∈ 𝔽q² ∖ 𝔽q, then f = h(x²) is irreducible iff r is not a square in 𝔽q². For r ≠ 0, both “r is a square in 𝔽q²” and “N(r) = b is a square in 𝔽q” are the single condition r(q²−1)/2 = 1, so they coincide. (iii) If h splits, f factors as two quadratics and is reducible. ∎

Counting with 1χ₂=−1 = (1−χ₂)/2,

|B(a₂)| = (1/4)[ q + Σ_b χ₂(b)χ₂(a₂²−4b) ] , |B(0)| = (q−1)/2 , |B(a₂)| = (q−1)/4 (a₂ ≠ 0) ,

since for a₂ ≠ 0 the standard quadratic sum gives Σbχ₂(b)χ₂(x−b) = −1 at the nonzero square x = a₂²/4. Verified at q = 17,41,73 for every a₂.

(f) The constant A

Proved With the sizes above, Parseval gives the closed form

Σ_{a₂}|B(a₂)| = (q²−1)/4 , S₀ = (q−1)²/4 + (q−1)³/16 , Σ_{c≠0} S_c = (q−1)A = q(q²−1)/4 − S₀ , A = (3q² + 2q + 3)/16 .

The step that makes it work is Σc≠0χ₂(c) = 0: the B-term drops out of the class sum. Verified exactly at seven primes (q = 17,41,73,89,97,113,137).

(h) The parity law, explicit

Proved (from the decomposition) verified exactly at seven primes.

E(h) = (q² − 2q − 7)/16 + ((a² + 1)/4)·χ₂(2h) T(h) ≡ E(h) (mod 2) .
Derivation

With Sc = A + Bχ₂(c), A = (3q²+2q+3)/16, B = m√q/2 = (a²+1)√q/4, and S₀ − A = q(q²−2q−7)/16, one has

E(h) = (S₀ − A)/q + (m/2)·χ₂(−h) = (q²−2q−7)/16 + ((a²+1)/4)·χ₂(2h) .

The two displayed forms agree because q ≡ 1 (mod 8) makes 2 a quadratic residue, so χ₂(2h) = χ₂(−h). ∎

All the m and E data above are at primes q ≡ 1 (mod 8); the closed form m = (a²+1)/2 presupposes the representation with a odd and b ≡ 0 mod 4 that this congruence guarantees. Stating the hypothesis matters: without it the two-square representation is not canonical.

Finding 4The single remaining identity

Verified exactly at 80 primesOpen Everything in Target B reduces to one identity about a quadratic exponential sum. Define

K(A,c) = Σ_w χ₂(A² − w²) ζ^{−cw/4} , H(a,c) = ζ^{−ca²/8}·K(A,c) , A = a²/2 .

K is real, and the two sums that carry the arithmetic are

Σ_A |K(A,c)|² = q² − 2q (clean, independent of c) Σ_A χ₂(A)·|K(A,c)|² = 2χ₂(c)·√q·Re J(χ₄,χ₄)² (the last identity)

The first is an orthogonality statement — K is, up to a Gauss factor, the Fourier transform of χ₂(A²−w²), and Parseval gives q² minus the two boundary terms. The second is the whole arithmetic content of the programme, and it is the one thing that is not proved. Its consequence is exact: since

Σ_a |H(a,c)|² = |K(0)|² + 2Σ_{A square ≠ 0}|K|² = q² − 2q + 2χ₂(c)√q·Re J(χ₄,χ₄)² ,

the identity would make Σa|H|² — and hence the whole c₄ chain and the parity law — unconditional.

Two stages of the reduction

Rounds 99–105 localise the difficulty twice, and both steps are worth recording because they are proved. First, expanding |T₂+T₃−T₄|² and summing over a₂:

bulk_c = (1/16)[ 2q² + Σ|H|² + 2χ₂(−1)χ₂(−c)q√q + 4q ] , Σ_{a₂}|H(a₂,c)|² = q² − 2q − 2χ₂(c)q√q + 4a²χ₂(c)√q , bulk_c = (3q² + 2q)/16 + (a²√q/4)·χ₂(c) .

So the entire a²-dependence of m lives in the single sum Σa₂|H(a₂,c)|²; every other term is a rational polynomial in q. That is a real theorem about where the arithmetic is hiding, and it is the reason the last gap can be stated as one line.

The falsification test, completed (019)

80 primes, exact The round-106 note left a cheap and sharp test: one more prime. In 019 the test was run exhaustively, and — more importantly — exactly. Two things came out of the test, and both sharpen the target rather than close it.

What is certified, and what is not

The one purely combinatorial step in the derivation is that “sum over the squares” is “sum over everything plus the χ₂-weighted sum”. That step is now a Lean theorem, for an arbitrary complex-valued function on ZMod p:

theorem Twist.twisted_parseval_step (f : ZMod p → ℂ) (h0 : f 0 = 0) : 2 * ∑ a, (if IsSquare a then f a else 0) = ∑ a, f a + ∑ a, (quadraticChar (ZMod p) a : ℂ) * f a

together with the pointwise form 2·1square = 1 + χ₂ and the sanity corollary that ZMod p has (p+1)/2 squares. All five declarations are elaborated by 019/lean/Twist.lean with only the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound).

What this certifies is that no arithmetic is hidden in the passage between the two sides — the identity's content is entirely in the character sum. What it does not certify is the character sum itself, which remains open. The green Lean file is a statement about the reduction, not about the identity.

What would close it

The programme's own probe (Probe 47, §4) is that the identity is the twisted Parseval for K: the untwisted sum is q²−2q, and twisting by χ₂ replaces the trivial character by χ₂, whose Fourier transform is the Jacobi sum. That is a plausible mechanism, not a proof. Concretely, a proof would go through a Salié/Kloosterman evaluation of Σa₂χ₂(a₂²−4v)χ₂(a₂²−4w) after completing the square — now with the χ₂(2) factor of the corrected form accounted for.

Finding 5The parity theorem: the twin indicator is not a divisor sum

ProvedLean This is the negative result that the programme treats as its honest core.

Lemma 5.1 (periodicity)

Let S be a finite set of divisors, all dividing L. Then for every g : ℕ → ℝ,

Σ_{d∈S, d|n} g(d) = Σ_{d∈S, d|n+L} g(d) .
Proof

For d ∈ S we have d ∣ L, so n+L ≡ n (mod d) and hence d ∣ n ⟺ d ∣ n+L. The two sums therefore have identical summands. ∎

A Selberg-type sieve weight n ↦ Σd|n, d≤Dλd is exactly of this form with S = {1,…,D} and L = lcm(1,…,D). Hence every bounded-support sieve weight is periodic. Lean: Periodic.divisor_sum_periodic.

Lemma 5.2 (the twin indicator is not periodic)

Proof

twinInd(3) = 1 because 3 and 5 are both prime. For any L ≥ 1, 3+3L = 3(1+L) is divisible by 3 and exceeds 3, hence composite, so twinInd(3+3L) = 0. If twinInd had period L, iterating the period would give twinInd(3) = twinInd(3+3L) — a contradiction. ∎

Lean: NonPeriodic.twinInd_not_periodic.

Theorem 5.3

¬ ∃ (S finite) (L ≥ 1) (∀ d∈S, d|L) (g : ℕ → ℝ), ∀ n, twinInd n = Σ_{d∈S, d|n} g(d) .
Proof

The right-hand side is L-periodic by Lemma 5.1; the left-hand side is not, by Lemma 5.2. ∎

Lean: NonPeriodic.twinInd_not_divisor_sum, on top of Periodic and DivisorSum (the latter also proves the multiplicative obstruction: a divisor sum ζ∗g with multiplicative g is multiplicative, and the twin indicator is not).

Scope correction. The synthesis states this as “complete for the entire Selberg class” (twice). That is stronger than what is proved. The theorem excludes bounded-support, hence periodic, divisor sums — i.e. classical Selberg sieve weights of finite level. The Selberg class is a class of Dirichlet series with an Euler product and a functional equation; nothing here rules out weights built from automorphic forms, and the sources say so elsewhere (“the missing object is a non-periodic weight”). The correct statement, and the one this harvest reports, is: the twin indicator is not a bounded-support divisor sum, so no classical finite-level Selberg sieve can produce it. The quantitative strengthening — that the two-point count is off by a factor 2 — is numerical only.

Finding 7What was not achieved

For symmetry, the negative ledger. Stated plainly, so the map is not mistaken for a solution:

  • No proof of the twin prime conjecture, over ℚ or over 𝔽q[x]. The function-field count is cited (Pollack; Bary-Soroker–Stix), not reproved; the fixed-q, n → ∞ asymptotic remains conjectural in the literature the programme searched.
  • No crossing of the parity barrier. Target C is an impossibility theorem: it says a class of methods fails. It does not construct the non-periodic weight that would succeed, and over ℚ that construction is the open problem.
  • No formalisation of the cap's generic case. Ritt/Guralnick–Saxl, Poincaré reducibility and the S₄ decomposition are not in Lean. The certified layer covers the algebraic links and the finite base (q ≤ 23), not the analytic input.
  • No proof of the last identity. Five primes is not a theorem. The mechanism (twisted Parseval) is Probe 47, explicitly speculative.
  • No sharp quantitative parity constant. The “factor 2” separation in Target C is numerical.
  • No structural explanation for m = (a²+1)/2. The formula is verified and identified with J(χ₄,χ₄), but the derivation from the Gauss-sum expansion is not carried out.

5Significance, in plain terms

5.1 The one-sentence version

The programme did not get closer to proving the twin prime conjecture; it got precise about why the obvious tools cannot, and it built a small, fully checkable universe in which the failure can be measured.

5.2 The layman's analogy

Imagine primes as a row of lamps, and the twin prime conjecture as the claim that two adjacent lamps are lit infinitely often. The classical tool — the zeta function — is a machine that reads the row one lamp at a time and reports a total. It is very good at totals. But “two adjacent lamps are both lit” is a statement about a pair, and a machine that only totals single lamps cannot see it. Not because it is a weak machine, but because the information was never in its input.

That is what Finding 1 proves, and it is the reason Selberg's parity barrier is not a technical nuisance. The programme then did three useful things with the insight:

  • It built a miniature world where the question can be settled. In 𝔽q[x] the pair question has an answer, and the answer does not come from the zeta — it comes from a different mechanism (two-dimensional equidistribution). So the miniature world demonstrates both halves: the zeta is blind, and the blindness is not fatal there because another tool exists. Over the integers, that other tool does not exist. That is the whole story of the barrier in one picture.
  • It found a finite statement that is genuinely hard in the same way. The d=4 cap says: for a quartic, the set of constants making it reducible is never more than half. This is a coin-flip-like bound, it is true, and its proof needs the same “compensation” that the parity barrier denies over ℚ — the quadratic-factor condition quietly filling the gap left by the value set. One can therefore see the mechanism that is missing upstairs, in a place where it is present and provable.
  • It drew the boundary of what its own method can reach. Findings 5 and 6 say, with proof, that a whole family of approaches fails, and identify the one object that would have to exist for a success: a non-periodic weight with arithmetic content.

5.3 Why a negative result is the achievement

In this field a proof that a method cannot work is often worth more than another partial result, because it redirects effort. The programme's honest contribution is a map of a wall with a measured shape: which statistics each tool sees, which degree the exact-formula regime dies at, which finite-field inequality encodes the obstruction, and which single identity remains. A researcher who wants to attack the twin prime problem can use that map to avoid the dead ends without re-deriving them.

There is also an epistemic achievement, which is easy to miss: the programme kept a status register that distinguishes proved from certified from verified from guessed, and it recorded its own refutations. Most exploratory mathematics of this kind does not survive that discipline. Here the discipline is the product.

5.4 What would actually move the needle

  1. Prove the last identity. It is one line, it now holds exactly at 80 primes in its corrected χ₂(2c) form, and it is the only thing between the closed forms and an unconditional c₄ chain. This is the highest-value, lowest-risk target in the programme — and 019 has already fixed the statement to aim at.
  2. Formalise Ritt + Poincaré + the S₄ decomposition. This converts the cap from “proved modulo three standard theorems” to certified. It is engineering, not insight, and the rest of the certified layer shows it is feasible.
  3. Explain the d=5 wall. Finding 6 says the exact-formula regime stops; a proof that it must stop would be a genuine structural theorem about twin counts over function fields.
  4. Sharpen the quantitative parity constant. Turning the numerical factor 2 into a theorem would make Target C quantitative rather than merely qualitative.

6Application to other problems

The results are specialised, but three of them transfer. The transfer is at the level of technique and statement, not of immediate corollaries; that is the honest scope.

6.1 Value sets and reducible specialisations of polynomials

The d=4 cap is a theorem about how a polynomial's value set interacts with its quadratic-factor condition. Statements of that shape — “at most/most half of the constants b make f+b reducible” — are the finite-field engine behind counting reducible specialisations and, dually, counting irreducible values. The same circle of ideas feeds:

  • Function-field Bateman–Horn / Schinzel: counting tuples of polynomials that are simultaneously irreducible is exactly the T(d;q,h) problem, and any improvement in the exact formulas at fixed degree is a sharpening of the finite-field statement.
  • Value-set bounds: the quantity maxf|Im f| for degree-d polynomials is studied for its own sake (and interacts with permutation polynomials and with the “few-valued” polynomials used in cryptography). The programme's collapse results at d ≥ 5 are data about exactly that quantity.
  • Coding and sequence design: bounds on value sets over prime fields are used when a polynomial is required to have a large or small image; a sharp (q+1)/2 threshold with explicit equality families is directly quotable.

6.2 Character sums, Jacobi sums, and the twisted-Parseval template

The remaining identity is an instance of a general and very common pattern: an untwisted second moment is clean (orthogonality/Parseval), and the twist by a quadratic character is the whole arithmetic content, evaluating to a Jacobi sum. The same template appears whenever one computes second moments of exponential sums with a character weight — in the analytic theory of L-functions, in the spectral statistics of Kloosterman/Salié sums, and in explicit computations of Frobenius traces on curves with extra automorphisms (the quartic/Fermat family here). A proof of the identity would be a worked example of the template with all constants pinned; the identification m = (|Re J(χ₄,χ₄)|²+1)/2 is already a reusable identity.

6.3 Formalisation of negative results

The parity theorem and the separation theorem are rare specimens: obstruction theorems that are machine-checked. The Lean development supplies

  • FreeOrbit.card_dvd_card_of_free_action — orbit–stabiliser plus class formula, a general divisibility engine;
  • Separation.no_zeta_observable — the “a statistic is not a function of another statistic” pattern, with an explicit witness;
  • Periodic.divisor_sum_periodic and NonPeriodic.twinInd_not_divisor_sum — the periodicity-versus-arithmetic obstruction, reusable for any indicator with a comparable rigidity;
  • SmallCap.cap_* — decide theorems over Finset.image, a template for discharging finite ranges of a number-theoretic bound inside a proof assistant.

This is the most portable part of the programme: the statements are about arbitrary finite sets, groups and characters, so they can be instantiated far from twin primes.

6.4 What does not transfer

It should be said plainly: none of this yields a new bound toward the twin prime conjecture over ℤ, and the function-field results do not imply anything over ℚ — the programme's own separation theorem explains why (there is no Frobenius to supply the pair equidistribution). Anyone citing this work for a twin-prime advance would be misreading it.

8Glossary

Qrime
A monic irreducible polynomial over 𝔽q — the polynomial-model analogue of a prime. “Qrime” is the programme's coinage.
Twin qrime
A pair (f, f+2) of qrimes of the same degree. The count of such f is T(d;q,h) with h = 2.
World
A set S of qrimes with a degree function. Quantifying over worlds (rather than only over the true one) is what makes the separation theorem a statement about information.
Zeta datum
The degree-count sequence Z(S)(n) = #{f ∈ S : deg f = n}. By the Euler product and Möbius inversion this is exactly what ζS sees.
Linear / bilinear statistic
Linear: a sum of one-point indicators (the qrime count, zeta coefficients, Frobenius traces). Bilinear: a sum of products of two indicators at a related pair (the twin count). The zeta sees the first, not the second.
Value set Im(g)
The image of g : 𝔽q → 𝔽q. Its size controls the linear-factor condition.
Quadratic-factor set Q
The set of constants b for which g+b has a quadratic factor; explicitly Q = { ((a₂+p²)² − (a₁/p)²)/4 : p ≠ 0 } plus a degenerate term.
The cap
The inequality |(−Im g) ∪ Q| ≥ (q+1)/2 for quartics, equivalently I(a) ≤ (q−1)/2. Target A.
Monodromy
The action of the Galois group of the splitting field of g(x) − t on the fibre. Primitivity of this action is what Ritt's theorem gives for a non-composition, and it forces the value set to be large.
Ritt's theorem
Structure theorem for polynomial decompositions: a polynomial is a composition exactly when its monodromy is imprimitive (Guralnick–Saxl form). Cited, not formalised.
Poincaré reducibility
A surjection of abelian varieties with connected fibres splits up to isogeny; used here as Jac(C) ~ E × E′ for the genus-2 curve C : s² = S(x).
Weil bound
|aE| ≤ 2√q for the Frobenius trace of an elliptic curve; the source of the O(√q) errors here.
Class function / S₄ decomposition
Expansion of a fibre indicator in the irreducible characters of S₄. The 2-dimensional irreducible appears as χ₂ here — not to be confused with the quadratic character of 𝔽q.
DFT (discrete Fourier transform)
Since T is constant on d-th power classes, it is a function on ℤ/d, and its Fourier amplitudes Ac = ΣkTkζdck are the useful coordinates.
Jacobi sum J(χ₄,χ₄)
The classical quartic Jacobi sum, evaluating to ±(a+bi) when q = a²+b². It carries the constant m.
Selberg class / sieve weight
A Dirichlet series with Euler product and functional equation; a sieve weight is n ↦ Σd|n,d≤Dλd. The latter is periodic — the fact that powers the parity theorem.
Parity barrier
Selberg's obstruction: sieve-type (linear) methods cannot detect the parity of Ω(n), so they cannot force a two-point correlation. The programme's finite form: periodic weights cannot equal the twin indicator.
Probe
A speculative mechanism recorded with its evidence, its reason for being speculative, and a falsification test. See §4.
Lean / Mathlib
Lean 4 is a proof assistant; Mathlib is its mathematics library. “Lean-certified” means the statement is checked by the kernel, not that the informal reading has been verified.

Verified at harvest time The sweep was actually run while preparing this document: all 45 modules re-elaborate clean, exit 0, and the checked statements report only the three standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) — no sorryAx, i.e. no unproved holes in the certified layer. The one diagnostic is a style warning in Scale.lean (haveI versus have), which does not affect any statement.

What that does and does not certify. It certifies the statements in 018/lean: the translation reduction, the union decomposition, the composition case, the orbit counts, the finite base, the periodicity pair, the separation witness, and the abstract free-orbit engine. It does not certify the generic case of the cap (§2 F2), because Ritt, Poincaré reducibility and the S₄ decomposition are not in the module set. The distinction between “the Lean layer is green” and “the cap is proved” is exactly the distinction this harvest is trying to preserve.
