# Round 113 — shift uniformity is false; the exact symmetry is arithmetic

*Plan fixed at the start of the round (`rounds.log`, BEFORE line).*

---

## 1. The envisioned path

Every route to the twin asymptotic needs a *level of distribution* statement: the pairs `(f, f+h)` must be
equidistributed modulo `d` uniformly in the shift `h`, up to `deg d ≈ n/2`. The weakest observable form of that
statement is:

```
T(n; h) / T(n; h') → 1      for every fixed pair of nonzero shifts h, h' .
```

If that holds, the singular series is the only arithmetic datum and the shift plays no role. It is cheap to
test at `q = 3, 5, 7`, and it is the input the large sieve would actually deliver.

## 2. Wild guess 54

> **Wild guess 54.** The twin count is asymptotically uniform in the nonzero shift: `T(n;h)/T(n;2) → 1` for every
> fixed `h ≠ 0`.

**Why guess it.** The singular series for `(f, f+h)` is `Π_P (1−2/Q)/(1−1/Q)²` with no `h` in it — no prime
divides a nonzero constant (Lean: `Shift.not_dvd_C`). So the main term is shift-independent; the guess is that
the finite-`n` counts converge to it uniformly in `h`.

**Falsification test.** Compute `T(n;h)` for all `h` and small `n` at `q = 5, 7` and look at the ratios.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 At `q = 3` the question is vacuous

| `n` | `h=1` | `h=2` | `h=3` | `h=4` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 6 | 6 | 48 | 6 |
| 6 | 6 | 6 | 116 | 6 |
| 7 | 15 | 15 | 312 | 15 |
| 8 | 36 | 36 | 810 | 36 |
| 9 | 105 | 105 | 2184 | 105 |

All nonzero shifts agree — because `𝔽₃^× = {±1}` and every element is an `n`-th power for either parity of `n`.
The `h = 3` column is the degenerate case `h ≡ 0`: it counts `f` with `f` irreducible, i.e. `T(n;0) = I(n)`
(`I(5) = 48`, `I(6) = 116`, `I(7) = 312`, `I(8) = 810`, `I(9) = 2184`) — a useful consistency check.

### 3.2 At `q = 5, 7` there are real classes

| `q` | `n` | `T(n;h)` for `h = 1..q−1` |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 3 | 10, 10, 10, 10 |
| 5 | 4 | **25, 20, 20, 25** |
| 5 | 5 | 98, 98, 98, 98 |
| 5 | 6 | **280, 240, 240, 280** |
| 7 | 3 | **21, 35, 28, 28, 35, 21** |
| 7 | 4 | 112, 112, 112, 112, 112, 112 |
| 7 | 5 | 560, 560, 560, 560, 560, 560 |
| 7 | 6 | **2527, 2667, 2576, 2576, 2667, 2527** |

The classes are exactly the orbits of the group generated by

```
h ↦ a^n h   (a ∈ 𝔽_q^× ,  the 018 scaling law)      and      h ↦ −h   (the 018 even symmetry).
```

Check: `q = 7, n = 3` — cubes mod 7 are `{1,6} = {±1}`, so the orbits are `{±1}, {±2}, {±3}`, giving
`21, 35, 28` ✓. `q = 7, n = 6` — sixth powers are `{1}`, so the orbits are `{h, −h}`, giving
`2527, 2667, 2576` ✓. `q = 5, n = 4` — fourth powers are `{1}`, orbits `{1,4}, {2,3}`, giving `25, 20` ✓.
`q = 5, n = 3` — cubes are everything, one orbit ✓.

**Wild guess 54 is refuted**: the shift dependence is real, and it is not small at these `n` (35 versus 21 is
`67%`). But the refutation is informative: the dependence is **exactly arithmetic**, not an error term. It is
the power-class structure of `𝔽_q^×` acting on the shift.

### 3.3 What this means for the level-of-distribution input

The correct uniformity statement must be formulated *modulo the class action*: `T(n;h)` is constant on orbits,
and within an orbit the main term is the shift-independent singular series. So any sieve or large-sieve argument
has to quotient by `𝔽_q^×/(𝔽_q^×)^n` (and by `±1`) before claiming uniformity — a finite, computable
correction, not an error term.

This also explains why the singular series is shift-free while the finite counts are not: the discrepancy is a
finite group invariant that persists at every `n`, so it never shows up in the `n → ∞` asymptotic.

## 4. Lean

`Shift.not_dvd_C`: a nonconstant polynomial does not divide a nonzero constant, hence every local factor is
`(1−2/Q)/(1−1/Q)²` and the Euler product is shift-independent. Standard axioms.

## 6. Next round (114)

With the shift structure settled, the remaining large-sieve question is quantitative: how large can the level
`deg d` be before the pair correlation deviates from the CRT prediction? That is the function-field analogue of
the level of distribution, and it can be measured directly: for each `κ`, compare
`#{f : f ≡ a (d), f+2 ≡ b (d)}` with the CRT value for all `d` of degree `≤ κ`. The deviation is exactly zero
(CRT), so the real question is the *weighted* version with the irreducibility indicator — the error of the
sieve, which is the boundary stratum of round 108. Round 114 will measure that error as a function of `κ`.
