Geometric & Harmonic Decomposition Lab

Two sets A and B. Each number is broken into unit fractions, like fractions are grouped, then smaller fractions are merged upward into as many copies of the largest fraction as possible.

1 · Geometric series (integers)

Each integer n is written as n·(1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + …). So every group 1/2k holds Σ(set) copies. The series is cut at K terms; the tiny tail is shown as the leftover. Merging pairs two 1/2k+1 into one 1/2k, from the smallest upward.

2 · Greedy harmonic decomposition (integers)

Each integer n is built from distinct unit fractions by the greedy rule: take the smallest unused denominator d with 1/d ≤ what remains. That runs along the harmonic series 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + … until the next term overshoots, then jumps (Egyptian-fraction greedy) and always finishes exactly. Exact rational arithmetic is used. The final result is always rolled up to the largest fraction 1/2, so an integer n appears as 2n × 1/2. Values are capped at 4 because denominators explode doubly-exponentially.

3 · Greedy harmonic decomposition (rational reals, largest fraction 1/2)

Each number x = p/q is built from distinct unit fractions starting at 1/2: take the smallest unused d ≥ 2 with 1/d ≤ what remains. Because x is rational, the greedy expansion always ends exactly, so there is no remainder and every term is a true fraction. Grouped fractions are then promoted as high as possible: k copies of 1/d are swapped for the equal number of copies of the highest unit fraction 1/e they make exactly (10 × 1/8 → 5 × 1/4 → 2 × 1/2 + 1/4), repeated until nothing moves, so the total gravitates to 1/2. Values are capped at 3 to keep denominators readable.
