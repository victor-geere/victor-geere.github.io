# Phase 3 — the no-go: what greedy-Egyptian selections can and cannot do

Synthesis of [`phase1-rate.md`](phase1-rate.md) and [`phase2-arithmetic.md`](phase2-arithmetic.md).
Scripts: [`phase1_rate.py`](phase1_rate.py), [`phase2_arithmetic.py`](phase2_arithmetic.py).

The three targets of `monotone-analogue.md` §6 were: (1) a monotone selection converging
faster than `N^{−1/2}` at a zero without external acceleration; (2) a greedy support series
`D_x(s) = Σ_{k∈A(x)}(k+2)^{−s}` with an exact Euler product or functional equation; (3) a
sharp no-go delimiting the framework. Targets **1 and 2 are both refuted**; here is the
resulting no-go.

## Setting

- A **selection** is a Dirichlet series `Σ_n a(n)n^{−s}`, `a(n) ∈ {−1,0,1}`.
- A **greedy support** `A(x) ⊂ ℕ` is the set of indices taken by the greedy Egyptian rule
  for `x ∈ (0,1]` on the alphabet `1/(k+2)`; writing `n_j = k_j + 2`, the Sylvester
  sparsity is `n_{j+1} ≥ n_j(n_j − 1) + 1`.
- `D_x(s) = Σ_{j} n_j^{−s}`.

## No-go theorem

**(A) Arithmetic structure: none.** `D_x(s)` has an Euler product only for the degenerate
singletons `A(x) = {m}` (i.e. `x = 1/m`), where `D_x(s) = m^{−s}` is a single local factor
and carries no information. In every other case the support is not multiplicatively closed
— `2·2 = 4` is missing from `{2}, {2,3}, {2,3,7}` and from the Sylvester ladder — and an
Euler product requires multiplicative closure. For irrational `x` the failure is *forced*:
Sylvester sparsity gives doubly-exponential growth (`n_j` has 14 digits at `j = 6`), while
a multiplicative semigroup generated by its least element `m` grows at most like `m^j`,
exponentially. Doubly exponential ≫ exponential, so **no irrational `x` has a
multiplicative support**. Hence:

> no greedy support carries arithmetic; the Dirichlet series `Σ_j n_j^{−s}` is an entire
> function with no Euler product.

**(B) Functional equation: only Mahler, and it creates a boundary.** The single exact
relation is the scaling `s → 2s`:

```
geometric model  n_j = q^{2^j} :   D(s) − D(2s) = q^{−s}          (exact; ratio 1.000000000000)
true Sylvester ladder          :   D(s) − D(2s) = 2^{−s}(1 + ε(s)),  ε(s) → 0 as s → ∞
                                   (measured ε = 0.236, 0.139, 0.035, 1.5·10⁻³, 2.3·10⁻⁶
                                    at s = 1, 4, 8, 16, 32)
```

On `Re s = 0` every term has modulus `1`, so the series diverges at *every* point of the
line: the abscissa of convergence is `0` and the line is a **natural boundary**. A
classical (Riemann-type) functional equation would furnish meromorphic continuation
across that line; therefore none exists. The only structure is a non-arithmetic
self-similarity — a Mahler equation — whose consequence is the boundary, not a functional
equation.

**(C) Zero-location rate: `N^{−1/2}` is a hard floor.** If `Σ_n a(n)n^{−s} = η(s)`, the
coefficients are forced: `a(n) = (−1)^{n−1}` (uniqueness of Dirichlet coefficients). So
there is no selection to optimise. For the forced selection, Abel summation with
`A(N) = Σ_{n≤N}(−1)^{n−1} ∈ {0,1}` gives

```
|η(s₀) − η_N(s₀)| = (1/2) N^{−1/2} (1 + o(1))          (measured constant 0.50000, slope −0.513)
```

so the truncation error is exactly `Θ(N^{−1/2})` and no selection can beat it. The barrier
is a *truncation* barrier: it falls only to methods that consume the infinite tail (the
Euler transform reaches `10⁻¹⁴` with ~100 terms), which is external acceleration.

## The three mechanisms are distinct — this is not one fact in three guises

| target | obstruction | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| arithmetic (Euler product) | non-multiplicativity | Sylvester sparsity (squaring) beats exponential multiplicative growth |
| analysis (functional equation) | natural boundary at `Re s = 0` | the Mahler scaling `s → 2s` propagates the boundary; no reflection |
| rate (`N^{−1/2}`) | uniqueness + integer running sums | `\|A(N)\| ≥ 1` forces the tail; only non-truncation summation escapes |

The unifying theme is narrow and should be stated as such: the greedy Egyptian support is
a **doubly-exponentially sparse, non-multiplicative, self-similar** set. Sparsity and
self-similarity give an entire function with a natural boundary; non-multiplicativity kills
the Euler product; and sparsity of the *coefficients* leaves the truncation tail — not the
selection — as the binding constraint on rate.

## What this means for the programme

- The greedy harmonic construction is Dirichlet-series-inert in a precise sense: **its only
  Dirichlet-series structure is a Mahler self-similarity** (arithmetic does enter elsewhere —
  the `σ = 1` zero-free proof uses unique factorisation, and the Sylvester numbers are
  pairwise coprime; see [`closed-form.md`](closed-form.md) §6.6, which corrects an
  overstatement here), and self-similarity of this
  kind produces natural boundaries rather than functional equations. That is the opposite
  of what a `ζ`-like object needs.
- The one positive result in the folder remains the monotone zero-locator contrast
  ([`monotone-analogue.md`](monotone-analogue.md) §2): the alternating selection converges
  to the zeros at `N^{−1/2}`, and Phase 1 shows this rate is *optimal and forced*.
- No route to `ζ`'s zeros, RH, or parity arises. Targets 1 and 2 are closed as impossible
  within the framework; target 3 is the table above.

## What would still be open (and is a different subject)

The Mahler object `D(s)` — an entire function with a natural boundary and an asymptotic
scaling relation — is a legitimate object of study in the theory of lacunary Dirichlet
series and Mahler functions. Its open questions (exact functional equation for some
explicit support; the zero distribution in the strip `0 < Re s < 1`, now pinned to 60 digits
in [`closed-form.md`](closed-form.md)) belong there, **not** to the zeta programme. I am
flagging it rather than claiming it.
