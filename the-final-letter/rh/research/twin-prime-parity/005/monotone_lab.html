The alphabet lab — which digits are monotone, and what monotonicity costs

Greedy threshold rule on an alphabet w0 > w1 > …: δk(x) = 1 ⟺ rk ≥ wk, rk+1 = rk − δkwk. A digit is monotone iff its support {x : δk=1} is a single prefix or suffix. The running-minimum theorem: a strict running minimum wk < minj<k wj forces δk non-monotone. If wk → 0 that happens infinitely often.
Trade-off. wk→0 gives an exact expansion but non-monotone digits. inf wk=c>0 can give monotone digits, but the sum stops at x − (x mod c): resolution floor c.
Binary digits: rows = x ∈ (0,1), columns = k. Black = δk(x) = 1.
