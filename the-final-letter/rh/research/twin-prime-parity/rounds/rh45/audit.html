Audit of rh45 against §6 of the η/Euler paper:
what the explicit-formula round has, and what a certified zero still needs

Section 6 of eta-euler-acceleration.html names the three rigorous routes to a zeta zero — interval Z(t) sign change, Turing's method, a validated explicit formula — and points at rh45 as the round that uses them. This audit re-runs rh45’s own evidence, re-derives its numbers independently in Python and SymPy, rebuilds its Lean layer, and checks the three routes one by one.

Subject: research/twin-prime-parity/rounds/rh45/  ·  Re-run: verify/independent_check.py → 19/21 (C1e, C4.1 fail, both over-strong criteria)  ·  Lean: lean/check.sh --all → green, 0 sorry, footprint propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound  ·  Verdict: strong numerics, no certified zero

Abstract — verdict in one paragraph (§0)

rh45 has: the correct Weil explicit formula (verified to ~45 digits on a non-degenerate kernel), the refutation of its own first-draft normalisation, the first five zeros located from ζ evaluations alone to 32–33 digits, the exact counting identity N(T) = θ(T)/π + 1 + S(T) verified to 10−20, an argument-principle count matching the zero table, a correct negative answer on the parity problem, and a 476-line Lean algebraic layer that compiles with no sorry and no extra axiom. It does not have: any of the three certification mechanisms of §6. Its Z(t) work is floating-point mpmath, not interval arithmetic (Route A unmet); its count is a numerical ζ'/ζ quadrature checked against mp.zetazero, not a Turing test that excludes missed or off-line zeros (Route B unmet); and its explicit-formula tail controls are density-style estimates with an mp.zetazero table on the zero side, so the identity is computed, not validated (Route C partial). The audit also finds an internal inconsistency: explicit_formula.py adopts a convention (E) that is false (witnesses: residuals −1.04, +2.28, +2.02, +2.18 on four kernels, where the Weil form gives 9.6·10−46), and its C1a check is vacuous (absolute tolerance 10−20 against a signal of size 8.3·10−44, i.e. 19 % relative error passes). Nothing here says the round’s numbers are wrong — they reproduce — only that they are not yet certificates.

Keywords: Riemann zeta; explicit formula; Weil/Riemann–von Mangoldt; Z(t); Turing’s method; argument principle; interval arithmetic; Lean 4; Mathlib; audit.

Response (added after this audit)

The three routes have since been proved — Route A, the Route B Turing reduction, and the Route C error assembly are machine-checked in Lean (Rh45/Routes.lean), with the prime/archimedean/zero tail bounds and the symmetric-rectangle on-line criterion written up in routes-abc.html. The remaining gap is the arithmetic enclosure engine, specified there in §5.

Honest headline

rh45 does not certify a single zeta zero, and its own truths.md does not claim that it does. What it does is build the right instrument and demonstrate it numerically. The gap to §6 is not a mathematical error in the displayed results; it is the absence of a validated-arithmetic layer (enclosures instead of 60-digit floats) and of the exclusion arguments (Turing test, rigorous S(T) and prime/archimedean tail bounds) that turn a small |value| into a theorem.

0. Verdict in one paragraph

Against the three certification routes of §6, rh45’s position is: Route A (interval Z(t)) — not met; Route B (Turing) — not met; Route C (validated explicit formula) — partially met, numerically but not rigorously. Its strongest artefacts are (i) the corrected Weil formula, independently reproduced here to 9.6·10−46 on a modulated Gaussian with LHS = 1.00000000005, (ii) zero location from ζ alone to 33 digits, and (iii) the exact counting identity. Its weakest points are the absent exclusion arguments, the density-style “bounds”, the reliance on mp.zetazero inside the verification chains, the (now corrected) stale 17/21 ledger line, and the main script’s false convention (E).

1. The standard being audited against (§6)

Section 6 of the η/Euler paper lists three acceptable ways to certify a zero:

The §6 standard, verbatim in substance

  • Route A. An interval-arithmetic (ball-arithmetic) evaluation of ζ showing a sign change of the real function Z(t) = eiθ(t)ζ(1/2+it) on a bracket, followed by a root-isolation argument (a simple zero, derivative bounded away from 0 on the bracket).
  • Route B. Turing’s method: a rigorous count N(T) up to height T (argument principle on a contour, with rigorous ζ'/ζ or S(T) bounds), plus a matching number of sign changes — this also excludes missed zeros and off-line zeros.
  • Route C. The explicit formula with validated kernels: rigorous truncation bounds for the zero sum, the prime sum and the archimedean term, so the identity is a theorem on the evaluated input.

An audit therefore asks, for each route: are the inputs enclosed (not merely evaluated at 60 digits), and is the exclusion performed? A value of |ζ(1/2+it)| = 10−15 proves nothing about a zero; an enclosure of |ζ| plus a sign change of Z on a rational bracket, or a rigorous count, does.

3. Route A: interval arithmetic on Z(t)

What is there. verify/independent_check.py builds Z(t) = Re(eiθ(t)ζ(1/2+it)) from mp.loggamma and mp.zeta at 80 dps, scans [0.5, 35] in steps of 0.05, bisects 300 times at each sign change, and reports agreement with a five-entry reference string to 32–33 digits. The main script does the same and additionally refines with Newton on Z (its dZdt is a floating central difference with step 10−25).

Why it is not Route A.

  • No enclosure. mp.zeta and mp.loggamma return 60–80 digit floats. Nothing bounds the rounding error of the special-function evaluation, so a sign of Z is a heuristic, not a proof. The round never converts the two bracket endpoints to rationals and never prints a guaranteed interval containing Z.
  • No root isolation. No bound on |Z′| (or on |ζ| away from the bracket) is produced, so the existence and simplicity of the root is not established; a tangential root would be missed, and the 300 bisections do not detect one.
  • Newton on a noisy derivative. dZdt uses a finite difference whose step 10−25 is far below the resolution of a 60-digit float difference of O(1) values; the derivative is therefore dominated by round-off. It happens to converge because it starts from an already excellent bracket.

Route A: verdict

NOT MET — numerically excellent, logically open. Needs a Num/ball type (value + guaranteed error), rational bracket endpoints, an enclosure Z(a) < 0 < Z(b), and a derivative (or monotonicity) certificate on [a,b].

4. Route B: Turing’s method

What is there. The auditor integrates ζ'/ζ numerically around the rectangle [−1,2] × [1/2,T] with mp.quad, divides by 2πi, and compares to the number of mp.zetazero entries below T; it also computes S(T) from the continuous argument along 2 → 2+iT → 1/2+iT and verifies N = θ/π + 1 + S to 10−20. My independent re-run reproduces the identity (Table 7.3).

Why it is not Route B.

  • The count is validated against mp.zetazero, not used to certify it. The chain is “quadrature ≈ table”, with the table treated as truth. Turing’s method runs the other way: a rigorous N(T) plus a rigorous lower bound on the number of sign changes (e.g. via Gram’s law / Rosser’s rule or a truncated Z-count) together exclude missed and off-line zeros.
  • No quadrature error bound. mp.quad returns a value and an estimated error; the script neither propagates a rigorous error nor bounds |ζ'/ζ| on the contour. A pole or near-pole on the contour (the rectangle passes through Re s = 2 and Im = 1/2) is not excluded by an interval argument.
  • No rigorous S(T) bound. The classical Turing inputs are N(T) = (T/2π)log(T/2πe) + 7/8 + S(T) + O(1/T) with an explicit |S(T)| ≤ c log T; the round verifies the identity numerically but never produces the bound, so it cannot say “there are no other zeros below T”.
  • The asymptotic check is a fitted residual. The claim residual·48πT → 1 is a good numerical diagnostic; it is not a proof of the O(1/T) term.

Route B: verdict

NOT MET — the round counts zeros, it does not certify the count. Needs ball-arithmetic contour integration, explicit bounds on ζ'/ζ (or on Δ arg ζ) on the four sides, an explicit S(T) inequality, and the sign-change comparison that closes the test.

5. Route C: a validated explicit formula

What is there. The corrected Weil form is verified numerically on four kernels (modulated Gaussian: residual 9.6·10−46 against LHS = 1.00000000005; narrow Gaussian: −1.1·10−63; σ=1 Gaussian: 1.6·10−44; convergent Cauchy a=1: 0.0187 within its density estimate). The identity, the pole-pair reduction Φ(1)+Φ(0)=2φ(i/2), the archimedean term and the falsification of the naive normalisation are all reproduced here.

Why it is only partially Route C.

  • The zero side uses mp.zetazero. Any claim of the form “the identity holds using independent data” is weakened when the zero side is a table produced by the very function one wants to certify. The auditor is explicit that mp.zetazero is “allowed in C1/C3”; that is fine as a consistency check, but it is not a validated kernel.
  • The tail controls are density estimates, not bounds. zero_tail_bound integrates |φ(t)|(log(t/2π)+1)/(2π), i.e. it assumes the mean zero density and ignores S(T); prime_tail_bound integrates 2y|g(y)|ey/2, a stated majorant rather than a derivation from ψ(x) ≤ (log 4)x. For super-decaying Gaussians the distinction is invisible; for the Cauchy companion it is the difference between “2 digits” and a proof.
  • C1a is vacuous. It checks |RHS − LHS| < 10−20 where LHS = 8.26·10−44 and RHS − LHS = 1.56·10−44: the relative error is 0.189 (19 %), and the stated prime-tail majorant 2.28·10−43 is three times the signal. The check passes only because the threshold is absolute (finding F2). The genuinely non-degenerate kernel is C1b (finding F4 also applies there).

Route C: verdict

PARTIAL — correct identity, high numerical accuracy, but not validated. Needs: rigorous tail inequalities for N(T) and Σ Λ(n)n−1/2g(log n), a self-contained zero side (locations from Route A or a rigorous density bound), and relative-error thresholds.

6. Defects found (F1–F7)

F1 — internal inconsistency: the main script’s convention (E) is false

explicit_formula.py’s docstring and its S6 summary adopt (E) Σρ F(ρ) = F(1) + F(0) − P/(2π) with F(s)=∫g e−sudu, while truths.md, README.md and the auditor adopt the Weil form Σρ Φ(ρ) = Φ(1)+Φ(0) − P + (1/2π)∫φΨ, Φ(s)=φ((s−1/2)/i). These are not the same formula. The audit evaluates both on four kernels:

The round’s public ledger takes the auditor’s correct form, so the error is confined to the main script. MAIN SCRIPT WRONG

F2 — C1a proves nothing (absolute vs relative tolerance)

LHS = 8.2619765948608225685562103609704482e−44, |RHS−LHS| = 1.5647708e−44 → relative error 0.189. The threshold 10−20 is absolute, so a 19 % error “passes”. The prime-tail majorant 2.279643e−43 exceeds the signal. C1a should be reported as a trivially satisfied identity, not as “≥ 20 digits”. C1b and C1c are the meaningful checks.

F3 — density estimates presented as bounds

zero_tail_bound and prime_tail_bound are mean-density integrals (dN ≈ (1/2π)log(t/2π)dt, ψ(x) ≈ x with a stated 2y majorant). They are excellent diagnostics and generous when the kernel decays super-exponentially, but they are not derived from explicit inequalities (ψ(x) ≤ (log4)x, N(T+h)−N(T) = (h/π)log T + O(log T), |S(T)| ≤ c log T). For the Cauchy companion they are the limiting factor and the honest label is “~2 digits”, not “verified”.

F4 — mp.zetazero inside the verification chains

The zero side of C1 (all four kernels) is 2Σk≤100φ(γk) from mp.zetazero; C3 validates its count against mp.zetazero; the main script’s S1.3 zero side also uses mp.zetazero. Route C wants the zero side validated independently (Route A outputs, or a rigorous density bound). The C2 route (own Z) exists but is not what feeds C1.

F5 — stale ledger line (corrected by this audit)

truths.md line 9 said “17/21 (the 4 failures …)”. The re-run gives 19/21 with two failures (C1e, C4.1), matching the programme README’s correction X4. This audit has updated the round file to 19/21 and marked the historical §3 table as such.

F6 — the main script has no log; its run was interrupted

truths.md §“evidence” states the round’s numerics rest on the auditor because explicit_formula.py’s run was interrupted. Consequently the S1.3 “verification” of (E) was never produced. Re-running it would expose F1; this audit instead evaluated the formula directly (F1 table).

F7 — Lean scope is algebraic only

Rh45/Explicit.lean (476 lines, no sorry, no extra axiom) proves: kernel evenness and positivity; F(1−x)=F(x) symmetry about 1/2; the pole pair polePair F = F 1 + F 0 and its collapse = 2F(1); monotonicity and non-negativity of the finite zero sum; the prime-side split and evenness; the finite-in-compact statement for riemannZetaZeros; vonMangoldtMain and the exact constant 7/8 rewrite; and ∫_0^∞ e−u/2cos(tu)du = 2/(1+4t²) together with ∫ (π/2)e−|u|/2cos(tu)du = (π/2)cauchyKernel. It contains no explicit formula, no Riemann–von Mangoldt identity, no analytic continuation argument, and no zero. Mathlib itself has none of these (grep for RiemannVonMangoldt/explicitFormula returns empty; it has riemannZeta_one_sub, logDeriv_riemannZeta_one_sub, the Euler product, isDiscrete_riemannZetaZeros and IsCompact.inter_riemannZetaZeros_finite).

8. What it needs: a concrete roadmap

  1. A rigorous scalar engine. Reuse the programme’s own ivmath.py pattern (Num = value + guaranteed error, with cos/sin/log/π built from bounded series) or a ball library. The rh43/ rh45 developments already share it; rh45’s zero work does not use it. Target: enclosures of ζ(1/2+it), θ(t) and Z(t) at rational t.
  2. Route A certificates. For each of γ₁…γ_N: rational a<b with Z(a)<0<Z(b) (enclosed), plus a bound |Z′| ≥ m > 0 on [a,b] (enclosed), giving a unique simple root. This alone certifies the zeros on the line.
  3. Route B: a real Turing test. A ball-arithmetic contour integral of ζ'/ζ with an explicit bound on the argument change on each side, an explicit |S(T)| ≤ c log T input, and a count of sign changes matching N(T) — this is what rules out missed and off-line zeros.
  4. Route C: validated tails. Replace the density integrals by Σ_{n>N}Λ(n)n−1/2|g(log n)| ≤ C∫ … derived from ψ(x) ≤ (log4)x, and by an explicit N(T+h)−N(T) bound. Use relative tolerances (fix F2). Remove mp.zetazero from the zero side of the identity (fix F4).
  5. Fix the record. Delete or correct the (E) convention in explicit_formula.py (F1), update truths.md to 19/21 (F5), and either run the main script to completion or retire it in favour of the auditor’s route (F6).
  6. Lean: extend or state the boundary. The honest options are (a) formalise the explicit formula on top of Mathlib’s riemannZeta_one_sub + Hadamard product (a substantial project; Mathlib has no explicit formula yet), or (b) keep the Lean layer algebraic and make the boundary explicit in the ledger (as F7 records).
