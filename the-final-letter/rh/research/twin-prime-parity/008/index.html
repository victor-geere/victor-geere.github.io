Attempting an Automorphic/Spectral Input for the Twin-Prime Parity Problem

Route 3 of dictionary 03 §5.4, item 3, applied to the shift-2 Liouville correlation S(x) = Σn≤x λ(n)λ(n+2): the input is located exactly, machine-checked at the arithmetic core, exhibited where it is known to work (function fields), and shown over ℚ to be blocked at the same parity barrier — now localised to two named gaps.

Abstract — verdict in one paragraph (§0)

The automorphic/spectral input is not a hypothetical: over 𝔽q[T] the analogous parity barrier is broken, by geometric methods (Sawin–Shusterman). The engine there is a trace function / ℓ-adic sheaf attached to the Möbius function, plus Deligne’s square-root cancellation, plus a genuinely geometric ability to “mimic the Möbius function by Dirichlet characters on special subspaces”. Over ℚ the sequence has no attached automorphic object at all (L(s, λ) = ζ(2s)/ζ(s) is a ratio of ζ, with no Hecke eigenvalues and no monodromy), and every sign that the number-field spectral toolkit can detect is a Dirichlet character, i.e. periodic in n mod q, hence parity-blind. The attempt therefore fails over ℚ, and the failure is no longer a vague “parity barrier”: it is the absence of (i) a spectral/sheaf object for λ and (ii) a non-periodic parity-breaking sign. The one genuinely new piece of arithmetic geography produced here is that the whole open content of S(x) = o(x) is a sum of λ over a quadratic polynomial, Σk≤K λ(4k²−1) — exactly the shape both the function-field and the number-field spectral methods attack.

Keywords: twin primes; parity barrier; Liouville correlation; automorphic input; trace functions; ℓ-adic sheaves; Deligne square-root cancellation; level of distribution; function fields; Lean 4; numerics.

Honest headline

This attempt does not break the parity barrier over ℚ. Nothing here proves S(x) = o(x) or the twin prime conjecture. What the attempt does is identify the missing input precisely — a trace/sheaf object with square-root cancellation together with a non-periodic parity-breaking sign — and show that over ℚ that object is absent. Route 3 reduces to routes 1–2 over ℚ.

1. The reduction: the open core is a quadratic-polynomial sum

Writing (as in the shift-2 audit) Sh(x) = Σn≤x λ(n)λ(n+h), and using only complete multiplicativity of λ (valid for all m, n, no coprimality): 

(R1)   S_2(x) = S_1(⌊x/2⌋) + C(⌊(x+1)/2⌋) ,        S_1(y) = Σ_{m≤y} λ(m)λ(m+1)
(R2)   C(K)   = Σ_{k≤K} λ(4k² − 1)
(R3)   λ((2k−1)(2k+1)) = λ(2k−1) λ(2k+1)

(R1) splits the even part (λ(2m)λ(2m+2) = λ(m)λ(m+1), since λ(2)² = 1) from the odd part; (R3) is complete multiplicativity; (R2) is (2k−1)(2k+1) = 4k²−1.

The point of (R2) is that it removes the “no Euler product” obstruction (dictionary F3/F8) as an excuse: although n ↦ λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative, the open core is a sum of the multiplicative λ over the quadratic polynomial k² − 1. Sums Σ μ(f(n)), Σ λ(f(n)) for quadratic f are the classical targets of spectral methods, over both ℚ and 𝔽q[T].

2. What the automorphic/spectral input has to supply

Abstracting the two known realisations (Friedlander–Iwaniec over ℚ; Sawin–Shusterman over 𝔽q[T]), a parity-breaking automorphic/spectral input has three components:

(S1) A spectral/geometric object attached to the sequence

Over ℚ: an automorphic form whose Hecke eigenvalues are the sequence (e.g. x²+y⁴ = N(x+iy²) in ℚ(i), giving a weight-1 Eisenstein series and its Grössencharacter — dictionary T14/T15).

Over 𝔽q[T]: a trace function, i.e. the trace of Frobenius on an ℓ-adic sheaf, attached to the Möbius function (Sawin–Shusterman 2008.09905).

(S2) Square-root cancellation for that object

Over ℚ: spectral equidistribution / Kuznetsov–Petersson (the Hecke eigenvalue input).

Over 𝔽q[T]: Deligne’s Weil II bound — the function-field Riemann hypothesis. This is the cleanest form of the input, and verify/03_function_field.py exhibits it exactly:

Kloosterman sums obey the Weil bound and Gauss sums are exactly √p. There is no such statement for Σn≤x λ(n) over ℚ; its square-root cancellation would already be a RH-strength input.

(S3) A parity-breaking sign, invisible to the sieve

This is the decisive component, and the one with no number-field realisation. Over 𝔽q[T], Sawin–Shusterman prove that “when restricted to certain special subspaces, the Möbius function over 𝔽q[T] can be mimicked by Dirichlet characters” (their abstract, verbatim).

Over ℚ, the only completely multiplicative signs that any L-function detects are Dirichlet characters, which are periodic modulo q and therefore parity-blind (dictionary Theorem 2.2); the index-parity instance is even identically invisible to the shift-2 correlation (dictionary Theorem 5.5). A sign that is not periodic is exactly what the bounded-depth class cannot use (dictionary Definition 1.11 / Theorem 2.1), and no such sign is known to exist for λ.

3. The input is realised over 𝔽q[T] (it is not hypothetical)

Sawin–Shusterman, On the Chowla and twin primes conjectures over 𝔽q[T] (arXiv:1808.04001): using geometric methods (trace functions, a function-field Burgess bound, and the character-mimicking of μ), they obtain a level of distribution close to 1 for μ in arithmetic progressions, resolve Chowla’s k-point correlation conjecture with large uniformity in the shifts, and establish the twin prime conjecture in quantitative form over 𝔽q[T]. The companion paper (arXiv:2008.09905) resolves the 𝔽q[u]-analog of Chowla’s conjecture on Möbius sums over polynomial sequences and of Bateman–Horn in degree 2, via cancellation in short sums of trace functions.

verify/03_function_field.py exhibits the consequences numerically. For 𝔽q[T], with Ch(n) = Σdeg f = n μ(f)μ(f+1) and Tw(n) = #{f : f, f+1 both irreducible}:

3, 86561810−300.004636102.5
5, 4625150−200.03202539.1

The Chowla correlation cancels (|Ch|/qn → 0) and twin irreducibles exist at the Bateman–Horn scale — both theorems there. Over ℚ the analogous statements (Σn≤x λ(n)λ(n+1) = o(x), twin primes) are open (Matomäki–Radziwiłł give only ≤ 1−δ; dictionary T5, shift-2 audit).

Char-2 caveat, recorded honestly.

Over 𝔽2 the shift f ↦ f+1 is degenerate (every irreducible of degree ≥ 2 has nonzero constant term, so f+1 is divisible by T), and Tw(n) = 0 for all n — the script shows this. This is a concrete instance of the “simple condition on the finite field” in the Sawin–Shusterman abstract.

4. Why it does not transfer to ℚ

(O1) No spectral object

L(s, λ) = ζ(2s)/ζ(s) is a ratio of ζ. λ is not the sequence of Hecke eigenvalues of any cusp form or Eisenstein series in a way that supports a Kusnetsov/Petersson bilinear input; the toy x²+y⁴ works because the polynomial factors in ℚ(i) and the representation numbers are Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series with an explicit Grössencharacter. The polynomial k² − 1 factors only over ℤ, giving no new field, no Hecke character, no sheaf.

(O2) The number-field spectral toolkit is parity-blind here

Dictionary 04 §6: every ζ-based structure in the programme (budgets, Brown completeness, the balance split, smoothing, the explicit formula) is “either sign-blind (index parity) or additive in log n”. The explicit formula sees Λ(n), i.e. prime powers, through zeros of ζ — an additive (in log n) object. A Dirichlet character twist is periodic. Neither detects Ω-parity at positive density.

(O3) The function-field engine has no number-field counterpart

The Sawin–Shusterman proof uses that the relevant cohomology is finite-dimensional over a finite field, so the Frobenius eigenvalues are algebraic integers of bounded modulus (Weil II) and the ℓ-adic monodromy group is a geometric object. Over ℚ there is no Frobenius, no monodromy group for μ, and no finite-dimensionality to exploit.

(O4) The missing quantitative input, measured

The linear (twin-prime) sieve needs a level of distribution θ > 1/2. verify/02_level_and_growth.py (x = 10⁶, M(x;q) = maxa |Σn≤x, n≡a (q) λ(n)|) shows the crossover exactly at q ≈ x1/2:

(O5) The quadratic-polynomial Mertens function cancels surprisingly well

It is the sharpest available numerical target for the spectral route: the automorphic/spectral input over ℚ must explain square-root cancellation in Σk≤K λ(k²−1).

5. The conditional transfer statement

The attempt can be stated as a precise implication. Let

(Hθ) (level of distribution for λ) Σq ≤ xθ maxa mod q |Σn≤x, n≡a (q) λ(n)| ≪A x (log x)−A for some θ > 1/2,

(A hypothesis of the same strength, and equivalent for the purpose of the conclusion by (R1), is the pair of statements C(K) = o(K) and S1(y) = o(y). The equivalence of the conclusions is a theorem; the equivalence of these two hypotheses is not claimed.)

Then the standard linear-sieve argument (dictionary T8/T13; Sawin–Shusterman’s function-field analogue) gives S2(x) = o(x) and a positive lower bound for twin primes. (Hθ) for any θ > 1/2 is open over ℚ, and:

  • (Hθ) is attainable over 𝔽q[T] (Sawin–Shusterman, geometric input);
  • over ℚ, the only mechanisms that supply (Hθ) are Type-II/bilinear (Friedlander–Iwaniec asymptotic sieve) or equidistribution to large moduli (Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec), i.e. route 1 or 2 of dictionary §5.4 — not route 3 in isolation;
  • the number-field “spectral” structures available for λ are parity-blind (O2), and the parity-breaking sign (S3) is exactly what has no ℚ-analogue (O3).

So the honest outcome of the attempt: route 3 reduces to routes 1–2 over ℚ. The automorphic/spectral language does not add a new input for the pair (n, n+2); it identifies the input (a trace/sheaf object with square-root cancellation and a non-periodic sign) and shows that over ℚ that object is absent.
