The polygamma series, proved:
the complex Gauss product, Weierstrass differentiation, and the archimedean budget of the explicit formula

Mathlib carries the complex Gauss product limit for Γ only pointwise, so it cannot be differentiated. This paper upgrades it to a locally uniform limit on the right half-plane, differentiates it with Weierstrass’ theorem, and thereby proves the Gauss–Hurwitz series for the derivatives of the digamma function — making the archimedean derivative budget of the explicit formula unconditional for every order k ≥ 1. Machine-checked in Lean 4 + Mathlib at research/twin-prime-parity/010/lean/Rh45/PolygammaSeries.lean.

Abstract — verdict in one paragraph (§0)

The archimedean term of the explicit formula needs a bound on Ψ(k), the k-th derivative of Ψ(t) = −log π + Re ψ(1/4 + it/2). The sharp classical budget is the Gauss–Hurwitz series ψ(k)(z) = (−1)k+1 k! ∑j≥0 (z+j)−k−1, which yields ‖ψ(k)(1/4+it/2)‖ ≤ k! ∑j (1/4+j)−k−1. Mathlib proves neither the series nor any asymptotic for ψ, so the round carried this as an explicit hypothesis. We discharge it. Mathlib does have the complex Gauss product GammaSeq(s,n) = ns n! / ∏j≤n(s+j) and its pointwise limit to Γ(s); what is missing is the locally uniform statement, without which the limit cannot be differentiated. We prove that statement (tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_GammaSeq) by a circle-Cauchy argument whose driving scalar converges by ordinary dominated convergence — no Vitali/Montel theorem is needed — apply Weierstrass’ derivative theorem k times, and obtain the polygamma series (digamma_polygamma_series, a HasSum), the norm bound (norm_deriv_digamma_le), and the unconditional budget ‖Ψ(k)(t)‖ ≤ psiSeriesBound(k) for k ≥ 1 (Rh45.archimedean_deriv_bound). The lone exception is k = 0: its series is the divergent harmonic series, psiSeriesBound 0 is the junk value 0 (formally psiSeriesBound_zero), and a valid replacement bound norm_digamma_le is supplied instead. The whole development is 13/13 green, sorry-free and axiom-clean.

Keywords: polygamma function; Gauss product formula; Weierstrass differentiation theorem; complex Gamma function; explicit formula; archimedean term; Lean 4; Mathlib; locally uniform convergence; Cauchy integral formula.

0. Verdict in one paragraph

The gap is closed for every order that the composite-Simpson assembly of the round uses. The archimedean input was one hypothesis — the Euler–Maclaurin/Hurwitz series for the derivatives of ψ — carried by Rh45/Archimedean.lean as archimedean_deriv_bound_of_digamma_series. Two new modules remove it: Rh45/GammaProduct.lean proves that Mathlib’s complex Gauss product converges locally uniformly on {Re s > 0} and hence that logDeriv Γn → logDeriv Γ there, and Rh45/PolygammaSeries.lean turns that into

Theorem (the bridge; machine-checked)

theorem Rh45.archimedean_deriv_bound (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) (t : ℝ) :
    ‖iteratedDeriv k Psi t‖ ≤ psiSeriesBound k

unconditional in t and in the function, for every k ≥ 1. It rests on the polygamma series as a HasSum, so the series is not merely bounded but identified.

The single remaining caveat is k = 0, and it is not an artefact: ∑j(1/4+j)−1 diverges, so psiSeriesBound 0 is the junk value 0 (Rh45.psiSeriesBound_zero) and the k = 0 instance of the old conditional theorem carried no information. A valid explicit bound is provided instead (Rh45.norm_digamma_le), so every slot of the Leibniz budget ∑k≤4 binom(4,k) Mk P4−k now has a proved input.

1. The problem: the archimedean derivative budget

The explicit formula of the round separates a sum over the nontrivial zeros, a prime term and an archimedean term. Evaluating the last one by composite Simpson requires a bound on the fourth derivative of the product φΨ, and the Leibniz rule reduces that to bounds ‖Ψ(k)(t)‖ ≤ Pk for k = 0,1,2,3,4 on the line on which the archimedean factor lives:

Definition (the archimedean line)

digammaLine t : ℂ := (1/4 : ℂ) + ((t/2 : ℝ) : ℂ) * I     --  re = 1/4
Psi t         : ℂ := Complex.digamma (digammaLine t)                          --  ψ(1/4 + it/2)
PsiBar t      : ℝ := -Real.log Real.pi + (Psi t).re

The classical budget is the Gauss–Hurwitz series for the polygamma functions,

ψ(k)(z) = (−1)k+1 k! ∑j≥0 (z+j)−k−1,    Re z > 0,   k ≥ 1,

whose immediate consequence on Re z = 1/4 (because ‖z+j‖ ≥ Re(z+j) = 1/4 + j) is

‖ψ(k)(1/4 + it/2)‖ ≤ k! ∑j≥0 (1/4+j)−k−1.

Everything downstream of this inequality was already formalised in Archimedean.lean: the chain rule Ψ(k)(t) = (i/2)k ψ(k)(1/4+it/2) that produces the 2−k factor of 

noncomputable def psiSeriesBound (k : ℕ) : ℝ :=
  (2 : ℝ) ^ (-(k : ℤ)) * ((k.factorial : ℝ) * ∑' j : ℕ, ((1 / 4 + (j : ℝ)) ^ (k + 1))⁻¹)

the Leibniz bound abs_iteratedDeriv_four_mul_le, and the composite-Simpson assembly simpsonSum_mul_error_le retired its own quadrature hypothesis in the previous pass. The series itself is the last input, and it is what this paper proves.

Why Mathlib could not supply it

Complex.digamma := logDeriv Complex.Gamma comes with the recurrence ψ(s+1) = ψ(s) + 1/s, the reflection and duplication formulas, and digamma_nat_add_one — and nothing else: no series, no asymptotic expansion, no polygamma. The series is classically obtained from the Weierstrass product for 1/Γ, which Mathlib also lacks. What Mathlib does have is the pointwise complex Gauss product limit, and a Weierstrass theorem on locally uniform limits. The bridge is therefore to link the two: upgrade the limit’s mode of convergence, then differentiate.

2. Definitions and notation

Definition 2.1 (Gauss sequence)

noncomputable def Complex.GammaSeq (s : ℂ) (n : ℕ) :=
  (n : ℂ) ^ s * n ! / ∏ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), (s + j)

so that GammaSeq(s,n) = ns n! / (s(s+1)…(s+n)). Mathlib (Beta.lean) proves the pointwise limit GammaSeq_tendsto_Gamma : Tendsto (GammaSeq s) atTop (ℕ(Gamma s)), by showing GammaSeq(s,n) = ∫0n (1−x/n)n xs−1 dx and passing to ∫0∞ e−x xs−1 dx = Γ(s).

Definition 2.2 (digamma, polygamma, logarithmic derivative)

Complex.digamma : ℂ → ℂ := logDeriv Complex.Gamma           --  ψ
deriv^[k] (logDeriv Gamma) s                                                  --  ψ(k)(s)
logDeriv f s := deriv f s / f s

iteratedDeriv k f x and deriv^[k] f x agree in Mathlib (iteratedDeriv_eq_iterate); both notations occur below.

Definition 2.3 (the round’s budget)

psiSeriesBound k := 2^(-k) * (k! * ∑' j, ((1/4 + j)^(k+1))⁻¹)

The factor 2−k is the chain rule Ψ(k)(t) = (i/2)k ψ(k)(1/4+it/2); the series is the Gauss–Hurwitz budget at Re z = 1/4.

Definition 2.4 (right half-plane and rectangle quantities)

U := {s : ℂ | 0 < s.re}                            --  the domain of the product/limit
rectBoundary g t₀ t₁ := ∫₀⁻¹ g(x+it₀) − ∫₀⁻¹ g(x+it₁) + i∫_{t₀}^{t₁} g(1+iy) − i∫_{t₀}^{t₁} g(iy)
rightPath    g t₀ t₁ := ∫_{½}⁻¹ g(x+it₀) + i∫_{t₀}^{t₁} g(1+iy) − ∫_{½}⁻¹ g(x+it₁)

both from RouteBAnalytic.lean: rectBoundary is the boundary of [0,1]×[t₀,t₁] in Mathlib’s normal form, rightPath the three-segment path I₃ of Route B.

So the classical proof of the polygamma series — differentiate the Weierstrass product — is blocked, but a different proof of the same theorem is available: Mathlib’s pointwise Gauss product is enough if the convergence can be improved to local uniformity, because then Weierstrass’ theorem does the differentiation for free. That improvement is the crux, and it is where the two new modules begin.

4. The complex Gauss product, locally uniformly

4.1 The uniform bound

The first ingredient is a bound on GammaSeq that is uniform on circles. The naive bound from the definition is useless for this purpose: on a circle the real part of ζ ranges over an interval [σ₀,σ₁], and ‖ζs‖ = nRe s while ‖∏(s+j)‖ ≈ n! nσ₀, giving ‖GammaSeq‖ ≲ nσ₁−σ₀, which grows. The integral representation removes the growth.

Lemma 4.1 (uniform bound; machine-checked)

theorem norm_GammaSeq_le (n : ℕ) {s : ℂ} (hs : 0 < s.re) :
    ‖GammaSeq s n‖ ≤ Real.Gamma s.re

Proof. For Re s > 0 and n ≥ 1, GammaSeq(s,n) = ∫0n (1−x/n)n xs−1 dx. On 0 ≤ x ≤ n one has 0 ≤ (1−x/n)n ≤ e−x (the second inequality is 1−y ≤ e−y). Taking moduli, ‖(1−x/n)n xs−1‖ = (1−x/n)n xσ−1 ≤ e−x xσ−1 with σ = Re s, and ∫0∞ e−xxσ−1dx = Γ(σ) (Real.Gamma_eq_integral). PROVED

The same module records analyticity of each GammaSeq(·,n) on the right half-plane (analyticAt_GammaSeq): it is exp(s log n) · n!/∏(s+j), a product of an entire function and a rational function whose poles lie at the non-positive integers.

4.2 From pointwise to uniform on a disc

Fix a closed disc D = closedBall(c,r) with D ⊂ U, and choose ρ > r with closedBall(c,ρ) ⊂ U. Write Fn(z) = GammaSeq(z,n) − Γ(z). Then Fn is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of closedBall(c,ρ), and Cauchy’s formula on the circle C(c,ρ) gives, for s ∈ D,

Fn(s) = (2πi)−1 ∮C(c,ρ) Fn(ζ)/(ζ−s) dζ.

On the circle, ‖Fn(ζ)‖ ≤ C uniformly in n, where C = supζ∈C(c,ρ) (Γ(Reζ) + ‖Γ(ζ)‖) is finite by Lemma 4.1 and compactness of the sphere. Parametrising the circle and using ‖ζ−s‖ ≥ ρ−r bounds the discrepancy by a scalar:

Lemma 4.2 (uniform Cauchy bound; machine-checked)

For every n and every s ∈ closedBall(c,r),

‖Fn(s)‖ ≤ (2π)−1 ρ (ρ−r)−1 An,    An = ∫02π ‖Fn(circleMap c ρ θ)‖ dθ.

This is the non-circularity that makes the argument work. The right-hand side does not ask for convergence of Fn on the circle — only for the convergence of the scalar sequence An. And An → 0 by the ordinary dominated convergence theorem on the finite-measure interval [0,2π]: the integrand θ ↦ ‖Fn(circleMap c ρ θ)‖ tends to 0 pointwise (this is exactly Mathlib’s pointwise GammaSeq_tendsto_Gamma) and is dominated by the constant C. No Vitali, Montel or Stieltjes–Osgood theorem — none of which is in Mathlib — is required.

Consequently Fn → 0 uniformly on closedBall(c,r), and since closed discs with closedBall(c,r) ⊂ U form a neighbourhood basis, Mathlib’s characterisation of local uniformity (tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_of_forall_exists_nhds) gives:

Theorem 4.3 (locally uniform Gauss product; machine-checked)

theorem tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_GammaSeq :
    TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn (fun n : ℕ => fun s : ℂ => GammaSeq s n) Gamma
      atTop {s : ℂ | 0 < s.re}

The module also records the closed-ball form tendstoUniformlyOn_GammaSeq_closedBall which carries the explicit hypotheses 0 < ρ, r < ρ, closedBall c ρ ⊂ {s | 0 < s.re}.

Remark 4.4 (why not the integral representation?)

The integral form GammaSeq(s,n) = ∫0n(1−x/n)nxs−1dx can also be pushed to local uniformity by a uniform-in-s dominated convergence argument on compact subsets of U. The circle-Cauchy route was chosen because every ingredient is already stated in the required form in Mathlib (Cauchy formula, dominated convergence, the TendstoUniformlyOn API), whereas the parameter-dependent integral route needs the uniformity to be reassembled by hand. Both are recorded here so that the choice is auditable.

5. Weierstrass differentiation

With Theorem 4.3 in hand, differentiation of the limit is Mathlib’s Weierstrass theorem: a locally uniform limit of holomorphic functions on an open set has its derivatives converging locally uniformly to the derivatives of the limit.

Theorem 5.1 (derivatives of the product limit; machine-checked)

theorem tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_deriv_GammaSeq (k : ℕ) :
    TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn
      (fun n : ℕ => fun s : ℂ => deriv^[k] (fun w => GammaSeq w n) s)
      (fun s : ℂ => deriv^[k] Gamma s) atTop {s : ℂ | 0 < s.re}

Obtained by iterating TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.deriv, each step feeding analyticAt_GammaSeq (a holomorphic function is DifferentiableOn the open half-plane) and using that the preceding limit is holomorphic there by TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn.

The logarithmic derivative is then obtained by a uniform quotient lemma: on every compact K ⋐ U the limit Γ is bounded away from 0 (Gamma_ne_zero_of_re_pos plus continuity and compactness), and once GammaSeq(·,n) → Γ uniformly on K the same lower bound holds for GammaSeq for large n; hence deriv GammaSeq / GammaSeq → deriv Γ / Γ uniformly on K.

Theorem 5.2 (log-derivative limit; machine-checked)

theorem tendstoLocallyUniformlyOn_logDeriv_GammaSeq :
    TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn (fun n : ℕ => logDeriv (fun w : ℂ => GammaSeq w n))
      (logDeriv Gamma) atTop {s : ℂ | 0 < s.re}

Finally, the elementary identity that turns the product into a finite object. Writing GammaSeq(s,n) = ns · n!/∏j≤n(s+j) and applying logDeriv in s gives log n from ns = es log n and −∑ (s+j)−1 from the product, with the constant n! contributing nothing:

Lemma 5.3 (finite log-derivative; machine-checked)

theorem logDeriv_GammaSeq (n : ℕ) (hn : n ≠ 0) {s : ℂ} (hs : 0 < s.re) :
    logDeriv (fun w : ℂ => GammaSeq w n) s
      = Complex.log (n : ℂ) - ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), (s + (j : ℂ))⁻¹

A local form logDeriv_GammaSeq_of_ne drops the hypothesis 0 < s.re in favour of ∀ j ≤ n, s+j ≠ 0, which is what the iteration in § 6 needs.

6. The polygamma (Gauss–Hurwitz) series

Everything is now in place. Differentiate Lemma 5.3 k times. The constant log n dies for k ≥ 1, and each summand (s+j)−1 differentiates to (−1)k k! (s+j)−k−1, so the sign is (−1)k+1:

Lemma 6.1 (iterated finite derivative; machine-checked)

theorem iteratedDeriv_logDeriv_GammaSeq (n : ℕ) (hn : n ≠ 0) {s : ℂ}
    (hs : ∀ j ≤ n, s + (j : ℂ) ≠ 0) (k : ℕ) :
    deriv^[k] (logDeriv (fun w : ℂ => GammaSeq w n)) s
      = if k = 0 then Complex.log (n : ℂ) - ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), (s + (j : ℂ))⁻¹
        else (-1 : ℂ) ^ (k + 1) * (k.factorial : ℂ)
               * ∑ j ∈ Finset.range (n + 1), (s + (j : ℂ)) ^ (-(k : ℤ) - 1)

Now take the limit. Applying Theorem 5.2 together with Theorem 5.1’s iteration to the functions logDeriv GammaSeqn gives deriv^[k] (logDeriv GammaSeqn) → deriv^[k] (logDeriv Γ) locally uniformly on U, hence pointwise; evaluating at s and substituting Lemma 6.1 identifies the partial sums. A HasSum is exactly the statement that the partial sums converge, so:

Theorem 6.2 (the polygamma series; machine-checked)

theorem digamma_polygamma_series (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) {s : ℂ} (hs : 0 < s.re) :
    HasSum (fun j : ℕ => (s + (j : ℂ)) ^ (-(k : ℤ) - 1))
      (((-1 : ℂ) ^ (k + 1) * (k.factorial : ℂ))⁻¹ * deriv^[k] (logDeriv Gamma) s)

Equivalently, and in the classical form of the Gauss–Hurwitz series, ψ(k)(s) = (−1)k+1 k! ∑j≥0 (s+j)−k−1 for Re s > 0 and k ≥ 1. The companion summable_polygamma_series records summability of the series.

Remark 6.3 (where k ≥ 1 is used, twice)

  • the log n term of Lemma 5.3 survives deriv^[0] but is killed by deriv^[k] for every k ≥ 1;
  • the series ∑ (s+j)−k−1 converges on Re s > 0 only for k ≥ 1; for k = 0 it is the harmonic series (see § 8).

7. The norm bound and the archimedean budget

The series is a complex identity; the bound only needs the triangle inequality and the elementary inequality ‖s+j‖ ≥ Re(s+j) = Re s + j. Since ‖(s+j)−k−1‖ = ‖s+j‖−k−1 and ‖∑'‖ ≤ ∑' ‖·‖ (norm_tsum_le_tsum_norm), and the majorant is a shifted p-series with p = k+1 ≥ 2:

Lemma 7.1 (norm bound; machine-checked)

theorem norm_deriv_digamma_le (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) {s : ℂ} (hs : 0 < s.re) :
    ‖deriv^[k] (logDeriv Gamma) s‖
      ≤ (k.factorial : ℝ) * ∑' j : ℕ, ((s.re + (j : ℝ)) ^ (k + 1))⁻¹

Termwise norm_polygamma_term_le: ‖(s+j)−k−1‖ ≤ (Re s + j)−k−1, using Complex.abs_re_le_norm.

Specialising to s = digammaLine t, where Re s = 1/4, and composing with the chain rule already in Archimedean.lean gives the round’s budget. This is the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 7.2 (archimedean derivative budget, unconditional; machine-checked)

theorem Rh45.digamma_series_bound (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) (t : ℝ) :
    ‖iteratedDeriv k Complex.digamma (digammaLine t)‖
      ≤ (k.factorial : ℝ) * ∑' j : ℕ, ((1 / 4 + (j : ℝ)) ^ (k + 1))⁻¹

theorem Rh45.archimedean_deriv_bound (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) (t : ℝ) :
    ‖iteratedDeriv k Psi t‖ ≤ psiSeriesBound k

The old conditional form archimedean_deriv_bound_of_digamma_series is retained for compatibility, but its hypothesis is now a theorem (digamma_series_bound), so archimedean_deriv_bound carries no analytic input at all. Both are [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]-clean.

Consequence for the round

The Leibniz budget ∑k≤4 binom(4,k) Mk P4−k of abs_iteratedDeriv_four_mul_le takes Pk = psiSeriesBound k for k = 1,2,3,4 with no hypothesis, and P0 from § 8. The composite-Simpson assembly simpsonSum_mul_error_le then yields the round’s (b−a)h⁴/180 · ∑k binom(4,k) Mk P4−k unconditionally.

7.1 The budget, and its sharpness

Two pictures make the theorem concrete. The budget is finite and explicit for every k, and — the point that is easy to miss — it is attained at t = 0. At s = 1/4 every term of the series is real and positive and ‖s+j‖ = 1/4 + j exactly, so the triangle inequality used in Lemma 7.1 is an equality; hence

‖Ψ(k)(0)‖ = 2−k k! ∑j (1/4+j)−k−1 = psiSeriesBound k.

The right-hand side is a Hurwitz zeta value, psiSeriesBound k = 2−k k! ζ(k+1, 1/4), so the budget of the round is not a lossy constant: it is the exact t = 0 value of the quantity it bounds.

Remark 7.3 (is the sharpness formalised?)

The equality ‖Ψ(k)(0)‖ = psiSeriesBound k is an immediate consequence of the machine-checked series digamma_polygamma_series evaluated at s = 1/4 ∈ ℝ, where all terms are positive reals; the derivation is one line from that theorem, and the 40-digit numerical check is the self-test of lib/polygamma_figs.py. It is stated here as a corollary of a proved theorem rather than as a separately formalised lemma, so that the paper does not overstate what the Lean file contains.

8. The case k = 0, handled honestly

The formalisation does not paper over the degenerate case; it records it. For k = 0 the series is ∑j≥0 (1/4+j)−1, which is the harmonic series up to a shift:

Lemma 8.1 (the k = 0 series diverges; machine-checked)

theorem Rh45.not_summable_quarter_add_inv :
    ¬ Summable (fun j : ℕ => ((1 / 4 + (j : ℝ)) ^ (0 + 1))⁻¹)

theorem Rh45.psiSeriesBound_zero : psiSeriesBound 0 = 0

Consequently psiSeriesBound 0 is the junk value of a divergent tsum, and the k = 0 instance of archimedean_deriv_bound_of_digamma_series asserts ‖Ψ(t)‖ ≤ 0 — unusable, exactly as the round’s own documentation had recorded.

What the round actually needs is a finite bound on ‖Ψ‖, and any valid one will do. The replacement uses the Weierstrass partial-sum form ψ(s) = −γ + ∑j(1/(j+1) − (s+j)−1) (obtained from logDeriv_GammaSeq together with the harmonic limit Real.tendsto_harmonic_sub_log) and bounds each term by the triangle inequality:

Theorem 8.2 (valid k = 0 bound; machine-checked)

theorem Rh45.norm_digamma_le (t : ℝ) :
    ‖Psi t‖ ≤ Real.eulerMascheroniConstant
      + (3 / 4 + |t| / 2) * (∑' j : ℕ, (((j : ℝ) + 1) * (1 / 4 + (j : ℝ)))⁻¹) + 4

The shape is explicit: γ from the constant, the factor 3/4 + |t|/2 ≥ ‖s−1‖ times a convergent series from the difference terms, and a fixed slack 4 absorbing the finitely many head terms. The bound is crude by design; it is only there so that the P0 slot of the Leibniz budget is provable.
