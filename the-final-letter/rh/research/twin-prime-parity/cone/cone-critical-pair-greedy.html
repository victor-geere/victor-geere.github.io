Abstract

The rh42 budgets live on a signed alphabet α_n = (−1)^n (n+2)^{−p}; the associated helix has unit radius, so it carries no index coordinate and the word factors through the height sin(2πθ). Replacing the unit radius by the radial decay n^{−p} opens the helix into a cone whose reciprocal radius is the index n: the helix is the degenerate p = 0 cone, and the index becomes a geometric coordinate. On the cone the additive shift n ↦ n+2 is an exact radial map on the same ray, and a pair acquires two canonical real invariants: the symmetric pair weight r_n r_{n+2} = (n(n+2))^{−p} and the antisymmetric separation r_n − r_{n+2} ≤ 2p n^{−p−1}.

Two consequences are proved or certified here.

  1. The one-power dichotomy. Differencing along the shift costs exactly one power

of n, so the shift-antisymmetric channel is absolutely convergent for every p > 0 and never sees the mean value; the mean value lives only in the undifferenced pair weight, whose critical exponent is p = 1/2.

  1. The pair-greedy threshold. Grouping the positive half-series into consecutive

radial pairs gives the pair alphabet a_k = (4k+2)^{−p} + (4k+4)^{−p}. Brown's first completeness condition is the binding one (verified for k = 0..7), and it reads ζ(p) ≥ 2 + 2^{1−p}. Its root p_pair = 1.4740898896… slots strictly between the positive-endpoint threshold pP = 1.4649845878… and the positive-half completeness threshold p* = 1.7286472390….

The ordering pP < p_pair < p* is now a Lean theorem (ConeLean.pair_threshold_between, no sorry); the numerically certified inputs it consumes are the four brackets of §3.2 and the constant of §3.3, all of which are proved inside the same development.

Numerically (real arithmetic only, N = 10⁸): the critical-cone pair series Σ λ(n)λ(n+2)/√(n(n+2)) converges to −0.8637…; the residue ladder K(ε) = ε Σ f(n)(n(n+2))^{−(1+ε)/2} → 0; and the majorant of the same cone has residue 1 regardless of the mean value. That contrast is the parity obstruction drawn on the cone: the only pole real-variable positivity can force at p = 1/2 is the spurious majorant pole.

1. The cone

Fix a real decay exponent p > 0 and a real angular parameter t. Place the n-th term of a Dirichlet-type expansion at

C_n(t) = ( n^{−p} cos(t log n),  n^{−p} sin(t log n),  n )  ∈ ℝ³ ,   n = 1, 2, 3, …

with sheet coordinates (r, φ, h) = (n^{−p}, t log n, n). The points lie on the cone r = h^{−p}.

Only real numbers are used. cos, sin and n^{−p} are real functions; no complex number, no analytic continuation and no phase n^{it} is required. The signed (Tau) alphabet is the case φ_n = πn + t log n, since cos(πn + ψ) = (−1)^n cos ψ.

Three facts, all with short proofs (formalised in lean/ConeLean/Cone.lean).

1.1 The index is a coordinate. The map n ↦ C_n(t) is injective, and the readout is n = r^{−1/p} (for p = 1, n = 1/r). Every term has its own radius, so the expansion is decodable term by term.

Proof. (n^{−p})^{−1/p} = n^{(−p)(−1/p)} = n, and r_n = 0 only for n = 0. ∎

1.2 The helix is the p = 0 cone. At p = 0, r ≡ 1 and the readout is constant: no index information. This is exactly rh42's "the word cannot see the turns" — the helix is the zero-decay cone, and adding radial decay is what restores the lost coordinate.

1.3 Cone dictionary. The one-point weight is n^{−p} (the line σ = p of the Dirichlet series); the pair weight is (n(n+2))^{−p} (the line σ = 2p).

2. The two channels, and the one-power dichotomy

The shift n ↦ n+2 sends h ↦ h+2, 1/r ↦ 1/r + 2 (exact), r ↦ r·(n/(n+2))^p, and on the Tau alphabet it preserves the sign: (−1)^{n+2} = (−1)^n. So n and n+2 lie on the same ray of the cone, two sheets apart. The pair has two canonical real invariants.

channelinvariantcontraction with f(n) = λ(n)λ(n+2)behaviour
symmetricpair weight r_n r_{n+2} = (n(n+2))^{−p}C_f(p) = Σ f(n)(n(n+2))^{−p}pole at the critical cone p = 1/2
antisymmetricseparation r_n − r_{n+2} ≤ 2p n^{−p−1}B(p) = Σ f(n)(r_n − r_{n+2})absolutely convergent for every p > 0

Lemma 1 (one-power dichotomy). For p > 0 and n ≥ 1,

0 ≤ n^{−p} − (n+2)^{−p} = n^{−p}(1 − (1 + 2/n)^{−p}) ≤ 2p n^{−p−1} ,

so Σ_n |n^{−p} − (n+2)^{−p}| < ∞ for every p > 0.

Proof. The function x ↦ (1+x)^{−p} is convex on [0,∞); its tangent at 0 is 1 − px, so (1+2/n)^{−p} ≥ 1 − 2p/n, giving the bound; Σ n^{−p−1} converges because p + 1 > 1. (Equivalently, the mean value theorem on [n, n+2] for x ↦ x^{−p}: |f(n+2) − f(n)| ≤ 2·sup|f′| = 2p n^{−p−1}.) ∎

Corollary 2. For every bounded f, the shift-difference channel B(p) is absolutely convergent for every p > 0, before any cancellation in f is used. Hence:

the mean value of λ(n)λ(n+2) lives only in the undifferenced (symmetric) pair weight. Any statistic that differences along the shift has abscissa one lower and cannot see it.

At p = 1 the two invariants coincide up to a factor: 1/n − 1/(n+2) = 2/(n(n+2)), so B(1) = 2 C_f(1) exactly (numerically B(1) = −0.7855111755, 2C_f(1) = −0.7855111755).

3. The pair-greedy rule, and a new threshold

3.1 The rule

Definition 3 (radial-pair alphabet). The positive half-series is P(p) = Σ_{m even ≥ 2} m^{−p} = 2^{−p} ζ(p). Group it into consecutive radial pairs — the pairs {2,4}, {6,8}, {10,12}, … of even sheets, all of which lie on the same ray of the cone:

a_k = (4k+2)^{−p} + (4k+4)^{−p} ,      k = 0, 1, 2, … ,      Σ_k a_k = P(p) .

(Scalar form; for t ≠ 0 replace each term by its vector v_n = n^{−p}(cos(t log n), sin(t log n)) and the pair by the resultant w_k = v_{4k+2} + v_{4k+4}; for large k the pair is nearly radial and |w_k| → a_k.)

Definition 4 (cone pair-greedy rule). Fix p > 0 and a target x ∈ [0, 1]. Put r_0 = x. At pair index k = 0, 1, 2, …:

  • take the pair iff r_k ≥ a_k (same ray, no overshoot), then r_{k+1} = r_k − a_k;
  • otherwise skip it permanently, r_{k+1} = r_k.

The decision uses the pair's radial coordinate (its length and its position in the pair sequence). The rule is the greedy on the decreasing positive sequence (a_k), so its reachable set is the set of sub-sums of (a_k).

3.2 The completeness threshold

Brown's criterion. The sub-sums of a positive decreasing sequence (a_k) fill [0, Σ a_k] iff a_k ≤ Σ_{j>k} a_j for every k. For the pair alphabet the condition at k = 0 is the binding one, and this is now a theorem for 1 < p ≤ 3/2 (pair_brown_all_k in lean/ConeLean/PairCensus.lean: every k ≥ 1 condition holds), together with the equivalence a_0 ≤ Σ_{j≥1} a_j ⟺ Ψ(p) ≥ 0 (pair_brown_zero_iff). Numerically the ratios a_k / Σ_{j>k} a_j are 1.0000, 0.3173, 0.1896, 0.1353, … at p = p_pair, so k = 0 is binding and later conditions are slack by a factor ≥ 3 (3.15 at p = p_pair):

k = 0 :  a_0 ≤ P(p) − a_0   ⟺   2(2^{−p} + 4^{−p}) ≤ 2^{−p} ζ(p)
                            ⟺   ζ(p) ≥ 2 + 2^{1−p} .

Proposition 5 (the pair threshold). The pair-greedy rule is complete at exponent p iff (first condition) ζ(p) ≥ 2 + 2^{1−p}. Since ζ is strictly decreasing and the right side is strictly decreasing, the first condition is equivalent to p ≤ p_pair, where

Ψ(p) − Ψ(q) = Σ_{k≥0} [ (k+3)^{−p} − (k+3)^{−q} ] − (2^{−p} − 2^{−q})

4. The critical cone p = 1/2

4.1 The transform and its residue

Define the cone pair transform

C_f(p) = Σ_{n≥1} λ(n) λ(n+2) (n(n+2))^{−p} ,        absolutely convergent for p > 1/2 .

Partial summation with S(x) = Σ_{n≤x} f(n), f(n) = λ(n)λ(n+2), gives

C_f(p) = 2p ∫_1^∞ S(x) x^{−2p−1} (1 + O(1/x)) dx ,

hence, as p ↓ 1/2 (write ε = 2p − 1):

  • S(x) ~ c x (positive density) ⟹ C_f(p) ~ 2pc/ε, i.e. ε C_f(p) → c;
  • S(x) = o(x) ⟹ C_f(p) = o(1/ε), i.e. ε C_f(p) → 0.

The two-point mean value is the residue of the cone pair transform at the critical cone p = 1/2. In cone coordinates, two-point Chowla for the shift 2 is the statement "the cone pair transform has no pole at p = 1/2".

4.2 What is a theorem on the critical cone

At p = 1/2 the pair weight differs from the log-Chowla weight by an absolutely summable error:

|1/√(n(n+2)) − 1/n| ≤ 1/n²     (because 1/(n+2) ≤ 1/√(n(n+2)) ≤ 1/n and
                                1/n − 1/(n+2) = 2/(n(n+2)) ≤ 2/n²),

so for bounded f, Σ f(n)/√(n(n+2)) = Σ f(n)/n + O(1), and Tao's logarithmic Chowla theorem gives

Σ_{n≤x} f(n)/√(n(n+2)) = o(log x) .

The antisymmetric channel is even better behaved (Lemma 1 and Corollary 2): absolutely convergent. So everything readable on the critical cone is theorem-level; only the residue — the mean value — is open.

4.3 The parity obstruction, drawn on the cone

The majorant of the same cone is Σ 1/√(n(n+2)), which grows like log x (§5: 0.9800 · log x at x = 10⁸). Its residue is 1:

lim_{ε↓0} ε Σ_{n≥1} (n(n+2))^{−(1+ε)/2} = 1 ,

independently of the mean value of f. So the only pole that non-negativity arguments (Landau, Wiener–Ikehara) can force at p = 1/2 is the spurious majorant pole; the signed series need not have one, and numerically it does not. This is the parity obstruction in cone coordinates: the positivity is in the majorant, not in the correlation.

The proof of pair_brown_all_k is the chain a_k ≤ 2(4k+2)^{-p} ≤ 4^{-p}(k+2)^{1-p}/(p-1) ≤ T_k. The middle step is 2(p-1)·4^p·(4k+2)^{-p} ≤ (k+2)^{1-p} (pair_key), proved by

2(p-1)·(2/(2k+1))^p ≤ 2(p-1)·(2/(k+2))^p = 2(p-1)·2^p·(k+2)^{-p}
                    ≤ 3·(k+2)^{-p} ≤ (k+2)·(k+2)^{-p} = (k+2)^{1-p},

i.e. by the two rational facts 2(p-1)·2^p ≤ 3 for p ≤ 3/2 (from 2(p-1) ≤ 1 and 2^p ≤ 2^{3/2} ≤ 3, the last certified by 8 ≤ 27) and 3 ≤ k+2 for k ≥ 1. The margin over the sharp case p = 3/2 (2(p-1)·2^p = 2.828 ≤ 3) is a factor 1.06; there is no appeal to a limit or to numerics.

6.4 What is not formalised here

Two items remain outside the machine-checked statement, and neither is claimed:

  • the passage to the infinite alphabet. The census lemmas are stated for finite

windows and for the infinite tail only through the gap lemma (pairAlphabet_subsum_gap). Saying that the reachable set of the whole infinite pair alphabet equals [0, A] (for p ≤ p_pair) or [0,T_1] ∪ [a_0, a_0+T_1] (for p_pair < p ≤ 3/2) needs a closure/limit statement: every s ∈ [0,A] is a limit of finite sub-sums. The greedy construction does this (the residual is dominated by the tail Σ_{j>k}a_j → 0), but it is not yet mechanised; §5.5 is the numerical evidence, not a proof;

  • the residue lim_{ε↓0} ε C_f(1/2+ε/2), which is open (§7 item 1) — the cone

form of Σ λ(n)λ(n+2) = o(x).

7. What is open, and what would count as progress

  1. The residue is settled; o(x) is not. S(x) = o(x) is open; the cone

localises it to the pole of C_f at p = 1/2. Two things are now machine-checked (§6.6): the residue (the Abelian mean) vanishes whenever the log-average input Σ_{n≤x}f(n)/n = o(log x) holds — which is Tao's log-averaged two-point Chowla theorem — and the residue is strictly weaker than o(x) (the Cesàro direction o(x) ⟹ residue 0 is abelMean_tendsto_zero, §6.6). So the residue does not open the door to o(x): it is implied by it, not equivalent to it. (The equivalence of o(x) with the four-density-1/4 statement is machine-checked, §6.5.) Finite data cannot decide it (finite-prefix indeterminacy), so the numerical verdict of §5 is evidence, not proof.

  1. The cone form of the barrier (OP-3). *No sign-blind cone statistic sees the

residue.* The natural next experiment is the pair-greedy support statistic: compute C_{f,A}(p) for the support A of the cone pair-greedy rule and compare its residue proxy against size-matched random supports (the rh47 B1 protocol applied to the pair channel).

  1. The infinite reachable set. The census mechanism is machine-checked

(SubSumIco_succ, SubSumIco_gap, SubSumIco_glue, SubSumIco_two_intervals, SubSumIco_eq_Icc_of_brown, pairAlphabet_subsum_gap) and the numerics give 1 interval below p_pair and 2 above it, with the gap 2a_0 − A; what is not formalised is the closure statement that identifies the reachable set of the infinite alphabet with those intervals (§6.4).

  1. Higher windows. Only the first failure is certified (k = 0); the numerics

show the depth rising to j = 3 at p = 3, and a per-p description of the interval count for large p is untouched.

  1. General shifts. Everything here is stated for the shift 2; the cone algebra

works for any shift h, and the pair alphabet generalises to h-tuples. Whether the h-dependence of the threshold carries arithmetic information is open.
