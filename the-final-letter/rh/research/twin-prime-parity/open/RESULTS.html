Threshold asymptotics and the reachable-fraction decay

A machine-checked asymptotic expansion for a positive half-series, formalised in Lean 4 with Mathlib. The programme's internal labels for the two questions treated here are OP-5 (the threshold-index asymptotics) and OP-6 (the reachable-fraction decay); both are defined in §2.4.

Abstract

We formalise in Lean 4 + Mathlib the critical-scaling asymptotics of the normalised boundary sum Φp(n) = ∑j≥1 (1 + j/n)−p, whose threshold equation Φp(n) = 1 defines the threshold index Pn studied in the programme's threshold-index problem. The scaling is p = un with u ∈ [1/2, 1] — so p and n are comparable, n ≈ p/log 2, and the summands decay by a factor tending to 1/2, i.e. the sum is geometrically dominated.

The main deliverable is a fully proved O(1/n) expansion with an explicit constant, | Φun(n) − 1/(eu − 1) | ≤ C/n,    C = ½ ∑k≥1 k2e−k/4 + 8, together with its second-order refinement | Φun(n) − 1/(eu − 1) − (u/2n)·ΦS(u) | ≤ C′/n2,    ΦS(u) = ∑k≥1 k2e−uk, the inversion of the threshold equation to u = log 2 + (3 log 2)/(2n) + O(n−2), and the resulting threshold-index statements Pn = (n + 3/2) log 2 + O(1) and its full form with the c/n term, proved in Asymptotic.lean (the third-order input is in Op56/PhiThird.lean). All of it is uniform in u ∈ [1/2, 1], which is exactly what makes the inversion applicable when u = Pn/n itself varies with n.

The work also retracts a misdiagnosis recorded in the earlier notes: the obstruction was described as the absence of Euler–Maclaurin summation for the Hurwitz tail at p ≫ n. Euler–Maclaurin is indeed absent from Mathlib, but it is also the wrong tool: at this scaling p/n → log 2, not p ≫ n, and every quantity of interest is controlled by the geometric reference sum ∑k e−uk together with elementary Taylor bounds on log(1 + x). The verified development follows that route.

1. Description: the problem and why this scaling

Let bn = 2−p(n+1)−p be the positive half-series of the programme, with budget P(p) = ∑n≥0 bn = 2−pζ(p) and tail TN = ∑n≥N bn. Brown's criterion at index N is the inequality bN ≤ TN+1, written IsComplete p N; the threshold nmin(p) is the least such N.

Three regimes matter, and only one of them is subtle:

  • Small n (n ≪ p): summands decay geometrically fast; easy.
  • Threshold (n ≈ p/log 2): summands decay by a factor → 1/2; the sum is dominated by roughly log2 many of its terms but is not finite-length.
  • Large n (n ≫ p): the tail is a Hurwitz-type sum; here Euler–Maclaurin would apply.

The threshold-index problem lives entirely in the middle regime. Numerically p/N = 1.000, 0.833, 0.769, 0.714, 0.699 at p = 2, 5, 10, 20, 100: the ratio tends to log 2 ≈ 0.6931, so p and n are comparable and p ≫ n is false. At p = 100, N = 143, eight terms already give 99.75% of the sum.

The earlier notes attributed the difficulty to the absence of Euler–Maclaurin for the Hurwitz tail at p ≫ n. This document records the correction: Euler–Maclaurin is both absent from Mathlib and irrelevant here. The right statement is the one proved below — the summands decay like 2−j, and the whole expansion is governed by ∑k≥1 e−uk = 1/(eu − 1) and elementary Taylor bounds on log(1 + x).

2.3 The elementary analytic inputs

Three Taylor/remainder bounds, all proved from scratch (Mathlib has the first-order log x ≤ x − 1 but not the higher-order two-sided forms needed here):

(a) Second-order log upper. For x ≥ 0: log(1+x) ≤ x − x2/2 + x3/3  — log_one_add_le_cubic. Proved by monotoneOn_of_deriv_nonneg on g(x) = x − x2/2 + x3/3 − log(1+x), since g′ = x3/(1+x) ≥ 0 and g(0) = 0.

(b) Fourth-order log lower. For x ≥ 0: x − x2/2 + x3/3 − x4/4 ≤ log(1+x)  — log_one_add_ge_quartic, from h(x) = x4/4 − x + x2/2 − x3/3 + log(1+x) with h′ = x4/(1+x) ≥ 0.

(c) Exponential remainders. |ez − 1 − z| ≤ ezz2 for z ≥ 0 (abs_exp_sub_one_sub_le), and |ez − 1 − z − z2/2| ≤ ezz3 for z ≥ 0 (abs_exp_sub_one_sub_sub_sq_le). The second uses Real.exp_bound below z ≤ 1 and triviality above.

Combining (a) and (b) gives the remainder sandwich used throughout: 0 ≤ x − log(1+x) − x2/2 + x3/3 ≤ x4/4,   (phi_r_bounds).

proved Second-order expansion. Phi_second_order: ∃ C > 0,   ∀ev n,   ∀ u ∈ [1/2, 1]:   | Φun(n) − 1/(eu − 1) − (u/2n)ΦS(u) | ≤ C/n2, with the explicit value C = ΦK4/4 + ΦK3/3 + 3, where ΦK4 = ∑k k4e−k/4 and ΦK3 = ∑k k3e−k/2.

5. Proof architecture

5.1 Why the sum is geometrically dominated

Write s = un, x = k/n, sx = uk, Tk = (1+x)−s, Gk = e−uk. The comparisons give Gk ≤ Tk ≤ Gkesx2/2, and since x ≤ 1 the factor is at most euk/2, which is dominated by Gk's own decay. Hence Φun(n) and ∑kGk = 1/(eu − 1) agree to O(1/n) after a Taylor step, and the whole expansion is a Taylor expansion of ed, where d = s(x − log(1+x)).

5.2 The first-order theorem

Split Φun(n) − 1/(eu−1) = ∑k<n(Tk−Gk) + ∑k≥n(Tk−Gk). The tail is ≤ ∑k>nTk ≤ n·21−un/(un−1), exponentially small. For k ≤ n, Tk − Gk = Gk(ed − 1) with d = y − eps, and a four-step chain Tk − Gk − Gky = Gk[ (ed − 1 − d) + eps ],    |ed − 1 − d| ≤ edd2 ≤ Tky2, gives ∑k≤n|Tk − Gk − Gky| ≤ ∑ke−uk/2(y2 + eps) = O(n−2) after the exponential-remainder bound (c). Adding ∑kGky = (u/2n)ΦS(u) + O(n−2) and bounding ΦS by a constant finishes the O(n−2) second-order statement; truncating at the O(n−1) level gives the stated first-order theorem with C = PhiC.

5.3 The structural fact that makes it work

The bound |Tk − Gk − Gky| ≤ Tky2 + Gkeps looks dangerous, because it involves Gked and ed is enormous for large k. It is in fact harmless: Gked = Tk exactly, so the "dangerous" factor cancels against Gk's decay. This was the make-or-break observation; an earlier attempt that wrote Gkey instead produced a bound that blows up.

5.4 The inversion

From Φun(n) = 1 and the second-order expansion, put s = 1 − 1/(eu−1) = (u/2n)ΦS(u) + ε. Then eu − 2 = s/(1 − s),    |s| ≤ 16/n,    u − log 2 = log(1 + s/2) + O(n−2) = s/2 + O(n−2), and s/2 = (u/4n)ΦS(u) + ε/2 = (u/4n)( ΦS(log 2) + O(n−1) ) + O(n−2) = (3 log 2)/(2n) + O(n−2). The 6 in the numerator is ΦS(log 2). The proof was landed incrementally as five sub-lemmas (threshold_one_sub … threshold_second_term), after a single 200-line attempt had failed on a sign error that the smaller lemmas exposed immediately.

5.5 The uniform step and the threshold-index theorem

Uniformity is what lets the threshold inversion be applied to u = Pn/n, which varies with n. With threshold_second_order_uniform in hand: | Pn/n − log 2 − (3 log 2)/(2n) | ≤ C/n2  ⇒  | Pn − n log 2 − (3/2)log 2 | ≤ C/n, which is op5_threshold_second_order. Prior to this, only op5_threshold_leading (O(1) with no constant) was available.

5.6 The third-order term (now formalised in Op56/PhiThird.lean)

The c/n term of thresholdAsym is the coefficient of n−2 in the expansion of Φ. Expanding ed one order further with d = un(x2/2 − x3/3) + O(x4) gives D(u) = −(u/3)∑kk3e−uk + (u2/8)∑kk4e−uk, and at u = log 2 this is −(26 log 2)/3 + (150 (log 2)2)/8 = 3.001218446. The residual E = Φun(n) − 1/(eu−1) − (u/2n)ΦS(u) − D(u)/n2 satisfies E·n3 → 4.34 at u = log 2 (and analogous constants at u = 0.6, 0.8), confirming O(n−3) and the correctness of D. Feeding this through the inversion is what produces c = 3(log 2)2 − (25/12)log 2.

6. What this means, and why it is useful

6.1 A false obstruction has been replaced by a true theorem. The notes recorded that the threshold-index problem was blocked on Euler–Maclaurin for the Hurwitz tail at p ≫ n. That diagnosis was wrong on two counts. First, the regime is p/n → log 2, not p ≫ n. Second, the correct tool is not an asymptotic summation formula at all but a geometric comparison: Tk is sandwiched between Gk = e−uk and Gkeuk2/(2n), and the whole expansion is a Taylor expansion of ed in d = s(x − log(1+x)). No Hurwitz-zeta asymptotics are involved. This materially changes what a future formalisation should attempt.

6.2 The deliverable is unconditional. PhiAsymptotic.lean compiles with no sorry and no axiom, and contains every item the objective names: the term-wise comparison lemmas, the geometric-sum identity ∑k≥1e−uk = 1/(eu−1), the finite/infinite split with the explicit geometric tail bound, and the resulting O(1/n) theorem — with an explicit constant PhiC = ½∑kk2e−k/4 + 8, which is stronger than the objective's "∃C" phrasing.

6.3 The results are effective, not merely asymptotic. Every constant is explicit and every lemma is stated for concrete n (e.g. n ≥ 32, n ≥ 4) rather than "for large n" in the informal sense. In particular threshold_scaling gives |u − log 2| ≤ 2·PhiC/n with PhiC computable, and the O(1/n2) inversion has an explicit constant 1008 + C0/2 + 8ΦL.

6.4 Uniformity is the reusable asset. The seven _uniform lemmas are not a technicality: any application where the scaling parameter varies with the index — which is exactly the situation for the threshold sequence Pn — requires them. The reusable pattern is that uniformity is free when the parameter-dependence sits in a closed form monotone in that parameter.

6.5 Lean-specific engineering results. The development documents several reusable facts about Mathlib that are not obvious: Euler–Maclaurin and summationByParts are absent; there is no abs_tsum_le_tsum_abs, so the tsum triangle inequality must be assembled from one-sided bounds; there is no summable_of_nonneg_of_le; there is no le_or_lt (use le_total) and no Real.exp_le_one_iff; tsum_pow_mul_geometric_of_norm_lt_one gives all geometric moments at once via Stirling numbers. These are recorded in the file headers and in the session notes.

7. Applicability

7.1 Within this programme

  • Threshold-index asymptotics. Proved in full: the second-order theorem Pn = (n + 3/2)log 2 + O(1), the third-order refinement Pn − thresholdAsym(n) = O(n−2), and the index form.
  • Reachable-fraction decay. The census-ratio law is already proved in Census.lean; composing it with the threshold-index asymptotics (proved) gives L(p) = p log p − (1 + log log 2)p + O(1), equivalently the exponential decay of measure(S1)/P(p).
  • Brown's criterion. Exact.lean contains the discrete existence result (exists_isComplete, witness N = 4p) and the spec/minimality of nmin; nmin is now connected to the Φ-threshold, which yields the index form.

7.2 Method transfer

The technique — splitting a sum into a geometric reference part and a Taylor-corrected remainder, with all constants explicit and all bounds uniform in the scaling parameter — applies to any threshold defined by an equation of the form ∑j(1 + j/n)−p = 1. That includes hypergeometric threshold problems, urn/occupancy thresholds with geometric weights, and Brown's-criterion-style minimal-index problems in additive combinatorics, where the index at which a sum crosses a budget is asymptotically linear in the exponent.

7.3 What is not claimed

No claim is made about the twin-prime parity problem itself; this document covers the asymptotic analysis of one criterion used in it. The three statements in Asymptotic.lean (the full threshold form, the index form, and the reachable-fraction decay) are all proved; Exact.lean retains one documented sorry (tailSum_integral_lower, a negative result: the antitone integral test is insufficient to prove existence because it discards the discrete mass TN+1 ≈ 2bN+1; existence was proved discretely instead).
