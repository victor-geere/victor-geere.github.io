## Statement

A Lee–Yang theorem is a theorem about a partition function with positive weights and positive (ferromagnetic) couplings. The plain partial sum \(H_M^{(s)}=\sum_{m\le M}m^{-s}\) has positive weights and no couplings — it is the one-particle primon gas of Section F.6 — and Montgomery's theorem [\[40\]](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/publication.html#ref-partialsums) says its zeros are not confined to any line. The polygon \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\) has weights \(c_m^{(N)}\) that are *negative* at the multiples of \(N\); by Lemma C.2 it is a combination of complex-weighted sums \(\sum\omega^{jn}n^{-s}\). So neither object is a candidate for the circle theorem as it stands: positivity of the register weights is not the relevant positivity, and the filter destroys even that. Knauf's chain [\[30\]](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/publication.html#ref-knauf), [\[31\]](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/publication.html#ref-contucci) confines its zeros because its couplings, not its weights, are positive — the interaction is between spins, and \(\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s)\) is the trace of a transfer operator. Open item F.4 is therefore sharpened: it asks for a transfer-operator (interacting) representation of the polygon with ferromagnetic couplings, not for a reweighting of the register. The paper's own nested-fibre geometry (Lemma 4.3.3) is a candidate for the "chain": successive fibres scaled by \(N^{-1}\) are successive spins, and the quadratic link is the chain length. Whether the induced couplings have a sign is not known and is the computable part of F.4.
