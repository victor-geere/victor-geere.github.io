## Statement

Corollary C.10 is not a defect of the present construction but a description of the space it lives in. By Lemma C.2 the polygon is a combination of depth-one Euler sums twisted by additive characters \(n\mapsto e(jn/q)\); the Davenport–Heilbronn twist is a different point of the same space. Nothing expressible in that basis distinguishes \(\zeta\) from \(f\), because the distinguishing property is multiplicative: \(\zeta\) is a single Euler product, whereas by Lemma C.4(ii) \(f\) is a \(\mathbb{C}\)-combination \(bL(s,\chi)+\overline{b}L(s,\overline{\chi})\) of two Euler products, and a sum of Euler products is not one. The two functions are complementary in exactly the features the framework does *not* use: \(f\) has a clean functional equation and no product, while the limit \((1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) has a product but only the twisted symmetry \(F(s)=\chi(s)\bigl[(1-N^{1-s})/(1-N^{s})\bigr]F(1-s)\). The conversion of Corollary 12.3 is therefore exactly as strong as it should be, and the missing input is identified sharply: an admissible additional hypothesis must be one that *fails* for \(f\). The cleanest such separator is the absence of zeros in \(\sigma>1\), true for \(\zeta\) by the Euler product and false for \(f\) by the theorem of Davenport and Heilbronn. This is the same doorway named in §14 and in [tensor.html](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/tensor.html): arithmetic positivity, which is a statement about primes and not about additive characters. Section C.4 makes that separator precise and records what happens when it is adjoined.
