## Statement

\(i\) \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) is odd, \(a_{5-r}=-a_r\), has mean zero, and is a fixed vector of the normalised finite Fourier transform: \((i\sqrt5)^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^{5}a_r e(rk/5)=a_k\) for every \(k\). Consequently \(f\) satisfies a Riemann-type functional equation relating \(f(s)\) to \(f(1-s)\), with the same gamma factor as an odd Dirichlet \(L\)-function of conductor \(5\). (ii) \(a^{\mathrm{DH}}\) is supported on the units mod \(5\), so \(f=\sum_{\chi\bmod 5}b_\chi L(s,\chi)\); numerically the principal and quadratic characters drop out and \[
f(s)=b\,L(s,\chi)+\overline{b}\,L(s,\overline{\chi}),\qquad b=\tfrac12-0.14203952\ldots\,i,
\] with \(\chi\) an odd primitive character mod \(5\). Thus \(f\) is a \(\mathbb{C}\)-combination of two primitive \(L\)-functions and has *no* Euler product. (iii) \(f\) has infinitely many zeros on \(\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12\) and also zeros off that line, including in \(\sigma>1\) [\[17\]](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/publication.html#ref-dh). An off-line zero inside the strip is \[
\rho_{\mathrm{DH}}=0.8085171824566374\ldots+85.69934848537759\ldots\,i
\] (Spira [\[18\]](../../geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/publication.html#ref-spira)), verified here to \(\lvert f(\rho_{\mathrm{DH}})\rvert<4\cdot10^{-21}\). It lies in \(S_\delta\) for every \(\delta\le 0.19\).
