Prime-spectrum tensor

A reconstructed research programme: finite formal core, exact computation, source claims

Status. Finite formal core and computation layer reconstructed. The source's defining inequality is an archived claim that the companion resolution refutes; it is not a theorem of this folder. The full dossier is in README.md. A finite 4×4 Gaussian Gram matrix PSD certificate at a conservative 10−4 interval margin is in WEIL-GRAM.md (C22), backed by the term-by-term tail proof in TAIL-PROOF.md; it is a finite computational certificate, not a Lean theorem and not RH.

What the programme is

tensor.html proposes to replace the Riemann hypothesis by a positivity statement on the additive group ℝ. It defines the prime current

Π(f) = Σn≥2 Λ(n)/√n · f(log n),   Θ(f) = ∫ℝ Φ(u) f(u) du,   P = Π + Θ,

where Φ is Riemann's even positive Jacobi density, and asks for

(⋆)   ⟨P, f * f̌⟩ ≥ 0   for every f in the Weil cone.

The note then defines the "prime spectrum" as the Bochner measure of P and reads the zeros of ξ as its atoms.

The companion tensor.md is the authority for the mathematical status of the source:

  1. Theorem A computes ⟨P, f*f̌⟩ exactly by the Weil explicit formula. The spectral resolution of P is signed: a positive pole term at ±i/2, a negative atom of weight 1/2 at each zero frequency, and a density negative near the origin.
  2. Theorem B shows that (⋆) is false: the explicit even witness f₀ gives ⟨P, f₀*f₀⟩ = −0.6773024781… < 0. The refutation is unconditional and does not bear on the Riemann hypothesis.
  3. Theorem C identifies the repair: Weil's current W (prime term with the opposite sign, digamma density, pole modes) has spectrum exactly Σρ δγρ; its positivity on the cone is Weil's classical criterion, hence equivalent to RH.
  4. The finite-tuple kernel form of (⋆) fails already for the density part: Φ is positive but not of positive type (Φ̂ = ½ Ξcos changes sign).

Honesty note. This folder does not claim (⋆), the positivity of P, the positivity of W, or RH. The analytic Theorems A–C remain source claims or open formalisation items. The Lean file proves only finite positivity and symmetry lemmas that the reconstruction actually uses, and the Python layer checks finite exact or high-precision computations.

The finite atomic model is the purely algebraic part of Theorem C: if the spectrum is a positive combination of atoms at real frequencies, the Gram form is non-negative. The infinite statement — that W's spectrum is exactly the zero multiset, and that its positivity is RH — is not formalised.

A first finite step toward the actual W (rather than the atomic model) is in WEIL-GRAM.md: a Gaussian test basis, the closed-form reflected convolution, and a numerical 4×4 Gram matrix whose prime side is active and whose smallest eigenvalue is 2.27·10−2. It is a numerical rung, not a certificate; the missing layer is an interval enclosure of Ω.

Open items and gaps

  1. RH / Weil positivity. The statement ⟨W, f*f̌⟩ ≥ 0 for all f in the cone is equivalent to RH and remains open. This folder does not assume it, prove it, or use it. A route assessment of the five proposed capture mechanisms, with a recommended finite first target, is in WEIL-POSITIVITY.md.
  2. Explicit formula. Formalising Lemmas 2.1–2.4 (Fourier transform of the square, Φ̂ = ½ Ξcos, contour shift, Weil explicit formula) is the main analytic gap.
  3. Infinite spectral resolution. The companion's Theorem A and Theorem C are analytic identities on the cone; the Lean file only treats a finite positive atomic model.
  4. Joint non-vanishing. The surviving open statement of tensor.md Corollary 6.2 — that K(α,·) and S(α,·) never vanish jointly for α ≠ 0 — is exactly RH and is not formalised.
  5. Higher tensors. Pair correlation and n-point analogues (tensor.html §7) are a programme, not results; no formal core is attempted here.
  6. Numerical scope. C5 uses the first 2×10⁴ prime powers and 13 Jacobi terms; the truncated tails are below the stated tolerances, but this is a reproduction of the source's witness, not a proof of Theorem B.
  7. Finite Gram matrix. WEIL-GRAM.md records a numerical PSD Gaussian Gram matrix, including a prime-active row, and a rational shadow certified by T12. The Ω interval layer (C9) now encloses Ω pointwise; the missing step is interval quadrature of the density term and the prime-tail bound, after which T8/T10 can certify the actual matrix.

Python layer

python/programme.py implements the exact objects:

  • von_mangoldt(n), prime_weight(n), prime_current(f, N);
  • phi_term(n, u), phi_series(u, terms) for the Jacobi density;
  • fhat0(t, gamma), g0(v, gamma), pole_weight(gamma) for the Gaussian witness;
  • atomic_kernel, mode_sum, gram_form, mode_sq_sum for the finite spectral model;
  • omega_density, gaussian_convolution, weil_gaussian_pole, weil_gaussian_density, weil_gaussian_prime, weil_gaussian_W, weil_gaussian_cross, weil_gaussian_gram, gram_eigenvalues for the Gaussian Weil Gram matrix;
  • omega_interval and omega_interval_width for the rigorous Ω enclosure layer;
  • gaussian_prime_tail_bound for the Gaussian prime-tail estimate;
  • weil_gaussian_density_simpson for the deterministic density quadrature prototype;
  • omega_t_jet and the jet.Jet interval Taylor algebra for the t-jet layer;
  • gaussian_density_panel_taylor for a certified Taylor panel of the density integrand;
  • gaussian_density_certified and gaussian_density_certified_multi for assembled certified density intervals.

Symbolic identities use sympy; the numerical checks use mpmath at 25–30 significant digits and state their tolerances in the tests. Importing the module has no side effects.

cd the-final-letter/topics/prime-spectrum-tensor
python3 -m pytest -q tests
