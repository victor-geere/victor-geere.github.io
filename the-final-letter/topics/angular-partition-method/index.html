Status. Method proposal with a checked exact core. The finite annular, sectorial and general-measure identities, including the infinite annular representation, the phase-carrying tail coil and the Mellin/Laplace zeta representation with its functional equation, growth bound and critical-strip finite-part regularization, are Lean-proved; the computation layer is exact. Applications to ζ, Euler products and the explicit formula are open. No finite truncation is used in the core. No RH proof yet.

The angular partition method — exact radial and sectorial representations of infinite series

Source: proposal from Victor Geere, 3 November 2026, following the infinite-level obstruction in the absolute polarized Hodge-index dossier. The proposal is to stop treating an infinite series only as a depth axis (partial sum plus tail) and to represent it as an angular/radial partition of a compact domain.

1. The depth axis and the angular axis

The standard treatment of a series is a truncation:

S = Σ_{n=0..∞} a_n = (Σ_{n<N} a_n) + (Σ_{n≥N} a_n).

The partial-sum-plus-tail topic is the programme’s certified instrument for this axis. It is powerful, but it makes the infinite object a limit of finite objects and makes the tail an external residual.

The angular/radial method uses a different coordinate. Picture the unit tube S¹ × [0,∞). Cutting it lengthwise gives layers 0,1,2,…: the depth axis. Cutting it crosswise gives the unit circle: the angular axis, where the sectors tile the whole circle at once. The radial version cuts a disk into annuli; the angular version cuts the circle into sectors; combining them gives annular sectors.

The point is not that the depth axis is wrong. The point is that it is not the only axis, and some series become exact on the angular/radial axis when no useful closed tail exists on the depth axis.

2. The general annular representation

Let a_n ≥ 0 and suppose Σ a_n < ∞. Define the squared tail radii

R_n² := Σ_{k≥n} a_k.

Then R_n² − R_{n+1}² = a_n, and the annuli

A_n := { z ∈ ℂ : R_{n+1} < |z| ≤ R_n }

are pairwise disjoint and tile the disk of radius R_0. With area normalized by μ(A) = area(A)/π,

μ(A_n) = R_n² − R_{n+1}² = a_n,
μ(∪ A_n) = R_0² = Σ a_n.

So every summable nonnegative series is the total measure of an annular partition of a disk. No geometric ratio is assumed. The geometric series is only the special case in which the radii themselves form a geometric progression.

2.1 The radius is the depth

The sequence n ↦ R_n is the exact bookkeeping index: it records how much mass remains beyond depth n. The term a_n is the area of the annulus between two consecutive radii. For σ > 1,

R_n² = Σ_{k≥n} k^(−σ),   n^(−σ) = R_n² − R_{n+1}²,   R_0² = ζ(σ).

Equivalently, R_n² = ζ(σ,n) = Σ_{k≥0} (n+k)^(−σ), the Hurwitz zeta at parameter n; for σ = 2 this is the trigamma function ψ₁(n). For example, ζ(2) = R_0² = π²/6, so the disk has radius π/√6 and the annulus at n has normalized area n^(−2). No truncation is involved. For 0 < σ ≤ 1, R_0 = ∞: the disk is infinite. That is precisely the divergence of the series, not a failure of the method.

Product bookkeeping. The same radii can index a product: ∏_{n=0..N}(1+a_n) = exp(Σ log(1+a_n)) turns a finite product into a sum of logarithms, which can itself be given an annular representation. The ratios R_n/R_{n+1} are the natural multiplicative bookkeeping weights, and ∏ R_n/R_{n+1} = R_0/R_{N+1} telescopes exactly. Sums and products are two coordinate choices on the same radial data.

2.2 Complex terms and angular bookkeeping

For an absolutely summable complex series c_n = |c_n| e^(iθ_n), apply the annular construction to |c_n| and place a sector of area |c_n| at angle θ_n in the annulus A_n. The result is a phase field on the disk, and Σ c_n = ∫_D e^(iθ(z)) dμ(z). For a conditionally convergent series, the angular placement is a summation method; absolute convergence or a proved rearrangement-invariance statement is required.

2.3 The phase-carrying tail coil

For an absolutely summable complex series c_n, define the complex tail Z_n = Σ_{k≥n} c_k. Then c_n = Z_n − Z_{n+1}, the finite sum is the chord Σ_{k<N} c_k = Z_0 − Z_N, and Z_n → 0. The coil is the sequence n ↦ Z_n in the complex plane, with radius r_n = |Z_n| and phase θ_n = arg Z_n. The cumulative tail sum is the radius, the angle carries the phase, and the whole sum is the starting point Z_0. This is a tail coil, not a partial-sum truncation.

The unit coil is w_n = Z_n/Z_0 (when Z_0 ≠ 0), so w_0 = 1 and w_n → 0: a normalized coil inside the unit disk. For ζ(s) in Re s > 1, take Z_n = Σ_{k≥n} k^(−s) and Z_0 = ζ(s); the coil is exact, although evaluating Z_0 is still the original problem.

This is formalised as phase_carrying_coil (decomposition, polar form and limit), ctail_telescope (the chord identity), and unitCoil_zero/unitCoil_tendsto_zero (the normalized coil). Other phase carriers considered: the partial-sum coil (a conical helix, but depth-based), the annular phase field (vector area integral; infinite formalisation open), and the exact tuple model (r_n,θ_n).

2.4 The self-similar special case

If the unresolved part is an exact similarity copy of the whole, S = a_0 + rS, then S = a_0/(1−r). This is the closed-form case. It is a special case of the annular representation, not a requirement of it.

3. The halving-circle normalization

The halving sectors have angles

π + π/2 + π/4 + ... = 2π.

In normalized turns the same partition is 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... = 1. Both statements describe the same tiling; the radian total is 2π. The Lean theorem sector_angle_hasSum records the radian form; the computation layer records both exact forms.

4. The method in six steps

  1. Choose the carrier. A circle, disk, annulus, sphere, or a contour compactified to one of these. Finite measure; measurable partition.
  2. Choose the axis. Radial annuli encode modulus/depth; angular sectors encode phase; annular sectors encode both.
  3. Identify the weights. Prove a_n = μ(X_n) exactly. In the general radial case this is a_n = R_n² − R_{n+1}².
  4. Use exactness. If the partition is self-similar, solve the fixed point. If the tail radii have a closed form, use it. If neither, keep the exact measure representation and state what is missing.
  5. Check summability and order. Absolute convergence for complex terms; otherwise the angular construction is a summability method.
  6. Only then estimate. If no exact transform is available, use certified partial-sum-plus-tail evaluation as a fallback. A finite estimate is an instrument, not a wall.

5. Worked exact examples

5.1 Geometric series as a self-similar cake

Take a disk of area A, cut off a sector of area a_0, and let the rest be a copy scaled by r. Then A = a_0 + rA, so A = a_0/(1−r). For a_0 = 1, r = 1/2, this is 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ... = 2.

5.2 The halving circle

The unit circle has total angle 2π; sectors of angle π, π/2, π/4, … tile it, with finite sums Σ_{k<n} π/2^k = 2π(1 − 2^(−n)).

5.3 Arbitrary weights: n^(−2)

The annular representation gives R_n² = Σ_{k≥n} k^(−2) and n^(−2) = R_n² − R_{n+1}². The total normalized area is R_0² = ζ(2) = π²/6. The representation is exact for every n; the closed form at n = 0 is the Basel value. This is the prototype of “the radius of decay is the depth of the term”.

5.4 Rational functions

For |z| < 1, 1/(1−z) = Σ z^n. This is the boundary-value form of the geometric cake.

5.5 The Mellin/Laplace radial representation (formalised)

For Re s > 0, n^(−s) = (1/Γ(s)) ∫_0^∞ t^(s−1) e^(−nt) dt. Summing over n and using Σ e^(−nt) = 1/(e^t − 1) gives

Γ(s) ζ(s) = ∫_0^∞ t^(s−1)/(e^t − 1) dt

for Re s > 1. This is another exact radial representation: the discrete index is traded for continuous depth, and the geometric sum in t is closed. It is formalised in lean/Mellin.lean: hasSum_zetaKernel, hasSum_mellin_zetaKernel, mellin_zetaKernel_eq_Gamma_mul_zeta, the Gamma-factor bridge completedRiemannZeta_eq_GammaR_mul_zeta, the completed functional equation completedRiemannZeta_functional_equation, and the vertical-strip growth bound mellin_zetaKernel_norm_le.

5.6 Critical-strip regularization and explicit-formula poles

The raw Mellin integral converges for Re s > 1. The finite part

M_reg(s) = Γ(s)ζ(s) − 1/(s−1) + 1/(2s)

subtracts exactly the two pole terms. On the convergence domain it is the raw annular total minus the poles: M_reg(s) = mellin(1/(e^t−1))(s) − 1/(s−1) + 1/(2s). This is regularizedMellin_eq_mellin_sub_poles. The finite part is differentiable in the critical strip 0 < Re s < 1 (regularizedMellin_differentiableAt), so it is the analytic continuation of the annular total into the strip. The completed version Λ₀(s) = Λ(s) + 1/s + 1/(1−s) is entire and satisfies Λ₀(1−s) = Λ₀(s).

Explicit-formula connection. The two subtracted residues are exactly the two pole terms of the explicit formula. The residue at s = 1 is 1 (regularizedMellin_residue_one) and the residue at s = 0 is −1/2 (regularizedMellin_residue_zero). In the test-function formula ∮ (Z'/Z)h = 2πi(Σ h(ρ) − h(0) − h(1)), the terms −h(0) − h(1) are the same pole contributions. The critical-strip finite part is therefore the analytic-continuation side of the explicit-formula contour; the completed finite part supplies the functional equation that pairs the zeros. This is a structural connection, not a new proof of Weil positivity.

Convergence of the subtracted kernel. With h(t) = 1/(e^t−1) − 1/t + 1/2 on (0,1) and h(t) = 1/(e^t−1) for t ≥ 1, the comparison bounds are |t^(s−1)h(t)| ≤ (3/2) t^(σ−1) on (0,1) and |t^(s−1)h(t)| ≤ 2 t^(σ−2) on (1,∞). The first is integrable because σ > 0, the second because σ < 1. Hence the integral converges throughout the critical strip. The bounds and their assembly are Lean-checked: norm_regularizedKernel_le, norm_zetaKernel_le_two_div, integrableOn_regularizedKernel_Ioo, integrableOn_regularizedKernel_Ioi, and mellinConvergent_regularizedKernel; convergence on the whole half-plane is mellinConvergent_regularizedKernel_of_pos, and holomorphy on 0 < Re s is differentiableAt_mellin_regularizedKernel.

Identification. For Re s > 1, split at 1; the kernels agree on (1,∞), and the difference on (0,1) is −1/(s−1) + 1/(2s). Hence mellin h = Γ(s)ζ(s) − 1/(s−1) + 1/(2s) = M_reg(s). Both sides are analytic in 0 < Re s < 1, so the identity extends to the strip. This is now Lean-proved: mellin_regularizedKernel_eqOn_upper and mellin_regularizedKernel_eqOn_lower handle the two half-strips, and mellin_regularizedKernel_eq_of_re_pos_of_lt_one extends across the real axis by continuity.

6. Exactitude first, estimation second

Look for an exact transform before accepting a finite estimate as the representation of infinity. A finite estimate is not a failure; it is a fallback. The two instruments are complementary:

AxisObjectStrengthLimit
depthS_N + R_Ncertified evaluator; arbitrary series with a tail boundneeds a closed tail or bound; the tail can be wide
angular/radialμ(X) = Σ μ(X_n)exact measure identity; no tailneeds a measurable partition; closed forms only in special cases

The angular method can discover an exact identity; the partial-sum-plus-tail layer can certify a numerical value when no exact identity is known. A good programme uses both.

7. Scope and obstructions

7.1 Absolute convergence and ordering

For a conditionally convergent series, rearranging terms can change the sum. An angular/radial construction groups terms in a chosen geometric order, so it defines a summability method. It agrees with the ordinary sum when the series is absolutely convergent or when the partition is proved rearrangement-invariant. For ζ(s) in the critical strip the Dirichlet series is not absolutely convergent, so this is essential.

7.2 Divergence is a radius, not a hidden tail

For a_n = n^(−σ), the squared tail radius is finite exactly when σ > 1. At σ ≤ 1, R_0 = ∞: the disk is infinite. The method locates the abscissa of convergence in the radius itself. It does not remove the need for analytic continuation in the critical strip.

7.3 Phase bookkeeping

For n^(−s) = n^(−σ) e^(−it log n), the modulus is radially representable, but the phase is not constant on an annulus. One must place angular sectors at the phases, separate real and imaginary signed parts, or use the Mellin/Laplace representation where the phase is carried by t^(s−1). This is the real bookkeeping problem for ζ, not polynomial versus geometric decay.

7.4 A representation is not automatically an evaluation

The identity gives Σ a_n = R_0². If R_0² has a closed form, the series is evaluated. If not, the identity is an exact representation, not a numerical value. For n^(−2) the closed form is π²/6; for general n^(−σ) no such closed form is known. The method does not manufacture closed forms.

7.5 The w = e^(−s) compactification is not required

If one maps depth to radius by w = e^(−s), then n^(−s) = w^(log n), and w = 0 is an essential singularity/natural boundary. That is a real obstruction to a finite-monodromy compactification. The annular representation of §2 does not use this map: it uses the tail radii directly. The two constructions should not be conflated.

7.6 What would be needed for the critical strip

  • a regularized annular representation whose total area is the analytic continuation, not the divergent sum;
  • a phase-carrying measure (vector-valued area) with a convergence theorem;
  • a Mellin/Laplace transform with the correct functional equation and growth bounds;
  • for Euler products, ∏(1+a_n) = exp(Σ log(1+a_n)) followed by an annular representation of the log-series.

11. Open questions

Resolved since the first version. The infinite annular representation, the phase-carrying tail coil, the Mellin/Laplace zeta representation, the Gamma-factor bridge, the completed functional equation, the vertical-strip growth bound, the critical-strip finite part and its differentiability, the two pole residues, the regularized-kernel comparison bounds, the Mellin convergence of the subtracted kernel in the critical strip, and the critical-strip identity mellin h = M_reg are now formalised or proved in text.

Remaining open questions.

  1. Vector area field. Define a vector-valued area measure whose integral is the complex sum, and prove the absolutely convergent case. The phase-carrying tail coil is formalised; this stronger area-field version is open.
  2. Products. Develop the product-to-sum annular representation for Euler products and the completed zeta.
  3. Bost–Connes. Periodic orbits have lengths log p; for one prime the angular method is exact, for all primes the logs are not commensurable. What is the correct finite-monodromy or regularized replacement?
  4. Exactness audit. For each infinite series in the programme, decide whether an exact transform exists before assigning a finite evaluator.
  5. Continuation beyond the strip. The finite-part regularization is currently set up for 0 < Re s < 1; extend the same annular/Mellin method to the full meromorphic continuation and connect it to the completed functional equation as an integral identity.
