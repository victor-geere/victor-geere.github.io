Riemann helix

A reconstructed research programme: elementary formal core, exact computation, and an audit of the archived RH-equivalence

Status. The elementary helix and torsion core is reconstructed and proved. The distributional limit and the Riemann Hypothesis itself remain open and are not proved here. The source remains an archived open statement. Full detail is in README.md.

What the programme is

The archived note proposes a geometric reformulation of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH). Write the non-trivial zeros with positive imaginary part as ρ_n = β_n + iγ_n, with 0 < γ_1 < γ_2 < … and no assumption on β_n ∈ (0,1). Choose a smooth increasing phase φ with φ(γ_n) = 2πn, and consider the unit-radius helix

C1(t) = (cos φ(t), sin φ(t), t).

The note claims that the torsion τ1 of C1 converges, as a distribution, to a prime-supported measure

τ0(t) = Σp,m (log p / pm/2) δ(t − m log p) + τarch(t),  (1)

independently of the β_n. It then lets r interpolate the real parts, r(γ_n) = β_n, and considers the varying-radius helix

Cr(t) = (r(t) cos φ(t), r(t) sin φ(t), t),

whose torsion τr acquires extra terms involving r′ and r″. The source's Theorem 4.1 asserts that RH (all β_n = ½) is equivalent to the existence of such an interpolation with τr = τ0.

This folder reconstructs the elementary geometry the argument actually uses, together with the algebraic criterion relating curvature and torsion. It does not formalise the distributional limit or the RH equivalence. The source claims are kept at their archived status, and the audit below records two defects in the source's proof sketch.

The classical helix formulae are the ones the source uses: for CR,c both curvature and torsion are constant, with τ = c/(R² + c²). A direct Frenet computation (verified symbolically in the Python layer) gives these closed forms.

Audit of the archived argument

The source is a short fragment (four sections, no detailed derivation of the distributional limit). Three observations matter for reconstruction.

1. The displayed torsion formula is not the Frenet torsion. For C1(t) = (cos φ(t), sin φ(t), t) the source displays

τ1 = φ′[φ′⁴ − φ′φ‴ + 3φ″²] / [(φ′² + 1)(φ′⁴ + φ″² + φ′⁶)].

Direct computation gives the denominator φ′⁶ + φ′⁴ + φ″². At constant φ′ = k the source's expression is k/(1+k²)², while the correct torsion is k/(1+k²). This is checked exactly by test_source_torsion_formula_disagrees (C3).

2. Torsion is not scale invariant. The source's (i) ⇒ (ii) proof says that C1/2 is “a scaling of the unit-radius helix” and that “scaling does not change the torsion”. Torsion has dimension 1/length: uniformly scaling the constant-rate helix by λ gives τ ↦ τ/λ. Moreover C1/2 as displayed is not a uniform scaling of C1, because the vertical coordinate is left equal to t. In the representative constant-rate case φ(t) = t, the radius-½, pitch-1 helix has τ = 4/5, not ½. The Lean theorems torsion_scale, torsion_not_scale_invariant, and torsion_half_ne_unit formalise the scaling law and this counterexample.

3. The density wording is slightly too strong. The source says {m log p} is dense in [0,∞). Its least element is log 2, so it is not dense in [0,∞). It is dense in [log 2,∞) assuming the prime number theorem (consecutive prime logarithms differ by → 0). The argument only needs density on the range of the ordinates γ_n, so this is a presentational rather than structural defect; it is recorded as source claim S6 and not used as a theorem.

These defects do not show that no geometric criterion of this kind can work. They do show that the archived proof sketch does not establish the stated equivalence, and that a repaired criterion must handle the physical dimension of torsion (for example by comparing a dimensionless combination such as τ/κ).
