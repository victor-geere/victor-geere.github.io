Nested spheres and continuous cone

A reconstructed research programme: elementary formal core, exact computation, archived claims left open

Status. Reconstructed elementary formal core and exact computation layer. Both archived notes claim a geometric proof of the Riemann hypothesis; that claim is not accepted and is recorded as an archived source claim.

What the programme is

The two notes attempt to convert the finite mean-zero filter of the Riemann-zeta programme into a geometric and measure-theoretic argument.

  • The nested-spheres note places the polygonal paths of the coherent sum on three-sphere fibres of the quaternionic Hopf fibration, scales successive fibre radii by N⁻¹, and claims that tightness plus pure-point limits supported on critical-line zeros imply RH.
  • The continuous-cone note replaces the integer scale N by a real radius R, asserts a local quadratic form with constant c₁ = 1/2, derives a differential inequality, and claims a power-saving depth-of-closure bound and hence RH.

The audit in summary/13-working-notes finds that the set-up, the exact filter identity, parity independence and the O(N⁻¹) sampling estimate are consistent with the canonical paper. It rejects the decisive steps: the local diagonal with constant 1/2 is false (the canonical Lemma 6.3 gives -1 + R∫φ²), the differential inequality is withdrawn, the second-moment bound omits an N³ term, and support of the limit measures only on the critical line is already RH-strength. Neither note is part of the canonical proof chain.

This folder reconstructs only the geometry, filter and regularity facts that survive. The RH conclusion, the residual-mass and tail bounds, the differential inequality and the pure-point limit theorem remain source claims or open items.

Why the archived proof does not go through

  1. The local diagonal is wrong. The continuous-cone note evaluates ∫(1-Rφ)(-φ) as -1/2 + O(R⁻¹). The correct value is -1 + R∫φ², which is not -1/2 and carries the R-dependence that the drift term needs. T7 formalises the correction; C5 verifies it exactly.
  2. The differential inequality inherits the error. Without a negative constant there is no drift, and the integrating-factor argument collapses. The canonical changelog and gaps.html record the inequality as withdrawn and do not use it.
  3. The second-moment bound drops a term. The archived C₀NT log(2T) estimate omits the additive N³ spike contribution; at fixed T and large N it is too strong to support the claimed O(N⁻¹) residual mass.
  4. The support claim is the gap. The canonical measure theory removes the left half-strip but not the right half-strip; a right-half zero remains an unrestricted atom. Asserting that limits live only on the critical line is already the open conversion, not a consequence.
  5. Neither note is in the canonical proof chain. They are working notes linked from the paper, not accepted proofs.

Open items and known gaps

  1. Uniform sampling geometry. T6 gives the per-edge chord bound, but the archived Lemma 3.2 also asserts uniformity in the height t for the Hausdorff distance to a complete fibre curve. That comparison is not formalised.
  2. The full purification identity. T5 is the mean-zero period sum. The infinite Dirichlet series and its meromorphic continuation remain outside the Lean core.
  3. The corrected local quadratic form. T7 proves the integral identity, not the differential inequality. Whether a modified inequality with a genuine negative drift can be derived is open.
  4. Residual mass and tails. The archived O(N⁻¹) and O(R^{-α/2}) estimates are not established; the canonical paper uses a different route.
  5. Support of the limit measures. Removing the right half-strip is the surviving open problem and is not a consequence of the geometric picture.
  6. RH itself. Neither note proves it. The canonical Corollary 12.3 is an equivalence/conversion, not a proof.
