What the programme is

The HST programme attempts a Hilbert–Pólya-style spectral theory of the Riemann zeta function built from Victor Geere's greedy harmonic reconstruction of the sine. Its elementary starting point is small and genuine: the harmonic angles

αn = π/(n+2),   n = 0,1,2,…

serve both as the step sizes of a greedy expansion of an angle θ ∈ [0,π] and as the left-child lengths of a binary tree of cylinder intervals. (The source prose says right child; its figure and the recursion give left — see R5 in the reconciliation.) Haar wavelets are attached to the split nodes, and the resulting coefficient map is called the Harmonic Sine Transform.

The word “programme” is load-bearing. The archive presents a coherent narrative — Haar basis, isometric HST, transfer operator ℒs, Fredholm determinant det(I−ℒs) = ζ(s)/ζ(2s)·eP(s), Weil explicit formula, spectral flow, Hilbert–Pólya and a GRH paper — but the load-bearing analytic steps are proof sketches or unsupported claims. This folder reconstructs the part that can actually be proved and computed.

Two different digit recursions. The guide's Section 3.1 defines r0 = θ/π, δn = ⌊(n+2)rn⌋, rn+1 = (n+2)rn − δn, which allows digits δn ≥ 2. harmonic-tree-haar.tex equation (1) instead uses the residual-subtraction rule δn = Θ(θ − An − αn), An+1 = An + δnαn, with digits in {0,1}. These are different algorithms. This reconstruction follows the residual-subtraction rule, because it is the rule used for the Haar tree, the threshold angles, and the pilot greedy harmonic decomposition. The guide also cites a greedy-harmonic-zeta-function.tex that is not in this folder, so the “eight-paper” count does not correspond to eight consistent, self-contained papers.

Reconstructed objects and notation

Angles are written θ; it is often convenient to use t = θ/π ∈ [0,1], so that αn/π = 1/(n+2).

ObjectReconstruction definition
Harmonic angleαn = π/(n+2)
Accumulated angleA0 = 0, An+1 = An + δnαn
Greedy digitδn(θ) = 1 if αn ≤ θ − An, else 0
Thresholdθn* = inf{θ : δn(θ) = 1}
Cylinder intervalIε = {θ : δk(θ) = εk, k < |ε|}
Split nodea node of length ℓ with αn < ℓ; left child length αn, right child length ℓ − αn
Haar wavelet (reconstructed)on a node of length L = a + b (left a, right b): ψ = −√(b/(aL))·1L + √(a/(bL))·1R
Accumulated massMn(θ) = Σk≤n δk(θ)/(k+2), so An = πMn

The source's displayed wavelet (equation (5) of harmonic-tree-haar.tex) uses coefficients 1/√αn on the right child and −1/√(ℓ−αn) on the left. The defect has two layers. Under the source's prose convention (right child length αn, left ℓ−αn) it has squared norm 2, not 1, and mean √αn − √(ℓ−αn) (T14). But the prose is itself swapped: on the actual tree the left child has length αn and the right ℓ−αn, so the displayed coefficients give squared norm a/b + b/a ≥ 2 and mean −a/√b + b/√a (T15, C10). The reconstruction uses the correctly normalised zero-mean wavelet above. The repair is proved in Lean and checked exactly in Python.

Source reconciliation

The imported corpus is not internally consistent. The full ledger has eighteen rows with source locations, verdicts and evidence in SOURCE-RECONCILIATION.md. The seven defects that change the mathematics are:

#DefectVerdictEvidence

The remaining rows — the logarithmic density law, the closed-form correlation kernel, the Fredholm determinant, the explicit formula and the self-adjoint family — are refuted or recorded as archived claims; none is promoted. R7 (Haar completeness) is now closed in density form by O1 (T45–T46).

T8–T10 close the convergence question. The proof is a two-line induction: if rn ≥ αn then rn+1 = rn − αn ≤ π/(n+1) − π/(n+2) ≤ π/(n+2), and if rn < αn then rn+1 = rn < π/(n+2). Squeezing between 0 and π/(n+1) gives convergence with an explicit rate, hence the expansion identity by uniqueness of sums. T11–T12 are the two hypotheses of the standard completeness argument for a Haar system: injectivity of the digit map and mesh-to-zero. Together with the real lift, the cylinder-step approximation and the Lp density theorem (T25–T46), these now prove Haar completeness in density form.

The crucial structural point is T5. It does not say that θ ↦ δn(θ) is a step function; C3 shows that it is not. The source's equation (3) asserts the step-function characterisation, and that assertion is false for the source's own equation (1). T5 is the weaker true statement that survives: the infimum of the selection set is exactly αn.
