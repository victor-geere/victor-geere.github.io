Periodic orbits on Bruhat–Tits trees

Status: reconstructed finite formal core + exact computation layer. The local Reshetikhin–Turaev identity and the global spectral claim remain Open

Source: archive/maths/rh/research/periodic-orbits/1_1.tex, part of a 28-file TeX development. Inventory: Scraps item 19 · Topic index · The Final Letter

What the programme is

The archived corpus tries to turn the periodic orbits of the p-adic shift into a local Euler factor. The chain is:

  1. Let Tp be the Bruhat–Tits tree of PGL(2, ℚp), with boundary ℙ¹(ℚp).
  2. The shift σp is induced by diag(p, 1), so on the boundary σp(z) = pz.
  3. A length-n periodic orbit of σp on Tp should give a framed braid β ∈ Bn.
  4. In 𝒞p = SU(2)p−2 at q = eπi/p, the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant of the closure is claimed to be RT𝒞p(β) = p−n.
  5. Summing these local weights over n should reproduce (1 − p−s)−1, and assembling the primes into an adelic transfer operator should make the non-trivial zeros of ζ a spectrum — hence RH.

Steps 1–2 and the finite modular data are genuine. Step 3 is asserted rather than constructed. Step 4 is the local identity. Step 5 is the global spectral claim, and nothing in the corpus proves even step 4. This dossier reconstructs the small finite core that can be stated and checked, and records the rest with its archived status.

The source contradicts itself

This is the central provenance fact, and the dossier must not smooth it over.

Source fileWhat it says about RT = p−n
3_1.texStates it as the Main Local RT-Weight Theorem, with a four-step proof roadmap.
3_2.texRetracts it. The Verlinde collapse is missing its Seifert data, and the claimed evaluation Gp = τp is false: the author computes Gp over ℚ(ζp) and reports a mismatch of magnitude and argument for every p ≤ 17. Conclusion: “the local identity therefore remains an open conjecture rather than a theorem.”
3_2a.texSurveys alternative braid-to-manifold identifications; states the route is open.
3_2b, 3_3, 3_4, 3_4a–c Claim to repair the proof using the column ℓ = (p−1)/2 and auxiliary twists fk = +1.
verify.pyThe verification script is circular: RT_alternative computes result = p**(-n) directly, then asserts the difference to p−n is small. It never evaluates the reduced Verlinde sum.
3_4c.texThe “complete runnable script” sets sum_ek_prime = n + n and sums only over even k, so it does not compute the sum it claims to verify.

The repair is also mathematically empty: with ℓ = (p−1)/2 one has 2ℓ + 1 = p, hence

Sjℓ = √(2/p) · sin(π(2j+1)p/p) = √(2/p) · sin(π(2j+1)) = 0,

so the “surviving sum” Σj dj θj Sjℓ is identically zero. A zero column cannot normalise to τpS0ℓ ≠ 0; the reduction would give RT = 0, not p−n. The Python layer verifies this exactly (row C3).

Open items and gaps

  1. The local RT identity. RT𝒞p(β) = p−n is not proved. The source’s own §3.2 leaves it open; the later repair is vacuous at the key column. A genuine proof would need (a) a fully explicit orbit-to-braid and braid-to-3-manifold map, (b) the Seifert invariants as functions of the p-adic slope, and (c) a non-circular evaluation of the resulting state sum.
  2. Braid and framing construction. The corpus gives illustrative words (β = σ1 for (p,n) = (3,2); β = σ1σ2σ1 for (5,3)) but no deterministic algorithm, and the isotopy lemma is asserted.
  3. Correct orbit count. Replace “pn+1 length-n orbits” by the primitive count C7, and identify which orbits carry which braid.
  4. Gauss-sum obstruction. Any repair must produce a non-zero reduced column and an evaluation that is not the classical quadratic Gauss sum; the corpus does neither.
  5. Global spectral claim. The adelic transfer operator, its self-adjointness, and the identification of its spectrum with the zeros are all outside this reconstruction and remain open. RH is not a theorem of this programme.

Lean layer

What is deliberately not formalised: the shift on Tp, the orbit-to-braid construction, the RT invariant, the Seifert identification, the Verlinde sum, and every global spectral statement. See lean/README.md.
