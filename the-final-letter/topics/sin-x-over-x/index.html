sin(x)/x with the greedy harmonic sine

A derived research programme: the exact greedy sine is sin; the finite-stage study is optional

Status. The finite-stage geometry and arithmetic are formalised in Lean and tested in Python. The global convergence of the greedy reconstruction is a source claim inherited from harmonic sine; it is tested numerically and recorded as open, not proved. No Riemann-hypothesis claim is made. The full dossier is in README.md.

Exact-object reading (the inherited theorem). The infinite greedy harmonic sine is exactly sin(x) after the rescaling y = x/π. The replacement in sin(x)/x is therefore the identity, and Euler's product, Basel, Wallis, the cotangent expansion and the ζ(2k) formulae hold unchanged. See The exact answer. The finite-stage material below is an optional study of truncations SN, not a claim about the exact object.

The question

The programme produces two different objects: the exact greedy harmonic sine G(x) = limN SN(x), which is sin(x) after the rescaling y = x/π, and its finite partial sums SN(x), which are different functions.

The original question — what happens to sin(x)/x when its sine is replaced by the greedy harmonic sine — is about the exact object. The answer is The exact answer: nothing changes. The rest of this page asks a narrower, optional question: what happens if the sine is replaced by a finite partial sum SN? For that question we use the normalised sinc of sinc, sine kernel, moments,

sinc(x) = sin(πx)/(πx)  (x ≠ 0),    sinc(0) = 1,
sinc_N(x) = S_N(x)/(πx) (x ≠ 0),    sinc_N(0) = 0.

The unnormalised sin(x)/x is sinc(x/π); every statement transfers by that rescaling. The value at the origin for the truncation is determined by continuity: SN vanishes on a whole neighbourhood of 0, so the continuous extension there is 0, not 1. For the exact object the removable value is 1, as for sin(x)/x.

The exact answer

Under the exact-object reading, G(x) = limN GN(x) = sin(x). Euler's product therefore applies verbatim and the replacement is the identity. There is no new Euler product to discover.

Taylor series. For the exact object,

sin(x)/x = 1 − x²/3! + x⁴/5! − ⋯.

For the exact reconstruction θ(x)=x/π, so substituting into the Taylor series of sin gives the displayed series directly. Comparing it with the Euler product gives −1/6 = −Σ1/n², hence ζ(2)=π²/6. In the normalised variable sinc(x)=sin(πx)/(πx), the coefficient is −π²/6.

The truncation is a different object. GN(x) = SN(x/π) vanishes on |x| < π/(N+1), so GN(x)/x is identically zero on that interval and every Taylor coefficient at 0 is 0. Thus the x² coefficient of the truncation is 0 for every N, while the exact coefficient is −1/6:

limN [x²](GN(x)/x) = 0  ≠  −1/6 = [x²](sin(x)/x).

Coefficient extraction and the greedy limit do not commute. That is a fact about the partial constructions, not about the exact greedy harmonic sine. The exact object is unchanged.

Truncation study (optional; not the greedy harmonic sine)

The original question is about the exact greedy harmonic sine. The answer is the exact-object section above: there is no difference. Everything below is a separate study of the finite partial sums SN; it is not a claim about the greedy harmonic sine and should not be read as one. The material is kept only because the sibling harmonic-sine programme and this repository's Lean layer formalise the finite rotations.

1. Truncations lose the value at the origin

No step is selected while the target is smaller than every available step. The first N steps are 1/2, 1/3, …, 1/(N+1), all larger than |x| when |x| < 1/(N+1), so

S_N(x) = 0 and sinc_N(x) = 0  for |x| < 1/(N+1).

At every fixed stage the reconstructed kernel is flat at the origin, while sinc(0) = 1. This is a statement about the truncations, not about the exact greedy sine. The exact object is formed by taking G = limN SN first; then G = sin and G(x)/x has the removable value 1. The discrepancy is a double-limit artefact:

lim_{N→∞} lim_{x→0} sinc_N(x) = 0,
lim_{x→0} lim_{N→∞} sinc_N(x) = 1.

The sequence of continuous extensions sincN (with sincN(0)=0) has pointwise limit sinc(x) for x ≠ 0 and 0 at x = 0; the continuous extension of the exact limit has value 1. So the truncations do not converge uniformly to the exact quotient at the origin. The exact object is still sin(x)/x; only the approximation loses the removable value.

2. Truncations converge to sinc off the origin, but not uniformly

Conditional on the residual invariant |θN(x) − x| ≤ 1/(N+1) (the source's tested bound) and the sine Lipschitz estimate,

|sinc_N(x) − sinc(x)| ≤ 1/((N+1)|x|)   (x ≠ 0).

Convergence is uniform on every set |x| ≥ a, with rate 1/((N+1)a), but not on any neighbourhood of the origin. At x = 0 the discrepancy is exactly 1 at every stage. The source's O(1/N) reconstruction rate does not transfer to sinc: dividing by x turns an absolute error into a relative one, and the relative error near the origin is O(1/(N|x|)).

3. Finite stages do not share the zeros of sinc

At x = 1 the greedy word is 1,1,0,0,1 (take 1/2, 1/3, skip 1/4 and 1/5, take 1/6 exactly), so

θ_1(1) = 1/2,  θ_2(1) = θ_3(1) = θ_4(1) = 5/6,  θ_N(1) = 1 (N ≥ 5).

Consequently, for odd integer k,

sinc_1(k) = 1/(πk),
sinc_N(k) = 1/(2πk)   (N = 2,3,4),
sinc_N(k) = 0         (N ≥ 5).

For even k the reconstruction reduces to the zero branch and vanishes at every stage. The zeros at odd integers are recovered only after five steps; before that, the reconstructed kernel takes non-zero values at x = ±π, ±3π, …. The zeros of sinc are simple and isolated; the zero set of sincN contains the interval |x| < 1/(N+1) and the intervals on which θN is an integer, so it has interior.

There is a compensating exactness result. If x is rational, the greedy Egyptian expansion terminates, so θN(x) = x for all sufficiently large N and sincN(x) = sinc(x) exactly. Examples: x = 1/2 needs N = 1; 1/3 needs N = 2; 2/7 needs N = 27; 3/7 needs N = 230. The required N blows up as x → 0, which is the pointwise face of the non-uniformity.

4. The analytic structure is destroyed at every finite stage

sinc is entire, even, bandlimited (its Fourier transform is the indicator of [−1/2,1/2]), and has the Euler product sinc(x) = Πn≥1(1 − x²/n²). At every finite N, sincN is a quotient of a step function by x:

  • it is piecewise smooth with jumps at the greedy breakpoints (for N = 1, a jump from 0 to 1/π at x = 1/2);
  • it is not continuous, hence not entire;
  • by the Paley–Wiener theorem it is not bandlimited;
  • it is flat on a neighbourhood of 0, so every derivative at the origin is 0, whereas sinc(x) = 1 − π²x²/6 + … has non-zero even Taylor coefficients;
  • it has no product formula of sinc type, because its zero set has interior.

The finite stages therefore cannot serve as a sampling kernel. The replacement preserves (in the limit) the values away from 0, but not the removable singularity or bandlimitedness.

5. The truncated integral approaches 1/2 slowly

The classical half-line integral is ∫₀∞ sinc(x) dx = 1/2. The reconstructed kernel is integrable in the improper sense (one period has zero mean by the odd reflection), and the numerical values are

6. The Euler product does not survive truncation

The truncation question is whether GN inherits the product. It does not; three finite-stage facts show why.

Euler's product is the prototype of a factorization over zeros:

sin(πx)/(πx) = Π_{n≥1} (1 − x²/n²).

Write P_M(x) = Π_{n≤M}(1 − x²/n²). Three finite-stage facts block an Euler-type factorization of sinc_N.

The constant term fails first. P_M(0)=1 for every M, while sinc_N(0)=0 for every N. The truncation therefore fails at the origin before any coefficient comparison. Even if sinc_N(0) were assigned the value 1, the equality would fail on the punctured interval 0 < |x| < 1/(N+1), where the product is non-zero and sinc_N vanishes. The obstruction is not repairable by a normalising constant: sinc_N is identically zero on |x| < 1/(N+1), so its zero set has interior, whereas a normally convergent product of non-zero linear factors has at most countably many zeros and no interval of them. There is no Euler-type factorization of sinc_N over isolated zeros.

The Basel coefficient comparison is vacuous. Euler expands P(x) = 1 − (Σ1/n²)x² + … and equates the x² coefficient with the Taylor coefficient −1/6 of sin(πx)/(πx), giving ζ(2)=π²/6. But sinc_N is flat on a neighbourhood of 0: all Taylor coefficients there vanish. Applying Euler's rule at finite stage would give Σ1/n²=0, i.e. ζ(2)=0. The harmonic sine cannot re-derive Basel at any finite stage. The only route is the limit N → ∞, where sinc_N → sinc off the origin and Euler's identity is recovered — but that is Euler's classical theorem, not a new proof.

Zeros and the cotangent expansion. For N ≥ 5, sinc_N(k)=0 at every non-zero integer. The zeros are nevertheless not the simple isolated zeros of sinc. Around 0 (and every even integer) there is an interval of zeros. At an odd integer the value is 0, but the function jumps across it: at x=1, sinc_N(1)=0 while the left limit is 1/(2π) and the right limit is −1/(2π) for N ≥ 5. The zero at x=1 has no order of vanishing; it is not an analytic zero. Correspondingly, the logarithmic derivative is not meromorphic: on intervals where sinc_N is non-zero, d/dx log|sinc_N| = −1/x plus delta contributions at the greedy breakpoints. Euler's companion identity

π cot(πx) = 1/x + Σ_{n≥1} (1/(x−n) + 1/(x+n))

is a meromorphic identity with no finite-stage analogue. The poles of the cotangent are a feature of the limit, not of the approximation.

What does survive: the Wallis point. At x=1/2 the first greedy step is exact: θ_N(1/2)=1/2 for every N ≥ 1, so sinc_N(1/2)=2/π exactly at every stage (T24–T26). This is the value of Wallis's product. The truncated Euler products decrease to the same limit:

What survives in the limit. If S_N → sin(πx), Euler's product is recovered, and with it ζ(2), the higher values ζ(2k), Wallis and the cotangent expansion. But the harmonic sine adds no independent proof: it reproduces Euler's theorem only after the limit. There is a superficial resonance between the two constructions — the Weierstrass factorization behind Euler's product has genus one because Σ1/n diverges, and the greedy reconstruction needs the divergence of Σ1/(n+2) for its angle approximation — but they use the harmonic sequence for different purposes. Euler's factors are indexed by the zeros n; the greedy steps are additive angle increments. The resonance is not an identity.

The cosine product is out of reach here. The companion identity cos(πx) = Π_{n≥0}(1 − 4x²/(2n+1)²) has its zeros at the half-integers, exactly the points where the greedy sine is exact at every stage. This is a suggestive coincidence, not a greedy cosine product: the source defines no greedy cosine, and this reconstruction does not invent one. The companion product is therefore left out of the ledger rather than tested against an undefined object.

Number-theoretic reading. Euler's sine product is not the Euler product of ζ; it evaluates ζ(2k) by symmetric functions of the zeros, not by multiplicative factors over primes. The greedy data is additive (a sum of unit fractions), so replacing the sine does not produce a multiplicative Euler product and says nothing about the prime factorization of ζ. Any claim that the harmonic sine "explains" ζ(2k) must pass through the sine product's zero factorization, which the finite stages do not have.

Open items and known gaps

  1. Reconstruction limit. θN(y) → y and SN(y) → sin(πy) are inherited source claims, tested but not proved. Every N → ∞ statement is conditional on this.
  2. Limit function. Once the limit is available, prove sincN(x) → sinc(x) for x ≠ 0 and sincN(0) = 0; the content is the failure of uniform convergence.
  3. Integral. The values approach 1/2 very slowly; convergence and rate are open here.
  4. Zero set. A precise description of the zero set of sincN as a union of greedy intervals is not written down.
  5. Bandlimitedness. The Paley–Wiener proof that no finite stage is bandlimited is not formalised; the Fourier transform of sincN is not computed.
  6. Termination. Eventual exactness on rationals is tested, not proved.
  7. No number-theoretic content. This topic says nothing about ζ, primes, or RH.
  8. Euler product. The finite-stage obstruction is a paper argument: an interval of zeros versus a discrete factorization. Formalising infinite-product zero sets is open, and no non-standard product over greedy breakpoints is proposed here.
