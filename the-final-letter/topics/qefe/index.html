The Final Letter · Topic dossier · Inventory item 35

Quantum–Egyptian programme — finite core, lattice theta, and certified zeta limit

Abstract. This dossier develops a machine-checked account — in Lean 4 against Mathlib — of the analytic number theory carried by the quantum dimensions of a modular tensor category, and of the arithmetic it generates. From the squared dimensions dᵢ² and their Egyptian denominators mᵢ = D²/dᵢ² it builds the categorical Dirichlet series Ψ(s) = Σ χ(mᵢ)mᵢ−s and its theta Mellin pair, proves the rank-one and ℤ² theta modular transformations, and closes the circle through completed L-functions, Galois signs and root numbers, and the zeta limits of half-spectra. The square-lattice series is identified as Λ_ℤ²(s) = 4π−sΓ(s)ζ(s)β(s) and shown to be self-dual, Λ(s) = Λ(1−s); the χ₄ scalar series is identified with 2β(s), and the completed beta functional equation Λ_β(s) = Λ_β(1−s) is derived by a characteristic-theta vector route rather than through the classical completed-L-function development, with Mathlib's completed L-function of χ₄ identified with the completed beta on the convergence half-plane and, by analytic continuation, everywhere. The same development yields the exact SU(2)₄ value at σ = 1/2, the root numbers W_Ising = eπi/8 and W_SU(2)₄ = i, and — with complete proofs and numerical certificates — the half-spectrum limits Σj≤k/2dj−2s → ζ(2s) and its signed analogue → η(2s). A structural theorem accompanies these computations: no finite exponential sum with non-zero total can satisfy a modular relation of any weight.

Beyond the categorical setting the dossier extracts a self-contained arithmetic library. It proves # {I ⊂ ℤ[i] : N(I) = n} = Σd|nχ₄(d) and the two-squares theorem r₂(n) = 4Σd|nχ₄(d), the ring equivalence 𝓞(ℚ(i)) ≃+* ℤ[i], and then carries the same machinery to ℚ(√−2) and ℚ(√−3): the rings ℤ[√−2] and ℤ[ω], the bridges 𝓞(ℚ(√−2)) ≃+* ℤ[√−2] and 𝓞(ℚ(√−3)) ≃+* ℤ[ω], the splitting laws p = a² + 2b² for p ≡ 1,3 (mod 8) and p = a² − ab + b² for p ≡ 1 (mod 3), all three local ideal counts at split, inert and ramified primes, multiplicativity, and the closed-form representation counts 2Σd|nχ₈'(d) and 6Σd|nχ₃(d).

The significance is that the finite categorical data determine the analytic invariants: a classification question about modular tensor categories becomes a certified computation about L-functions, root numbers and ideal counts. This places the classical theta–Mellin–functional-equation circle — Riemann zeta, Dirichlet beta, Dedekind zeta and Legendre duplication — inside a single verified development, joins it to verified arithmetic statistics (two-squares, x² + 2y², and the Eisenstein norm form) and to certified rational enclosures, and extends the textbook two-squares picture to the √−2 and Eisenstein settings. It supplies a uniform template for the remaining class-number-one imaginary quadratic fields, a mechanised route to ideal-count identities beyond the classical repertoire, and, through the SU(2)k half-spectrum, a foothold on the tower-limit question beyond the finite case.

Refutation of the displayed equation

The source's Theorem 3.1 displays a gamma factor Γ((1−s+a)/2) in the numerator. That factor has poles at s = 1 + a + 2n, while the categorical Dirichlet polynomial Ψ(s) = Σ χi mi−s is entire. At the pole the identity cannot hold: the right-hand side is infinite meromorphically, or zero under Mathlib's Γ(−n) = 0 convention, while Ψ is finite and non-zero.

The dossier formalises the obstruction for the two worked examples:

  • Gamma_neg_one (T200): Mathlib's value of Γ(−1) at the pole is 0;
  • gammaRatio_ising_pole (T201): gammaRatio 0 3 = 0;
  • qefe_fails_ising (T202): isingPsi 3 ≠ qefeRhs 4 0 1 isingPsi 3;
  • gammaRatio_su2_pole (T203): gammaRatio 1 4 = 0;
  • qefe_fails_su2 (T204): su2Psi 4 ≠ qefeRhs 12 1 1 su2Psi 4.

The point-evaluation form uses Mathlib's junk-value convention at the pole. The structural statement is now proved for both worked examples: the residual is unbounded in norm as s → 3⁻ (T208–T211) and as s → 4⁻ (T212–T215), so the refutation no longer depends on Mathlib's Γ(−n) = 0 convention.

Lemma 2.2 and the finite identity

For a finite integral MTC datum with d₀² = 1, the Egyptian denominators satisfy Σᵢ 1/mᵢ = 1 and, over the non-unit objects, Σᵢ≠₀ 1/mᵢ = 1 − 1/D². Both identities are formalised in Lean (T4–T5) and checked exactly for the Ising and SU(2)₄ data (T13).

The Mellin transform of the finite theta

With Θ(τ) = Σᵢ χ(mᵢ) exp(−π τ / dᵢ²) and Re s > 0, 

∫₀^∞ τ^(s−1) Θ(τ) dτ = Γ(s) π^(−s) Σᵢ χ(mᵢ) dᵢ^(2s)
                        = Γ(s) π^(−s) D^(2s) Ψ(s).

This is the exact dictionary between the finite theta and the categorical Dirichlet polynomial; it is checked symbolically in the Python layer (T15).

Lattice theta and the square-lattice L-series

For a > 0, the rank-one lattice theta is Θaℤ(τ) = Σn∈ℤ e−πa²τn². Mathlib's Gaussian Poisson summation gives the weight-1/2 transformation (T8): 

Θ_a(1/τ) = (√τ / a) Θ_(1/a)(τ).

The square-lattice theta Θℤ²(τ) = Θℤ(τ)² therefore transforms with weight 1 (T9): 

Θ_ℤ²(1/τ) = τ Θ_ℤ²(τ).

Its Mellin transform is the Epstein zeta of ℤ². Using r₂(n) = 4Σd|nχ₄(d), 

Λ_ℤ²(s) = 4 π^(−s) Γ(s) ζ(s) β(s),

where β is the Dirichlet beta function. The completed series is self-dual, Λℤ²(s) = Λℤ²(1−s) (P1); the proof is in L-SERIES-Z2.md. This is the concrete model of the categorical L-series; the general even-lattice case is the standard Poisson step.

Galois signs and root numbers

Ising. With r = √2 and signs ε = (1,−1,1), the normalized S-matrix satisfies S(−r)ij = εiεjS(r)ij for every r (T10). Under √2 ↦ −√2 this is the Galois sign datum ε = (1,−1,1) with identity permutation (T10).

SU(2)₄. The nontrivial conjugation √3 ↦ −√3 admits no real sign/permutation datum: comparison at the (1,1) entry forces r ∈ {0,−1,2}, incompatible with r² = 3 (T11). The Galois automorphism σ13 acts trivially on the S-matrix, swaps objects 2 and 4, and has all signs +1; it is detected on the T-matrix, which swaps θ₂ and θ₄.

The normalized Gauss root number is W = D−1 Σᵢ dᵢ² θᵢ. For the standard T data, WIsing = eπi/8 and WSU(2)₄ = i (T16, T17).

The zeta limit

The correct large-k statement uses the half-spectrum of quantum dimensions. For s > 1/2, 

lim_{k→∞} Σ_{j=0}^{⌊k/2⌋} d_{j,k}^(−2s) = ζ(2s),
lim_{k→∞} Σ_{j=0}^{⌊k/2⌋} (−1)^j d_{j,k}^(−2s) = η(2s) = (1 − 2^(1−2s)) ζ(2s).

The unsigned full-spectrum sum gives 2ζ(2s); the alternating full-spectrum sum is zero for odd k and 2η(2s) for even k. The convergence is computed at k = 2048 (P2); the proof is in HALF-SPECTRUM.md.

Lattice realisation

For an even lattice L, the lattice VOA V_L has simple modules indexed by L*/L, all of quantum dimension 1, and D² = det L = |L*/L|. Consequently a single-lattice realisation with n = D² forces every dᵢ² = 1 (T12). Non-pointed categories such as Ising and SU(2)₄ require a non-lattice VOA; the Dong–Li–Mason lattice-VOA theorem is the cited input (REFERENCES.md). The dossier does not use a converse realisation theorem for non-pointed MTCs.

Proofs and references

There are no load-bearing named inputs left. The two former inputs are resolved: LatticeThetaMellinChi4 is T219 (the identity, proved by T120), and RegularizedAnnularLatticeSeries is retired in favour of the SU(2)_k tower question.

  1. Lattice VOAs — Dong–Li–Mason. For an even lattice L, V_L is rational, its simples are indexed by L*/L, and every simple has quantum dimension 1; hence D² = det L = |L*/L| (T12). Citations: Dong–Li–Mason, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 9 (1996); Dong, J. Algebra 161 (1993). The dossier does not use a converse realisation theorem for non-pointed MTCs.
  2. Galois symmetry — Coste–Gannon and Bantay. The Galois action on the modular S-matrix factorises as a permutation times signs. Citations: Coste–Gannon, Phys. Lett. B 323 (1994); Bantay, Comm. Math. Phys. 233 (2003). Ising is T10; the SU(2)₄ √3 case is T11.
  3. Lattice theta and Epstein zeta. The rank-n Poisson transformation and the Mellin transform of the lattice L-series are standard (Cassels; Conway–Sloane; Iwaniec–Kowalski). Rank one and ℤ² are T8–T9; the ℤ² completed series is P1.
  4. Half-spectrum zeta limit — in-house. The limit Σj≤k/2 dj−2s → ζ(2s) and the signed η(2s) analogue are proved in HALF-SPECTRUM.md using Jordan's inequality and dominated convergence. P2 is the numerical certificate.
  5. MTC definitions — Bakalov–Kirillov; Etingof–Gelaki–Nikshych–Ostrik. T4–T5 take a finite list of positive dᵢ² summing to D² as a hypothesis; they do not construct the MTC.

Full bibliographic details, DOIs, and the exact statement supported by each reference are in REFERENCES.md. The in-house limit proof is in HALF-SPECTRUM.md.
