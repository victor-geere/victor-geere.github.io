The missing bridge

Status: O4 proved (T57), scope-audited. The hard direction FiniteWeilPositivity → RH is Lean-checked and T20 gives the converse, so RH ↔ FiniteWeilPositivity is formalised for the finite zero-side Gram matrix built from the Gaussian-window family. This is a Gaussian-restricted, zero-side form of the classical criterion (no arbitrary test functions, no archimedean/prime terms); RH itself is not claimed, and the remaining open direction is to prove FiniteWeilPositivity. The elementary core (on-line sum of squares, off-line obstruction, PSD convexity, entrywise margin transfer) is Lean-checked. The tractability scouting rates the remaining mechanisms 1/5, 1/5 and 2/5 for available slack; the O2 rounds close the linear, interpolation and width-flow versions as equivalent to positivity or non-monotone, and the O3 first round does not support the finite uniform-gap shape. The O4 hard direction is closed in the finite Gaussian scope; what remains is to prove FiniteWeilPositivity itself.

Part of the weil-positivity hub, the O2 dossier, the formal core, the topic index, and The Final Letter.

Programme

The weil-positivity hub names the zero-side positivity statement and proves the elementary equivalence with RH. A review of that hub found that its advertised blocks ⇒ RH theorem is vacuous as formalised: the hypothesis OffLineBlockPSD is refuted by the hub's own not_offLineBlockPSD_of_offLine. The real content of a bridge is therefore one of three mechanisms.

This topic records those three mechanisms as research objectives, states what each would have to prove, and harvests from index-lean the existing proofs that are useful for each. It does not assume any of them is reachable.

Objective 1 — a manifest sum of squares for the prime side

An identity that writes the prime side as a manifest sum of squares, i.e. an extension of the rank-one factorization off the critical line — which is exactly RH.

What exists. On the critical line the factorization is complete: gaussianZeroTest_eq_mode_mul gives Ha−b(1/2+it) = φa(t) conj φb(t), and zeroSideGramReal_term turns the Gram entry into cosine and sine mode products. onLineQuad_eq_sum_sq writes the quadratic form as an explicit sum of squares; onLineQuad_nonneg is its corollary.

What is missing. Off the critical line there is no factorization with a nonnegative weight: offLineBlock_not_psd exhibits a separation whose isolated block is indefinite, and offLine_growth shows the term is unbounded. On the arithmetic side, vonMangoldt_nonneg proves every coefficient of the prime sum is nonnegative, and prime_side verifies the prime contribution is a sum of nonnegative terms. That is not enough: the archimedean density Ω(t) = Re ψ(1/4+it/2) − log π is signed (negative at t = 0) and the assembled entry takes both signs. A manifest sum of squares would have to come from the whole prime/archimedean assembly.

Why it is hard. An off-line zero would need a rank-one representation with a positive weight, but the off-line contribution grows like e|c||δ|. Any such representation is false as soon as one off-line zero exists.

Objective 2 — a positivity-preserving deformation

A positivity-preserving deformation/continuation argument on the Weil form — an entirely new analytic mechanism.

What exists. posSemidef_convexCombination proves the PSD cone is convex, posSemidef_add_smul proves closure under nonnegative one-parameter deformations, and differentiable_paleyWiener, hasDerivAt_paleyWiener and contour_shift_paleyWiener supply the differentiability and contour-shift machinery. paleyWiener_one_sub and paleyWiener_conj give the reflection symmetries.

What is missing. Convexity is not a deformation argument: it says that if both ends are PSD the path is PSD. What is needed is a path from a manifestly positive object to the Weil form that stays PSD and is analytically controlled at every step. No such path is known; the contour shift moves the test function but does not preserve a sign.

Objective 3 — a weaker, satisfiable statement

A genuinely weaker, satisfiable statement that implies positivity and is independently provable.

What exists. strict_margin_transfer (harvested posDef_of_entry_bound) proves strict positivity with an entrywise margin is stable: C − t·I positive definite, |G − C| ≤ ε entrywise and ε·n < t imply G.PosDef. centre_sub_margin_posDef and centre_certificate_satisfiable instantiate this at the rational 4×4 Gaussian centre. gramQuad_eq_norm_sq and gramMatrix_posSemidef give the finite sum-of-squares shadow.

What is missing. The margin scales as t/n and decays with dimension. For the infinite Weil form there is no n and no uniform entrywise margin; no weaker statement implying infinite positivity is known.

Tractability scouting

The three objectives are not weaker than RH in the way that matters. If any is completed for the full cone of Gaussian test families, it yields Weil positivity, and Weil positivity yields RH by the classical Weil criterion (the ⇒ direction is the nontrivial half; in this repo the full-block domination FullBlockWithin is the step that is not yet formalised, and the hub's isolated-block version is vacuous). So the question is not whether each would prove RH — all three would — but how much slack each offers, and what a first real step looks like.

Scouting O1 — a manifest sum of squares

What success looks like. An explicit identity P(c) = Σr ψr(c) conj ψr(c) (or a positive-definite symbol) for the prime-side kernel P(c) = Σn Λ(n) n−1/2 hc(log n), valid for every separation c, together with the archimedean and pole terms. The on-line identity onLineQuad_eq_sum_sq is the prototype.

What is already known. The factorization is complete on the critical line and fails off it (T3, T5). It is tempting to read vonMangoldt_nonneg as “the prime side is manifestly nonnegative”, but that is a statement about the scalar entry, not the quadratic form. C7 is the correction: every scalar term of the prime kernel is nonnegative, and the symmetrised kernel still has negative eigenvalues for the standard four-centre family at σ = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2. The sign of the kernel is therefore not termwise, and a sum-of-squares identity has to mix primes (and probably the functional equation) rather than group them term by term.

What would raise the estimate. A theta-type identity pairing n with 1/n and turning the von Mangoldt sum into a square; a positivity-preserving transform that diagonalises the prime kernel; or an arithmetic identity that identifies the prime kernel with the zero-side kernel before positivity is imposed.

Cheapest falsification. Extend C7 to the assembled prime + archimedean + pole kernel and test PSD on larger families. Under RH the assembled kernel must be PSD, so a numerical failure would indicate a sign or normalisation error, not a disproof; the useful signal is the size of the negative eigenvalue relative to the archimedean term.

Estimate: 1/5. O1 is the goal restated, not an independent route. It is worth pursuing only if a concrete factorization candidate appears.

Scouting O2 — a positivity-preserving deformation

What success looks like. A family Wt of quadratic forms with W0 a manifest sum of squares (or a finite certificate) and W1 the Weil form, plus a theorem that t ↦ λmin(Wt) does not cross zero.

What is already known. The PSD cone is convex (T6) and closed under nonnegative one-parameter deformations (T7), and the Paley–Wiener machinery is differentiable and admits the contour shift. But convexity says only: if both endpoints are PSD, the path is PSD. Eigenvalue continuity plus a sign at both ends does not prevent two crossings; one needs monotonicity, a maximum principle, or an interlacing argument.

What would raise the estimate. An explicit flow whose semigroup is positivity-preserving (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, Mehler, or a completely positive semigroup) and whose generator is the Weil form; a monotonicity formula for λmin under σ or c; or a Weyl-type inequality comparing the Weil form to a known operator.

Cheapest falsification. Interpolate numerically between the 4×4 certificate and a nearby kernel and track λmin; if it crosses zero on the natural path, that deformation is dead. The test is cheap and discriminating.

Estimate: 1/5 after the second round. The first round closes the linear and interpolation versions as equivalent to positivity, and the second round kills the Gaussian-width flow. No surviving candidate remains.

Scouting O3 — a weaker, satisfiable statement

What success looks like. A statement S such that (i) S is provable without assuming RH and (ii) S implies positivity on the full cone.

The logical trap. (i) and (ii) together are a proof of RH. So S cannot be “weaker” in the sense of “less than RH”; it can only be weaker in hypothesis strength while still sufficient — a sufficient condition whose proof uses structure that the full positivity statement does not expose. The finite certificate is the cautionary case: centre_certificate_satisfiable is independently provable and the margin statement is stable, but it is not sufficient for the infinite form, and its margin t/n decays with dimension (C5). A “satisfiable” S that is not also sufficient is worth nothing here.

Two plausible shapes. (a) A dense subcone C of the admissible cone with positivity on C, plus a uniform bound that lets positivity pass to the closure. (b) Approximate positivity with an explicitly dominated error, where the archimedean and pole terms absorb the residual. Both need uniform control of the Weil form, which is the hard analysis.

What would raise the estimate. A precise definition of the subcone and a proof that the Weil form is continuous in a topology where the subcone is dense; or a negative theorem showing that no checkable sufficient condition of a given class exists, which would redirect effort.

Cheapest falsification. Push the finite certificate to n = 5, 6, ... and measure the margin decay; if the margin collapses faster than 1/n, the finite route is dead without a new idea.

Estimate: 2/5. The most promising logical shape, because it permits slack; the danger is that the slack is illusory and the sufficient condition is RH again. The first round below does not support the finite uniform-gap shape, so the 2/5 now refers only to the dense-subcone/continuity shape.

Bottom line

  • All three objectives would prove RH if completed. O1 is equivalent to RH; O2 and O3 are sufficient conditions that would prove it.
  • None can yield more than RH: each is a sufficient condition for a statement equivalent to RH, so a completed objective is a proof of RH and nothing stronger.
  • O1 has no slack and is not a stepping stone; O2 is the only place a new analytic mechanism can enter; O3 is a search for a genuine sufficient condition, and its first task is to define the subcone precisely.
  • A completed objective is a proof of RH, not evidence for it. Nothing in this folder changes that.

O2 first round — no-crossing, or no free lunch?

The scouting note proposed three mechanisms: monotonicity, a maximum principle, or interlacing. The first round tests the first two on the finite model and records what survives.

T11 — positivity-preserving maps need not be eigenvalue-monotone. posSemidef_sandwich proves that the congruence A ↦ S A Sᵀ preserves positive semidefiniteness for every real S. C8 shows that it can lower the least eigenvalue: for A = [[2,1],[1,2]] and S = diag(1, 1/2), the least eigenvalue drops from 1 to about 0.349, and for S = diag(1,0) it drops to 0. So a maximum principle that knows only “positivity is preserved” does not give a monotonicity theorem for λ_min.

T12 — the linear path has no slack. linear_path_posSemidef_iff proves the exact characterisation

(∀ t ≥ 0, (A + t • B).PosSemidef) ↔ A.PosSemidef ∧ B.PosSemidef.

So a linear interpolation from a known-PSD block to the Weil block is PSD for all t ≥ 0 exactly when the velocity block is already PSD. If the Weil endpoint is indefinite, the path crosses zero. C9 is the two-dimensional instance: with A = I and B = [[0,1],[1,0]], λ_min = 1 − t crosses at t = 1; with B = I, λ_min = 1 + t never crosses.

The continuity obstruction. More generally, if A is PSD and B is not, then every continuous path from A to B crosses λ_min = 0, because λ_min is continuous and changes sign. A no-crossing theorem for a path from a known-PSD object to the Weil form is therefore equivalent to the Weil form being PSD. It cannot be a weaker statement: the “no-crossing” formulation has the same content as positivity.

The canonical flow is blocked. The de Bruijn–Newman heat flow is the standard nonlinear deformation of the Riemann Ξ-function: Ht(z) = ∫0∞ et u² Φ(u) cos(zu) du has only real zeros for t ≥ Λ, and RH is equivalent to Λ ≤ 0. Rodgers and Tao proved Λ ≥ 0 (The de Bruijn–Newman constant is non-negative, Forum Math. Pi 8 (2020)), so the cooling direction t < 0 has non-real zeros. The heat flow therefore cannot be used to propagate positivity backwards to t = 0; the most natural maximum-principle candidate is known to fail in exactly the direction that would help. (This is a statement about the Ξ-flow, not about the Gaussian Weil form directly, but it is the canonical instance of the mechanism.)

What survives. A nonlinear flow Wt with all three of: a genuine monotonicity theorem for λmin (not merely positivity preservation), a manifestly PSD limit, and a link from the limit back to the Weil form. None of the three is available, and the first round does not supply one. What it does supply is a clean closure of the naive readings.

The logical reading. T12 and the continuity obstruction show that O2 is not a weaker statement than positivity: it is a different proof strategy for the same statement. The PSD cone is closed, so a limit of PSD forms is PSD; a continuous path, a discrete sequence, or a monotone flow all prove the same endpoint. The same is true of O1, which is an algebraic proof strategy for the same statement. Only O3 is formulated as a genuinely weaker statement. So the scouting table's “would it prove RH?” is not the interesting question for O1 and O2: both would, because both are the same theorem approached from a different angle. O2's value is methodological — a new mechanism — not logical — a smaller target.

Revised reading of the rating. The 2/5 in the scouting table now refers only to the surviving nonlinear-flow version. The interpolation and linear versions are 1/5: T12 shows they are equivalent to positivity, and the continuity obstruction shows that no continuous path can do better.

O2 second round — the width flow is not monotone

The surviving O2 candidate was a monotone flow in the Gaussian width σ. This round kills it.

T13–T16 — the on-line weight is not monotone. The on-line kernel weight is gaussianWeight σ γ = 2πσ² exp(−σ²γ²), with derivative 4πσ exp(−σ²γ²)(1 − σ²γ²) (hasDerivAt_gaussianWeight). The derivative is positive at σ = 1/(2γ) and negative at σ = 2/γ (gaussianWeight_deriv_pos, gaussianWeight_deriv_neg), so the weight is neither monotone nor antitone in σ (gaussianWeight_not_monotone). The σ-flow therefore fails on the on-line part, before any arithmetic enters.

C10 — the full Gram is not monotone either. For the 4×4 arithmetic Gram on the centres −0.3, −0.1, 0.1, 0.3, the derivative of λ_min along the minimising eigenvector is +0.196 at σ = 0.02 and −0.258 at σ = 0.03. The sign change confirms the failure for the assembled 4×4 form, not just the on-line weight.

Verdict. O2 is parked at 1/5. The linear version is equivalent to positivity (T12 and the continuity obstruction); the σ-flow is not monotone (T13–T16, C10); the canonical heat flow is blocked by Rodgers–Tao. The only surviving version is a nonlinear flow with a genuine λ_min monotonicity theorem, and there is no candidate.

O3 first round — the uniform gap collapses

The scouting proposed two shapes for a weaker statement. The first — uniform positivity on a fixed interval — is directly testable.

C11 — the finite gap is not uniform. Take n equally spaced centres in [−0.3, 0.3], form the Toeplitz arithmetic Gram K(|i−j|h), and compute λ_min for n = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 at σ = 0.05:

If the continuous kernel had a positive gap, the Toeplitz eigenvalues would grow like 1/h as h → 0, not vanish. The observation is consistent with a continuous kernel that is positive semidefinite but not positive definite on the interval, or with a gap below the numerical resolution; higher precision is needed to distinguish the two.

Verdict. The uniform-gap shape of O3 is not supported at the tested resolution. The only surviving shape is the dense-subcone/continuity one, which needs a uniform bound on the Weil form — the missing FullBlockWithin/TailEntryBound estimate again. O3 stays at 2/5, but with less room than before.

Does the angular partition method retire the tail bound?

The angular partition method (APM) is the natural candidate for making the FullBlockWithin/TailEntryBound obstruction obsolete: its angular/radial axis is advertised as an exact measure identity with no truncation. The investigation below reads the APM's own claims and applies them to the specific tail in this dossier.

What the APM claims. For a summable nonnegative series, the annular representation gives Σn an = μ(X) = R0² with squared tail radii Rn² = Σk≥n ak; the finite sums are chords of the tail coil. In the summary table of APM §6 the angular/radial axis is described as “exact algebraic/measure identity; no tail”, against the depth axis's “needs a closed tail or a bound”.

What it does not claim. The same table says the angular limit is “closed forms only in special cases”. APM §7.4 is explicit: “A representation is not automatically an evaluation… The method does not manufacture closed forms.” For ζ(s) in the convergence domain, the coil is exact “although evaluating Z0 is still the original problem” (APM §2.3). For the discrete zeta sum, APM §5.7 is decisive: the even-Bernoulli sampling defect survives, “Nothing in the angular construction removes it”, and “Parity does not remove the tail.” The APM's exactness audit classifies the explicit-formula zero sum and Weil positivity (row A15) as O open, and APM §7.10 lists ZeroTailSummable and FullBlockWithin among the remaining named analytic inputs.

Why the zero-side tail is not covered. The annular representation is for a nonnegative series, or for an absolutely summable complex series via the vector-area/signed-measure version (APM §7.3). The zero-side Gram entry is real but signed: the on-line weights 2πσ² exp(−σ²γ²) are positive, but the cosine factors change sign, and the off-line quadruple contribution is signed as well. C12 makes the point concretely: the total on-line weight is positive (0.0196 for the first four ordinates at σ = 0.05) while the Gram entry at separation c = 2 is negative (−0.0088). The nonnegative annular area represents the total weight, not the signed entry; applying the APM to the entry requires a signed measure, and the sign is exactly the positivity question. Moreover, finiteness of the annular area is the summability input ZeroTailSummable, not a consequence of the representation: the identity R0² < ∞ restates the input.

What the APM does contribute. The regularised Mellin kernel and the finite-part continuation (APM §5.6, mellinConvergent_regularizedKernel, and the two-strips-beyond continuation regularizedKernel₂) are genuine tail-subtraction results on the analytic side: they make the vertical Mellin integral convergent by subtracting exactly the pole terms. That can reduce the VerticalLogDerivIntegrable/contour-shift inputs of the explicit formula. It does not touch the zero-set tail: FullBlockWithin is a bound on the contribution of other zeros to a Gram entry, not a vertical Mellin integral.

Verdict. The APM restates a tail as a radius or a measure, and it does so exactly; that is a change of bookkeeping, not an elimination. It gives a closed form only when the tail radius has one (geometric, p-series in the convergence domain, the continuous integral 1/(σ−1)). For the discrete zeta sum and the zero-side Gram entry, it explicitly does not: the even-Bernoulli tail survives and the zero sum is open in the APM's own audit. It therefore does not make the missing-bridge tail bound obsolete. The most it can do for ZeroTailSummable is restate it as R0² < ∞, which is the same input; for FullBlockWithin it offers nothing new. Its regularised continuation may help the analytic assembly, not the zero-side obstruction.

The maximal-distance precondition of the full-block route

The full-block obstruction needs a bound B on the “other” zeros for a fixed off-line quadruple Q. This section records a structural precondition that the programme has not checked.

The growth rate. The closed form offLineQuad_re_exp is S(c) = 2A(ecδcos(cγ+θ) + e−cδcos(cγ−θ)), so a quadruple with distance |δ| from the critical line grows like ec|δ|. The growing term comes from the reflected zero 1−ρ, which is present for every off-line zero.

T17 — an off-line quadruple in the “other” block defeats every uniform bound. no_uniform_bound_of_offLine_quadruple proves that for δ ≠ 0 there is no B with |S(c)| ≤ B for all c: for every B some separation has |S(c)| > B. So a fixed B cannot bound the other zeros for all separations.

The precondition. If B is allowed to depend on c, the domination inequality still requires the chosen Q to have strictly larger |δ| than every other zero. Otherwise a larger-|δ| quadruple grows faster and eventually dominates Q. C13 makes this concrete at σ = 0.05: the maximum of |S(c)| over c ∈ [0,40] is 1.04 for |δ| = 0.1 and 30.4 for |δ| = 0.2, and the larger one exceeds any fixed bound as c grows.

Is the precondition known? No. The known zero-free line Re s = 1 gives |δ| < 1/2, but nothing in the programme (or in the standard zero-free region) gives a zero whose |δ| is strictly maximal. If the off-line zeros have |δ| values with no strict maximum, the clean domination argument cannot be completed. The escape — choosing c so that the larger-|δ| contributions cancel by phase — is an almost-periodic/Diophantine problem, and a uniform bound would need control of the zero set far beyond what the programme has.

The fork.

  1. Prove a strict maximal-|δ| zero. This is a strong structural statement about ζ, not known; it is not implied by the zero-free line, and if RH fails it is plausible that the off-line zeros accumulate without a strict maximiser.
  2. Develop a phase-selection argument. Choose c so that the larger-|δ| contributions cancel. This is hard and no such argument is in the programme.
  3. Return to the arithmetic side. The explicit formula turns the full zero-side entry into pole + prime + archimedean terms; the obstruction then becomes a prime-sum estimate in a Gaussian window, which is the classical route. It does not need a maximal off-line zero.

The first is not affordable with the current programme, the second has no candidate, and the third is the only route that does not rest on an unproved structural assumption. The next investment should be the arithmetic-side estimate, not FullBlockWithin.

The arithmetic side — first round

The classical route (Weil's criterion) evaluates the Weil form on the arithmetic side: the explicit formula writes the zero-side entry as pole + prime + archimedean. This round checks the prime-sum structure that the estimate has to control.

The prime sum and its main term. For the Gaussian test, the prime contribution is

P(c,σ) = Σn Λ(n) n−1/2 hc(log n), hc(u) = √π σ exp(−(u−c)²/(4σ²)).

A smooth-weight PNT (dψ(x) ≈ dx) gives the main term

Pmain(c,σ) = ∫ eu/2 hc(u) du = 2πσ² exp(σ²/4) exp(c/2).

This is exactly the growing half of the pole term Hc(0) + Hc(1) = 4πσ² exp(σ²/4) cosh(c/2):

pole(c) − Pmain(c,σ) = 2πσ² exp(σ²/4) exp(−c/2).

So the exponential growth of the pole is matched by the prime main term; the residual is the PNT error plus the e−c/2 half-pole and the archimedean term.

C14. For σ = 0.2, P/Pmain at c = 10 is 1.000004; for σ = 0.05 it is 1.013. The narrow-window regime is much less accurate: the Gaussian window in n has width e8σ, so small σ is exactly where the smooth-weight PNT error is significant. This is the same σ = 0.05 regime as the O4 certificate.

The exact Gram-entry identity. The main term identity is even stronger than a leading-order statement: for every c,

pole(c) = Pmain(c) + Pmain(−c),

because Pmain(±c) = 2πσ² exp(σ²/4) exp(±c/2) and 4πσ² exp(σ²/4) cosh(c/2) is their sum. Hence the symmetrised arithmetic Gram entry is

K(c) = [Pmain(c) − P(c)] + [Pmain(−c) − P(−c)] + arch(c).

The pole term cancels exactly, and the entry is the sum of the two Gaussian-window PNT errors plus the archimedean term. C15 checks this to 1e−12. This is the precise form of “the arithmetic side is a prime-sum estimate”: the object to bound is the PNT error in the two windows ±c, plus arch(c).

The cancellation (C16). The two pieces are individually indefinite. At σ = 0.05 on the four-centre family, the PNT-error Gram has λmin ≈ −0.0097 and the archimedean Gram has λmin ≈ −0.0946, while their sum has λmin ≈ +0.0227. So the arithmetic-side positivity is a cancellation between the prime-window error and the archimedean term, not a termwise positivity. The estimate must control the joint sum, not the PNT error alone.

What the estimate must deliver. A uniform smooth-weight PNT with an explicit error term for the Gaussian window, plus a bound on the archimedean term, such that the residual is controlled. This is the “prime-sum estimate” of the classical route. It does not by itself prove positivity: the prime sum is a signed quadratic form in the test function (the autocorrelation g * g̃ changes sign), and the archimedean term is signed. The residual's sign is Weil positivity.

Verdict. The arithmetic side is a genuine route, and the main-term cancellation is exact. The hard part is the narrow-window PNT error at small σ. The next concrete step is to state the full Weil criterion in the full Gram-matrix form and to write the residual as a PNT error plus the archimedean term, then test its sign on a family at σ = 0.05.

Full Weil positivity — the finite statement

The hub's WeilPositivity is the on-line kernel identity; the object of the classical criterion is the full zero-side Gram matrix over all zeros. The finite version is now formalised.

The definition. FiniteWeilPositivity says: for every finite set S of nontrivial zeros, every finite family of centres a, and every width σ, the real part of the Gram matrix

Gij = Σρ ∈ S Hai − aj(ρ)

is positive semidefinite.

T18–T39.

  • realRankOne_posSemidef — a real rank-one matrix u uᵀ is PSD.
  • finiteWeilGramReal_posSemidef_of_onLine — if every ρ ∈ S has Re ρ = 1/2, the finite Gram matrix is PSD. Each on-line zero contributes Re(vi conj vj) = Re vi Re vj + Im vi Im vj, a sum of two real rank-one matrices.
  • rh_implies_finiteWeilPositivity — RH implies FiniteWeilPositivity.

This is the easy direction of Weil's criterion, and it is non-vacuous: the hypothesis is satisfiable (RH is the intended case), unlike the hub's OffLineBlockPSD antecedent, which is refuted by not_offLineBlockPSD_of_offLine.

The hard direction. FiniteWeilPositivity → RH for the finite Gaussian zero-side Gram matrix is now Lean-proved (T57); the infinite/cone version remains outside the formalisation. What remains open is FiniteWeilPositivity itself (the arithmetic side). The maximal-|δ| analysis shows that the zero-side domination route to it has an unproven precondition; the classical route uses the arithmetic side and the explicit formula, as recommended above.

What this fixes. The hub's no_offLine_of_all_blocks_psd was vacuous; rh_implies_finiteWeilPositivity is the correct formal counterpart of the easy direction. The dossier now has both directions named, with the easy one proved and the hard one open.

Exhaustion verdict

At this point every non-RH-strength route in the dossier has been either proved, reduced, or closed as unsupported.

Closed or reduced.

  • O1 (manifest sum of squares for the prime side): RH restated, no slack; the prime kernel is indefinite (C7).
  • O2 (positivity-preserving deformation): linear and interpolation versions are equivalent to positivity (T12); the σ-flow is not monotone (T13–T16, C10); the heat flow is blocked by Rodgers–Tao.
  • O3 (weaker satisfiable statement): the finite uniform-gap route is unsupported (C11); only the dense-subcone/continuity shape remains, which needs a uniform bound.
  • Zero-side domination (FullBlockWithin): requires a strict maximal-|δ| zero (T17); no such zero is known.
  • APM: restates tails as radii; does not retire the tail bound; the zero sum is open in its own audit.
  • Arithmetic side: the pole cancels exactly against the two Gaussian-window prime main terms (C15); the PNT error and the archimedean term are individually indefinite and their sum is positive (C16). The cancellation persists under refinement, but the gap collapses (C17), matching C11.
  • Full Weil positivity, easy direction: RH ⇒ FiniteWeilPositivity is Lean-proved (T20).

What remains is RH-strength.

  1. O4 — the hard direction of Weil's criterion. FiniteWeilPositivity → RH is proved for the finite Gaussian zero-side Gram matrix (T57); the infinite/cone version remains outside this formalisation, and FiniteWeilPositivity itself is the remaining open input.
  2. A uniform arithmetic estimate. A bound on the joint PNT-error + archimedean sum that does not collapse as the family grows. C17 shows the finite-family gap collapsing, so this is a genuine analytic input, not a bookkeeping gap.
  3. A strict maximal-|δ| zero (or a phase-selection argument replacing it), if the zero-side route is to be revived.

Verdict. The hard direction of the classical Weil criterion is proved for the finite Gaussian zero-side Gram matrix (T57), closing O4 in that scope. The dossier remains a map of the wall, not a proof of RH: FiniteWeilPositivity (equivalently RH) on the arithmetic side is the remaining open problem.

Lean layer

lean/Topic.lean proves T1–T39 by harvesting GaussianZeroSide and Transfer and adding the on-line sum-of-squares identity, the PSD convexity lemmas, the σ-flow non-monotonicity, the maximal-|δ| obstruction, and the finite-zero-set easy direction of Weil positivity. It does not prove O1, O2, O3 or O4, and it does not prove the infinite/cone Weil form.

Open items and known gaps

  • Objective 1 (O1) — no manifest sum of squares for the prime side off the critical line is known; the finite on-line identity is complete and the obstruction is the exponential off-line growth (T3, T5). The scouting verdict is that O1 is RH restated and has no slack (1/5).
  • Objective 2 (O2) — the linear versions are equivalent to positivity; the Gaussian-width flow is not monotone (T13–T16, C10); the heat flow is blocked. No candidate remains (1/5).
  • Objective 3 (O3) — the finite uniform-gap route is dead (C11); only the dense-subcone/continuity shape remains, which needs the missing uniform bound (2/5).
  • No-go for the existing hub — the blocks ⇒ RH implication in weil-positivity is vacuous as formalised because its antecedent is refutable. This topic does not repair that statement; it records what a non-vacuous replacement would have to prove.
  • Full-block step — even a completed objective gives Weil positivity only for the full cone; the passage from full positivity to RH is the classical Weil criterion, and its formalisation here still needs the full-block domination FullBlockWithin.
  • Hard direction of Weil's criterion (O4) — FiniteWeilPositivity → RH is open; the maximal-|δ| analysis rules out the clean zero-side domination route, and the arithmetic side is the recommended one.
  • Scope — nothing here is evidence for RH, and no objective is claimed as partially solved.
