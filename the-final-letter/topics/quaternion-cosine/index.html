Quaternion–cosine framework

A reconstructed research programme: elementary formal core, exact computation, and preserved open claims

Status. The archived source is a speculative, conversation-derived essay. This folder reconstructs its precise elementary content—quaternion units, a lifted cosine phase, a divisor detector, an alignment energy, and a warping factor—and proves those facts in Lean and Python. The source’s central conjecture about the Riemann hypothesis is not proved and is recorded as open.

Provenance and status

Source: archive/essays/rh/riemann-hypothesis.html, “Exploring a Quaternion-Cosine Geometric Approach to the Riemann Hypothesis”. Inventory: Scraps item 39 · Topic index.

Related archived restatements: rh-cosine-quaternion-framework.html and dictionary-cos-quaternions.html. Neither adds a proof; both repeat the same conjectural programme.

The source is explicit about its status:

“While the approach remains highly speculative and currently lacks rigorous justification, it offers an interesting visualization-based intuition…” (source, §1)

Its conclusion says the framework “remains far from a rigorous proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.” Section 8 lists the acknowledged gaps: no bridge from the continuous energy functional to prime sums, no rigorous connection between cosine alignment and zeros of ξ, no sharp nonlinear exponential-sum bounds, an unjustified logarithmic-to-geometric substitution, and uncontrolled constants.

What the source does define precisely enough to reconstruct is a small elementary core: Hamilton quaternion units, a lifted cosine phase, a scalar divisor detector, the periodicity cos(2πn) = 1, an alignment energy, and a warping factor 2σ − 1. The leap from these objects to the Riemann hypothesis is not reconstructed.

Reconstructed objects and notation

ObjectDefinitionRole in the source
Quaternion unitsi, j, k with i² = j² = k² = −1, ij = k, ji = −kThe algebra in which the two cosine phases are separated.
Divisor detectorD(x,p) = cos(2πp/x) + cos(2πx) − 2Claimed to vanish exactly at positive divisors x of p.
Cosine phaseφ(x) = cos(2πx) i + cos(2πp/x) jLifts the two scalar cosines into orthogonal quaternion coordinates.
Lifted displacementδ(x) = (1 − cos(2πx)) i + (1 − cos(2πp/x)) jThe vector from the target i + j to the phase.
Alignment energyE(x) = (1 − cos(2πx))² + (1 − cos(2πp/x))²Squared distance from perfect alignment; always non-negative.
Warping factorw(σ) = 2σ − 1Vanishes on the critical line; models functional-equation distortion.
Warped heightt′ = t / (2σ − 1)Formal expression only; undefined at σ = 1/2.

Two notational points matter. First, the source’s prose writes t′ ~ t/(2σ−1), while its sample Python code uses effective_t = t / abs(sigma - 0.5); these differ even by a factor of two. The reconstruction records the prose expression exactly and treats the code version as an inconsistency. Second, the source sometimes says alignment with “i + j (or any scaled version thereof)”; the energy E vanishes only at (1,1), so the formal core uses i + j exactly.

T4 is the source’s elementary divisor-detector claim, formalised for real x ≠ 0; for integer p the two integer conditions are exactly divisibility, and the Lean statement does not assume p is an integer. The Lean proof of T4 uses T3 twice. T1 and T2 are the quaternion facts the source actually uses; they are not rfl restatements, since the product and squared norm are computed from the structure of ℍ[ℝ].

Python layer

python/programme.py implements exact sympy computations:

  • phase(x,p) — the quaternion cos(2πx) i + cos(2πp/x) j as a four-tuple;
  • quaternion_mul, quaternion_norm_sq — Hamilton product and squared norm;
  • detector(x,p) — cos(2πp/x) + cos(2πx) − 2;
  • alignment_energy(x,p), displacement_quaternion(x,p);
  • is_divisor(p,x), divisors_exact(p), detector_on_divisors(p);
  • warp_factor(σ), warped_height(t,σ);
  • is_aligned(x,p, tol=None).

All decisions in the tests are exact; the only approximate helper is the optional tol branch of is_aligned, which is not used by the suite. Run from the topic directory:

cd the-final-letter/topics/quaternion-cosine
python3 -m pytest -q tests

Current result: 7 passed (about 0.3 s). The suite covers every Computation row and the edge cases x ≤ 0, p ≤ 0, and p = 1.

Open items and known gaps

#KindStatementWhy it is open
O1Open / conjectureinfx E(x) = 0 is equivalent to ξ(σ+it) = 0.The energy–zero bridge is missing in both directions; the source identifies this as the central gap (§8.2).
O2Open / conjectureThe warping t′ ~ t/(2σ−1) follows from ξ(s) = ξ(1−s).It is asserted, not derived; the source’s own code uses a different factor. A rigorous treatment needs a precise definition of “effective frequency”.
O3Open / conjectureFor σ ≠ 1/2, infx E(x) > c > 0 for some universal c.The off-line lower bound is not established, and no explicit constant is given.
O4Open / conjecturePrime-phase cancellation connects E to sums over primes.Requires nonlinear exponential-sum bounds that the source acknowledges are beyond current technology.
O5Open / conjectureThe “dyadic condition” of the central conjecture is well defined.The source refers to a dyadic expansion “aligned with the natural logarithmic spacing of primes” but states no precise condition; it cannot be formalised as written.
O6Open / conjectureThe pointwise divisor identity can be upgraded to a statement about minimisation over x > 0.T4 and C3–C5 verify the detector and energy at exact points; they say nothing about minimisation, which is where the RH conjecture lives.
O7Open / conjectureProbabilistic or spectral input (GUE, Berry–Keating, Connes) supplies a proof.That discussion is contextual in the source, not part of a proved argument.
