# The arithmetic obstruction

**Status:** the Arakelov segment is standard mathematics, recorded as source
claims S3–S4. The refinement of the wall stated at the end is this dossier's
interpretation, not a theorem.

## What exists: an arithmetic intersection form

Arakelov (1974) extended intersection theory to arithmetic surfaces: a regular
proper model `𝔛 → Spec O_K` of a curve over a number field, with a Green's
function added at each archimedean place. Divisors meet in a real-valued
pairing

\[
D \cdot E \in \mathbb{R},
\]

which combines the finite fibres with the archimedean contribution.

The arithmetic Hodge index theorem is a theorem. Faltings and Hriljac (1985)
identified the Arakelov self-intersection of a degree-zero divisor with the
negative of the Néron–Tate height:

\[
D \cdot D = -\hat h(D), \qquad \deg D = 0 .
\]

The height pairing `\hat h` is positive definite on `J(K) ⊗ R` (Northcott).
Hence the Arakelov intersection form is **negative definite** on the
degree-zero part modulo principal divisors. The negative-definite half of the
Hodge index, the half that the function-field proof uses, is available.

The simplest arithmetic surface whose zeta function involves `ζ` is `P¹_Z`,
with

\[
\zeta_{P^1_{\mathbb Z}}(s) = \zeta(s)\,\zeta(s-1).
\]

So the intersection form is not missing for `ζ`'s ambient geometry.

## What is missing: a Frobenius correspondence

Over `F_q` the Frobenius endomorphism is a single morphism. It generates the
Galois group of `F̄_q / F_q`, its fixed points are the rational points, and its
Lefschetz trace is the zeta function. A correspondence with a trace is exactly
what the surface `C × C` is for.

Over `Q` there is no analogue:

- `Gal(Q̄/Q)` is not generated by one element; the Frobenius at `p` is a
  conjugacy class, defined only up to conjugation, not a correspondence on a
  surface.
- The Euler product is a product of local data, not the characteristic
  polynomial of one operator. Multiplying local factors does not produce a
  global correspondence.
- The functional equation `s ↦ 1-s` is a symmetry of the completed `ξ`. It
  plays the role of the `α ↦ q/α` pairing, but a symmetry is not a
  correspondence: it gives the relation between the two halves of the
  spectrum, not the spectrum itself.
- The archimedean place is infinite-dimensional. Its contribution is a Green's
  function, not a finite intersection matrix.

## The refined statement

On the intersection-form route the wall is **not** the absence of a positivity.
The positivity is a theorem (Faltings–Hriljac plus Northcott). The wall is the
absence of the arithmetic object whose spectrum the positivity would constrain:

> an intersection form without a correspondence cannot see the zeros.

This refines W1 rather than contradicting it. The programme's "arithmetic
positivity" is a conjunction: an arithmetic object and a positivity. In the
function-field case both are present, and the positivity is the Hodge index.
In the number-field case the positivity is present and the correspondence is
not. A proof of RH by this route would have to supply the correspondence.

## What would count as a construction

A Weil cohomology for `Spec Z` with:

1. a Frobenius correspondence `F` whose Lefschetz trace is `ξ`;
2. a polarization, i.e. a positive-definite intersection form compatible with
   `F` and with the functional equation;
3. a Hodge-index statement relating the two.

This is the Hilbert–Pólya problem in intersection-theoretic dress. No such
object is known; the finite shadows are the Weil Gram matrices of
[`../../prime-spectrum-tensor/WEIL-GRAM.md`](../../prime-spectrum-tensor/WEIL-GRAM.md),
whose numerical positive definiteness is a finite check, not the infinite
positivity. The infinite positivity is equivalent to RH, as
[`../../prime-spectrum-tensor/WEIL-POSITIVITY.md`](../../prime-spectrum-tensor/WEIL-POSITIVITY.md)
records.

## The finite shadow

For a finite test-function basis the intersection form becomes a Gram matrix.
`prime-spectrum-tensor` checks a `4 × 4` Gaussian instance numerically positive
definite (smallest eigenvalue `2.27 · 10⁻²`). That instance is now wired into
this dossier and certified with an enclosed `Ω` integral: see
[`04-certified-shadow.md`](04-certified-shadow.md). The form exists, the
positivity is certified at finite rank, and the passage to the infinite cone
remains the open problem.
