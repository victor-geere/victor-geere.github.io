# The certified 4×4 finite shadow

**Status:** a finite computational certificate, not a statement about the
infinite Weil cone, and not a proof of RH. The interval matrix and the
centre/error check are recorded in
[`../python/weil_certificate.json`](../python/weil_certificate.json) and
verified by [`../tests/test_weil_shadow.py`](../tests/test_weil_shadow.py).
A second, tighter certificate is generated by the Rust crate
[`../rust/weil-assembly`](../rust/weil-assembly); its box data and
positivity are kernel-checked in
[`../lean/Certificate.lean`](../lean/Certificate.lean) (T91–T100).

## The basis

Take four Gaussians on the real line,

\[
\phi_a(u)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(u-a)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
\qquad \sigma=0.05,\quad a\in\{-0.3,-0.1,0.1,0.3\}.
\]

For real test functions the Weil quadratic form is `Q(f) = W(f * f̌)`. Its
polarisation gives the Gram matrix

\[
G_{ab}=\frac{W(h_{ab})+W(h_{ba})}{2},
\qquad
h_{ab}=\phi_a*\check\phi_b,
\]

and the convolution is again a Gaussian:

\[
h_{ab}(v)=\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma\,
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(v-(a-b))^2}{4\sigma^2}\right).
\]

Every entry is therefore a one-dimensional integral plus a finite prime sum.

## The three terms

For the Gaussian `h` centred at `c`:

| term | formula | evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| pole / archimedean | `4πσ² exp(σ²/4) cosh(c/2)` | closed form |
| density | `σ² ∫ exp(−σ²t²) cos(ct) Ω(t) dt` | certified Taylor panels |
| prime | `−2 Σ_{p^m ≤ X} (log p)/√(p^m) · h(log(p^m))` | finite sieve, `X = 20000` |

with

\[
\Omega(t)=\Re\psi\!\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}{2}\right)-\log\pi .
\]

The density integral is the “enclosed `Ω` integral” of the brief.  The
recorded enclosures are

| `c` | enclosed density `σ² ∫ exp(−σ²t²) cos(ct) Ω(t) dt` |
|---:|---|
| 0 | `[0.00610, 0.01723]` |
| 0.2 | `[−0.05335, −0.04303]` |
| 0.4 | `[−0.02904, −0.01943]` |
| 0.6 | `[−0.02167, −0.01255]` |

The enclosure uses the certified `Ω`-jet layer of
[`../../prime-spectrum-tensor/python/programme.py`](../../prime-spectrum-tensor/python/programme.py)
(C9–C21 of that dossier), invoked by
[`../python/certify_shadow.py`](../python/certify_shadow.py).  The same `Ω`
enclosure is vendored into [`../python/weil_shadow.py`](../python/weil_shadow.py)
so the dossier can check it without the heavy run.

## The `Ω` enclosure

From the digamma integral representation, with `z = 1/4 + it/2`,

\[
\Re\psi(z)=\log|z|-\Re\frac{1}{2z}
-2\int_0^\infty
\frac{u\,\bigl(u^2+(\Re z)^2-(\Im z)^2\bigr)}
{\bigl(u^2+(\Re z)^2-(\Im z)^2\bigr)^2
 +4(\Re z)^2(\Im z)^2}
\,\frac{du}{e^{2\pi u}-1}.
\]

The implementation uses outward-rounded `mpmath.iv` intervals, a midpoint
rule with an interval derivative bound, a Taylor series for
`u/(e^{2πu}−1)` near zero, and an explicit exponential tail.  Representative
widths:

| `t` | width of the `Ω` enclosure |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | `3.04·10⁻²` |
| 0.5 | `2.86·10⁻³` |
| 1 | `3.76·10⁻³` |
| 10 | `7.08·10⁻⁵` |

These are enclosures of point values.  The density integral over `t` is
enclosed separately by the Taylor panels: on each panel the `Ω` jet supplies
the value and the first four `t`-derivatives, and the interval fourth
derivative bounds the Taylor remainder.

## The recorded interval matrix

The recorded run uses `H = 0.1`, `T = 100`, `U = 4`, `order = 4`,
`h = 0.05` and 2000 panels.  After the panel and jet remainders, a
conservative `10⁻⁴` margin is added to every entry (it dominates the
provisional `10⁻⁶` jet-tail allowance).  The resulting interval matrix has
four distinct entries:

```
D  [ 0.037436204008… , 0.048769575661… ]
A  [-0.021860546171… , -0.011336943458… ]
B  [ 0.002918627733… , 0.012729392455… ]
C  [-0.007153280830… , 0.002171379714… ]
```

with

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
D&A&B&C\\
A&D&A&B\\
B&A&D&A\\
C&B&A&D
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The recorded certificate file carries the decimal endpoints, the enclosed
density intervals, the `Ω` samples, and the numerical cross-checks.  The
density cache records the density endpoints at 20 digits, and the Gram
endpoints are stored as shortest round-tripping decimals; the conversion
error (`< 10⁻¹⁶`) is covered by the `10⁻⁴` margin and the `10⁻⁹` operator
slack.

The numerical matrix (30 digits) has

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| diagonal `W(σ)` | `0.043135958394838…` |
| prime term at `c = 0.6` | `−0.03648163…` |
| eigenvalues | `0.02270974`, `0.02696128`, `0.04485248`, `0.07802034` |

Every interval entry contains its numerical counterpart (C14), and the
smallest numerical eigenvalue is comfortably positive.  The certificate does
not rely on the numerical eigenvalues; they are a cross-check.

## The centre/error certificate

Let `G0` be the entrywise centre and `t` the maximum row sum of half-widths,
plus a `10⁻⁹` conversion slack:

\[
t=0.02109567190089\ldots
\]

For every symmetric matrix `G` in the interval box,

\[
\|G-G_0\|_2 \le t .
\]

The exact rational matrix `G0 − tI` has positive leading principal minors

```
0.02200721793…,  2.087993118…·10⁻⁴,  1.495841626…·10⁻⁶,  9.975169568…·10⁻⁹,
```

and an exact factorisation

\[
G_0-tI = L\,d\,L^{\mathsf T},
\]

with `L` unit lower triangular and `d` positive.  The JSON carries the exact
rational `L` and `d`; the verifier checks the identity with no tolerance, and
Lean T15 (`ldl_posDef_rat`) turns any such factorisation into positive
definiteness.  Hence `G0 − tI` is positive definite, so

\[
G \succeq G_0-tI \succ 0
\]

for **every** symmetric member of the box.  In particular the true Gram
matrix — which lies in the box by the enclosures — is positive definite.

### The concrete embedding

The rationals are not left only in Python.  `lean/Topic.lean` defines
`shadowL`, `shadowD` and `shadowP` from the certificate, proves

```lean
theorem shadow_factor :
    shadowL * diagonal shadowD * shadowLᴴ = shadowP
```

by kernel arithmetic (`ext`, `fin_cases`, `norm_num`), and closes

```lean
theorem shadowP_posDef : shadowP.PosDef
```

with `ldl_posDef_rat`.  The proof uses no `native_decide`: `#print axioms`
reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.  Test C23 checks
that the embedded rational constants match the certificate.

### The interval argument

The recorded box is `G0 ± E`, where `G0` is the centre and `E` the matrix of
half-widths.  Two lemmas close the gap from the box to positivity.

**Row-sum bound (T18).**  For a symmetric `D` and every `x`,

\[
\left|\sum_{i,j} D_{ij}x_ix_j\right| \le t\sum_i x_i^2
\qquad\text{whenever}\qquad
\sum_j |D_{ij}| \le t \ \text{ for every } i.
\]

The proof is the AM–GM inequality `2|x_i||x_j| ≤ x_i² + x_j²`: summing it over
the symmetric matrix gives the two row sums, each at most `t`.

**Box inequality (T19).**  If `A` is Hermitian and

\[
|A_{ij}-(P+tI)_{ij}| \le E_{ij},
\qquad
\sum_j E_{ij} \le t,
\]

then `xᵀPx ≤ xᵀAx` for every `x` — that is, `A ⪰ G₀ − tI`.  Write
`A = P + tI + D`; the row-sum bound gives `xᵀDx ≥ -t‖x‖²`, hence
`xᵀAx ≥ xᵀPx`.

**Positivity (T20).**  With `P` positive definite in addition, `A` is
positive definite.

All three are Lean theorems in [`../lean/Topic.lean`](../lean/Topic.lean).

### The recorded numerical instance

The C24 checks are formalised as well.  `shadowA` is the recorded 30-digit
numerical matrix as exact rationals, `shadowE` the half-widths and `shadowT`
the exact operator slack.  `shadowA_herm`, `shadowA_mem_box` and
`shadowE_row` prove symmetry, entrywise containment and the row-sum bound;
`shadowA_posDef` applies `posDef_of_box_rat` to conclude that the recorded
numerical matrix is positive definite.  These are kernel arithmetic
(`norm_num`), with no `native_decide`.

The Python certificate checks the same hypotheses on the recorded box (C24):
the numerical matrix is inside `G0 ± E`, the centre is `P + tI`, and the row
sums of `E` are at most `t`.  The analytic input that the *true* matrix lies in
the box is the certified density/prime computation; that is the remaining
non-Lean step.

### The rational alternative

The project already contains a rational shadow of `G`: `L4Shadow` in
[`../../prime-spectrum-tensor/lean/Topic.lean`](../../prime-spectrum-tensor/lean/Topic.lean)
with `L4Shadow_posSemidef` (T12 of that dossier), a rounded Cholesky factor
whose product is within `4·10⁻¹³` of `G`.  It is a genuine rational matrix,
and its PSD proof is Lean.  But it is not by itself a certificate for `G`:
the proximity is a numerical measurement, not a rigorous entrywise bound, and
the shadow is only positive *semidefinite*, so it has no positive margin to
absorb a perturbation.  It certifies the shadow, not its neighbour.

The rational object that does close the implication is the box.  The triple
`(P, E, t)` is rational: `P = G0 − tI` is rational and positive definite
(T15–T17), `E` and `t` are rational, and T19 proves

\[
G \in G_0 \pm E \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G \succeq P .
\]

The transcendental matrix has disappeared from the Lean statement.  It
occurs only in the hypothesis `G ∈ G0 ± E`, which is itself a statement about
rational bounds on the entries of `G`.

So the rational alternative exists, but it is not a rational *matrix equal to*
`G`; it is the rational *box and lower bound*.  What is not yet rational-Lean
is the certificate for `G ∈ box`: the interval enclosure of `Ω` and of the
density integral.  The project has the analytic ingredients — `PsiBar`,
`archimedean_deriv_bound`, `digamma_norm_le_quarter` — and the
`partial-sum-plus-tail` library has rational `π` and `log` enclosures, but no
assembled Lean enclosure of `Ω` or of the density integral.  That assembly is
new formalisation, not a missing mathematical input.

multi-thousand-line formalisation.

The fourth minor is `≈ 10⁻⁸`.  This is a near-boundary certificate: the
smallest eigenvalue of the numerical matrix is `2.271·10⁻²`, and the interval
widths consume most of the margin.  That is why the check must be the
centre/error form rather than the direct interval determinant, which
straddles zero at the fourth minor.

## What the certificate proves

- **Proved by computation and Lean:** the true 4×4 Gaussian Weil Gram matrix
  lies in the recorded interval box (computation), and every Hermitian matrix
  in that box satisfies `A ⪰ G₀ − tI` and is positive definite (T18–T20).
  Hence the finite Gram matrix is positive definite.
- **Not proved:** positivity of the infinite Weil cone.  A finite subspace
  can be positive while an off-line zero drives a different test function
  negative.  This is a rung, not a bridge.
- **Lean-checked:** the concrete `L`, `d` and `P` are embedded in
  `lean/Topic.lean`, the factorisation `L d Lᵀ = P` is kernel-checked, and
  `P.PosDef` follows from T15.  The interval step is T18–T20: any Hermitian
  matrix in the box satisfies `A ⪰ G₀ − tI` and is positive definite.  The
  recorded numerical instance is T21–T24.  The Rust-generated
  `lean/Certificate.lean` repeats the whole check for the tighter box
  (T91–T100), including a distinct numerical representative `certA`.  The
  remaining computational step is the enclosure that the true matrix
  satisfies the box hypotheses (Rust exact-rational intervals plus MPFR
  directed rounding), not a Lean proof.
- **Residual audit item:** the `10⁻⁶` jet-tail allowance is conservative and
  dominated by the `10⁻⁴` margin; a term-by-term derivative-tail proof would
  remove it.  It does not affect the positive margin at the recorded width.

## The Rust certificate (T91–T100)

The Rust assembly re-derives the box with exact rational endpoints (MPFR
evaluation at 256 bits, directed rounding, then exact conversion).  It uses
`T = 150`, `H = 10⁻³`, 150000 panels and a `10⁻⁴` margin.  The density
intervals are an order of magnitude tighter than the Python ones:

| `c` | Rust density enclosure | half-width |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | `[0.011679028007451, 0.011721753682151]` | `2.1·10⁻⁵` |
| 1/5 | `[−0.048103497659846, −0.048060771984609]` | `2.1·10⁻⁵` |
| 2/5 | `[−0.024056561637094, −0.024013835961319]` | `2.1·10⁻⁵` |
| 3/5 | `[−0.016861793953728, −0.016819068277416]` | `2.1·10⁻⁵` |

Pole and prime intervals have width `10⁻¹⁵` (the emission grid); the
`Ω` samples have width `10⁻⁹` for `t > 0` and `1.24·10⁻⁵` at `t = 0`, where
the rational `γ` interval dominates.

The resulting operator slack is

\[
t = 0.000485452351017,
\]

with leading principal minors of `G₀ − tI`

```
0.042650506120968,  1.547170032005666·10⁻³,
5.600856710003812·10⁻⁵,  2.024113445192226·10⁻⁶.
```

`lean/Certificate.lean` embeds the centre, half-widths, slack, the exact
rational `LDLᵀ` factors and a distinct 12-digit matrix `certA`.  It proves
`certP_posDef` from `certD_pos` and `cert_factor`, and then
`certA_posDef` via the box theorem `posDef_of_box_rat` (T91–T100).  The file
contains no placeholder, extra axiom or floating-point literal.

The remaining trust boundary is explicit: the passage from the Lean
pointwise `Ω` enclosure (T79) and the T90 derivative bound to the rational
density intervals is a Rust computation.  It is checked independently by
`tests/test_rust_certificate.py` (15 tests), which recomputes the density,
pole, prime and `Ω` values with mpmath and verifies the exact `LDLᵀ` identity
with `Fraction` arithmetic.  Closing that boundary inside Lean is the
quadrature/prime-sum formalisation described in
[`../HANDOFF-RUST.md`](../HANDOFF-RUST.md) §13.

## Intersection-form reading

The Gram matrix is the matrix of the finite intersection form on the span of
the four Gaussians, in the sense of the Weil form `W`.  Positive definiteness
is the finite positivity of that form.  This is a *different* positivity from
the function-field Hodge index of the main dossier: the Hodge index is a
signature statement on `NS(E × E)`, while the shadow is the positivity of
`W` on a four-dimensional test subspace.  Both are finite positivity
certificates; neither is the infinite statement.

What the shadow adds is that the number-field side now has a checked finite
positivity with an enclosed archimedean integral, not just a numerical
eigenvalue.  What it does not add is a correspondence or a cone theorem.
