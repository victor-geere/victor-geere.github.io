The Final Letter · topics · status

An intersection form for ζ — the function-field template and the arithmetic obstruction

The finite Hodge-index core is proved; the correspondence is not.

Status: exploratory. No intersection form for ζ is constructed. The finite Hodge-index core is Lean-proved (T1–T100, no sorry, axiom or admit); the function-field template, the Arakelov segment, the Hecke shadow and the scaling correspondence and the angular Bochner framework are source claims S1–S19; the certified 4×4 shadow is recorded with an enclosed Ω integral (C11–C24); the obstruction is this dossier's interpretation. The Python suite has 60 passing tests, including the Rust-generated certificate (C25–C39), the Frobenius-alternative checks (C40–C42), the angular Bochner checks (C43–C45), the Riemann–Roch-square checks (C46–C48) and the dimension-inequality check (C49).

Objective. The brief allowed a Lee–Yang measure or an intersection form for ζ. This page takes the intersection-form arm and asks what can actually be built: the finite positivity, the function-field template, and the reason the template does not transplant.

Verdict. The positivity can be built; the object it should act on cannot. In the function-field proof the Hodge index is the positivity. For ζ the arithmetic analogue of the Hodge index is a theorem (Faltings–Hriljac plus Northcott). What has no number-field analogue is the Frobenius correspondence. The arithmetic-positivity wall therefore becomes a correspondence wall on this route.

What this dossier does

  1. Builds and proves the finite core: the rank-three intersection form on E × E, the ample class, the Hodge index inequality and its exact sum-of-squares defect.
  2. Records the template: Weil's theorem, the intersection numbers, and what a ζ analogue would demand.
  3. Records the obstruction: the Arakelov intersection form exists and the height pairing is positive definite, but there is no Frobenius correspondence over Q.
  4. Records why the Lee–Yang arm was not taken: the polygon's certified zeros have disjoint real parts, and a measure built from ξ′/ξ would be circular.
  5. Certifies the 4×4 Gaussian Weil Gram matrix with an enclosed Ω integral and an exact centre/error check: a finite shadow, not a proof of the infinite cone.
  6. Proposes an alternative to the missing Frobenius correspondence: a Hecke shadow for automorphic L-functions and a scaling correspondence on the adele class space for ζ, with the residual polarization isolated.

The finite model

On the rank-three lattice spanned by H = {pt} × E, V = E × {pt} and the diagonal Δ of E × E, with

H² = V² = Δ² = 0,   H·V = H·Δ = V·Δ = 1,

the Gram matrix is

0 1 1
1 0 1
1 1 0

The ample class A = H + V + Δ has A² = 6, and the Hodge index inequality is

6 D² ≤ (A·D)²,   defect = 2((a−b)² + (b−c)² + (c−a)²),

with equality only when D is a multiple of A. On A⊥ the form is negative definite. The model is indefinite: (H+V)² = 2 and (H−V)² = −2. The positivity lives on the quotient by the ample direction, as in the Hodge index theorem.

The certified finite shadow

The number-field candidate is the Weil form W. On four Gaussians φa(u)=exp(−(u−a)²/(2σ²)) with σ=0.05 and centres −0.3, −0.1, 0.1, 0.3, the Gram matrix Gab=(W(hab)+W(hba))/2 has entries built from a closed-form pole, a density integral against Ω(t)=Re ψ(1/4+it/2)−log π, and a finite prime sum.

python/certify_shadow.py wires the certified Ω/density layer of prime-spectrum-tensor into this dossier and records python/weil_certificate.json. The density integral is enclosed on Taylor panels with an interval Ω jet; a 10−4 margin covers the provisional jet remainder. The 4×4 interval matrix is then certified by the centre/error check: t is the maximum row sum of half-widths, and the rational matrix G₀−tI has positive leading minors and an exact L d LT factorisation with d > 0. The recorded L and d are checked with no tolerance, and the same rationals are embedded in lean/Topic.lean: T15 turns the factorisation into positive definiteness, T16 checks the embedded identity and T17 closes the concrete instance, with no native_decide. The fourth minor is about 9.98·10−9, so the certificate is near-boundary, not vacuous.

The interval step is also Lean. T18 is the row-sum bound |xTDx| ≤ t‖x‖²; T19 proves A ⪰ G0 − tI for any Hermitian matrix in the box; T20 adds positivity of P. The Python certificate checks the hypotheses on the recorded box (C24). The remaining analytic input is the enclosure that the true matrix is in the box.

Rational alternative. The project's L4Shadow (T12 of prime-spectrum-tensor) is a rational PSD shadow of G, but its 4·10⁻¹³ proximity is numerical, not a rigorous bound, and a PSD matrix has no positive margin to absorb a perturbation. The rational object used here is the box (P, E, t): P is rational and positive definite, and T19 proves G ⪰ P from rational entrywise bounds. The transcendental matrix is absent from the Lean statement; only the enclosure G ∈ G0 ± E remains computational.

The enclosure layer. That enclosure is the only remaining formal obligation: sharp rational bounds for γ, π and log 2; a pointwise enclosure of Ω (the first leaf, ψ(1/4), is T25); an interval quadrature of the density integral; a finite enclosure of the prime sum (2328 prime powers); and the rational assembly. The Rh45 bundle has the analytic skeleton (PsiBar, derivative and Simpson bounds), but not the assembled layer.

What it proves is finite: the four-dimensional Gram matrix is positive definite. What it does not prove is the infinite Weil cone. The Ω enclosure is mpmath.iv plus explicit panel bounds, not a Lean proof; the finite algebra of a rational shadow is T12–T19. Details: note 4.

The alternative to Frobenius

A single algebraic Frobenius over Q cannot exist. A fixed correspondence has a fixed trace, while the Frobenius traces ap vary; and a finite determinant has only O(T) zeros up to height T, while ξ has T log T. Two replacements survive:

  • the Hecke correspondence Tp ⊂ X0(N) × X0(N), an algebraic cycle over Q with Tp = Fp + p Fp−1 on H¹. The Ramanujan–Petersson bound is RH for the associated L-function, but the eigenvalue for ζ itself is 1, so it sees the pole, not the zeros;
  • the scaling correspondence S = {(λx, x)} on the adele class space AQ×/Q×, whose trace formula is the explicit formula. Its periodic orbits are the prime powers and its absorption spectrum is the zeros.

The second reaches ζ but leaves the polarization open: the missing Hodge-index positivity becomes the Weil positivity, which is equivalent to RH. The wall moves from a missing object to a missing positivity. Details: note 5; finite checks: python/frobenius_alternative.py and tests/test_frobenius_alternative.py.

The angular Bochner attack

The accompanying angular Bochner framework gives finite-level positive matrices and a damping filtration, and its undamped projective limit is the Weil functional. Note 6 proves the equivalence: the alternative's polarization is the framework's uniform positivity. The finite-level matrices are positive unconditionally, but the passage to the uniform statement is exactly the classical mean-value closure and cannot be made from inside it. The no-closure corollary says that finite-level Bochner positivity, projective refinement and classical mean-value estimates do not imply the polarization; a proof needs a new uniformity or a new positivity outside that closure.

The finite sector model in python/angular_bochner.py has smallest eigenvalues decaying roughly like c/m, so the finite-level positivity gives no uniform gap. That is an illustration, not a proof. See note 6 for the theorem and the source claims S10–S12.

The Riemann–Roch square

The 2018 Riemann–Roch strategy reduces RH to the positivity of s(f,f) = D(f)•D(f), where D(f) = ∫ f(λ)Ψλ d*λ is a divisor on the square of the Scaling Site. If s(f,f) > 0, a Riemann–Roch theorem on the square would make D(f) or −D(f) effective, contradicting degree/codegree zero.

Note 7 proves the local HRR on the periodic-orbit tori: for a complex torus A, χ(A,O(D)) = D²/2, and D² > 0 makes D or −D effective. The global step is a longitudinal HRR on the square of the complex lift. The note isolates the three technical obstructions: the Todd class of the foliation, the log Λ divergence, and the Witt lift of the correspondences. This is the precise missing leg.

Three attacks and the wall

Note 8 consolidates the three routes. The Riemann–Roch square proves the local HRR but not the global longitudinal HRR. The arithmetic Hodge index of Yuan–Zhang does not apply to Spec Z; a proper polarized realization would already prove RH, so none is known. The Sonin gap c|ĝ(0)|² is the central value, not a pole; it is off-diagonal, cannot be absorbed by de Branges or Riemann–Roch moves, and c = 0 is false. All three reduce to one absolute/off-diagonal positivity, equivalent to RH.

The known legs do not force the fourth. A proof of the fourth would be a proof of RH; this is the precise answer to the “structure demands the legs” hypothesis.

The function-field template

For a curve C/Fq of genus g, with P1(T) = Π(1 − αiT),

Z(C,T) = P₁(T) / ((1−T)(1−qT)),   RH: |αᵢ| = √q.

The Castelnuovo–Weil argument uses the Frobenius graph's intersection numbers Γ·H = 1, Γ·V = q, Γ·Δ = q + 1 − a, the Hodge index, and the functional equation pairing α with q/α. For an elliptic curve the same positivity is the positive-definite Rosati involution with ππ̄ = q. Standard references: Weil (1948), Deligne (1974).

The arithmetic obstruction

Arakelov's intersection theory gives a real-valued pairing on an arithmetic surface. Faltings–Hriljac identifies it on degree-zero divisors with the negative Néron–Tate height, which is positive definite. The positivity is therefore available.

What is not available is a correspondence. Over Fq, Frobenius is a single morphism whose trace is the zeta function. Over Q, Frobenius at p is a conjugacy class; the Euler product is a product of local factors; the functional equation is a symmetry, not a correspondence; and the archimedean place contributes a Green's function, not a finite matrix.

So: an intersection form without a correspondence cannot see the zeros. This refines wall W1: the missing input is still an arithmetic positivity/multiplicativity input, but here the missing conjunct is the arithmetic object, not the positivity.

Why not the Lee–Yang arm

A positive measure whose Stieltjes transform is ξ′/ξ would be defined from the zeros, so its positivity is RH. Independently, the programme's N = 2 polygon has certified zeros with real parts around 0.57802 and 0.80014, in disjoint intervals: no single vertical line contains both. F.4's confinement is impossible for that object (C46/C47, B26/B28). The bridge survey records the closure in §6.

  • The correspondence. A Frobenius for Spec Z: a correspondence whose Lefschetz trace is ξ, with a polarization. This is the whole gap.
  • The infinite positivity. Finite Weil Gram matrices are numerically positive definite; the infinite positivity is RH.
  • The archimedean place. A regularised intersection pairing at infinity with a finite-dimensional quotient is not constructed.
  • The general Hodge index. Only the rank-three E × E instance is formalised.
  • The finite shadow with a certified Ω. The 4×4 Gaussian Gram matrix is numerically positive definite but its archimedean integral is not yet enclosed.

What not to claim

  • No intersection form for ζ is built here.
  • The function-field argument does not transplant; the correspondence is missing.
  • The positivity is not the obstruction on this route; the correspondence is.
  • The Hasse checks verify the function-field conclusion on examples; they are not a proof about ζ.
  • F.4 for the polygon is not reopened; the off-critical zeros are certified.
