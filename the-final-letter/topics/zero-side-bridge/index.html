The zero-side bridge

From the finite Weil–Gram certificates to the sum over the non-trivial zeros

Status. The contour half is Lean-checked (GaussianZeroSide.lean, in the absolute-polarized topic); the equality with the arithmetic side W is numerical to 10−17 (C1) and open as a theorem. Three analytic obligations remain. This folder is the programme home: it owns the numerical harness, the acceptance criteria, and the route from the finite certificates to Σρ ĝc(γρ). Full dossier in README.md.

Abstract

The finite Gaussian Weil–Gram ladder certifies that the arithmetic side of the Weil explicit formula is positive definite on bases of up to seven points. The other side of that formula expresses the same numbers as a sum over the non-trivial zeros: for the Gaussian bump hc(v) = √π σ exp(−(v−c)²/(4σ²)) whose transform is ĝc(t) = 2πσ² exp(−σ²t²) exp(−ict),

Σρ ĝc((ρ − 1/2)/i)  =  (W(c) + W(−c))/2  =  gaussianGramEntry 0 c σ,

the last equality following from the symmetry of the zero set. This programme is the step from that identity to a theorem. The contour half is already Lean-checked — the zero-side test function, its values at the pole and at 1/2 + it, and the rectangle contour identity for a finite zero set — and the numerical agreement of the two sides is at 10−18 for the five separations of the 5 × 5 basis (C1). What is missing is the analytic evaluation: shifting the contour from Re s > 1 into the critical strip, term-by-term integration of the von Mangoldt series against Hc, a polynomial growth bound for Z'/Z in the strip to kill the rectangle tails, and a formal limit of the finite zero sums. Nothing here is a proof of the Riemann hypothesis: the identity is an identity of the same numbers, and it holds whether or not the zeros are on the line.

The last two are the spectral explanation of the finite certificates: the zero side is a Gram matrix of real cosine/sine modes, so any finite set of on-line frequencies gives a PSD matrix, and an off-line zero is exactly a term that breaks the rank-one structure. The trivial zeros are not summed: they are carried by the archimedean term, and any formalisation must keep that bookkeeping explicit.

The three open obligations

  1. The prime/archimedean evaluation. On Re s > 1 the library has Z'/Z·H·i = −(arch·H·i + LΛ·H·i). Missing: the shift into the strip (zeros and the pole at s = 1) and the term-by-term integration of the von Mangoldt series against Hc.
  2. The zero-sum limit. zero_sum_tail_bound bounds the rectangle tail by (M/2π)(perimeter' + perimeter); on the vertical sides |Hc| decays like exp(−σ²T²), so a polynomial growth bound for Z'/Z in the strip kills it — standard, not formalised.
  3. The zero set. The infinite sum needs multiplicities; the natural formal object is a limit of the rectangle divisor sums, avoiding an explicit enumeration.

Honesty note. This folder does not prove the bridge, Weil positivity, or RH. The contour half is Lean-checked; the equality with W is numerical to 10−17; the three obligations above are open. The target identity holds regardless of where the zeros are, so closing it does not by itself give positivity — see SUB-CONE.md for where that obstruction sits.
