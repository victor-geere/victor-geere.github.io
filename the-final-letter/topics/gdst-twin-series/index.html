GDST twin series

A reconstructed research programme: finite formal core, exact computation, source claims

Status. Finite formal core and exact computation layer reconstructed. The archived programme's infinite analytic claims and its Riemann-hypothesis characterisation are preserved as source claims, not promoted to theorems.

Source: gdst-twin-series.html · LaTeX

What the source is

The archived paper is a research programme, not a proof paper. It defines the Greedy Dirichlet Sub-Sum Transform (GDST) twin series — a selected series E(θ,s) and a rejected series Ω(θ,s) — from the greedy harmonic digits δ_n(θ), and sets out four phases of open problems around them.

The paper is explicit that it "does not claim a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis". Its central "Theorem 1.1 (RH Equivalence)" is quoted from a companion foundational paper and is described as an equivalence to be investigated; it is not proved in the archived programme and it is not proved here. The related foundational paper is inventoried separately as item 9.

The source folder also contains the foundational-results paper, a Riemann–Siegel hybrid note, an animation, and the PDF. Only the twin-series programme paper is reconstructed in this folder. The claims of the accompanying papers are not audited or adopted here.

Reconstructed objects

Let θ ∈ [0, π], let x = θ/π, and let the greedy harmonic fractions be α_n = π/(n+2). The greedy digits are defined by the residual recursion

ρ_0 = x,     δ_n(θ) = 1 iff ρ_n ≥ 1/(n+2),     ρ_{n+1} = ρ_n − δ_n(θ)/(n+2).

Equivalently, in angle form, δ_n(θ) = 1 iff the residual angle is at least α_n. The reconstruction works with the following finite prefixes of N terms (n = 0, …, N−1):

ObjectDefinition
Dirichlet weightterm n s = (n+2)^(-s)
Selected prefixE_N(θ,s) = Σ_{n<N, δ_n(θ)=1} (n+2)^(-s)
Rejected prefixΩ_N(θ,s) = Σ_{n<N, δ_n(θ)=0} (n+2)^(-s)
Full prefixH_N(s) = Σ_{n<N} (n+2)^(-s)
Infinite twinsE(θ,s), Ω(θ,s) — the N → ∞ limits, source claims only

The finite complement identity E_N(θ,s) + Ω_N(θ,s) = H_N(s) is the exact combinatorial core. It holds for every binary selection word, not only for greedy digits; the greedy rule enters through the Python computation of δ_n(θ). The source's infinite identity E + Ω = ζ(s) − 1 requires convergence on Re s > 0 and is not formalised.

Open items and known gaps

  1. Infinite convergence and ζ. The step from H_N to ζ(s) − 1, and from the finite twins to E and Ω, is not formalised. It needs the source's summation-by-parts argument and a treatment of the limit.
  2. RH equivalence. The source's Theorem 1.1 is an archived claim, not a theorem. No Lean declaration or pytest test asserts it. In particular this folder does not claim the Riemann hypothesis, GRH, twin primes, Chowla, Sarnak, or Elliott.
  3. Greedy-specific formal content. T4 holds for every binary word. The genuine greedy recursion (residual bounds, threshold structure) is in the related item 9 folder; this folder deliberately keeps the twin-series complement as its formal core.
  4. Balance-angle argument. The source's existence proof invokes an intermediate-value property for a limit of step functions; the source itself notes that regulated functions need not be Darboux. The gap is recorded as S6/O4, not repaired here.
  5. Companion papers. The results paper and the Riemann–Siegel hybrid are not audited. Their stronger analytic and numerical claims are outside the scope of this reconstruction.
