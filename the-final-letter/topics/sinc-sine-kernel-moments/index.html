Sinc, sine kernel, moments

A reconstructed research programme: finite formal core, exact computation, archived claims kept separate

Status. The finite sinc/sine-kernel layer is formalised in Lean and tested in Python. The archived zeta and random-matrix claims are recorded as Source claim or Open / conjecture, never as theorems. The full dossier is in README.md.

Provenance and what the source is

The primary source is archive/essays/sinc-zeta.html, with the companion essays kosters-zeta-moments-sinc.html and shifted-moments-l-theta.html. They are short AI-generated expository surveys (the first is attributed to Grok 3), not a new proof or a self-contained development. Inventory entry Scraps item 42.

The reconstruction therefore extracts the finite kernel and moment bookkeeping that the source actually defines, and leaves the zeta asymptotics outside the formal core.

Normalisation inconsistency. The source defines sinc(x) = sin(πx)/(πx) but quotes Kösters' limit with the factor sinc(π(μ−ν)). Under its own definition that would be sin(π²(μ−ν))/(π²(μ−ν)); the intended kernel is sin(π(μ−ν))/(π(μ−ν)). This reconstruction formalises the normalised kernel sincN and records the source's displayed expression as a source claim with an ambiguity note.

Python layer

python/programme.py implements exact sympy mathematics: sinc, sine_kernel, montgomery_F, kosters_kernel, shifted_moment_matrix, moment_ledger, sinc_taylor_coeff, sinc_taylor_polynomial, and sinc_series. Numerical checks use sympy's N(..., 40) with stated tolerances; mpmath is not required. The module has no import side effects.

Open items and known gaps

  1. Montgomery pair correlation. The source states the conjecture but gives no precise counting statement. A formal version needs the zero-counting function and a limit over test functions.
  2. Kösters' theorem. The second- and fourth-moment asymptotics are cited, not proved; they need the analytic theory of ζ and contour integrals.
  3. GUE / random-matrix statistics. The finite sine kernel is formalised; the determinantal point process and its spacing statistics are not.
  4. Infinite moment matrices. C7 proves finite Hermitian bookkeeping only; positive semidefiniteness of an infinite kernel is open here.
  5. Source normalisation. The archive's sinc(π(μ−ν)) conflicts with its own definition of sinc; the reconstruction uses the normalised kernel and records the discrepancy.
  6. No zeta content in Lean. The Lean file deliberately says nothing about ζ, its zeros, the Riemann hypothesis, or primes.
