Frozen truths

A reconstructed research programme: elementary formal core, exact computation, archived source claims

Status. The elementary last-chord layer is reconstructed and formally proved. The ninety numbered entries of the archived source are preserved; five appear as the finite theorems T1–T5, and the rest are recorded as source claims or open items. The Riemann hypothesis is not proved here. The full dossier is README.md.

Provenance

truths.md is a 509-line sidecar to the geometric-dynamical programme. It collects ninety numbered statements (“frozen truths”) that the author regards as independent of later choices of path-closure, measure, or “balanced/unrestricted” vocabulary. Its opening paragraph says explicitly that it is not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and that anything depending on a later definition is restated as an inequality or omitted.

The file also lists three withdrawn falsehoods: a wrong \(L^1\) integral, a wrong local diagonal c₁ = ½ρ², and a false lower bound on |η| at an off-line zero. They are recorded below as withdrawn, not as theorems. A bundle copy of the source is at geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions/truths.md.

Withdrawn falsehoods

These are the source's own corrections and are not ledger claims:

  • ∫(1−Rφ)(−φ) du = −½ is false; the integral is −1 + R∫φ².
  • The local diagonal of Section 6 is not c₁ = ½ρ².
  • At an off-line zero, |η_N(N²)(ρ)| ≥ c N1/2−ε on the tightness range is false; Corollary 4.3.1 gives the opposite upper bound for σ > ¼ + ε/2.

Lean layer

lean/Topic.lean imports Mathlib, opens the FrozenTruths namespace, and proves T1–T7. It defines lastChord N s = ((1−N)/2) * N^(−2s) and lastChordSquare N = (N−1)²/(4N²). The proofs use only elementary order, field, Real.rpow, limit, Real.log/Real.exp, and Real.cosh facts; there is no analytic estimate and no sorry/axiom/admit.

What is not formalised

The formal core deliberately stops at the algebraic last-chord layer. In particular it does not contain:

  • the uniform Euler–Maclaurin expansion of 3.2 or the floor of 4.4;
  • the two-sided tracking 11.3 or the product-at-twins 11.4;
  • the construction of Riemann's Φ, the cosine representation 12.1, or any statement about the zeros of its transform;
  • the mean-value, Rouché, or Jensen/Li arguments;
  • the Riemann hypothesis, twin primes, Chowla, Sarnak, or Elliott.

These are source claims or open items above, not theorems of this reconstruction.
