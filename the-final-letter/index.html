The Final Letter

Two walls, one kind of missing input: from the Riemann hypothesis to the parity barrier

What this is. This page is the canonical reading artefact; the Markdown files are the source text. A unified research programme built from summary/ (the geometric DQPT/spectral-measure programme for RH), summary-rh/ (the greedy harmonic sine/twin-prime parity programme), and Objective III (the certified partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator). It does not prove RH or the twin-prime conjecture. It sets out the conversion, the Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness, the finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem, the five-bridge refutation, the certified computation layer, and a Lean spine, at five different levels of evidence: written proof, Lean, certified computation, argument about this programme's own routes, and Lean for the finite layer only. The final note argues that the failure is structural.

Description

What this is

the-final-letter/ is the synthesis of two research corpora and one certified-computation layer:

  1. summary/ — the geometric DQPT/spectral-measure programme for the Riemann hypothesis, based on the canonical paper publication.html.
  2. summary-rh/ — the greedy-harmonic-sine/twin-prime parity programme in ~/workspace/rh, based on the 13-section ledger and 15 published papers.
  3. ~/workspace/rh/research/twin-prime-parity/012/ — the partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator (Euler–Maclaurin + exact-rational certificates), reconstructed in topics/partial-sum-plus-tail.

The two corpora and the computation layer are not merged by copying their files. They are rewritten into a single argument with a shared vocabulary, a shared status ledger, a shared wall/bridge analysis, and a shared Lean spine.

The three objectives

  1. Riemann objective — geometric dynamical phase transitions. Realise the non-trivial zeros of ζ as the support of a spectral measure built from finite Euler-sum polygons, and test whether the balanced and unrestricted supports agree. The dossier is topics/geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions.
  2. Parity objective — greedy harmonic sine / twin-prime parity. Determine whether the greedy harmonic sine — a deterministic, sign-blind object — can solve the parity problem for the twin-prime conjecture by producing a positive-density correlation with Ω. The dossier is topics/greedy-harmonic-sine-parity.
  3. Partial-sum-plus-tail objective — certified evaluation. Build a certified evaluator for ζ(s), ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ): Euler–Maclaurin splits the function into a finite partial sum, the Bernoulli corrections and an explicit tail; exact rational interval arithmetic encloses every factor; a straight-line certificate is replayed by an independent checker and kernel-checked in Lean; the transcendental enclosures for π, exp, log, sin and cos are Lean theorems. The dossier is topics/partial-sum-plus-tail. See the detailed section The third objective.

The first two objectives are the two walls; the third is the positive computation layer they need. The three are independent as mathematical claims, but they share the same discipline: state the source claim, separate the proved finite core, and keep the open input explicit.

The first two questions

The Riemann question. Can the non-trivial zeros of ζ be realised as the support of a spectral measure built from finite Euler-sum polygons, and can the equality of the balanced and unrestricted supports be proved?

The parity question. Can the greedy harmonic sine — a deterministic, sign-blind object — solve the parity problem for the twin prime conjecture by producing a positive-density correlation with Ω?

The first two answers

Riemann. The programme proves the conversion: RH is equivalent to the equality of the supports of the balanced and unrestricted measure families. It proves that the additive toolbox cannot prove RH, by running the same construction on the Davenport–Heilbronn function and correctly reporting a false RH there. The remaining gap is the absence of a right-half atom of the unrestricted measure, equivalently the joint non-vanishing of K = ¼ Re ξ and S = ¼ Im ξ, equivalently Weil positivity for the repaired current.

Parity. The programme proves the finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem: two functions agreeing on a prefix can have different finite correlation sums, so a finite prefix does not fix the correlation. It proves that the greedy support is Sylvester-sparse, that the shifted product is not multiplicative, and that the five proposed bridges (support parity, budget parity, ζ-machinery, positive-density support, arithmetic alphabet) fail. The remaining gap is a sign-blind rule with positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω, made precise as OP-19.

The common shape

Both programmes follow the same four-step shape:

  1. Convert the wall into an equality or a correlation statement.
  2. Prove a sharpness/litmus: the conversion is not vacuous, and the local toolbox cannot force the conclusion. The Davenport–Heilbronn twist is the RH litmus; the finite-prefix theorem and the Sylvester-sparsity theorem are the parity litmus.
  3. Locate the missing input as a global arithmetic positivity, not an estimate.
  4. Formalise the part that is formalisable — the finite-prefix core, the definitions of the walls, and the elementary facts — and leave the walls as definitions, not axioms.

The final letter is the statement of that common shape, and the argument that the shape is a wall rather than a door. The third objective follows the same discipline: it proves the rational cell, the transcendental enclosures, and the Euler–Maclaurin identity and tail bound, and leaves only the replay wiring explicit as future work.

What the programme is not

It is not a proof of RH. It is not a proof of the twin prime conjecture. It is not a proof of Chowla, Sarnak, or Elliott. It is not a claim that the two walls are equivalent; they are structurally analogous, not logically equivalent. It is not a claim that no future method can close them; it is a claim that the methods surveyed here cannot.

The third objective

The two walls are the programme’s negative results. The third objective is positive: a certified partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator for ζ, ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ).

It decomposes the function by Euler–Maclaurin into a finite partial sum, the Bernoulli corrections, and an explicit tail R_K with a rational bound. Every operation is recorded in a straight-line certificate; the rational cell is replayed by an independent Python checker and kernel-checked in Lean (Expr.wf, Expr.wf_sound), and the transcendental enclosures (π, log, exp, sin, cos) are kernel-checked in the Lean library Transcend.lean. The concrete ζ(2) replay’s atom boxes are discharged in ReplayAtoms.lean (cert_mem_closed).

Abstract

This programme unifies two research corpora that were pursued separately: the geometric dynamical-phase-transition (DQPT) programme for the Riemann hypothesis, and the greedy-harmonic-sine programme for the twin-prime parity problem. The first converts the Riemann hypothesis into an equality of supports of two families of spectral measures, proves that its own additive toolbox cannot prove the hypothesis by transferring the construction to the Davenport–Heilbronn function, and identifies the missing input as arithmetic positivity. The second proves a finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem for the two-point Liouville correlation, shows that the greedy support is Sylvester-sparse, and disposes of all five proposed bridges from the greedy sine to parity. It identifies the missing input as a sign-blind rule with positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω.

The unified diagnosis is that neither wall is an estimate wall. Both are global-input walls: the local, additive, finite-prefix, density, or sign-blind data available to the two programmes cannot produce the required conclusion. The Riemann wall needs a positivity/multiplicativity input that fails for the Davenport–Heilbronn twist; the parity wall needs a positive-density multiplicative coupling that fails for the greedy support. The two missing objects are not identical, but they are structurally the same kind of object: an arithmetic positivity that the local machinery cannot manufacture.

The programme’s achievements are a conversion theorem, a sharpness theorem, a finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem, a five-bridge refutation, a unified two-wall map, a Lean formalisation of the finite-prefix core and the two wall statements, and a certified computation layer for ζ, ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ). The programme does not prove the Riemann hypothesis, the twin-prime conjecture, Chowla for k ≥ 2, Sarnak’s conjecture, or Elliott’s conjecture.

A third, positive objective supplies the computation layer the two walls need: a certified partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator. Euler–Maclaurin splits the function into a finite sum, the Bernoulli corrections, and an explicit tail; exact rational interval arithmetic encloses every factor; a straight-line certificate records every rational operation, is replayed by an independent checker, and is kernel-checked in Lean. The eta cos/sin split is proved in the same core, and the transcendental enclosures (π, log, exp, sin, cos) are kernel-checked in Transcend.lean. The Euler–Maclaurin identity and the explicit tail bound are Lean theorems for K ≥ 1 (EulerMaclaurin.lean, T36–T43). A closed rectangle enclosure of ζ(s) for every Re s > 1, with no atom hypothesis, and a target-width certificate format are also proved (ReplayGeneral.lean, Width.lean, T44–T51); no theorem about either wall yet uses a certified enclosure as a hypothesis, and a symbolic width bound is still open. The evaluator is integrated as the programme's computation layer, not as a bridge.

The final note argues that the futility is structural rather than accidental. More computation, more estimates, more finite prefixes, and more local structure cannot close these walls; the missing object is global arithmetic positivity. The letter is a map of the wall, not a key to the door.

Keywords: Riemann hypothesis; twin primes; parity barrier; Chowla; Liouville function; finite-prefix indeterminacy; Davenport–Heilbronn; Weil positivity; Euler–Maclaurin; interval arithmetic; certified evaluation; Lean 4; formalisation.

Hypotheses

The programme is organised around eight hypotheses. They are stated so they can be attacked, weakened, or refuted. None is an axiom of the Lean development.

H1 — The RH wall is an arithmetic-positivity wall

The remaining gap in the Riemann programme is not a missing estimate. It is the absence of an arithmetic positivity/multiplicativity input that would exclude a right-half atom of the unrestricted measure, equivalently the joint non-vanishing of K = ¼ Re ξ and S = ¼ Im ξ, equivalently Weil positivity for the repaired current.

Status: supported by the Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness theorem and the failure of the tensor positivity (★).

H2 — The parity wall is a missing positive-density coupling

The remaining gap in the parity programme is not a missing estimate of the greedy word. It is the absence of a sign-blind rule with a positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω. The five proposed bridges fail for structural reasons, not for want of numerical precision.

Status: supported by the barrier ledger P1–P19, B1–B5.

H3 — Finite prefixes do not determine finite correlations

A finite prefix of values does not fix the finite correlation. For two-point Chowla this is the finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem, Lean-certified; it is a statement about finite sums, not a proof that asymptotic reasoning is impossible. For RH, the analogous observation is that finite numerical certificates at bounded height (e.g. T=15, T=33) do not prove the asymptotic.

Status: the finite-scale statement is proved for the Chowla slice; the RH remark is methodological, not a formal theorem.

H4 — Any bridge must fail for the Davenport–Heilbronn twist

A result can close the RH gap only if it consumes an Euler product, a positivity, or a self-adjointness that the Davenport–Heilbronn function does not have. This is the litmus of Corollary C.10.

Status: proved as a limitative consequence for the additive toolbox of the paper.

H5 — Any bridge must be pointwise, not density-only

Density statements are blind to a density-zero set of off-line zeros (Bombieri–Hejhal). The measures μ,ν escape this only because the tempering weight charges individual atoms. A Lee–Yang theorem for the polygon would have to be pointwise in the same sense.

Status: supported by the barrier ledger; F.4 remains open.

H6 — The two walls share a common missing object

The missing inputs are not identical, but they are the same kind: a global arithmetic positivity that the local machinery cannot manufacture. The RH side calls it Weil positivity/(M); the parity side calls it a positive-density coupling to Ω.

Status: structural hypothesis; the programme’s central interpretive claim.

H7 — Formalisation clarifies but does not close the walls

Lean can formalise the finite-prefix core, the definitions, and the elementary implications. It cannot formalise the missing global input without assuming it. The formalisation is a map of the provable layer, not a proof of the wall.

Status: demonstrated by the FinalLetter Lean development.

H8 — The futility is structural

The failure is not a lack of effort, compute, or cleverness. It is the structure of the available data: finite prefixes, additive characters, sign-blind rules, and density statements cannot produce the missing global object. More of the same cannot close the wall.

Status: the programme’s final assessment; argued in the final note.

C1 — Riemann hypothesis

Every non-trivial zero of ζ has real part 1/2.

Formally, in the Lean synthesis:

def RiemannHypothesis : Prop :=
  ∀ s ∈ riemannZetaZeros, 0 < s.re → s.re < 1 → s.re = 1/2

Status: OPEN. The programme proves a conversion, not the conjecture. See 05 — Proofs and summary/05.

C2 — Twin-prime conjecture

There are infinitely many primes p such that p+2 is prime.

Formally:

def TwinPrimeConjecture : Prop :=
  ∀ N : ℕ, ∃ p : ℕ, N < p ∧ Nat.Prime p ∧ Nat.Prime (p + 2)

Status: OPEN. The programme proves that the greedy-sine objects cannot reach it; it does not prove the conjecture. See summary-rh/03.

C3 — Chowla for k ≥ 2

For distinct h₁,…,h_k,

Σ_{n≤x} λ(n+h₁)⋯λ(n+h_k) = o(x).

The k=2, h=(0,2) case is the two-point target. The programme proves the finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem, which shows no finite computation can decide it.

Status: OPEN for every k ≥ 2. Log-averaged k=2 and odd k are known; log-averaged even k ≥ 4 is open.

C4 — Sarnak’s Möbius-disjointness

For every deterministic sequence a of zero entropy,

Σ_{n≤x} μ(n)a(n) = o(x).

Status: OPEN in general. Chowla implies Sarnak; the equivalence is only log-averaged.

C5 — Hypothesis (M) / Weil positivity

(M): the limit function has no zeros in σ > 1.

Equivalently, in the tensor programme, positivity of the Weil current W on the cone. Under (M) the Davenport–Heilbronn obstruction lapses, but (M) does not prove RH; it is the missing arithmetic positivity.

Status: OPEN as an input. True for ζ; false for the Davenport–Heilbronn function.

C6 — OP-19 log-indicator barrier

For every log-arc rule A_I = {n : log n mod 2π ∈ I},

C_I(x) = Σ_{n≤x} 1_{A_I}(n) λ(n) = o(x).

Status: OPEN and well-posed. The single arc I=[0,π) is the concrete instance. See summary-rh/03/04-op-19-log-indicator.md.

C7 — F.4 Lee–Yang property for the polygon

There is a ferromagnetic spin system whose finite-volume partition functions are η_N^{(N²)}, or whose zeros are confined to a line for every N.

Status: OPEN. Passes the Davenport–Heilbronn litmus; the computable part is the transfer-operator form. See summary/09.

C8 — de Bruijn–Newman Λ = 0

The de Bruijn–Newman constant satisfies Λ = 0; equivalently RH. Known: 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.22.

Status: OPEN. See summary/12.

C9 — Jensen/Li tower

The Jensen polynomials of the even moments of Riemann’s Φ are hyperbolic at every degree; equivalently the Li coefficients are non-negative; equivalently RH.

Status: OPEN. Fixed degree at large shift is known (Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier); uniformity in degree is the full tower.

C10 — Uniform slow passage

On the tightness window, |ζ′(ρ)| ≤ N^ε for every critical-line zero ρ with |γ| ≤ N^{1/2-ε}.

Status: OPEN/conjectural; random-matrix prediction (Hughes–Keating–O’Connell) says yes. Not used by any theorem.

What is not a conjecture

The finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem, the Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness, the Sylvester-sparsity theorem, the conversion theorem, and the Lean-proved rational/transcendental layer of Objective III are proved. They are not conjectures. The programme’s discipline is to keep the two lists separate.

Proofs

This is the proof ledger of the unified programme. It separates five things that are often conflated:

  1. Proved in the source — a written proof in a paper or ledger.
  2. Machine-checked — a Lean proof in the source repository.
  3. Conversion — a proved equivalence that does not prove either side.
  4. Sharpness/litmus — a proved negative result showing that a class of methods cannot close the wall.
  5. Certified computation — a Lean-checked finite calculation with a replayable certificate, not an asymptotic theorem.

A. The Riemann side

A1. The conversion theorem

Statement. The following are equivalent:

  1. every non-trivial zero of Ξ lies on the critical line;
  2. every unrestricted path-closure is balanced for all sufficiently large N;
  3. every weak-star limit of the unrestricted measures is supported on the critical line;
  4. the balanced and unrestricted limiting measures coincide, with common support the non-trivial zeros.

Status: proved equivalence; it does not prove RH.

Source: summary/05, publication.html Corollary 12.3.

A2. The Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness theorem

Statement. At Spira’s off-line zero ρ_DH = 0.80851718… + 85.69934849… i, the relative test fires and the prefactor test fails, so the DH measures have different supports. The conversion returns the correct verdict where RH is false.

Status: proved. It shows the conversion is sharp.

Source: summary/06.

A3. The limitative consequence

Statement. No theorem deducible from the additive toolbox (Euler-sum pair, Euler–Maclaurin, Jensen, Rouché, minimum principle, Riemann–von Mangoldt, classical floor) can imply RH. Any such theorem would apply verbatim to the DH twist and contradict A2.

Status: proved. This is the formal version of “the additive toolbox cannot prove RH.”

Source: summary/06.

A4. The tensor refutation and repair

Statement. The proposed inequality (★) is false; the witness gives −0.677302…. The correct positive statement is Weil positivity for the repaired current W; its spectral resolution is exactly the zero measure.

Status: proved (Theorem B and Theorem C in tensor.md).

Source: summary/10.

A5. The modular shadow and straightening field

Statement. The modular shadow is exact at positive integer weight and has no transfer to complex zeros. The straightening field’s holonomy is the zero count; under the Rouché condition (E.1), the count is computable from two finite Euler sums.

Status: proved, with (E.1) as an explicit hypothesis.

Source: summary/07, summary/08.

B. The parity side

B1. The factor-complexity law

Statement. For the greedy word, with L ≥ max(span, a_m+1),

p(L) = min(a_m+1, N−L+1) + [L ≤ N−a_m−1].

Status: proved; Lean-certified in Rh41.

Source: summary-rh/01/02.

B2. Budgets, Brown reachability, parity lock

Statement. P = 2^{−p}ζ, N = (1−2^{−p})ζ−1, P+N = ζ−1, P−N = 1−η, P > N; Brown’s criterion; the signed support is parity-locked.

Status: proved; Lean at p=2,4.

Source: summary-rh/01/03.

B3. Balance and the equal split

Statement. P_N + M_N = Re S_N; balance is equivalent to Re S_N = 0; balance is attained for every N ≥ 2; S_3(t) ≠ 0 for all t; S_2(π/log 2) = 0.

Status: proved; Lean-certified.

Source: summary-rh/01/04.

B4. Smoothing does not remove √N

Statement.

|S_N^φ(1/2+it) − ζ(1/2+it)| / √N → |M_φ(s)|,

with M_φ(s)=∫₀¹ φ(u)u^{−s}du.

Status: proved (paper) + verified numerically.

Source: summary-rh/01/05.

B5. The corrected explicit formula

Statement. The Weil explicit formula with the archimedean term; N(T)=θ(T)/π+1+S(T); finite-window certificates T=15, T=33.

Status: proved (paper) + verified; the T=15/33 certificates are unconditional finite-window statements, not RH.

Source: summary-rh/01/06.

B6. Finite-prefix indeterminacy

Statement. For every a ≠ 0 and every h, there is a function g that agrees with λ below a, differs from λ, and reproduces corr h N on every prefix with N+h<a. The slice perturbation at the flipped index is

corrSlice h a a − corr h a = −2λ(a)λ(a+h) − 2λ(a−h)λ(a).

Status: proved; Lean-certified.

Source: summary-rh/01/07.

B7. Sylvester sparsity and no Euler product

Statement. The greedy support satisfies m_{i+1} ≥ m_i(m_i−1)+1, density ≈3.9·10⁻⁵; f(n)=λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative; no Euler product.

Status: proved + verified; shiftProd non-multiplicativity Lean-certified.

Source: summary-rh/01/08.

B8. One-point Chowla is PNT

Statement. Σ_{n≤x}λ(n)=o(x) is equivalent to PNT.

Status: theorem (classical). Correction X1.

Source: summary-rh/04/03.

Findings

F1 — The Riemann conversion is complete

The geometric programme proves the equivalence between RH and the equality of the balanced and unrestricted supports. The conversion is not a proof of RH, but it is a complete translation: no part of the implication is missing. The remaining gap is the equality itself.

F2 — The additive toolbox self-limits

The Davenport–Heilbronn twist has every feature the construction consumes — functional equation, mean-zero periodic coefficients, Euler–Maclaurin comparison, classical floor — and no Euler product. The framework transfers verbatim and reports a false RH at Spira’s zero. Therefore no theorem using only the ingredients in that list can prove RH. This is a limitative result about that list, not a heuristic about every conceivable method.

F3 — The parity programme self-limits

The greedy harmonic sine is a rich object: a proved complexity law, exact budgets, Brown reachability, a parity lock, a balance identity, and the sharp partial-sum error. But all five bridges to the parity problem fail: the support carries no λ-parity signal, the budgets carry only index parity, the correlation has no Euler product, the support is Sylvester-sparse, and an arithmetic alphabet is circular. The programme’s own verdict is that the goal is unreachable as it stands.

F4 — Finite prefixes do not determine finite correlations

The finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem is the one clean new theorem. Two functions can agree on an arbitrarily long prefix yet have different finite correlation sums; the perturbation has ℓ²-mass exactly 4. Because only finitely many terms change, the two correlation sums differ by a bounded amount and have the same asymptotics. The theorem is Lean-certified. It shows that a finite prefix cannot fix a finite correlation; it is not a formal statement that finite reasoning cannot decide the limit.

F5 — Density statements are blind

Bombieri–Hejhal show that “almost all” statements about zeros of a linear combination of L-functions are blind to a density-zero set of off-line zeros. The RH measures escape this only because the tempering weight charges individual atoms. Any bridge that is only density-level cannot close either wall.

F6 — The missing inputs are the same kind of object

The RH wall needs an arithmetic positivity/multiplicativity input: (M), Weil positivity, the absence of a right-half atom, or the joint non-vanishing of K and S. The parity wall needs a sign-blind rule with positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω. They are not the same statement, but they are the same kind of object: a global arithmetic positivity that the local machinery cannot manufacture.

F7 — Computation is not the bottleneck

The programme has extensive numerics: certified zeros at T=15 and T=33, Fisher zeros, parity probes, Chowla correlations to 10⁹, and a Lean corpus. None of it decides the asymptotic statements. The finite-prefix theorem explains why: a finite computation can refute, but cannot establish, the limit. The bottleneck is the missing global input, not compute.

F8 — Lean confirms the core, not the walls

The Lean development is sorry-free and axiom-free. It formalises the finite-prefix core and the well-posedness of the two walls. It does not formalise the conversion theorem or the sharpness theorem, and it does not prove either wall. Formalisation is a microscope, not a key.

F9 — The two walls are independent but structurally analogous

The programme does not prove RH ↔︎ twin primes, or RH ↔︎ Chowla. The two walls are logically independent. But they share a shape: a local/additive/finite-prefix method reaches a sharp limit, and the missing ingredient is a global arithmetic positivity. The analogy is structural, not a theorem of equivalence.

F10 — The map is the achievement

The programme’s achievement is not a proof of either wall. It is the map: the conversion theorem, the sharpness theorem, the finite-prefix theorem, the five-bridge refutation, the two-wall diagnosis, the Lean core, and the certified evaluator. The map is complete enough to tell a future researcher where not to dig.

F11 — Certified evaluation is an instrument, not a key

Objective III supplies a rational rectangle containing ζ(s), ζ(s,a) or L(s,χ), with every rational operation recorded in a straight-line certificate, replayed by an independent checker, and checked in Lean. The interval algebra, certificate soundness, truncation transport, eta cos/sin split and transcendental enclosures are Lean-proved. But the evaluator is not a bridge: no theorem about either wall uses a certified enclosure as a hypothesis, and the final ζ enclosure depends on the Euler–Maclaurin identity and tail bound, which are now Lean-proved for K ≥ 1. Its contribution is that finite-scale claims can now be made exact and replayable instead of floating point. That strengthens the audit and the instrument kit; it does not decide an asymptotic statement. The open task is R13–R15 in Further research.

Significance

1. Negative results with positive content

The programme’s central results are negative: the additive toolbox cannot prove RH; the greedy sine cannot solve parity; finite prefixes cannot decide Chowla. But each negative result has positive content:

  • the Davenport–Heilbronn litmus tells you which future inputs could work;
  • the five-bridge refutation tells you which parity strategies are dead;
  • the finite-prefix theorem tells you why computation cannot settle the limit.

A negative result that closes a door is more useful than a positive result that opens a wrong one.

2. A reusable litmus test

The Davenport–Heilbronn test is a general method: run your construction on a function that has every feature you use except the one you need. If it reports the wrong answer, your method cannot be the proof. This is a transferable methodological contribution, not just a fact about ζ.

3. A formalised no-go core

The finite-prefix theorem is formalised in Lean, with no sorry and no axiom. It is a small, reusable no-go result: finite data cannot determine a correlation limit. The formalisation makes the no-go precise and machine-checkable.

4. A unified vocabulary

The programme gives a shared vocabulary for two fields that usually do not talk to each other: the measure-theoretic conversion of RH and the parity/Chowla barrier. “Right-half atom”, “sign-blind rule”, “positive-density coupling”, “finite-prefix indeterminacy”, and “global arithmetic positivity” can be used in the same sentence without equivocation.

5. A correction to the rhetoric of progress

The programme is a corrective to the rhetoric that more numerical probing, more data, or more numerical zeros will close these problems. The finite-prefix theorem is a precise statement that they will not. This matters for how the subject is presented to students, funders, and the public.

6. A pedagogical map

The two-wall map is a teaching device. It shows what a proof of RH would have to consume, what a proof of twin primes would have to consume, and why the standard toolkits stop where they do. It is a map of the edge of the known, not a claim to have crossed it.

7. A record of failure, kept honestly

The programme keeps its errors: X1–X6, the O(1/p) correction, the false ceiling law, the withdrawn barrier sentence, the 015 dimension-law retraction. The errata are part of the significance. A research programme that hides its failures is less useful than one that maps them.

8. A reusable certified computation layer

Objective III is a positive residue of the programme even though it does not move either wall. It gives an exact-rational interval engine, a straight-line certificate format, an independent replay checker, and a Lean soundness theorem, together with kernel-checked enclosures for π, exp, log, sin and cos and a closed ζ(2) replay. Any future finite-scale claim about ζ, ζ(s,a) or L(s,χ) can use this layer instead of floating point. The layer is honest about its boundary: the Euler–Maclaurin identity and the explicit tail bound are Lean theorems for K ≥ 1 and now yield a closed rectangle enclosure for Re s > 1, but the evaluator is not yet a hypothesis in a theorem about a wall, and a symbolic width bound is not certified. That boundary is part of the significance, not a defect to hide.

Novelty

The individual results are not all new. The conversion theorem, the Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness, the finite-prefix theorem, and the barrier ledger come from the two source corpora; the evaluator is reconstructed from the third source. The novelty of the-final-letter/ is the synthesis, the formalisation, and the integration of the computation layer.

N1 — The two-wall diagnosis

The claim that the Riemann wall and the parity wall are the same kind of wall — a missing global arithmetic positivity, not a missing estimate — is new as a unified thesis. The two programmes were pursued separately; this letter puts them in one frame.

N2 — Finite-prefix indeterminacy as a general no-go

The finite-prefix theorem is a result about two-point Chowla. The letter reads it as a general no-go principle: finite data cannot determine the limit of a correlation, so numerical verification is structurally incapable of closing the wall. This reading is new, and it is the conceptual bridge between the two programmes.

N3 — The Davenport–Heilbronn litmus as a general method

The DH test is used in the RH programme as a sharpness proof. The letter elevates it to a general filter: any proposed bridge must fail for the DH twist, or it is not consuming the missing arithmetic input. This is a transferable methodological contribution.

N4 — A single barrier ledger

The letter merges P1–P19, B1–B5, N1–N3 with the RH conversion/sharpness and the finite-prefix theorem into one barrier ledger. The merged ledger is new even though its entries are not.

N5 — A Lean spine for the synthesis

The FinalLetter Lean development is new: it defines the two walls in Mathlib’s vocabulary, defines two-point Chowla, and proves the finite-prefix theorem in a form that connects the two programmes. It is a small but real formalisation of the synthesis, not a reprint of Rh46.

N6 — The final-letter form

The programme is written as a letter rather than a paper: it states what has been achieved and what has not, and it ends with a note on futility. The form is a deliberate rejection of the hype cycle that surrounds RH and twin primes. It is a literary and methodological novelty, not a mathematical one.

N7 — A certified evaluator inside the letter's argument

Objective III is not a new numerical method: Euler–Maclaurin, rational interval arithmetic, Machin's formula, and Taylor enclosures are standard. What is new here is the arrangement: the evaluator is reconstructed as a programme with a 51-row Lean ledger, a 14-row computation ledger, an independent certificate replay, kernel-checked transcendental enclosures, and a Lean-proved Euler–Maclaurin layer, now consumed by a closed rectangle enclosure for Re s > 1, with the eta wiring and the symbolic width left explicitly open. The letter reads that layer as an instrument for finite-scale claims, not as a bridge. The integration is the novelty; the mathematics of the evaluator is inherited.

What is not novel

  • The conversion theorem, the DH sharpness, the finite-prefix theorem, the barrier ledger entries, and the Lean finite-prefix proofs are from the source corpora.
  • The individual open problems (OP-1…OP-19, F.4, Λ=0) are from the literature or the source programmes.
  • The two walls are known separately; only the unified diagnosis is new.
  • The evaluator's algorithm and the transcendental enclosure methods are standard; the reconstruction and the integration are the contribution, not the invention of the method.

Further research

The programme’s open problems are not all equally useful. This list is ranked by what would actually move a wall, not by what is easy to compute.

Tier 1 — would move a wall

R1. Prove or refute OP-19 (the log-indicator barrier)

The well-posed replacement for OP-3: for every log-arc rule A_I, is C_I(x) = Σ_{n≤x}1_{A_I}(n)λ(n) = o(x)? A proof would be a genuine parity result; a counterexample would kill the sign-blind programme.

Why it matters: it is the cleanest precise statement of “can a sign-blind rule with no arithmetic input force a multiplicative correlation?” Status: open. See summary-rh/03/04.

R2. Prove or refute Weil positivity for the repaired current

The tensor programme reduces the RH positivity to Weil positivity for W. A proof would close RH; a counterexample would refute it. The current W has the correct spectral resolution; the positivity is the open statement.

Why it matters: it is the exact form of the missing arithmetic positivity on the RH side. Status: open/classical. See summary/10.

R3. Prove or refute (M) in a class that contains ζ

(M): no zeros in σ>1. Under (M), the DH obstruction lapses, but (M) does not prove RH. The useful version is a multiplicativity/positivity input that fails for a sum of two Euler products and is strong enough to reach σ=1/2.

Why it matters: it is the sharpest known separator between the additive and multiplicative worlds. Status: open as an input; see summary/06.

R4. A Lee–Yang/confinement theorem for the polygon

F.4: a ferromagnetic spin system whose finite partition functions are η_N^{(N²)}, or any reweighting confining every finite zero to a line. It passes the DH litmus; the computable part is the transfer-operator form.

Why it matters: it is the one known mechanism that forces finite zeros onto a line. Status: open. See summary/09.

Tier 2 — would sharpen a wall

R5. de Bruijn–Newman Λ = 0

Known 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.22; RH is Λ=0. A proof of Λ=0 would close RH. A proof of Λ>0 would refute it.

R6. Jensen/Li tower uniformity

Fixed degree at large shift is known; uniformity in the degree is the full tower. A proof would close RH; a counterexample would refute it.

R7. Uniform slow passage on the tightness window

|ζ′(ρ)| ≤ N^ε on |γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}. This is a GUE/HKO prediction, not used by any theorem. It would make parts of the conversion uniform.

R8. Uniform isolation

A pair-correlation bound on zeros with a neighbour within N^{−κ}. It would make the isolation clause of Definition 5.1 uniform.

Tier 3 — formalisation and methodology

R9. Formalise the conversion theorem in Lean

A substantial measure-theory/analytic-number-theory project. It would make the RH conversion machine-checked, but it would not prove RH.

R10. Formalise the greedy/budget/balance layers

Rh41, Rh42, Rh43, Rh45, Rh47, Op56, ConeLean, TppLean already contain substantial Lean. A unified FinalLetter development could import them and expose a single API.

R11. Make OP-18 well-posed

OP-18 is not well-posed as phrased; on a fixed class it is the existential dual of OP-19. The useful task is to fix the class and state the dual precisely.

R12. A formal “no finite prefix” meta-theorem

The finite-prefix theorem is about one slice. A general meta-theorem — any predicate determined by finitely many values cannot imply a limit statement — would be a reusable no-go principle. It is not currently formalised.

R13. Formalise Euler–Maclaurin and the explicit tail bound

Objective III's rational cell is Lean-proved, but the identity that its replay value is ζ(s,a) (S1) and the explicit tail bound (S2) are now Lean-proved for K ≥ 1. What remains is to wire the general Lean S1/S2 theorem into the evaluator's certificate replay; the analytic content is formalised.

Why it matters: it is the only remaining gap between the evaluator and an unconditional certified enclosure of ζ(s), ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ).

R14. Use a certified enclosure as a hypothesis

No theorem in the letter yet consumes a certified enclosure. Candidate targets are finite-window zero counts, interval Newton or bisection for zeros on a line, and rigorous evaluation of a truncated explicit formula. The point is not to replace the analytic proof but to remove floating point from a finite-scale step that a theorem actually cites.

Why it matters: this is the difference between an evaluator as a library and an evaluator as part of an argument.

R15. Certify enclosure width, not only soundness

inflate is sound for every r ≥ 0, so a certificate can always be widened. The recorded narrow widths are Python measurements, not Lean theorems. A width theorem — or a certificate format that records a target width and checks it — would make “the enclosure is narrow” a checkable claim rather than a measurement.

Why it matters: soundness without a width bound is enough for containment but not for the efficiency claims that make a certified evaluator useful.

What not to do

  • Do not run more zeros. The finite-prefix theorem says finite computation cannot decide the Chowla limit; the T=15/33 certificates are finite-window results, not RH.
  • Do not sharpen an estimate that the DH twist also satisfies. The DH litmus says it cannot be the missing input.
  • Do not treat density statements as pointwise. Bombieri–Hejhal show they are blind to density-zero off-line sets.
  • Do not promote CONJECTURED heuristics to targets. Eleven of the 36 harvested items are recorded expectations, not commitments.
  • Do not restore X1–X6. The errata are part of the result.
  • Do not treat a zero inside an interval as a proof that a zero exists. The evaluator certifies containment, not a sign change or a zero count.
  • Do not quote a width that the Lean layer does not check. inflate is sound for every non-negative radius; the recorded narrow widths are measurements.

The walls

A wall is a place where a method class reaches a proved or structural limit. The programme identifies eight. They are not all independent; the first two are the headline walls, the rest are the mechanisms that make them hard.

W1 — The arithmetic-positivity wall (RH)

What it blocks. The Riemann programme converts RH into the equality of the balanced and unrestricted supports. The equality is equivalent to the absence of a right-half atom of the unrestricted measure, to the joint non-vanishing of K = ¼ Re ξ and S = ¼ Im ξ, to Weil positivity for the repaired current, and to (M) plus a conversion that reaches σ=1/2.

Why it is a wall. The additive toolbox cannot force positivity. The Davenport–Heilbronn twist has every additive feature and no Euler product, and the framework correctly reports a false RH at Spira’s zero. Therefore no theorem from the additive toolbox can be RH (Corollary C.10).

What would breach it. A global arithmetic positivity/multiplicativity input that fails for a sum of two Euler products and is strong enough to exclude a right-half atom.

Status. The conversion is proved; the positivity is open.

W2 — The parity wall (twin primes, Chowla)

What it blocks. The greedy harmonic sine is a deterministic, sign-blind object. The twin-prime/Chowla target needs a positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω. The programme proves the greedy support is Sylvester-sparse (density ≈3.9·10⁻⁵), the budgets carry only index parity, the correlation has no Euler product, and the arithmetic-alphabet bridge is circular.

Why it is a wall. Selberg’s parity barrier: purely sieve-theoretic information cannot distinguish even/odd Ω. Every bridge that stays sign-blind is either too sparse or circular.

What would breach it. A sign-blind rule with a positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω, or a proof of OP-19, or a genuinely new arithmetic input.

Status. The finite-prefix theorem, the Sylvester-sparsity theorem, and the five-bridge refutation are proved; the coupling is open.

W3 — The finite-prefix wall

What it blocks. No finite prefix of values can decide an asymptotic correlation statement. The finite-prefix indeterminacy theorem makes this precise for two-point Chowla: a function can agree with λ on [1,a) and change the correlation later; the perturbation has ℓ²-mass exactly 4.

Why it is a wall. A finite computation can refute an asymptotic claim by finding a counterexample, but cannot establish the limit. The theorem is Lean-certified. Objective III is the finite instrument this wall permits: it certifies finite-scale values exactly, but it does not turn a finite certificate into a limit.

What would breach it. Nothing, for the finite-prefix method. It is a no-go result, not a conjecture. The only way past it is to use a global argument, not more prefix data.

Status. Proved.

W4 — The Euler-product wall

What it blocks. f(n)=λ(n)λ(n+2) is not multiplicative; the shifted product has no Euler product. The Dirichlet series of f does not factor over primes, so the standard multiplicative-function toolkit does not apply.

Why it is a wall. The RH side’s missing input is also an Euler-product/positivity input. The DH function has a functional equation but no Euler product; the correlation has no Euler product. Both walls sit at the same absence.

What would breach it. A genuinely new way to handle non-multiplicative correlations, or a proof that a multiplicative surrogate carries the needed information.

Status. Proved obstruction (no Euler product); no breach known.

W5 — The self-adjointness wall (Hilbert–Pólya)

What it blocks. A Hilbert–Pólya operator would prove RH by self-adjointness. The programme’s CCM/helix work reduces the problem to a WKB/trace-formula conjecture (CCM Conjecture 1); the canonical paper’s tensor work reaches Weil positivity instead. Neither supplies the operator.

Why it is a wall. Self-adjointness is a strong global positivity. The local geometric constructions do not produce it. The trace-formula picture passes the DH litmus but has no constructed operator.

What would breach it. A self-adjoint operator whose spectral determinant is ξ, with the required positivity.

Status. Open; CCM Conjecture 1 is the precise obstruction.

W6 — The uniformity wall

What it blocks. The RH conversion needs uniformity in height: the tightness window |γ| ≤ N^{1/2−ε}, the isolation clause, and the vanishing aperture. The parity programme needs uniformity in scale: the log-average vs natural-density gap.

Why it is a wall. The known methods give local or averaged statements. The conversion needs pointwise uniformity; the parity target needs a fixed shift, not an average. The gap is structural (Tao’s entropy-decrement remark; Frantzikinakis–Lemańczyk–de la Rue for Cesàro).

What would breach it. A pointwise uniformity theorem, not a density statement.

Status. Open/conditional (GUE, pair correlation, Lindelöf).

W7 — The density-vs-pointwise wall

What it blocks. “Almost all” statements are blind to a density-zero set. Bombieri–Hejhal show this for zeros of linear combinations of L-functions. A density-zero off-line zero can survive any density statement.

Why it is a wall. The RH measures escape it only because the tempering weight charges individual atoms; the parity target needs a pointwise correlation, not a density statement.

What would breach it. A pointwise theorem, e.g. a Lee–Yang/confinement property for every finite zero.

Status. Proved blindness; pointwise bridge open (F.4).

W8 — The well-posedness wall

What it blocks. Some open problems are not propositions as stated. OP-3 (“no sign-blind rule seeing n only through log n”) is false in its literal reading because log is injective and can encode arithmetic. OP-18 collapses to the existential dual of OP-19. The barrier sentence X5 was too wide and was withdrawn.

Why it is a wall. A problem that is not well-posed cannot be proved or refuted. The programme’s repair is OP-19: restrict the description of the rule (log-arc rules), not its value.

What would breach it. A precise definition and a definite truth value. OP-19 is the model.

Status. OP-19 is well-posed and open; OP-18 needs the same treatment.

Possible bridges

A bridge is a proposed route from the available data to the far side of a wall. The programme evaluates each bridge by three tests:

  1. Does it pass the Davenport–Heilbronn litmus? It must fail for the DH twist, or it is not consuming the missing arithmetic input.
  2. Is it pointwise or only density-level? Density statements are blind to density-zero off-line sets.
  3. Is it proved, conditional, or open? A bridge that is only a re-description is not a bridge.

B1 — Weil positivity / the explicit formula

What it would give. Positivity of the Weil current W on the cone is equivalent to RH; its spectral resolution is exactly the zero measure.

Status. The correct form is proved (Theorem C of tensor.md), but the positivity is open. The naive current P and its inequality (★) are refuted, so the bridge must use W, not P.

Verdict. The right bridge on the RH side; the far side is open.

B2 — Hypothesis (M) and Saias–Weingartner

What it would give. Under (M) — no zeros in σ>1 — the DH obstruction lapses and the conversion becomes GRH for one L-function.

Status. Conditional. (M) is true for ζ, false for DH. It removes the limitative result but does not prove RH; multiplicativity is known only to reach within o(1) of σ=1.

Verdict. A bridge to a conditional conversion, not to RH.

B3 — Lee–Yang / ferromagnetic confinement

What it would give. A ferromagnetic spin system whose finite partition functions are the polygon would confine every finite zero to a line. This is F.4.

Status. Open. It passes the DH litmus because it uses a positivity a sum of two partition functions does not have. The computable part is the transfer-operator form.

Verdict. The most promising structural bridge; no construction yet.

B4 — Hilbert–Pólya / CCM

What it would give. A self-adjoint operator whose spectrum is the zero ordinates.

Status. Open. The CCM programme reduces it to Conjecture 1 (the ground-state eigenvector is a prime sum). The canonical programme’s tensor work reaches Weil positivity instead. No operator is constructed.

Verdict. A bridge in principle; the far side is a conjecture.

B5 — Heat flow / de Bruijn–Newman

What it would give. Λ=0 is RH. Known 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.22.

Status. Open. The flow is a genuine deformation of Φ; the endpoint τ=0 is the wall. The programme’s cosine/Jensen/Li analysis locates the same object.

Verdict. A quantitative bridge; the last 0.22 is not a proof.

B6 — Log-arc rules / OP-19

What it would give. A well-posed parity statement: is C_I(x)=o(x) for every log-arc rule?

Status. Open. It replaces the unstatable OP-3. A proof would be a genuine parity result; a counterexample would kill the sign-blind programme.

Verdict. The cleanest parity bridge; no proof yet.

B7 — The straightening field

What it would give. Holonomy equals the zero count; under the Rouché condition (E.1), the count is computable from two finite Euler sums.

Status. Proved identities; (E.1) is explicit. No admissible contour is known to reach the critical line (Remark E.11).

Verdict. A finite-data bridge to the zero count, not to RH.

B8 — The tensor / Weil current

What it would give. The exact spectral resolution of W is the zero measure; positivity is RH.

Status. Proved resolution; open positivity. The naive P is refuted.

Verdict. The correct real-variable form of B1; same wall.

B9 — Transfer operator

What it would give. The computable part of F.4: write the polygon as a trace over the N-spin chain and compute the sign of the induced couplings for small N.

Status. Tractable, not done. A negative coupling settles F.4 in the negative for that representation; positive couplings for all N would be the first Lee–Yang-shaped input that passes the litmus.

Verdict. The most concrete next computation; not a proof.

B10 — Finite-prefix data

What it would give. Nothing asymptotic. It can refute, not establish.

Status. Proved no-go: finite-prefix indeterminacy.

Verdict. Not a bridge. It is the proof that a certain class of bridges cannot exist.

B11 — Certified evaluation (explicitly not a bridge)

What it would give. Rational enclosures of ζ(s), ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ) with a replayable certificate. It gives exact finite-scale values, not an asymptotic statement.

Status. The rational cell, certificate replay, eta split, transcendental enclosures, the Euler–Maclaurin S1–S2 layer and a closed rectangle enclosure for Re s > 1 are Lean-proved. No wall theorem uses an enclosure as a hypothesis yet; a target-width certificate format is proved, but a symbolic width bound is not.

Verdict. Not a bridge. It is the instrument a future bridge can use, and the letter says so rather than promoting it.

Final note

What has been achieved

This programme has achieved six things.

1. A conversion. The Riemann hypothesis has been translated into an equality of supports of two measure families. The translation is complete. It does not prove the hypothesis, but it removes the ambiguity about what a proof would have to show: the absence of a right-half atom, the joint non-vanishing of K and S, Weil positivity for the repaired current.

2. A sharpness theorem. The Davenport–Heilbronn twist has every feature the additive construction uses and no Euler product. The construction transfers verbatim and reports a false Riemann hypothesis at Spira’s zero. Therefore no theorem from the additive toolbox can prove RH. This is not a failure of effort; it is a proved limit.

3. A finite-prefix theorem. Two functions can agree on an arbitrarily long prefix and have different correlation sums. The perturbation has ℓ²-mass exactly 4. No finite computation can decide two-point Chowla. The theorem is Lean-certified, sorry-free, and axiom-free.

4. A barrier map. The parity programme proves the factor-complexity law, the budgets, Brown reachability, the parity lock, the balance identity, the sharp error at a zero, the failure of smoothing to remove √N, the corrected explicit formula, the Sylvester-sparsity theorem, and the non-multiplicativity of the shifted product. It disposes of all five bridges from the greedy sine to parity.

5. A Lean spine. The FinalLetter development defines the two walls in Mathlib’s vocabulary, defines two-point Chowla, and proves the finite-prefix theorem in a form that connects the two programmes. The build is [check] OK, with no sorry and no axiom.

6. A certified computation layer. Objective III reconstructs the partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator as a programme: exact rational interval arithmetic, a straight-line certificate replayed by an independent checker, a Lean soundness theorem, the eta cos/sin split, kernel-checked transcendental enclosures, a closed ζ(2) replay, and a Lean-proved Euler–Maclaurin identity and tail bound for K ≥ 1. It is the instrument the walls need, not a bridge across either. No theorem about a wall yet uses a certified enclosure as a hypothesis, and the width is a measurement rather than a theorem.

What has not been achieved

The Riemann hypothesis is not proved. The twin-prime conjecture is not proved. Chowla for k ≥ 2 is not proved. Sarnak’s conjecture is not proved. Elliott’s conjecture is not proved.

The programme does not prove that these walls are unclimbable. It proves that the methods surveyed here cannot climb them. That is a different and more honest claim.

The futility

The futility is not the futility of mathematics. It is the futility of a certain kind of attack: more zeros, more digits, more finite prefixes, more local estimates, more sign-blind rules, more density statements. Each of these can refute. None of them can establish the limit. The finite-prefix theorem is the precise form of that sentence for Chowla; the Davenport–Heilbronn theorem is the precise form of it for the additive RH toolbox. The certified evaluator is not a counterexample to that sentence: it makes finite claims exact, but it does not make a finite claim asymptotic.

The wall is not a mistake in the proof. The wall is the proof that the method has reached its edge.

This is why the final letter is a letter of resignation — not from mathematics, but from the illusion that the wall is a calculation. The calculation is done. The map is drawn. The door is not in this house.

What remains

The two walls remain. OP-19 remains. Weil positivity remains. F.4 remains. Λ=0 remains. The Jensen/Li tower remains. The conversion remains an equivalence, not a proof. The finite-prefix theorem remains a no-go, not a victory. The evaluator's Euler–Maclaurin identity and tail bound are Lean theorems for K ≥ 1 and yield a closed rectangle enclosure for Re s > 1, but no wall theorem consumes a certified enclosure yet, and only a concrete certificate width, not a symbolic width bound, is checked.

But something remains that was not there before: a precise map of where the walls are, what they are made of, which bridges fail and why, and what a future bridge would have to consume. A map is not a key, but it is not nothing. It is the difference between being lost and knowing that you are at the wall.

The last sentence

The final letter does not end with a theorem. It ends with a location.

The Riemann hypothesis and the twin-prime conjecture are not behind a computation. They are behind an arithmetic positivity that the local methods cannot manufacture. We have proved the absence of the local key for the methods surveyed here, and drawn the shape of the lock.

That is what has been achieved. The futility is that the lock is still locked.
