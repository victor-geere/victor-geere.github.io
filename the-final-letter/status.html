2. The two walls (first two objectives)

2.1 The Riemann wall

The Riemann programme converts RH into an equality of supports. Let Ξ be the completed zeta function, K = ¼ Re ξ and S = ¼ Im ξ. The conversion theorem proves the equivalence of:

  1. every non-trivial zero of Ξ lies on Re s = 1/2;
  2. every unrestricted path-closure is balanced for all sufficiently large N;
  3. every weak-star limit of the unrestricted measures is supported on the critical line;
  4. the balanced and unrestricted limiting measures coincide, with common support the non-trivial zeros.

This is an equivalence, not a proof. The Davenport–Heilbronn sharpness theorem shows that the conversion returns the correct verdict for a function with an off-line zero (Spira's zero), so the additive toolbox that proves the equivalence cannot be the missing input. The tensor programme then refutes the naive positivity (★) and identifies the correct positive statement: Weil positivity for the repaired current W, whose spectral resolution is exactly the zero measure. That positivity is open.

What is proved on this side. The conversion (A1), the DH sharpness (A2), the limitative consequence (A3), the tensor refutation and repair (A4), and the modular shadow/straightening-field identities (A5). What is not proved. RH itself, Weil positivity, (M), F.4, Λ = 0, the Jensen/Li tower, and uniform slow passage.

2.2 The parity wall

The parity programme studies the greedy harmonic sine and its two-point Liouville correlation. It proves:

  • the factor-complexity law for the greedy word;
  • the budget identities, Brown reachability and parity lock;
  • balance and the equal split;
  • that smoothing does not remove the √N barrier;
  • the corrected explicit formula and the finite-window certificates T=15, 33;
  • finite-prefix indeterminacy: for every a ≠ 0 and every h, a function g can agree with λ below a, differ from λ, and reproduce every prefix of corr h N with N+h < a;
  • Sylvester sparsity and the non-multiplicativity of n ↦ λ(n)λ(n+2);
  • the refutation of all five proposed bridges from the greedy sine to parity.

The missing input is a sign-blind rule with positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω. Density statements and finite prefixes cannot supply it.

Why both are “global-input walls”. Neither wall is an estimate wall. On the Riemann side the missing object is a positivity/multiplicativity that fails for the Davenport–Heilbronn twist. On the parity side it is a positive-density multiplicative coupling that fails for the greedy support. They are structurally analogous, not logically equivalent.

3. The third objective and its Lean layer

Objective III is the positive computation layer. For a > 0, s = σ + it, b = a + M, it decomposes the Hurwitz zeta by Euler–Maclaurin:

ζ(s,a) = Σ_{k<M} (a+k)^{-s}
       + ∫_M^∞ (a+x)^{-s} dx
       + b^{-s}/2
       + Σ_{j=1}^K B_{2j}/(2j)! · (s)_{2j-1} · b^{-s-2j+1}
       + R_K(s),

with the explicit tail bound

|R_K(s)| ≤ |(s)_{2K+1}| · 2ζ(2K+1)/(2π)^{2K+1} · b^{-σ-2K}/(σ+2K).

Every rational operation is recorded in a straight-line certificate; the certificate is replayed by an independent Python checker and kernel-checked in Lean (Expr.wf, Expr.wf_sound). The transcendental factors are enclosed by Lean theorems in Transcend.lean. The concrete ζ(2) replay has every atom discharged in ReplayAtoms.lean.

4. How the third objective applies to the first two

The honest answer has three parts: where it applies, where it cannot, and what the logical shape of a genuine application would be. The third objective is a finite-scale instrument. Both walls are asymptotic/global statements. That mismatch is the whole story.

4.1 The general shape of an application

A certified enclosure proves a statement of the form

∀ x ∈ [lo, hi],  value(x) ∈ [L, U],

or, for a concrete replay, a single closed-box membership. A theorem about a wall has the form

∀ s with ζ(s)=0 and 0 < Re s < 1,  Re s = 1/2
∀ h,  Σ_{n≤N} λ(n)λ(n+h) = o(N).

A finite family of boxes does not imply a universal statement. The correct use of the evaluator is therefore as a hypothesis-discharger inside a theorem schema:

Template. Suppose a theorem has the form (finite-scale premise P) ∧ (transfer principle T) ⇒ (wall statement). The evaluator can discharge P by a Lean-checked certificate. It cannot supply T. The programme currently has no T that is not exactly the missing global input. That is why R14 is open and why B11 is not a bridge.

4.2 Application to the Riemann wall

Where it applies

  • Zero containment and localisation. Given a rational rectangle, the evaluator can certify that ζ takes a value in a box on that rectangle. With a sign change or an argument-principle count, this can support a finite-window zero statement. Containment alone is not existence; the evaluator certifies 0 ∈ box, not “there is a zero”.
  • Finite-window explicit formula. The T=15/33 certificates are unconditional finite-window statements. The evaluator can evaluate the truncated explicit formula and the archimedean term rigorously on a finite window, replacing floating point in a statement the programme actually cites (B5).
  • The Davenport–Heilbronn litmus. The off-line zero of the DH twist can be enclosed and checked rigorously in a neighbourhood. This makes the sharpness witness machine-checkable at finite scale.
  • Finite-dimensional positivity tests. For a fixed test function f, the Weil current W(f) and finite Gram matrices can be evaluated with enclosures. A negative value is a rigorous refutation of a proposed positivity statement on that test class.
  • Finite identities used by the map. The eta cos/sin split and the L-reduction are finite identities; the evaluator certifies their numerical instances.

Where it cannot apply

  • Global support equality. RH is a statement about all zeros. A finite set of enclosures cannot exclude an off-line zero outside the boxes.
  • Weil positivity over an infinite cone. Positivity for every test function in a cone is an infinite-dimensional statement. Finite-dimensional checks do not imply it.
  • “Inequality (★) is true”. The programme already has a refutation; the evaluator can sharpen the witness, not prove a false statement.
  • The DH litmus itself. Enclosures are additive and apply verbatim to the DH twist. A bridge built only from enclosures would therefore fail the litmus. This is a structural reason, not a limitation of precision.

Verdict for Objective I

instrument, not input The evaluator can make finite-scale Riemann-side claims exact and auditable. It cannot supply the global positivity that the wall lacks. Its legitimate Riemann-side roles are (i) finite-window certificates, (ii) rigorous local zero work, and (iii) finite-dimensional refutation of proposed positivity statements.

4.3 Application to the parity wall

Where it applies

  • Finite correlations. corr h N = Σ_{n≤N} λ(n)λ(n+h) is a finite integer sum. The evaluator's arithmetic layer can certify exact values and interval bounds for finitely many N.
  • The finite-prefix witness. The slice perturbation identity and the h=2, a=5 witness can be checked exactly, including the ℓ²-mass 4.
  • Refutation of proposed bridges. A bridge that predicts a sign, a density, or a correlation on a finite range can be tested; a mismatch is a rigorous refutation. The five-bridge refutation is the model: the failures are structural, and finite computation can corroborate the counterexamples.
  • Greedy-word data. Exact support, gaps, budgets and digit-word statistics can be certified on finite ranges.

Where it cannot apply

  • The limit o(N). Finite-prefix indeterminacy says that for any finite prefix there is a different function reproducing it. A certificate about finitely many N is therefore compatible with every possible limit behaviour that the programme can currently express. The finite-prefix theorem is precisely the statement that this kind of evidence cannot decide the wall.
  • Positive-density multiplicative coupling. The missing object is a rule coupling to Ω at positive density. An evaluator computes values; it does not manufacture an arithmetic coupling.
  • Sign-blindness. The evaluator is sign-blind: it knows values, not the multiplicative origin of a sign pattern. More precision does not change that.

Verdict for Objective II

refutation only The evaluator can refute a finite-range parity claim and can certify finite correlations. It cannot establish the asymptotic statement, and the finite-prefix theorem explains why a finite certificate cannot be promoted to one.

4.4 The one place the third objective could become a hypothesis

R14 asks for a theorem whose only numerical input is a certified enclosure. The cleanest candidates are:

  1. Finite-window zero counts. A theorem of the form “if ζ has exactly n zeros in the rectangle R and ζ has a sign change on each of n disjoint intervals, then …” The count and the signs can be certified. This is still a finite-window theorem, not RH.
  2. Interval Newton / bisection. A certified enclosure with a derivative bound can prove existence and isolation of a zero in a box. Again local, not global.
  3. Truncated explicit formula. A finite-window positivity statement for a fixed test function can be certified. It is a finite-dimensional shadow of Weil positivity, not the infinite-dimensional statement.

Each is a genuine theorem with an enclosure as a hypothesis. None closes a wall. Building one of them is the right next step because it converts the evaluator from a library into part of an argument, and it makes precise exactly how much of the wall remains.

6. What would bridge the walls, and what each bridge requires

A bridge is a route from the available data to the far side of a wall. The programme tests every candidate by three questions:

  1. Does it pass the Davenport–Heilbronn litmus? It must fail for the DH twist, or it is not consuming the missing arithmetic input.
  2. Is it pointwise or only density-level? Density statements are blind to density-zero off-line sets.
  3. Is it proved, conditional, or open? A re-description is not a bridge.

6.2 What each bridge requires

B1 / B8 — Weil positivity for the repaired current

What it would give. Positivity of W on the cone is equivalent to RH; its spectral resolution is exactly the zero measure.

What is already there. The correct statement is identified (Theorem C of tensor.md). The naive current P and (★) are refuted, so the bridge must use W, not P.

What is required to build it.

  1. A precise cone. Fix the test-function class and the inner product on which W is positive. The statement must be strong enough to imply the support equality and weak enough to be provable.
  2. An arithmetic positivity. Prove W(f*f̄) ≥ 0 using a multiplicativity/Euler-product input that fails for the DH twist. This is the missing global input. It cannot be an estimate that DH also satisfies.
  3. The explicit-formula bridge. Show that positivity of W is equivalent to the critical-line support. The explicit formula supplies this; the archimedean and prime terms must be handled pointwise.
  4. A finite-scale shadow. Identify the finite-dimensional Gram matrices whose positivity is necessary, and certify them with enclosures. This is where Objective III can legitimately help: it can test finite shadows and refute false strengthenings. It cannot prove the infinite cone.
  5. Formalisation. The finite shadows and the explicit formula can be formalised; the global positivity is a research theorem, not a Lean engineering task.
Why the evaluator does not shorten this. A certificate proves W(f) ∈ [L,U] for one f. Weil positivity is ∀ f in the cone, W(f) ≥ 0. The quantifier is the wall.

B2 — Hypothesis (M) and Saias–Weingartner

Requires. A proof of (M) — no zeros in σ>1 — in a class containing ζ but failing for a sum of two Euler products, plus a multiplicativity input strong enough to reach σ=1/2. (M) alone removes the DH obstruction and gives a conditional conversion; it does not prove RH. The evaluator can certify zeros and values, not the class statement.

B3 / B9 — Lee–Yang / ferromagnetic confinement and the transfer operator

Requires. A ferromagnetic spin system whose finite partition functions are the polygon, or a reweighting confining every finite zero to a line (F.4). The computable part is the transfer-operator form: write the polygon as a trace over the N-spin chain and compute the sign of the induced couplings. A negative coupling settles F.4 negatively for that representation; positive couplings for all N would be the first Lee–Yang-shaped input that passes the litmus. The evaluator can compute finite partition functions and couplings; it cannot construct the system or prove the all-N statement.

B4 — Hilbert–Pólya / CCM

Requires. A self-adjoint operator whose spectrum is the zero ordinates. CCM reduces this to Conjecture 1 (the ground-state eigenvector is a prime sum). The evaluator can check finite spectra and finite sums; it cannot produce the operator or prove the conjecture.

B5 — Heat flow / de Bruijn–Newman

Requires. Λ=0. Known 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.22. The flow is a genuine deformation; the endpoint τ=0 is the wall. The evaluator can certify finite-time zero-free regions and values; it cannot close the endpoint.

B6 — Log-arc rules / OP-19

Requires. A sign-blind rule AI with positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω for which CI(x)=Σn≤x1AI(n)λ(n)=o(x). A proof is a genuine parity result; a counterexample kills the sign-blind programme. The evaluator can compute CI(x) on finite ranges and refute; it cannot prove the limit.

B7 — The straightening field

Requires. An admissible contour reaching the critical line (condition E.1). The identities are proved; the contour is open. The evaluator can compute the two finite Euler sums; it cannot supply the contour.

B10 — Finite-prefix data

Status. A proved no-go. It can refute, not establish. The finite-prefix theorem is the precise statement that a certain class of bridges cannot exist. The evaluator lives on the same side of that no-go: a finite certificate is finite-prefix data.

B11 — Certified evaluation (explicitly not a bridge)

What it gives. Rational enclosures of ζ(s), ζ(s,a) and L(s,χ) with a replayable certificate. It gives exact finite-scale values, not an asymptotic statement.

Status. The rational cell, certificate replay, eta split, transcendental enclosures, Euler–Maclaurin S1–S2 (K ≥ 1), a closed rectangle enclosure for Re s > 1, a target-width certificate checker, and one critical-strip enclosure-as-hypothesis theorem (ζ(1/2+5i) ≠ 0) are Lean-proved. No wall theorem uses an enclosure as a hypothesis yet.

Verdict. Not a bridge. It is the instrument a future bridge can use, and the letter says so rather than promoting it.

6.3 The common requirement

Every genuine bridge must supply an arithmetic positivity that the local, additive, finite-prefix, density-level or sign-blind data cannot manufacture. On the Riemann side this is Weil positivity for W; on the parity side it is a positive-density multiplicative coupling to Ω. The two objects are structurally analogous, not identical. The evaluator supplies neither. What it supplies is the ability to make the finite shadows of either bridge exact, and to refute a proposed bridge on a finite witness.

What would make the third objective part of a bridge. A theorem schema with a finite-scale premise that is necessary for the wall and whose remaining gap is exactly the global input. The evaluator would discharge the premise. The programme has candidates for the finite-scale premise (§4.4) but no theorem in which that premise is both necessary and sufficient up to the global input. Building such a theorem is the research task; building the certificate is the engineering task.

7.1 What was actually proved this round

The next theorem was attacked in its rectangle form, which is the analytic content of the replay. In ReplayGeneral.lean:

theorem zeta_mem_box {S : Cx} (hS : 1 < S.re.lo) (M K : ℕ)
    (hM : 1 ≤ M) (hK : 1 ≤ K) {s : ℂ} (hs : Cx.mem s S) :
    Cx.mem (riemannZeta s) (zetaBox S M K)

This is a closed rational rectangle containing ζ(s) for every s in a rational rectangle with Re s > 1, for arbitrary M ≥ 1 and K ≥ 1. There is no atom hypothesis. The box zetaBox is a sum of five ball components, each discharged by a proved lemma:

  1. each partial-sum term, by the complex-power bound norm_cpow_neg_le_radius (using ‖b−s‖ = b−σ, then σ ≥ ⌊Re lo⌋);
  2. the integral, by integral_Ioi_norm_iteratedDeriv_hurwitzTerm and a rational majorant (integralRadius);
  3. the boundary term (boundaryRadius);
  4. each Bernoulli correction, by the iterated-derivative formula iteratedDeriv_hurwitzTerm_eqOn plus a rising-factorial bound (norm_bernoulli_le);
  5. the tail, by the proved S2 bound plus the crude arithmetic ζ(2K+1) ≤ 2 and π ≥ 3 (norm_tail_le).

The box is deliberately crude; it is an existence enclosure, not a narrow one. It is checked at s = 2, M = 3, K = 2:

theorem zeta_two_mem_box : Cx.mem (riemannZeta 2) (zetaBox (pointBox 2) 3 2)

The decomposition into W1–W5 is implemented as follows: W1 is zeta_eq_hurwitzZetaNP_one; W2 is the integral bound; W3 is the power and Bernoulli atom bounds; W4 is the uniform tail bound; W5 is the assembly zeta_mem_box. The design note is replay-wiring.md.

A precise distinction the round exposed. The generated ζ(2) file Replay.lean/ReplayAtoms.lean proves cert.eval σ ∈ cert.box with the atom values σ instantiated as expected. It does not prove that cert.eval σ is ζ(2): the tail atom is set to 0, and the outer inflation accounts for the tail radius analytically. The missing link from the certificate value to ζ is exactly what S1/S2 supplies, and zeta_mem_box now supplies it uniformly for Re s > 1. This is why P1 is described as partial rather than done: the analytic bridge exists, but it is not yet emitted as the wf-checked Expr for symbolic parameters.

7.2 What not to do

  • Do not run more zeros to “support” RH. Finite computation cannot decide a universal statement; the finite-prefix theorem is the precise form of this for Chowla.
  • Do not sharpen an estimate that the DH twist also satisfies. The litmus says it cannot be the missing input.
  • Do not treat density statements as pointwise. Bombieri–Hejhal show they are blind to density-zero off-line sets.
  • Do not promote CONJECTURED heuristics to targets.
  • Do not restore X1–X6.
  • Do not treat a zero inside an interval as a proof that a zero exists.
  • Do not quote a width that the Lean layer does not check.
