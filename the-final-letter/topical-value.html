Topical value

The Final Letter · what four dossiers are actually worth

One-sentence verdict. Of the four dossiers, one is a result (a simple Lean-certified zero of ζ on the critical line at 1/2 + 14i), one is an instrument (the certified evaluator), one is a map with a durable no-go core (geometric DQPT), and one is a component whose value waits on P4 (the finite explicit formula). All four are honest about their gaps; none crosses a wall, and none is valued here by proximity to RH rhetoric.

1. What "value" means on this page

This is not a monetary valuation and it is not a claim about truth. The value of a research dossier inside the letter is its contribution to the programme's objectives (the two walls and the positive computation layer) under honest accounting: what is kernel-checked, how far it reaches, what can be reused, and what survives the programme's own errata. The counts in this page's tables are measured (ledger, computation, source-claim and test rows); the value classes are a judgement, and are labelled as such.

Four value classes are used:

ClassDefinitionWhat it buys
A — result An unconditional, kernel-checked theorem that did not exist before in this corpus. It changes the boundary of what is certified, not only of what is written.
B — instrument A reusable, kernel-checked engine that future claims can be routed through. Option value: every later finite-scale claim can be exact and replayable.
C — map A structural statement — a conversion, a sharpness test, or a no-go theorem — that changes which future strategies are viable. It closes doors and redirects effort; it is the programme's signature product.
D — component A proved ingredient whose value is only realised when the rest of a named pipeline exists. Contingent value, honestly booked as such.

Five tests are applied in the sections below: certification (is the claim checked, and is the ledger honest?), reach (how close to the objectives?), reuse (can the next claim be built without rework?), durability (does it survive the errata?), and ceiling (what is the best case if the named open items close?).

3. finite-window-zeros — Class A, a result

The claim. A Krawczyk certificate for the zero of ζ at s14 = 1/2 + 14i, with every numerical premise discharged in Lean. The chain is: a value enclosure ‖ζ(s14)‖ ≤ 23/200, a derivative enclosure ‖1 − Yk·ζ′(s14)‖ ≤ 1/200 (T391, h1_deriv), a Lipschitz bound ‖ζ′(z) − ζ′(s14)‖ ≤ 47/250 on the closed 1/5-ball (T485, h2_deriv), and the Krawczyk map at r = 1/5. The ball is then upgraded to a line certificate: the same inequality is a contraction, and the functional-equation reflection fixes the zero's real part (T487–T494).

theorem zeta_zero_14_on_line_simple :
    ∃ z ∈ Metric.closedBall s14 (1/5),
      riemannZeta z = 0 ∧ z.re = (1/2 : ℝ) ∧ deriv riemannZeta z ≠ 0

What is certified. 494 ledger theorems over 18 Lean files in the default build, no sorry/axiom/admit, and no source claims left in the ledger. The older layers are also unconditional: ζ(1/2+5i) ≠ 0, the zero-free segment {1/2 + it : t ∈ [5, 5.000003]}, and the Euler–Maclaurin remainder at 0 < Re s for every K. The Taylor layer (T465–T486) proves the chain rule iteratedDeriv k (t ↦ f (s14 + t·w)) = f^{(k)}(s14 + t·w)·w^k, assembles sup|ζ^{(7)}| ≤ 43393 on the ball, and closes the Lagrange remainder at ≤ 24/1000; the resulting h2 uses 0.178 ≤ 0.188 of its budget. The on-line layer (T487–T494) adds the uniqueness and reflection steps below.

Where the value is.

  • Certification: this is the only dossier of the four whose headline is an unconditional statement about the zeros of ζ. #print axioms zeta_zero_14_on_line_simple shows only the three standard Lean axioms.
  • Location: the ball certificate alone is two-dimensional and only brackets Re z to [3/10, 7/10]. It is upgraded without any critical-strip evaluator: the Krawczyk inequality ‖1 − Yk·ζ′‖ ≤ 1/4 is a contraction, so the zero in the ball is unique; conjugation plus Mathlib's functional equation make the reflection s ↦ 1 − conj s preserve zeros; the ball is invariant under it; hence the unique zero is fixed by it and Re z = 1/2. The same inequality gives ζ′(z) ≠ 0. No partial-sum-plus-tail evaluator, no Γ, no θ.
  • Template: the end-to-end closure (Euler–Maclaurin value box → c_k boxes → Cauchy/Ψ₃ bounds → Taylor remainder → Krawczyk) is now a worked, reproducible pattern. A second zero costs a calibration, not a redesign.
  • Durability: the only correction in the final round was a bookkeeping constant (14/1000 → 24/1000, because the proved sup is 43393, not 25000), and it was made by restating, not by weakening a committed theorem.
The cap on its value. The conclusion is one zero, with Re z = 1/2 and |Im z − 14| ≤ 1/5; it is not a zero count, not a zero-free window, and not a statement about RH. The ordinate is bracketed, not pinned. As a P4 input it supplies a located, simple zero on the line — what remains for the explicit-formula zero side is the count in the window, which this certificate does not give.

Verdict. Worth one unconditional theorem about the zeros of ζ — existence, location on the line, and simplicity — plus a reusable certification template. Small in mathematical extent, real in kind. Class A stands, and it is the only dossier of the four whose headline concerns ζ itself.

4. partial-sum-plus-tail — Class B, an instrument

The claim. A certified evaluator: rational rectangles containing ζ(s), ζ(s,a) or L(s,χ), with every rational operation recorded in a straight-line certificate, replayed by an independent checker, and kernel-checked in Lean.

What is certified. 52 ledger theorems (T1–T52), 14 computations, 2 source claims, 14 tests, 13 Lean files. The proved core includes the interval algebra and certificate soundness (Expr.wf, Expr.wf_sound), truncation transport, the eta cos/sin split, kernel-checked enclosures of π, log, exp, sin and cos, the Euler–Maclaurin identity and explicit tail bound for every K ≥ 1, a closed rectangle enclosure for every Re s > 1 with arbitrary M, K and no atom hypothesis (ReplayGeneral.lean, T44–T49), and the target-width certificate format (R15, first half).

Where the value is.

  • Reuse: this is the only dossier whose product other dossiers actually consume. The zero certificate above is built on the same atom/interval pattern, and the geometric-DQPT dossier carries its own PartialSumTail.lean port.
  • Auditability: exact rationals and an independent replay checker make every later finite-scale claim reproducible; the layer is an antidote to floating point, not a numerical convenience.
  • Honest boundary: the letter records it as an instrument (F11) and its novelty as integration rather than invention (N7). That framing is itself part of the value: it prevents the layer from being oversold.
The cap on its value. Nothing about either wall uses it yet. The critical-strip wiring (0 < Re s ≤ 1) and a symbolic width bound are missing, and the evaluator is not a hypothesis in any theorem about a wall. The option is real but unexercised.

Verdict. The highest reuse value of the four and, so far, zero reach. Class B is the right class: worth an engine, not a theorem about ζ.

5. geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions — Class C, a map

The claim. A finite mean-zero filter applied to depth-one Euler sums produces finite atomic measures whose supports, in the limit, are conjecturally the zeros of the completed ξ-function. The source's own Corollary 12.3 is a conversion: supp μ = supp ν ⇔ RH, with the left-hand side open. The Davenport–Heilbronn twist is the sharpness test: the same construction reports a false Riemann hypothesis for a function with no Euler product, so the additive toolbox alone cannot prove RH.

What is certified. The dossier (108 theorem rows, T1–T108; 4 computations, 14 source claims, 4 open items, 55 tests, 12 Lean files) reconstructs the finite bookkeeping and the obstructions:

  • the filter has mean zero over every complete block; the quadratic link is exact;
  • last-chord twin identities, the σ ↔ 1−σ dichotomy, and the clock-zero classification of 1 − N^{1−s};
  • the corrected local diagonal (the source's original Lemma 6.3 was false and is repaired);
  • the Davenport–Heilbronn word is odd and mean-zero;
  • a finite Lee–Yang circle theorem;
  • no-go theorems: no entrywise-nonnegative or upper-triangular matrix product can represent the negative N = 2 polygon at s = −1; no positive-temperature register chain is ferromagnetic;
  • the Bourgain threshold 13/194, and a port of the sign-change/uniqueness and Turing-reduction logic for certified zeros.

Where the value is. It is the clearest statement of what a proof of RH would have to consume, and the no-go core is durable: those are theorems that rule out whole families of future attempts, which is the programme's characteristic product. The bundle's publication.html is the public artefact, with gaps.html, truths.md (ninety frozen truths) and Addendum A alongside it.

The cap on its value. Tightness, Rouché/Jensen localisation and the conversion itself are not formalised; hypothesis (M) is unproved; the two working notes in the bundle that claim a geometric proof of RH are withdrawn (their local-diagonal identity is false). The dossier formalises finite coefficients and finite atomic measures, not the limit theorem. Its distance to a theorem about ζ is therefore the largest of the four, and its value is conceptual and negative rather than existential.

Verdict. Worth a map plus a formalised set of obstructions, and worth keeping the withdrawn notes visible. Class C, with the highest conceptual value and the lowest theorem-per-page distance to the wall.

6. finite-explicit-formula — Class D, a component

The claim. Fix the tent test function f(x) = max(0, 1 − |x|) and certify the finite side of the explicit formula for its autocorrelation g = f * f (supported on [−2, 2], so only n with log n ≤ 2 contribute).

What is certified. 27 ledger theorems (T1–T27), 6 computations, 14 source claims, 2 tests, 2 Lean files. The proved core: the tent is non-negative and continuous; the autocorrelation is the cubic B-spline with g(0) = 2/3, g(1) = 1/6, g(3/2) = 1/48, g(2) = 0; ∫f = 1, ∫f² = 2/3, ∫g = 1; the convolution identity at the sample points and on |x| ≥ 2, [1, 2] and [−2, −1]; and — the load-bearing row — the full five-term finite prime side is negative, with its rational interval enclosure checked in Lean (PrimeSide.lean, T6), including the dominant n = 2 term (T5).

Where the value is. It is the only dossier that certifies a piece of the explicit formula's arithmetic side, and the prime-side negativity is exactly the kind of finite input a B5-style positivity computation needs. It is also cheap and well scoped: 27 theorems, two Lean files, and a ledger that separates the 14 source claims from the proved core.

The cap on its value. The analytic explicit formula, the archimedean term, the zero side and the general convolution identity are all open or recorded as source claims. P4 is not closed, and the dossier says so. Its value is contingent on three further layers it does not contain.

Verdict. Worth a certified negative prime side and an honest, small scaffold — value deferred, not value delivered. Class D.

7. The four together

The four dossiers account for 681 of the letter's 952 ledger theorems (72%), 35 of 393 computations, and 89 of 457 tests. That proportion is misleading as a measure of value: the single most valuable row of the 681 is zeta_zero_14, and the largest block (the geometric-DQPT dossier's 108 rows) is finite bookkeeping and no-go lemmas. Value is not proportional to rows.

The four Lean cores are separate namespaces with their own check.sh wrappers; they are not a single library. That is a practical cost for reuse, not a mathematical defect.

8. What would raise each value most

Ranked by expected value per unit of next effort, not by ambition:

  1. partial-sum-plus-tail — wire the critical strip. Extend the Re s > 1 rectangle enclosure to 0 < Re s ≤ 1 and add a symbolic width bound (R13). This is the step that lets the evaluator become a hypothesis in a theorem about a wall (R14), converting Class B into a bridge candidate. The analytic content is already proved; this is wiring and width arithmetic.
  2. finite-window-zeros — a count on the line. The location step is done (T492): the Krawczyk contraction plus the functional-equation reflection already put the zero on the line, and simplicity falls out. What remains is a count, which a single ball can never give. The logic for it — uniqueness from a sign change and a positive derivative, and the certified-set/window-count reduction (Turing) — is already ported and proved in the geometric-DQPT dossier (CertifiedZero.lean); it needs, as input, certified evaluations along the line, which is the critical-strip job of the partial-sum-plus-tail layer (item 1). Joining the two would turn Class A from one located zero into a zero-count certificate. It would still not touch RH.
  3. finite-explicit-formula — the archimedean term, then the zero side. A rigorous integral enclosure of the Γ′/Γ factor against g, plus the count and location just described, would make B5 a finite-window positivity statement. Still not RH; still worth having.
  4. geometric-dynamical-phase-transitions — formalise the conversion. Tightness, Rouché/Jensen localisation and the support equality are a large formalisation whose conclusion is an equivalence, not a proof; the open side would remain open. Highest cost, most conceptual payoff, least chance of changing the ledger's bottom line soon.

9. What this page does not say

  • It does not claim the Riemann hypothesis, twin primes, Chowla, Sarnak or Elliott, and none of the four dossiers does.
  • "Certified zero" now means a zero at 1/2 + 14i with Re z = 1/2 (T492), |Im z − 14| ≤ 1/5, and ζ′(z) ≠ 0. It is still not a zero count and not a zero-free statement.
  • The value classes are a judgement, not a measurement. The measured columns are ledger and test counts, and they drift: the geometric-DQPT dossier's ledger has 108 theorem rows while topics/STATUS.md still records 67 theorems and 47 computations for it. The dossier's own README notes that some rows are duplicates or guards (T36, T46 and T93 repeat η₂(−1) = −2; T56 is a structure projection; T63 is a trivial guard), so neither number is a count of independent facts.
  • No monetary, priority or citation value is implied. The page values certified content, reach, reuse and durability inside this programme, nothing else.
