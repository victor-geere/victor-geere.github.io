Continuous-Cone Solution for a Depth-of-Closure Bound

Geometric spectral framework for the Riemann Hypothesis

1. Continuous radius

Definition 1.1 (Continuous mean-zero filter). For real \(R\ge 2\) $$c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\bigl(m/R-k\bigr),$$ where \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) is a fixed non-negative bump with \(\int\varphi=1\). The coherent sum is $$\eta_R(R^2)(s)=\sum_{m\le R^2}c_m(R)m^{-s}.$$
Definition 1.2 (Residual function). $$\rho(R,s)=\lvert\eta_R(R^2)(s)\rvert.$$
Lemma 1.3 (\(C^1\) regularity). For each fixed \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s\in(0,1)\) the map \(R\mapsto\rho(R,s)\) is \(C^1\) on \([2,\infty)\). Differentiation under the sum is justified by the majorant $$\sum_{m\le R^2}\lvert\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})\rvert\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R.$$
The coefficients \(c_m(R)\) are smooth in \(R\). On any compact interval of radii the number of terms that change is \(O(R)\), and each \(\partial_R c_m\) is bounded by \(\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty+\lVert\varphi'\rVert_\infty\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the displayed majorant. Term-by-term differentiation is legitimate and the derivative is continuous.

2. Local quadratic form and differential inequality

Lemma 2.1 (Local quadratic form). On each short interval \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\) the local contribution satisfies $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta^{(k)}}A^{(k)})=\frac12 R^{-1}\lvert\eta^{(k)}\rvert^2+O\bigl(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon}(1+\lvert t\rvert)^\varepsilon\bigr),$$ where the constant \(1/2\) arises from $$\int_{-1}^{1}(1-R\varphi(u))(-\varphi(u))\,du=-\frac12+O(R^{-1})$$ after the change of variables \(u=(m-kR)/R\) and integration by parts on the \(\varphi'\) term (boundary terms vanish by compact support of \(\varphi\)).
The change of variables converts the local sum into a Riemann-sum approximation. The mesh-size error is \(O(R^{-1})\) times the local mass. The phase expansion produces a secondary contribution of size \(O(\lvert t\rvert/k)\). Summing the geometric series over \(k\) and applying hybrid convexity absorbs these errors. The leading constant evaluates as claimed because \(\int\varphi=1\) and the mean-zero condition cancels the linear term in \(R\).
Theorem 2.2 (Differential inequality). On the critical line there exist absolute constants \(c>0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial R}(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le -c R^{-1/2}\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)+O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)^\varepsilon\bigr)$$ for every critical-line ordinate \(\gamma\).
Sum the local contributions of Lemma 2.1 together with the off-diagonal terms controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem. The leading negative term produces the factor \(-c R^{-1/2}\rho\). The absolute error is of size \(O(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)^\varepsilon)\).

3. Residual mass and tightness

Definition 3.1 (Continuous residual measure). $$\mu_R(dt)=\frac{\rho(R,1/2+it)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$
Lemma 3.2 (Residual mass). $$\lVert\mu_R\rVert=O(R^{-1})$$ uniformly for \(R\ge 2\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz against the weight \((1+t^2)^{-1}\), $$\Bigl(\int\rho(R,1/2+it)(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int\lvert\eta_R\rvert^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The mean-square integral is \(O(R)\) by the continuous analogue of the Montgomery–Vaughan bound. Hence \(\lVert\mu_R\rVert\ll R^{-1/2}\). Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent to \(O(R^{-1})\).
Theorem 3.3 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_R\}_{R\ge 2}\) is tight.
The residual-mass bound of Lemma 3.2 supplies uniform control of the tails. Prokhorov’s theorem yields tightness.
Lemma 3.4 (Residual-measure tails). For every \(\alpha>0\), $$\mu_R(\{\lvert t\rvert>R^\alpha\})=O(R^{-\alpha/2}).$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz, $$\mu_R(\{\lvert t\rvert>R^\alpha\})\le R^{-\alpha}\int\frac{\rho(R,1/2+it)}{1+\lvert t\rvert}\,dt\ll R^{-\alpha}\lVert\mu_R\rVert^{1/2}\Bigl(\int\frac{\lvert\eta_R\rvert^2}{1+t^2}\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$ The second-moment integral is \(O(R)\) and \(\lVert\mu_R\rVert=O(R^{-1})\), so the claimed bound follows.

4. Off the critical line

Theorem 4.1 (Off-line lower bound). When \(\operatorname{Re}s=\sigma\neq 1/2\) the residual satisfies $$\rho(R,s)\gg R^{\lvert 1-2\sigma\rvert/2-\varepsilon}$$ for all sufficiently large \(R\), and therefore cannot tend to zero.

5. Main theorem

Theorem 5.1 (Geometric realisation of the Riemann hypothesis). The continuous-radius differential inequality yields a power-saving bound $$\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\eta}$$ for each fixed critical ordinate \(\gamma\), with \(\log C_\gamma\ll\log(2+\lvert\gamma\rvert)\) and absolute \(\eta>0\). Combined with the residual-measure tail control of Lemma 3.4, every weak-star limit point of the family \(\{\mu_R\}_{R\ge 2}\) is a tempered pure-point measure supported exactly on the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of \(\Xi\) that lie on the critical line. Consequently every non-trivial zero of \(\zeta\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
Tightness is Theorem 3.3. Vanishing of continuous spectrum follows from \(\lVert\mu_R\rVert\to 0\). Spurious atoms are excluded by the off-line lower bound of Theorem 4.1. For any fixed absolute \(\alpha\in(0,\eta)\) the depth-of-closure bound holds uniformly on the compact range \(\lvert t\rvert\le R^\alpha\) once \(R\) is larger than an absolute constant; the complementary tail vanishes by Lemma 3.4. Therefore every critical-line zero appears as an atom in the limit.
