The prime-spectrum tensor

A pairing on \(\mathbb{R}\) that replaces the Riemann hypothesis as a question about \(\xi\)

Victor Geere · 20 August 2026

This note is not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, and it is not a frozen truth. The functions \(\zeta\), \(\eta\), and \(\xi\) are generating functions. The strip, the off-line zeros, the Jensen tower of \(\Phi\), the hyperbolic-cosine tilt, Li’s jet at \(s=1\), and the right-half atoms of \(\nu\) are what the same relationship looks like after it has been Laplace-transformed into \(\mathbb{C}\). The relationship itself is a 2-tensor on the additive group \(\mathbb{R}\): the completed prime current is of positive type. Its Bochner spectrum is a positive measure on \(\mathbb{R}\). That measure is the zeros. They are real because the group is \(\mathbb{R}\).

1. Introspection: the tensors that actually appear

Every object in this programme is a kernel, a Gram form, or a current. Rank and the group it lives on decide whether it is the relationship between primes and zeros, or a generating function of that relationship.

  1. Mellin kernel \(x^{-s}\) on \(\mathbb{R}_+\times\mathbb{C}\). This is how \(\zeta\) and \(-\zeta'/\zeta\) are written. The second factor is already \(\mathbb{C}\).
  2. Cauchy kernel \((s-\rho)^{-1}\) on \(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\). Logarithmic derivatives, Hadamard products, Rouché. Poles in a strip.
  3. Fourier kernel \(e^{iu\xi}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\). Unitary. No strip. This is the kernel that does not leave the group on which the primes actually sit after \(u=\log n\).
  4. Weil 2-tensor. The quadratic form \(f\mapsto\langle\mathcal{P},f*\check f\rangle\) on a cone of even test functions. Positive-semidefiniteness of this form is the classical positivity criterion, rewritten as a Gram tensor on \(\mathbb{R}\).
  5. Hankel tensors of \(\Phi\). \(H_{k\ell}=m_{k+\ell}\) with \(m_{2k}=\int\Phi(u)u^{2k}\,du\). The Jensen tower of truths.md §15. A dead end of the cosine picture: hyperbolicity of every degree is the Riemann hypothesis again, after the Laplace transform has been taken.
  6. Last-chord pairing. \(H_N(s)H_N(1-s)=(N-1)^2/(4N^2)\). A 2-tensor remnant of evenness, not of positive type. Frozen in truths.md §11.
  7. Discrete Gram tensor of the filter. Lemma 7.2 of publication.html: the kernel \((mn)^{-1/2}\min(m,n)/\max(m,n)\) against the mean-zero coefficients \(c_m^{(N)}\). Positive-definite by Parseval, because it is the Fourier transform of \((1+t^2)^{-1}\). It is the Weil tensor of the integers after a mean-zero filter, not of the primes.
  8. Marty metric. \(2\lvert dw\rvert/(1+\lvert w\rvert^2)\) on the Riemann sphere. Needed only after the relationship has been sent to \(\mathbb{C}\) and compactified. On \(\mathbb{R}\) it is not used.
  9. \(n\)-point tensors. \(\mathcal{P}^{\otimes n}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) and its Fourier dual \(\mathrm{Z}^{\otimes n}\). For \(n=2\) this is pair correlation against Hardy–Littlewood. Higher \(n\) is determinantal structure. The Riemann hypothesis is only the \(n=1\) support statement.

The dead ends of the last two notes — the cosine moment \(K(\alpha,\gamma)\), the Jensen jet, Li at \(\sigma=1\), right-half \(\nu\)-atoms — all live in (1), (2), (5), and (8). They are accurate pictures of an object that has already been moved off \(\mathbb{R}\). The replacement below stays with (3), (4), and (7).

2. Packaging

\(\zeta(s)=\sum n^{-s}\) is the Mellin generating function of the constant sequence \(1\). The logarithmic derivative \(-\zeta'/\zeta(s)=\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-s}\) is the Mellin generating function of von Mangoldt’s weights. The factor \(1-2^{1-s}\) that produces \(\eta\) is an Euler factor at \(2\). The factor \(\tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\) that produces \(\xi\) is the Poisson summation of the Gaussian, written in the Mellin coordinate, times a polynomial that kills the pole. None of these functions is the relationship between zeros and primes. Each is a Laplace transform of that relationship, taken so that Cauchy’s theorem becomes available. The complex plane is an artefact of wanting poles.

The primes sit in \(\mathbb{R}_+\), or on \(\mathbb{R}\) after \(u=\log n\). A unitary spectrum sits on \(\mathbb{R}\) as frequencies. Off-line zeros are not points of that group. They are Laplace modes \(e^{\alpha u}e^{i\gamma u}\) with \(\alpha\neq 0\), visible only after test functions have been tilted.

3. Data on \(\mathbb{R}\)

Let \(f\) run through the Weil cone: even functions on \(\mathbb{R}\) whose Fourier transforms are holomorphic in a strip about the real axis and of rapid decay on verticals (the classical Weil class; not all of Schwartz, because the prime current grows like \(e^{u/2}\,du\)). Write \(\check f(u)=f(-u)\).

Definition 3.1 (Prime current, archimedean current).
\[ \Pi(f)=\sum_{n\ge 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,f(\log n),\qquad \Theta(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(u)\,f(u)\,du, \] where \(\Phi\) is Riemann’s even positive Jacobi density (truths.md 12.1). Set \(\mathcal{P}=\Pi+\Theta\).

The weight \(n^{-1/2}\) is not the Riemann hypothesis. It is the self-dual normalisation of Poisson summation for the Gaussian, the same scale as the quadratic truncation \(M=N^2\) in the polygonal paper (truths.md 12.7). The critical line, as a coordinate, is already in \(\Theta\). What is not already in \(\Theta\) is whether the spectrum of \(\mathcal{P}\) is a measure on that line.

4. The equation

Defining equation.
\[ \bigl\langle\mathcal{P},\, f*\check f\bigr\rangle \;\ge\; 0 \qquad\text{for every }f\text{ in the Weil cone.} \tag{\(\star\)} \] Equivalently, the translation-invariant 2-tensor \(K(u,v)=\mathcal{P}(u-v)\) is positive semi-definite on that cone.

If \((\star)\) holds, Bochner’s theorem on the cone supplies a unique even tempered positive measure \(\mathrm{Z}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) with \(\langle\mathcal{P},f\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Z},\hat f\rangle\). The atoms of \(\mathrm{Z}\) are frequencies \(\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\). That measure is the zeros. They are real because they are a measure on \(\mathbb{R}\).

Definition 4.1 (Prime spectrum).
The prime spectrum is the Bochner measure \(\mathrm{Z}\) of \(\mathcal{P}\), when \((\star)\) holds. No entire function is named.

This replaces the Riemann hypothesis as a question about \(\xi\). It replaces the need to locate poles of a meromorphic function in a strip. It replaces \(\eta\) and \(\zeta\) as primary objects: they are Mellin transforms of the constant sequence and of \(\Lambda\), which are how one writes \(\Pi\) if one insists on \(\mathbb{C}\). The zeros–primes relationship is \((\star)\).

The same equation in kernel language, without Bochner’s name: for all finite real tuples \((u_j)\) and complex coefficients \((a_j)\), \[ \sum_{j,k}a_j\overline{a}_k\,\mathcal{P}(u_j-u_k)\;\ge\;0, \] whenever the pairing is defined. That is the 2-tensor.

5. What the dead ends were

An off-line zero of \(\xi\) is a term \(x^{\sigma+i\gamma}=e^{\sigma u}e^{i\gamma u}\) in the explicit formula with \(\sigma\neq\tfrac12\). That is not a Fourier mode on \(\mathbb{R}\). It becomes visible only after the test function is tilted, \(f(u)\mapsto f(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\) with \(\alpha=\sigma-\tfrac12\). The integral \[ K(\alpha,\gamma)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\cos(\gamma u)\,du \] from the further-research footnote is exactly \(\Theta\) evaluated on a tilted cosine — the Cauchy picture of a failure of \((\star)\). The Jensen tower and Li’s jet are the Taylor expansions of that tilt at \(t=0\) and at \(s=1\). The right-half atoms of \(\nu\) are the same failure, sampled by a Dirichlet polynomial. All of them are accurate. All of them have already left \(\mathbb{R}\).

The \(\gamma=0\) slice \(K(\alpha,0)>0\) (truths.md 15.1) is \((\star)\) on the constants, which is the trivial Fourier mode. Positivity of \(\Phi\) solves that slice because \(\Theta\) is already a positive density. The mixed slice is \((\star)\) on oscillating test functions, where \(\Pi\) and \(\Theta\) must cancel in a way that does not introduce Laplace modes. That is the whole remaining statement, written on the group where it is native.

6. What the polygonal paper already has

Lemma 7.2 is a positive 2-tensor, \[ \sum_{m,n\le N^2}c_m^{(N)}\overline{c}_n^{(N)}\,(mn)^{-1/2}\frac{\min(m,n)}{\max(m,n)}\;\ge\;0, \] because the kernel \(\exp\bigl(-\lvert\log(m/n)\rvert\bigr)\) is the Fourier transform of \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) and the left-hand side is \(\pi^{-1}\int\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\tfrac12+it)\rvert^2(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\). This is \((\star)\) for the mean-zero integer current, not for \(\Pi\). The integers with weights \(c_m^{(N)}\) are not the primes with weights \(\Lambda(n)\). The last-chord product tending to \(\tfrac14\) is evenness of a single monomial, which is the functional equation, not positive type.

The geometric construction therefore already knows the shape of the 2-tensor and already knows it is positive for a filtered copy of the integers. The replacement of the Riemann hypothesis is the same positivity after Möbius inversion to \(\Lambda\), plus the archimedean density \(\Phi\). Passing from \(c_m^{(N)}\) to \(\Lambda\) is not a lemma of Euler–Maclaurin. It is \((\star)\).

7. Higher tensors

Fourier duality on \(\mathbb{R}\) lifts: \[ \mathcal{P}^{\otimes n}\;\xrightarrow{\;\mathcal{F}^{\otimes n}\;}\;\mathrm{Z}^{\otimes n} \quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^n. \] For \(n=1\), \(\mathrm{Z}\) is a measure on \(\mathbb{R}\) — that is \((\star)\). For \(n=2\), the off-diagonal of \(\mathrm{Z}^{\otimes 2}\) is pair correlation, dual to the Hardy–Littlewood 2-tensor of \(\Pi\). The sine kernel and GUE are statements about this 2-tensor, not about \(\xi\). They are not required to replace the Riemann hypothesis. They are what the relationship looks like once \(n=1\) is a measure on \(\mathbb{R}\) and one asks for independence of the atoms.

8. What this does not do

Equation \((\star)\) is not proved here. Defining the zeros as the Bochner spectrum of \(\mathcal{P}\) does not identify that spectrum with the poles of the meromorphic continuation of \(\sum\Lambda(n)n^{-s}\) unless \((\star)\) holds. If \((\star)\) fails, the Fourier transform of \(\mathcal{P}\) is not a measure, and the Laplace picture in \(\mathbb{C}\) will show poles off the line. The equation replaces the need to talk about those poles. It does not prove they are absent.

Further research, restated. A proof, had it existed in the polygonal paper, would have been a proof that \(\int\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\cos(\gamma u)\,du\) cannot vanish for \(\alpha\neq 0\). Writing that sentence as a theorem would be lying. The adjacent truths define the object; they do not force \(\Phi\) to be in the class that kills non-real zeros. In the language of this note, that sentence is \((\star)\) on tilted test functions. The untilted form \((\star)\) is the same sentence, on \(\mathbb{R}\), without \(\xi\).
