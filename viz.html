Coordinates

Publication Definition 3.1: the polygonal path lives in \(\mathbb{C}\). The height of \(s=\sigma+it\) is the remaining axis. World coordinates are therefore

\[ x=\operatorname{Re}\eta,\qquad y=t=\operatorname{Im}s,\qquad z=\operatorname{Im}\eta. \]

Ticks at \(0\), \(0.5\) and \(1\) sit on all three axes. The first term \(1^{-s}=1\) is at \(\operatorname{Re}=1\). A non-trivial zero of \(\zeta\) (hence of \(\eta\) and of \(\xi\)) sends \(\eta(s)\) to the origin of \(\mathbb{C}\), so the endpoint lies on the \(t\)-axis at that height — that is what “sits at zero” means. The slider \(\sigma=\operatorname{Re}s\) is the strip abscissa, not a spatial axis; the critical line is \(\sigma=\tfrac12\). The nested globe is the unit-diameter fibre in this same \(\mathbb{C}\): hinged at \(0\), centre at \(\tfrac12\), far pole at \(1\), translated to the current height \(t\).

What is drawn

Each translucent globe is a three-sphere fibre, drawn as an \(S^2\subset\mathbb{R}^3\) stand-in for the Hopf fibre \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow S^{11}\to\mathbb{HP}^2\) of nested_spheres_RH.html, Definition 2.1. The polygons slider sets how many term-polygons are drawn. Index \(n=0,1,\dots\) is 0-based, so the \(n\)th polygon has \(n+1\) vertices (Davenport–Heilbronn rotations through the \((n+1)\)-st roots of unity, including the trivial root \(1\)): term \(n=0\) is \(1^{-s}=1\), a single point at \(1+0i\) with its \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) orbit (the unit great circle) running through it; \(n=1\) is a \(2\)-gon (a line, rotation \(\pi\)) likewise carrying its orbit; \(n=2\) a triangle, and so on. The sphere is a book hinged at the origin (the spine is the imaginary axis through \(0\)). Each equatorial disk passes through \(0\); the first term’s centre is \(1+0i\). At \(90^\circ\) the two covers form an L at the origin; at \(180^\circ\) they lie flat, equators on the real line, tangent at \(0\) — a figure 8 from above. The globe is a ball of diameter \(1\), hinged at the origin, centre at \(0.5\). Term \(0\) (the \(1\)-gon) sits at \(1+0i\), the far pole opposite the hinge. Every later \(n\)-gon is chords of a concentric circle about that same centre, with radius \(0.5\,n^{-\sigma}\) (later terms smaller). Pages fan equally in angle between the covers. Sphere-mesh (off by default) is the wireframe; nested \(S_N\) keeps the translucent shells. The nested-spheres note writes the rotations as \(2\pi k/N\) for \(k=1,\dots,N-1\) — the non-trivial \(N\)th roots used in the mean-zero filter. Including \(k=0\) recovers the vertex at \(1\) and a complete \(N\)-gon. Later terms sit on smaller shells. The unwrapped phase \(\lvert t\rvert\log n\) grows, but that is not the size of the term. The term \(n^{-s}\) has modulus \(n^{-\sigma}\) (the step length in the polygonal path) and consecutive-phase \(\Delta\arg=-t\log(1+1/n)\approx -t/n\), both decreasing in \(n\); the Davenport–Heilbronn turn is \(2\pi/n\), also decreasing. The nested-spheres note scales fibre radii by \(N^{-1}\). Here \(r_n=\lvert n^{-s}\rvert=n^{-\sigma}\), so \(n=1\) is the point at \(1+0i\) on the unit circle, and later \(n\)-gons nest inside it. The polygon is still rotated by \(\arg(n^{-s})=-t\log n\).

Dirichlet \(\eta\)-path

The red polyline is Dirichlet eta, not zeta and not the paper’s mean-zero filter \(\eta_N^{(N^2)}\). With truncation \(M=N^2\),

\[ \eta_M(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}(-1)^{m-1}\,m^{-s}. \]

Vertex \(n\) is the partial sum through term \(n\): it starts at the first term \(1^{-s}=1+0i\) (gold), odd terms are added, even terms are subtracted (the \(+-\,+-\) Dirichlet signs). The identity \(\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)\) holds wherever the series make sense, and \(1-2^{1-s}\neq 0\) at the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta\) (equivalently of \(\xi\)), so those zeros are zeros of \(\eta\). The partial-sum polyline is drawn in the plane of the current height \(t\), starting at \((1,t,0)\) and ending at \((\operatorname{Re}\eta_M,\,t,\,\operatorname{Im}\eta_M)\). At a zero that endpoint is the origin of \(\mathbb{C}\), i.e. the point \((0,t,0)\) on the \(t\)-axis. The cyan curve is the same endpoint as a function of height, \(u\mapsto\eta_M(\sigma+iu)\), so it meets the \(t\)-axis at the zeros. Off a zero both miss the axis. Default \(t\approx 14.13\) is the first critical-line ordinate, a convenient check, not a proof of RH.

Hausdorff distance, not Hausdorff dimension

The tweet that suggested the look interpolates Hausdorff dimension from a globe \(G_n\) to \(G_{n+1}\) by a Cantor contraction. The paper uses a different Hausdorff quantity: the Hausdorff distance on the fibre between two compact sets \(A,B\),

\[ d_H(A,B)=\max\Bigl\{\sup_{a\in A}\mathrm{dist}(a,B),\;\sup_{b\in B}\mathrm{dist}(b,A)\Bigr\}. \]
Nested-spheres Lemma 3.2 / publication Lemma 4.3.3: \(d_H(\text{\(N\)-gon},\,\text{complete curve})=O(N^{-1})\) uniformly in the height \(t\), because each edge subtends an angle \(O(N^{-1})\) on a fibre of radius \(O(1)\). Here the \(n\)th term-polygon is the regular \((n+1)\)-gon of \((n+1)\)st roots of unity, so the same bound reads \(O(1/(n+1))\) on that shell.

The cyan tube is that \(O(N^{-1})\) neighbourhood. If the polygon lies in the tube, the sampled path has not left the complete curve by more than the lemma allows. For a regular \(N\)-gon inscribed in a circle of radius \(R\) the exact distance is the sagitta \(R\bigl(1-\cos(\pi/N)\bigr)\), which is \(O(N^{-2})\); the paper quotes the coarser angular bound \(O(N^{-1})\).

Nested spheres themselves meet at the origin as \(N\to\infty\). That intersection is a point (Hausdorff dimension \(0\)), while each fibre curve has dimension \(1\). Dimension of the limit set is not used in the theorems; distance of approximating curves is.

Hilbert zoom

Publication Lemma 4.3.4: granularity \(N^{-1}\) is the Hilbert cell size; the tightness window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) is the unit interval; the canonical rescaling is the DQPT depth \(D_N=N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\). The slider scales the nested family the way Hilbert polygons zoom toward a point of convergence. Collapse of the zoomed endpoint to the origin is an envelope path-closure. Identity L is a lower bound on the speed of that collapse off the critical line, not on \(\lvert\eta\rvert\); Euler–Maclaurin still predicts extra envelope collapses in the band \(\sigma\in(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)\).

Hilbert zoom scales the nested \(S_N\) fibres about the origin of \(\mathbb{C}\) at the current \(t\). The red path and the cyan height curve stay in unscaled \(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\), so that \(0\), \(0.5\) and \(1\) do not move.
