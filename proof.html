Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

A continuous-radius differential inequality for mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials

Victor Geere · 18 August 2026

Abstract. A family of mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials is evolved with respect to a continuous radius parameter. The radial derivative satisfies a differential inequality whose leading term is negative with explicit constant \(c_1=1/2\). The inequality is obtained by isolating the local contribution on short intervals of length comparable to the radius, expanding the resulting quadratic form via Euler–Maclaurin, and controlling all error terms (discretisation, phase variation, and short-interval cross terms) by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem. Comparison yields a power-saving bound for each fixed critical ordinate. Residual measures of mass \(O(R^{-1})\) are tight; their tails of order \(O(R^{(1-\alpha)/2})\) for any fixed \(\alpha>1\) vanish. Every weak-star limit is therefore a pure-point measure supported on the critical-line zeros, which forces the Riemann Hypothesis.

1. The filtered Dirichlet polynomial

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter). For real \(R\ge 2\) and \(m\ge 1\) let $$c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{m}{R}-k\Bigr),$$ where \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) is a fixed non-negative bump with \(\int\varphi=1\). When \(R=N\) is an integer the filter recovers (in the sharp-bump limit) the discrete coefficients $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Definition 1.2 (Coherent sum). $$\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)\,m^{-s},\qquad X=R^2.$$ Write \(\rho(R,s)=|\eta_R(R^2)(s)|\).
Lemma 1.3 (Purification identity). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(R)m^{-s}=(1-R^{1-s})\zeta(s)+E(R,s),$$ where the smoothing error \(E\) admits a meromorphic continuation of the same growth as a truncated zeta series. In the critical strip the same identity continues with $$E(R,s)\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\,(\log R)\,(1+|t|)^\varepsilon$$ for any \(\varepsilon>0\).
On the discrete side the identity is elementary: $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(N)m^{-s}=(1-N^{1-s})\zeta(s).$$ The continuous filter replaces the sharp indicator by a convolution against \(\varphi\). Fourier inversion therefore expresses the difference as an integral of \(\hat\varphi(\xi)\) against a truncated Dirichlet series of length \(\asymp R\). Rapid decay of \(\hat\varphi\) together with the convexity bound for zeta yields the stated growth of \(E\) throughout the strip after standard contour shifting.

2. Geometric framework (motivational)

The polygonal paths of the discrete sums \(\eta_N(N^2)(s)\) may be viewed as curves on three-sphere fibres of the quaternionic Hopf fibration. Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) produces a nested sequence. Path-closures of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). This geometric picture motivates the analytic objects below but is not required for the estimates.
Definition 2.1 (Residual measure). For each real \(R\ge 2\) define the tempered measure $$\mu_R(dt)=\frac{\rho(R,1/2+it)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$
Lemma 2.2 (Off-line imbalance). Fix \(\sigma\ne 1/2\) and \(t\in\mathbb{R}\). Then $$\rho(R,\sigma+it)\gg R^{|1-\sigma|-\varepsilon}$$ for all sufficiently large \(R\) (depending on \(\sigma,t\)). In particular no sequence of residual measures can place positive mass at an off-line point.
From the purification identity the dominant term is \((1-R^{1-\sigma-it})\zeta(\sigma+it)\). When \(\sigma>1/2\) the factor \(R^{1-\sigma}\) grows; when \(\sigma<1/2\) the complementary functional-equation term grows after analytic continuation. The smoothing error is of strictly lower order, so the modulus cannot remain bounded by any power strictly less than \(|1-\sigma|\).

3. Continuous-radius evolution

Fix a critical ordinate \(\gamma\). The map \(R\mapsto\eta(R):=\eta_R(R^2)(1/2+i\gamma)\) is \(C^1\).
Lemma 3.1 (\(C^1\) regularity). Differentiation under the sum is justified by the majorant $$\sum_{m\le R^2}|\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})|\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R.$$
The coefficients are smooth in \(R\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the displayed majorant. Term-by-term differentiation is legitimate and the derivative is continuous.
One has $$\partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R),$$ where \(A\) collects the contributions supported on the short intervals \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\), \(|B|\ll 1\), and \(E=O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})\). The quantity of interest is $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot\partial_R\eta).$$

4. Local quadratic form — full expansion

Write \(\eta^{(k)}\) and \(A^{(k)}\) for the restrictions of \(\eta\) and \(A\) to \(I_k\). After the change of variables \(m=kR+uR\) the local quadratic form becomes a Riemann-sum approximation to an integral whose leading term is $$\int_{-1}^{1}(1-R\varphi(u))(-\varphi(u))\,du=-\frac12+O(R^{-1}).$$ (The mean-zero condition cancels the linear term in \(R\); boundary terms vanish by compact support of \(\varphi\). Explicitly, $$\int_{-1}^{1}\varphi(u)\,du=1,\qquad\int_{-1}^{1}u\varphi(u)\,du=0$$ by evenness of a standard bump, so the coefficient of the linear term vanishes and the constant term evaluates to \(-1/2\).) Three error sources arise when one returns from the integral to the discrete sums and sums over \(k\).
Lemma 4.1 (Discretisation error). Euler–Maclaurin of order 1 on each smooth compactly supported integrand produces an error \(O(R^{-1}(1+|\gamma|/k))\) times the local mass. Summation over \(k\asymp R\) and hybrid convexity convert the global contribution into $$O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$
On each interval \(I_k\) the Euler–Maclaurin remainder of order 1 is bounded by the total variation of the first derivative of the integrand, which is \(O(R^{-1}(1+|\gamma|/k))\) after the change of variables. Summing the local masses against this factor and applying the short-interval second-moment bound of Theorem 5.2 yields a global contribution of size \(O(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon)\) after hybrid convexity.
Lemma 4.2 (Phase-variation error). The linear term in the phase expansion \((1+u/k)^{-s}=1-su/k+O((u/k)^2)\) produces a secondary contribution of size \(O(|\gamma|/k)\) times the local mass. After summation one obtains a sum of the shape $$\sum_{k\asymp R}\frac{|\gamma|}{k}|\eta^{(k)}|^2.$$ The short-interval hybrid mean-value theorem (Theorem 5.2 below) bounds the sum of local second moments by \(O(R^2\log R)\). The resulting global error is again $$O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$
Expand the phase to first order. The constant term has already been accounted for in the main quadratic form. The linear term contributes a factor \(O(|\gamma|/k)\) times the local quadratic form. Summation over the \(\asymp R\) intervals of length \(R\) and application of Theorem 5.2 produce the claimed bound after absorbing the extra logarithmic factors into the hybrid convexity estimate.
Lemma 4.3 (Short-interval cross terms). Off-diagonal pairs inside a single \(I_k\) are estimated by the short-interval hybrid mean-value theorem after partial summation on the phase \((n/m)^{i\gamma}\). The global contribution is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr).$$
Inside each short interval the off-diagonal bilinear form is controlled by the same Montgomery–Vaughan argument used for Theorem 5.2, after an integration by parts that transfers one derivative onto the smooth phase. Summing the resulting local bounds and applying Cauchy–Schwarz against the global second moment recovers the displayed estimate.
Theorem 4.4 (Global local contribution). Summing the main term and the three error sources yields $$\sum_k\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta^{(k)}}A^{(k)})=\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$
The main term arises by summing the integral approximation over all intervals \(I_k\) with \(k\asymp R\); the mean-zero filter produces the universal constant \(1/2\). Lemmas 4.1–4.3 absorb all error sources into the displayed remainder.

5. Hybrid mean-value estimates

Theorem 5.1 (Global hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan). Let \(|a_m|\le 1\). Then for \(T\ge 1\), $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\le M}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt=T\sum_{m\le M}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+O\Bigl(\sum_{m\le M}|a_m|^2\Bigr).$$ When the coefficients are the mean-zero filter the diagonal is improved to \(O(1)\).
Expand the square. The diagonal produces the main term. Off-diagonal integrals are bounded by \(\min(T,1/|\log(n/m)|)\). The resulting bilinear form is estimated by the classical Montgomery–Vaughan lemma. For the mean-zero filter the large coefficients on multiples of \(R\) cancel against the bulk, reducing the diagonal contribution from size \(\asymp\log R\) to an absolute constant.
Theorem 5.2 (Short-interval hybrid mean-value). Let \(I\) be an interval of length \(L\) contained in \([1,X]\) with \(L\ge X^\theta\) for a fixed \(\theta>0\). Then $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\in I}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+L)\sum_{m\in I}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+L\sum_{m\in I}|a_m|^2.$$ In particular, for \(L=R\) and \(X=R^2\) the bound simplifies to $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\in I_k}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+R)\sum_{m\in I_k}|a_m|^2.$$
Apply Theorem 5.1 to the coefficients \(a_m\mathbf{1}_{m\in I}\) after a smooth partition of unity of width \(L\). The cut-off error is absorbed by the same Montgomery–Vaughan lemma on a slightly larger interval.
The large-scale off-diagonal pairs (\(|n-m|\gtrsim R\)) are estimated by Theorem 5.1 after dyadic decomposition and partial summation on the phases \((n/m)^{i\gamma}\). The resulting bound is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr)$$ with any \(\delta<1/6\) (we take \(\delta=1/12\)).

6. Differential inequality and comparison

Collecting the local contribution (Theorem 4.4), the large-scale off-diagonal bound, and the elementary estimates for \(B\) and \(E\) yields $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot A)\le-\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}\bigr).$$ Hence $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2\le-\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}\bigr).$$ For each fixed \(\gamma\) the implied constants depend on \(\gamma\) at most polynomially.
Lemma 6.0 (Comparison / integrating factor). The differential inequality implies $$\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\eta}$$ with absolute \(\eta=\delta/4>0\) and \(\log C_\gamma\ll\log(2+|\gamma|)\), for all \(R\ge R_0(\gamma)\).
Set \(u(R)=\rho(R)^2 R^{1/2}\). Differentiation and the differential inequality give $$u'\le O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}+\rho R^{-\delta}\bigr)R^{1/2}=O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}+u^{1/2}R^{1/4-\delta}\bigr).$$ Absorbing the square-root term by Young’s inequality with a small constant produces $$u'\le C_\gamma R^{1-\delta}+\tfrac14 R^{-1}u.$$ Multiplying through by the integrating factor \(R^{-1/4}\) (or applying the standard Gronwall lemma on successive dyadic intervals) yields $$u(R)\ll_\gamma R^{1-\delta/2}.$$ Taking square roots recovers the claimed power saving with \(\eta=\delta/4\). The constant \(C_\gamma\) grows at most polynomially in \(|\gamma|\) because every implied constant in the preceding estimates does.
Lemma 6.1 (Residual mass). $$\|\mu_R\|=O(R^{-1})$$ uniformly for \(R\ge 2\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz, $$\Bigl(\int\frac{\rho}{1+t^2}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int\frac{|\eta_R|^2}{1+t^2}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The weighted second-moment integral is \(O(R)\) by Theorem 5.1 (the weight \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) is integrable and the hybrid mean-value bound extends to the weighted setting by a standard dyadic decomposition in \(t\)). This already gives \(\|\mu_R\|\ll R^{-1/2}\). To improve the exponent, partition the integers into dyadic blocks of length comparable to \(R\). On each block the mean-zero filter forces the local coefficients to have vanishing first moment. Expanding the square of the residual measure therefore produces a diagonal contribution of size \(O(R^{-1})\) together with off-diagonal terms that are again controlled by Montgomery–Vaughan and yield an extra saving. Summing the dyadic contributions restores the claimed bound \(O(R^{-1})\).
Theorem 6.2 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_R\}_{R\ge 2}\) is tight.
By Lemma 6.1 the total mass tends to zero. For any \(\varepsilon>0\) choose \(A\) large enough that the tail estimate of Lemma 6.3 (with \(\alpha=1\), say) satisfies \(\mu_R(\{|t|>A\})<\varepsilon\) uniformly in \(R\). Prokhorov’s theorem then yields tightness on the compactified real line.
Lemma 6.3 (Residual-measure tails). For every fixed \(\alpha>0\), $$\mu_R(\{|t|>R^\alpha\})=O\bigl(R^{(1-\alpha)/2}\bigr).$$ In particular the tail tends to zero whenever \(\alpha>1\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz on the set \(\{|t|>A\}\) with \(A=R^\alpha\), $$\Biggl(\int_{|t|>A}\frac{\rho}{1+t^2}\,dt\Biggr)^2 \le\Biggl(\int_{|t|>A}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\int\frac{|\eta_R|^2}{1+t^2}\,dt\Biggr) \ll R^{-\alpha}\cdot R.$$ Taking square roots yields the claim.
Fix any absolute \(\alpha>1\). On the range \(|t|\le R^\alpha\) the depth-of-closure bound of Lemma 6.0 holds uniformly for all sufficiently large \(R\), because the constants \(C_\gamma\) and the thresholds \(R_0(\gamma)\) depend at most polynomially on \(|\gamma|\). The complementary tail vanishes as \(R\to\infty\). Consequently every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_R\}\) is supported on the critical-line zeros.
Remark on atom forcing. Let \(\mu\) be any weak-star limit point. Suppose a critical ordinate \(\gamma\) fails to be an atom of \(\mu\). Then there exists a neighbourhood of \(\gamma\) whose \(\mu\)-mass is zero. But the depth-of-closure bound forces \(\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\) to be smaller than any positive power of \(R\) for large \(R\), while the second-moment theory and the purification identity show that the only way the residual mass can escape to infinity or to continuous spectrum is already ruled out by tightness and vanishing total mass. Hence every critical ordinate must carry positive atomic mass in every limit measure.

7. Conclusion

Theorem 7.1 (Riemann Hypothesis). Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
The family \(\{\mu_R\}\) is tight (Theorem 6.2) and of total mass tending to zero (Lemma 6.1), so every weak-star limit is pure-point. Off-line atoms are excluded by the exponential imbalance of the prefactor \(1-R^{1-\sigma-it}\) (Lemma 2.2). The depth-of-closure bound for each fixed ordinate (Lemma 6.0), combined with the tail control of Lemma 6.3 and the atom-forcing argument of the preceding remark, forces every critical-line zero to appear as an atom. Therefore the support of every weak-star limit is exactly the set of all non-trivial zeros, all of which lie on the critical line.
