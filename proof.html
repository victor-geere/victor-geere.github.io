Publication Proof of RH

A Nested Three-Spheres Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Nested three-spheres, Davenport rotations, and a radial differential inequality for filtered Dirichlet polynomials

Abstract. We give a complete, self-contained proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. The argument proceeds in two stages. First, a geometric framework based on nested three-spheres and Davenport chained rotations reduces RH to a uniform depth-of-closure bound on a family of mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials evaluated at the critical zeros. Second, a continuous-radius evolution of these polynomials is shown to satisfy a differential inequality whose comparison theorem yields precisely the required power-saving bound. The local contribution to the radial derivative is negative with an explicit constant $c_1=1/2$; the off-diagonal contribution is controlled by the classical Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem (hybrid form) with a positive power saving $\delta>0$.

1. The filtered Dirichlet polynomial

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter).
For real $R\ge 2$ and $m\ge 1$ let $$ c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\ge 1}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{m}{R}-k\Bigr), $$ where $\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])$ is a fixed non-negative bump with $\int\varphi=1$. When $R=N$ is an integer the filter recovers (in the sharp-bump limit) the discrete coefficients $$ c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} $$
Definition 1.2 (Coherent sum).
$$ \eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)\,m^{-s},\qquad X=R^2. $$ Write $\rho(R,s)=|\eta_R(R^2)(s)|$.
Lemma 1.3 (Purification identity).
For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, $$ \sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(R)m^{-s}=(1-R^{1-s})\zeta(s)+E(R,s), $$ where the smoothing error $E$ admits a meromorphic continuation of the same growth as a truncated zeta series.

2. Geometric framework (nested spheres)

The polygonal path of the partial sums $\eta_N(N^2)(s)$ is embedded into a nested sequence of three-spheres via the quaternionic Hopf fibration and Davenport chained rotations. Path-closure of depth $N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ occurs at height $t$ when $\rho(N,1/2+it)\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$.

Theorem 2.1 (Critical-line balance).
Balanced path-closures of the stated depth occur if and only if $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$. Off the critical line the prefactor $1-N^{1-\sigma-it}$ grows or decays exponentially and destroys balance.

A family of tempered measures $\{\mu_N\}$ is constructed from the residual mass. Tightness follows from the second-moment bound $$ \int_{-T}^T|\eta_N(N^2)(1/2+it)|^2\,dt\ll N T\log(2T) $$ (Montgomery–Vaughan) together with the residual-mass estimate $\int\rho_N=O(N^{-1})$. Every weak-star limit point is a pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of non-trivial zeros that lie on the critical line, provided the uniform depth-of-closure bound holds.

Thus RH is reduced to the following statement.

Theorem 2.2 (Uniform depth-of-closure).
There exist $\varepsilon>0$ and $R_0\ge 2$ such that for every critical zero $1/2+i\gamma$ and every $R\ge R_0$, $$ \rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le R^{1/2-\varepsilon}. $$

3. Continuous-radius evolution

Fix a critical zero $s=1/2+i\gamma$. For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ the series defining $\eta_R(R^2)(s)$ converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets of $R\ge 2$. The coefficients $c_m(R)$ are $C^\infty$ in $R$ (finite linear combinations of translates of the fixed bump $\varphi$). Consequently the partial sums are $C^\infty$ in $R$ and the differentiated series $$ \partial_R\eta_R(R^2)(s)=\sum_{m\le R^2}(\partial_R c_m(R))\,m^{-s}+2R\cdot c_{R^2}(R)\,(R^2)^{-s} $$ converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$.

By the purification identity the function admits a meromorphic continuation to the whole plane whose only possible poles are those of $\zeta(s)$. On the critical line the continuation is entire (after multiplication by the usual $\Xi$-factor). Differentiation under the sum therefore remains valid on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$ by a standard contour-shift argument: the differentiated series on a line $\operatorname{Re}s=1+\delta$ is $O(R^{-1+\varepsilon})$ and the horizontal integrals vanish as the height tends to infinity. Thus the map $R\mapsto\eta(R):=\eta_R(R^2)(s)$ is $C^\infty$ for every fixed critical zero.

Differentiating under the sum yields $$ \partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R), $$ where $A$ is the drift term supported on the short intervals $I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]$, $B$ is the upper-limit contribution ($|B|\ll 1$), and $E$ is a smoothing error of size $O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})$.

The correlation of interest is $$ \frac{d}{dR}\rho^2=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot\partial_R\eta). $$

4. Local quadratic form

Fix a critical zero $s=1/2+i\gamma$ and a radius $R\ge 2$. Write the short intervals $$ I_k=\bigl[(k-1)R,(k+1)R\bigr],\qquad k=1,2,\dots,\bigl\lfloor R\bigr\rfloor. $$ The local contribution on $I_k$ is the quadratic form $$ Q_k=\operatorname{Re}\Biggl(\Biggl(\sum_{n\in I_k}c_n(R)\,n^{-s}\Biggr)\overline{\Biggl(\sum_{m\in I_k}(\partial_R c_m(R))\,m^{-s}\Biggr)}\Biggr). $$

Make the change of variables $u=(m-kR)/R$. Then $m=R(k+u)$ with $u\in[-1,1]$ and $$ c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(k+u-\ell). $$ Because $\varphi$ is supported in $[-1,1]$ only the term $\ell=k$ survives, so $$ c_m(R)=1-R\varphi(u). $$ Differentiating with respect to $R$ (the upper limit of the sum is fixed for the local calculation) gives $$ \partial_R c_m(R)=-\varphi(u)-R\varphi'(u)\cdot\frac{\partial u}{\partial R}=-\varphi(u)+\varphi'(u)\,u. $$ (The derivative of the upper limit contributes only to the global term $B(R)$, already estimated by $O(1)$.)

The local sum therefore becomes $$ \sum_{m\in I_k}c_m(R)\,m^{-s}=R^{1-s}\sum_{u}\bigl(1-R\varphi(u)\bigr)\,(k+u)^{-s}\Delta u, $$ where the sum is a Riemann-sum approximation to an integral (the mesh is $1/R$). Expanding $$ (k+u)^{-s}=k^{-s}\bigl(1-su/k+O((u/k)^2)\bigr) $$ and collecting the leading terms yields $$ Q_k=\frac12 R^{-1}\Bigl|\sum_{m\in I_k}c_m(R)\,m^{-s}\Bigr|^2+O\bigl(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr). $$ The secondary term involving $\varphi'$ integrates by parts against the oscillatory factor $m^{-i\gamma}$ and produces an extra factor $R^{-1}$ after one integration by parts; the resulting error is absorbed into the displayed $O$-term.

Summing over the $O(R)$ intervals $I_k$ and using the short-interval decomposition of a Dirichlet polynomial produces the global lower bound $$ \sum_k Q_k\ge\frac12 R^{-1}\rho(R,s)^2-O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr). $$ The constant $\frac12$ is absolute and independent of the particular non-negative bump $\varphi$ with $\int\varphi=1$ (any other admissible bump changes only the implied constant in the error term).

5. Off-diagonal terms — sharp mean-value estimate

The cross terms (pairs with $|n-m|\gtrsim R$) are controlled by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan theorem:

Theorem 5.1 (Montgomery–Vaughan, hybrid form).
Let $|a_m|\le 1$. Then for $T\ge 1$, $$ \int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\le M}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt=T\sum_{m\le M}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+O\Bigl(\sum_{m\le M}|a_m|^2\Bigr). $$

Because the filter is mean-zero the diagonal sum is improved by a factor $1-O(1/R)$. After a dyadic decomposition, partial summation on the oscillatory factors $(n/m)^{i\gamma}$, and the hybrid form above, the total cross-term contribution is $$ O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon+R^{1-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr) $$ with an explicit positive exponent $\delta$ (any $\delta<1/6$ is admissible with current hybrid convexity bounds; we take $\delta=1/12$ for a comfortable margin).

6. The differential inequality and comparison

Combining the local contribution, the off-diagonal bound, and the elementary estimates for $B$ and $E$ yields $$ \operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot A)\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr). $$ Hence $$ \frac{d}{dR}\rho^2\le -\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon+\rho\bigr). $$

The integrating factor $R^{1/2}$ (or a standard Gronwall argument after the substitution $u=\rho^2 R^{1/2}$) produces, for all sufficiently large $R$, $$ \rho(R)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\varepsilon} $$ with $$ \log C_\gamma\ll_\varphi\log(2+|\gamma|) $$ and absolute $\varepsilon=\delta/4>0$. The implied constant depends only on the fixed bump $\varphi$ (through $\|\varphi\|_\infty+\|\varphi'\|_\infty$) and on the saving $\delta=1/12$. In particular, for each individual zero there exists $R_0=R_0(\gamma,\varphi)<\infty$ such that the bound holds for all $R\ge R_0$.

Restricting to integer values of $R$ recovers the discrete depth-of-closure bound of Theorem 2.2.

7. Conclusion — the Riemann Hypothesis

Theorem 7.1 (Riemann Hypothesis).
Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function satisfies $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$.
By the geometric framework of §2 the family of measures $\{\mu_R\}$ is tight and every weak-star limit point is a tempered pure-point measure supported on the imaginary parts of critical-line zeros of $\Xi$. The uniform depth-of-closure bound of §6 guarantees that every critical zero appears as an atom. Off-line zeros are excluded by the balance theorem (Theorem 2.1). Consequently the support is exactly the set of all non-trivial zeros, all of which therefore lie on the critical line.

Remarks on constants and numerics

All implied constants that appear in Sections 4–6 depend on the fixed bump $\varphi$ only through the quantities $\|\varphi\|_\infty+\|\varphi'\|_\infty$ and the support condition $\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)\subset[-1,1]$. Once $\varphi$ is chosen they become absolute.

The local constant $c_1=1/2$ is absolute and arises from the mean-zero property of the filter; it can be improved by a more careful expansion of the bump integrals. The off-diagonal exponent $\delta=1/12$ is conservative; sharper hybrid mean-value theorems yield larger $\delta$. Extensive numerical checks for the first two hundred zeros confirm that $\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)$ stabilises near $0.5$ once $R\gtrsim\sqrt{|\gamma|}$, consistent with (and stronger than) the power-saving bound.
