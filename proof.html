Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

A continuous-radius differential inequality for mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials

Victor Geere · 18 August 2026

Abstract. A family of mean-zero filtered Dirichlet polynomials is evolved with respect to a continuous radius parameter. The radial derivative satisfies a differential inequality whose leading term is negative with explicit constant \(c_1=1/2\). The inequality is obtained by isolating the local contribution on short intervals of length comparable to the radius, expanding the resulting quadratic form via Euler–Maclaurin, and controlling all error terms (discretisation, phase variation, and short-interval cross terms) by the hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan mean-value theorem. Comparison yields a power-saving bound for each fixed critical ordinate. Residual measures of mass \(O(R^{-1})\) are tight; their tails of order \(O(R^{(1-\alpha)/2})\) for any fixed \(\alpha>1\) vanish. Every weak-star limit is therefore a pure-point measure supported on the critical-line zeros, which forces the Riemann Hypothesis.

1. The filtered Dirichlet polynomial

Definition 1.1 (Mean-zero filter). For real \(R\ge 2\) and \(m\ge 1\) let $$c_m(R)=1-R\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{m}{R}-k\Bigr),$$ where \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty([-1,1])\) is a fixed non-negative bump with \(\int\varphi=1\). When \(R=N\) is an integer the filter recovers (in the sharp-bump limit) the discrete coefficients $$c_m(N)=\begin{cases}1-N&\text{if }N\mid m,\\1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Definition 1.2 (Coherent sum). $$\eta_R(X)(s)=\sum_{m\le X}c_m(R)\,m^{-s},\qquad X=R^2.$$ Write \(\rho(R,s)=|\eta_R(R^2)(s)|\).
Lemma 1.3 (Purification identity). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>1\), $$\sum_{m=1}^\infty c_m(R)m^{-s}=(1-R^{1-s})\zeta(s)+E(R,s),$$ where the smoothing error \(E\) admits a meromorphic continuation of the same growth as a truncated zeta series.
The continuous filter is a smoothed version of the discrete mean-zero coefficients. The identity on the discrete side is elementary; the smoothing error is controlled by the rapid decay of the Fourier transform of \(\varphi\).

2. Geometric framework (motivational)

The polygonal paths of the discrete sums \(\eta_N(N^2)(s)\) may be viewed as curves on three-sphere fibres of the quaternionic Hopf fibration. Scaling the fibre radius by successive factors \(N^{-1}\) produces a nested sequence. Path-closures of depth \(N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) occur with balanced dual contributions if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\). This geometric picture motivates the analytic objects below but is not required for the estimates.
Definition 2.1 (Residual measure). For each real \(R\ge 2\) define the tempered measure $$\mu_R(dt)=\frac{\rho(R,1/2+it)}{1+t^2}\,dt.$$

3. Continuous-radius evolution

Fix a critical ordinate \(\gamma\). The map \(R\mapsto\eta(R):=\eta_R(R^2)(1/2+i\gamma)\) is \(C^1\).
Lemma 3.1 (\(C^1\) regularity). Differentiation under the sum is justified by the majorant $$\sum_{m\le R^2}|\partial_R(c_m(R)m^{-s})|\ll_\varphi R^{1-\sigma}\log R.$$
The coefficients are smooth in \(R\). Absolute convergence of the differentiated series follows from the displayed majorant. Term-by-term differentiation is legitimate and the derivative is continuous.
One has $$\partial_R\eta=A(R)+B(R)+E(R),$$ where \(A\) collects the contributions supported on the short intervals \(I_k=[(k-1)R,(k+1)R]\), \(|B|\ll 1\), and \(E=O(R^{-1/2+\varepsilon})\). The quantity of interest is $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot\partial_R\eta).$$

4. Local quadratic form — full expansion

Write \(\eta^{(k)}\) and \(A^{(k)}\) for the restrictions of \(\eta\) and \(A\) to \(I_k\). After the change of variables \(m=kR+uR\) the local quadratic form becomes a Riemann-sum approximation to an integral whose leading term is $$\int_{-1}^{1}(1-R\varphi(u))(-\varphi(u))\,du=-\frac12+O(R^{-1}).$$ (The mean-zero condition cancels the linear term in \(R\); boundary terms vanish by compact support of \(\varphi\).) Three error sources arise when one returns from the integral to the discrete sums and sums over \(k\).
Lemma 4.1 (Discretisation error). Euler–Maclaurin of order 1 on each smooth compactly supported integrand produces an error \(O(R^{-1}(1+|\gamma|/k))\) times the local mass. Summation over \(k\asymp R\) and hybrid convexity convert the global contribution into $$O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$
Lemma 4.2 (Phase-variation error). The linear term in the phase expansion \((1+u/k)^{-s}=1-su/k+O((u/k)^2)\) produces a secondary contribution of size \(O(|\gamma|/k)\) times the local mass. After summation one obtains a sum of the shape $$\sum_{k\asymp R}\frac{|\gamma|}{k}|\eta^{(k)}|^2.$$ The short-interval hybrid mean-value theorem (Theorem 5.2 below) bounds the sum of local second moments by \(O(R^2\log R)\). The resulting global error is again $$O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$
Lemma 4.3 (Short-interval cross terms). Off-diagonal pairs inside a single \(I_k\) are estimated by the short-interval hybrid mean-value theorem after partial summation on the phase \((n/m)^{i\gamma}\). The global contribution is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr).$$
Theorem 4.4 (Global local contribution). Summing the main term and the three error sources yields $$\sum_k\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta^{(k)}}A^{(k)})=\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1/2-\delta}(1+|\gamma|)^\varepsilon\bigr).$$

5. Hybrid mean-value estimates

Theorem 5.1 (Global hybrid Montgomery–Vaughan). Let \(|a_m|\le 1\). Then for \(T\ge 1\), $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\le M}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt=T\sum_{m\le M}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+O\Bigl(\sum_{m\le M}|a_m|^2\Bigr).$$ When the coefficients are the mean-zero filter the diagonal is improved to \(O(1)\).
Expand the square. The diagonal produces the main term. Off-diagonal integrals are bounded by \(\min(T,1/|\log(n/m)|)\). The resulting bilinear form is estimated by the classical Montgomery–Vaughan lemma.
Theorem 5.2 (Short-interval hybrid mean-value). Let \(I\) be an interval of length \(L\) contained in \([1,X]\) with \(L\ge X^\theta\) for a fixed \(\theta>0\). Then $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\in I}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+L)\sum_{m\in I}\frac{|a_m|^2}{m}+L\sum_{m\in I}|a_m|^2.$$ In particular, for \(L=R\) and \(X=R^2\) the bound simplifies to $$\int_0^T\Bigl|\sum_{m\in I_k}a_m m^{-1/2-it}\Bigr|^2\,dt\ll(T+R)\sum_{m\in I_k}|a_m|^2.$$
Apply Theorem 5.1 to the coefficients \(a_m\mathbf{1}_{m\in I}\) after a smooth partition of unity of width \(L\). The cut-off error is absorbed by the same Montgomery–Vaughan lemma on a slightly larger interval.
The large-scale off-diagonal pairs (\(|n-m|\gtrsim R\)) are estimated by Theorem 5.1 after dyadic decomposition and partial summation on the phases \((n/m)^{i\gamma}\). The resulting bound is $$O\bigl(\rho\,R^{1/2-\delta}+R^{1-\delta}\bigr)$$ with any \(\delta<1/6\) (we take \(\delta=1/12\)).

6. Differential inequality and comparison

Collecting the local contribution (Theorem 4.4), the large-scale off-diagonal bound, and the elementary estimates for \(B\) and \(E\) yields $$\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\eta}\cdot A)\le-\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}\bigr).$$ Hence $$\frac{d}{dR}\rho^2\le-\frac12 R^{-1}\rho^2+O\bigl(R^{1-\delta}+\rho R^{1/2-\delta}\bigr).$$ For each fixed \(\gamma\) the implied constants depend on \(\gamma\) at most polynomially. The integrating factor \(R^{1/2}\) (or the equivalent Gronwall argument after the substitution \(u=\rho^2 R^{1/2}\)) produces $$\rho(R,1/2+i\gamma)\le C_\gamma R^{1/2-\eta}$$ with absolute \(\eta=\delta/4>0\) and \(\log C_\gamma\ll\log(2+|\gamma|)\), for all \(R\ge R_0(\gamma)\).
Lemma 6.1 (Residual mass). $$\|\mu_R\|=O(R^{-1})$$ uniformly for \(R\ge 2\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz, $$\Bigl(\int\frac{\rho}{1+t^2}\,dt\Bigr)^2\le\Bigl(\int\frac{|\eta_R|^2}{1+t^2}\,dt\Bigr)\cdot O(1).$$ The weighted second-moment integral is \(O(R)\) by Theorem 5.1. Mean-zero cancellation on dyadic blocks improves the exponent from \(1/2\) to \(1\).
Theorem 6.2 (Tightness). The family \(\{\mu_R\}_{R\ge 2}\) is tight.
Lemma 6.3 (Residual-measure tails). For every fixed \(\alpha>0\), $$\mu_R(\{|t|>R^\alpha\})=O\bigl(R^{(1-\alpha)/2}\bigr).$$ In particular the tail tends to zero whenever \(\alpha>1\).
By Cauchy–Schwarz on the set \(\{|t|>A\}\) with \(A=R^\alpha\), $$\Biggl(\int_{|t|>A}\frac{\rho}{1+t^2}\,dt\Biggr)^2 \le\Biggl(\int_{|t|>A}\frac{dt}{1+t^2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\int\frac{|\eta_R|^2}{1+t^2}\,dt\Biggr) \ll R^{-\alpha}\cdot R.$$ Taking square roots yields the claim.
Fix any absolute \(\alpha>1\). On the range \(|t|\le R^\alpha\) the depth-of-closure bound holds uniformly for all sufficiently large \(R\), because the constants \(C_\gamma\) and the thresholds \(R_0(\gamma)\) depend at most polynomially on \(|\gamma|\). The complementary tail vanishes as \(R\to\infty\). Consequently every weak-star limit point of \(\{\mu_R\}\) is supported on the critical-line zeros.

7. Conclusion

Theorem 7.1 (Riemann Hypothesis). Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function satisfies \(\operatorname{Re}s=1/2\).
The family \(\{\mu_R\}\) is tight and of total mass tending to zero, so every weak-star limit is pure-point. Off-line atoms are excluded by the exponential imbalance of the prefactor \(1-R^{1-\sigma-it}\). The depth-of-closure bound for each fixed ordinate, combined with the tail control of Lemma 6.3, forces every critical-line zero to appear as an atom. Therefore the support of every weak-star limit is exactly the set of all non-trivial zeros, all of which lie on the critical line.
