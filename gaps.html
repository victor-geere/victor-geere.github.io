LThere exist \(\delta\in(0,1/4)\), \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/6)\) and \(c>0\) such that, for every zero \(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) of \(\Xi\) with \(\lvert\sigma-\tfrac12\rvert\ge\delta\) and every integer \(N\) large enough that \(\lvert\gamma\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), \[ \bigl\lvert\eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\bigr\rvert\ge c\,N^{1/2-\varepsilon}. \] Geometrically this is non-degeneracy of the Hilbert zoom of Lemma 4.3.4: nested completed curves, granularity \(N^{-1}\), parameter window \(\lvert t\rvert\le N^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), rescaled by \(D_N\). The fractal therefore limits L to that scale and window — the unique zoom in which the approximations behave like Hilbert polygons on a unit interval. Collapse of the zoom is a balanced path-closure; L says the zoom does not collapse at off-line zeros. Analytically the identity remains open: Corollary 4.3.1 still gives \(\lvert\eta(\rho)\rvert\ll(1+\lvert\gamma\rvert)N^{1-2\sigma}=o(D_N)\) in the band, so Euler–Maclaurin predicts extra points of convergence of the same zoom. If L holds, Proposition 4.6 and Theorem 12.4 turn Corollary 12.3 into a theorem.
