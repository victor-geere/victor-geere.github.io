The remaining gap, collapsed

Frozen identities: truths.md §§11–14. Nothing below is a proof of the Riemann hypothesis. It is the same gap written at the unique place a proof would have to sit. Item T is closed (Lemmas 9.2–9.6); it concerned the tails of the measures, not the position of the atoms.

The last chord \(H_N(s)=\tfrac12(1-N)N^{-2s}\) multiplies across twins as an identity of monomials, for every \(s\):

\[ H_N(s)\,H_N(1-s)=\frac{(N-1)^2}{4N^2}\to\tfrac14. \]

On the tightness window a zero of \(\Xi\) tracks this chord to relative error \(O(N^{-2\varepsilon})\), so

\[ \eta_N^{(N^2)}(\rho)\,\eta_N^{(N^2)}(1-\rho)\to\tfrac14. \]

Both factors remain of order one if and only if \(\sigma=\tfrac12\). Off the line, one factor tends to \(0\) (south pole, unrestricted atom) and the other to \(\infty\) (north pole, discarded by Marty). That is the whole geometric content of an off-line quadruple.

The same dichotomy in the cosine kernel: \(\Xi(\tfrac12+it)\) is a constant multiple of \(\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cos(tu)\,du\) with \(\Phi>0\) even, and at an off-line zero the reweighted moment \(\int\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\cos(\gamma u)\,du\) vanishes for \(\alpha=\sigma-\tfrac12\neq 0\), together with its odd companion \(\int\Phi(u)\sinh(\alpha u)\sin(\gamma u)\,du\); non-vanishing of the even moment alone would already forbid the zero. Positivity of \(\Phi\) does not forbid that vanishing. Weil positivity, Laguerre–Pólya membership of the cosine transform, de Bruijn–Newman \(\Lambda=0\), and the absence of a right-half atom of \(\nu\) are four names for the claim that it does not happen.

The unique self-dual scale is proved. The occupation of any other scale by a zero of this \(\Phi\) is the remaining statement. Adjacent truths locate it; they do not cancel it.

Further research. A proof, had it existed here, would have been a proof that \(\int\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\cos(\gamma u)\,du\) cannot vanish for \(\alpha\neq 0\). Writing that sentence as a theorem would be lying. The adjacent truths define the object; they do not force \(\Phi\) to be in the class that kills non-real zeros.

Where that equation sits

Frozen as truths.md §15. Write \(K(\alpha,\gamma)=\int_0^\infty\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\cos(\gamma u)\,du\).

Three slices, of which two are already decided.

  • \(\gamma=0\): \(K(\alpha,0)=\int\Phi(u)\cosh(\alpha u)\,du>0\). No oscillation, positivity of \(\Phi\) wins. An identity, like the last-chord product.
  • \(\alpha=0\): \(K(0,\gamma)\) vanishes at the on-line zeros. Oscillation is supposed to win.
  • \(\alpha\neq 0\), \(\gamma\neq 0\): \(K(\alpha,\cdot)\) is real-analytic, so it vanishes if and only if it changes sign. This is the remaining slice.

\(L^2\) positivity of \(K(\alpha,\cdot)\) is Parseval against \(\Phi^2\cosh^2(\alpha u)\), always true, and does not prevent zeros. That is the withdrawn envelope again.

The mixed slice is the Taylor jet of the cosine transform at \(t=0\): the even moments \(m_{2k}=\int\Phi(u)u^{2k}\,du\). Those moments exist. The Jensen polynomials they generate are real-rooted at every degree if and only if \(\Xi_{\mathrm{cos}}\) is Laguerre–Pólya. The same jet, written where the filter \(1-N^{1-s}\) dies, is Li’s coefficients at \(s=1\). The polygon can read the moments at \(t=0\); it is blind at \(\sigma=1\). Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier occupy this place asymptotically at fixed degree. Uniformity in the degree is the full tower, which is the Riemann hypothesis.

There is no closer point: one step back is the solved slice \(K(\alpha,0)>0\); one step forward is the tautology \(K\neq 0\). The same sentence, written on \(\mathbb{R}\) without \(\xi\), is the positive-type pairing of tensor.html.
